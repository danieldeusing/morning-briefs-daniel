Qwen3.8-Max: the headline, and the table underneath it

Alibaba released Qwen3.8-Max on Aug 3, 2026, a mixture-of-experts model with 2.4 trillion parameters. The coverage frames it as a model that beats Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol. Deeper in the same source's fuller text sit the two coding figures that go with that claim — and they show something else: "It reports 67.7 on SWE-bench Pro against Fable 5's 80.0, and 73.5 on FrontierSWE against Fable 5's 88.8."

That's not a close race. It's 12.3 points behind on the harder of the two coding boards, 15.3 on the other. For the toolbox, the blunt read is this: Qwen3.8-Max is no substitute for the model you'd trust with today's hard change. It's a very large runner-up — which, if the weights actually show up, can still be worth a lot.

marktechpost.com · Aug 3, 2026, retrieved Aug 4, 2026 · vendor figures, not an independent board
Mixture-of-Experts (MoE): a model split internally into many specialists, only a small share of which handle any given request. Like a big law firm: the door lists two hundred lawyers, but three of them work your case. That's why the total parameter count says almost nothing about speed and cost — what matters is the number of active parameters, meaning how many specialists actually run per request. A 2.4-trillion-parameter model with few active parameters can run cheaper than a model a tenth its size that always fires up everything. That's exactly the number Alibaba didn't publish.

The real story is the jump against itself

Stack Qwen3.8-Max against its own predecessor instead of against Anthropic, and the picture looks completely different: DeepSWE 1.1 goes from 21.6 to 56.6, FrontierSWE from 40.7 to 73.5. One generation, and the gap to the top shrinks from "not in the same room" to "one board's width."

That's the number that matters for open weights. Not whether the model wins today, but how fast the gap is closing — because a model whose weights you can download doesn't compete with the top model, it competes with what you'd otherwise be paying. To actually try it today, though, it's still missing a license, size figures, and any independent measurement.

Why three sites show the same board differently: none of these three sites is SWE-Bench. They're aggregators that collect third-party results, sometimes re-measure some themselves, and pour it all into their own table. Like three newspapers counting the same election: one already has every precinct in, one is still waiting on two, one rounds differently. As long as the gaps are wide, that never shows. When the top of the field sits — as it does here — within about a point, the counting method decides from there on, not the candidate. One of the sites says so itself: the best models sit "within 1.0 points," it notes, with the benchmark approaching saturation for top models.

One board over, and suddenly DeepSeek wins

How much the choice of board shapes the outcome shows up on LiveCodeBench. On today's dated state, DeepSeek holds the top four spots there: V4-Pro-Max at 0.935, V4-Flash-Max at 0.916, two entries of V3.2 at 0.833, then MiniMax M2 at 0.830. No Anthropic model in the top 5 — on the three Verified boards above, between one and seven Anthropic models come before the first non-Anthropic entry, depending on the aggregator.

Both observations can be true at once, because the boards test different things: Verified measures fixing real bugs in real projects, LiveCodeBench measures solving self-contained programming problems from continuously refreshed contests. My read: for agentic work inside your own repository, Verified is the closer proxy; for "write me this function," it's the other one. Anyone who knows both numbers doesn't buy the model with the loudest superlative — they buy the one that matches the board.

27 days until the new Sonnet price

This is today's item with a hard date. Claude Sonnet 5 has run at an intro price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens since launch. Anthropic gives a specific cutoff for that: "through August 31, 2026." From Sep 1, 2026, it's $3 and $15 — a 50% increase on both sides, with nothing changing about the model itself.

In practice: whatever large, token-hungry runs are still on the table this month — migrations, mass refactors, a pass over the entire repository — cost a third less in August than in September. That's no reason to rush, but it is a reason not to push the two or three expensive projects into fall.

Which model is actually your default in the first place

The mapping is less clear-cut than it sounds, and worth a look at your own settings. Per the Claude Code changelog, Sonnet 5 has been the default model for Pro, Team Standard, and Enterprise since the week of Jun 29 — with a native 1M-token context and adaptive thinking on by default. On Max, Team Premium, and API accounts, the default has instead been Opus 4.8 since late May. So anyone on Max only feels the Sonnet price change where they explicitly select Sonnet or run through the API.
