The Discount Lands Exactly Where Agents Get Expensive

On Sep 1, 2026, Anthropic made Claude Fable 5.1 generally available — via the API, the cloud platforms, and the Claude desktop app. But the news for you isn't in the model, it's in the bill: reading from the prompt cache now costs 75% less, just $0.25 per million tokens. Anthropic itself puts the effect at roughly 25% savings under typical load and up to 45% for heavily agentic tasks. The regular rate is unchanged: for Fable 5.1, Anthropic explicitly cites the same rates as Fable 5 — $10 per million input, $50 per million output. The page lists no separate price for Mythos 5.1.

Why the cache line item specifically: An agent works in loops. At every step — read a file, run a test, write a patch — the entire prior context comes along again: system prompt, project rules, thirty files, the whole conversation so far. If the vendor has cached that block, it doesn't have to recompute it every time — like a copy shop that already has the template loaded. After twenty steps, that repeated block is the bill, and the clever answer at the end is pocket change. That's exactly the line item Anthropic just cut to a quarter.
$1.00 per million cache reads → since Sep 1 → $0.25

What's independently confirmed so far is mainly that the model exists — and of all things, via a tool. Cline v4.1.17, released Sep 2 at 05:40 UTC, switches its Anthropic default from Claude Opus 5 to Claude Fable 5.1 and updates the defaults for 57 providers along with it. That's not a benchmark. But a tool maker flipping its default within a day is a signal you can't buy.

My take. Try it The discount isn't a gift to everyone — it's a gift to a particular working style. If you run long sessions with stable context, you collect it; if you restart at every doubt, you throw it away each time. The test costs you an hour: run the same agentic task twice, once with your current default model, once with Fable 5.1 — and afterward, don't compare the benchmark table, compare the bill. That's the one number from this announcement you can verify yourself.

Same Model, Two Doors — and an Invisible Watermark

Alongside Fable 5.1, Claude Mythos 5.1 arrived the same day. Anthropic's own phrasing: Fable 5.1 is generally available, Mythos 5.1 only through "trusted access programs" — with safeguards explicitly built for work in cybersecurity and biosciences. A sibling model that exists, but not for you.

Both carry an invisible watermark in their output, in the sense of the EU AI Act — and that's stated on Anthropic's own announcement page: a watermark as a "numerical way to determine how likely it is that Claude helped write a piece of text," introduced for the output of all models released after Aug 2, 2026, and "invisible to anyone without the detection API." So "invisible" doesn't mean hidden special characters, but a statistical pattern in word choice: a detection tool can pick it up, a human sees nothing — like a watermark in paper that only shows up against the light. Caveat: only the framing that these are the first Claude models with a watermark appears exclusively in secondary reporting — Anthropic itself states a cutoff-date rule, not a debut.

My take. For prose, that makes sense. For code, it's a strange construction: source code is syntactically tight — there's little room for the kind of word-choice variation such a signal would even consist of. And what's left of it once the patch runs through a formatter, gets merged, and rewritten three times? No source I found answers that. The question turns practical the moment a client contractually wants to know which parts of their code came from a model. If you're shipping model output into someone else's codebase, better to ask yourself that question before they ask it of you.

Yesterday a Rumor, Today a Price List With an Expiration Date

Update since yesterday In the Sep 2 brief, Gemini 3.8 Flash was still an unconfirmed press report with an internal codename. Today the model card is out: Gemini 3.8 Flash and the variant Gemini 3.8 Flash Cyber were released on Sep 2, 2026. Context: up to 1M tokens input, 64,000 tokens output.

The price has a built-in expiration date. $0.75 per million input tokens and $3.75 per million output tokens — but only until Dec 31, 2026. As of Jan 1, 2027, it's $1.50 and $7.50, exactly double.

And now the uncomfortable part. Google's own model card renders its benchmark table as an image — unreadable to a fetch. The only figure I can quote verbatim from a source today is 54.9% on HLE-Verified, a test set for multi-step reasoning. The Terminal-Bench and DeepSWE figures circulating everywhere, I couldn't find verbatim in any primary source. So they don't appear here.

My take. Launch pricing with a doubling date is a different product than a cheap model. For four months you budget at $0.75, then on New Year's Day your per-unit cost changes without a single line of your code changing. Put Jan 1 on your calendar now, not in December. As for the Cyber variant: my sources say nothing about its access terms — at Anthropic, the cyber counterpart is explicitly locked down. Two labs, within 48 hours, both with a security-tinted sibling model: that's the real story of this week, and it doesn't show up on any bench table.

The Independent Table Doesn't Know Any of Them

Four new models in 48 hours, four vendor tables. The one independent leaderboard I could pull today — llm-stats.com for SWE-Bench Verified, page timestamp Sep 3, 2026 — lists none of them. Still on top: Claude Fable 5, no .1, at 95.0%.

SWE-Bench Verified — Top 8, as of Sep 3, 2026 (llm-stats.com)

Seven of the eight spots go to Claude models; only at #8 does another vendor show up, with DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Max. That says less about the models than about the board: if you're not submitted and measured, you don't appear. On SWE-Bench Verified, a model gets real, already-solved bug reports from open-source projects and has to write a patch itself; success counts only if the test suite comes back green afterward. Closer to real work than a quiz — but getting added to the table is its own process, and it takes longer than a product announcement.

How worthless a vendor figure without a version number is, Terminal-Bench shows today. Anthropic cites 55.8% for Fable 5.1 and 60.9% for Mythos 5.1 — explicitly on Terminal-Bench 4.0. The circulating Gemini figures refer to Terminal-Bench 2.1. Same name, different test; put the numbers side by side and you're comparing nothing. As a contrast: a blog post quotes a single forum comment that puts Gemini 3.8 Flash on Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 19.1% against 51.8% for Claude Opus 5. I put no stock in an anonymous comment — but the gap to the roughly 90% circulating for the other version shows the problem in one line.

And the boards that should settle this don't: swebench.com loads its Verified table via JavaScript, so a fetch returns no numbers. The Aider leaderboard has been frozen since Apr 12, 2025 — its leader is still "o1" at 84.2%. Almost a year and a half. As a basis for decisions, that's not a board anymore — it's a monument.

My take, and I'll defend it. A benchmark figure without a version number, without a described setup, and without independent verification is advertising. Exactly that kind of figure showed up four times today. Don't wait for the board — by the time Fable 5.1 is on it, 5.2 will be out. Build yourself the two-hour test from your own codebase instead: five real tasks from your repository, the same environment, the same grading, once per model. It's messier than SWE-Bench and still the better number, because it measures your code, not the task pool everyone trains against.

Briefly First — and Overtaken the Same Day

Alibaba released the snapshot Qwen3.8-Max-0902 on Sep 2, 2026 — hosted, not open. On Arena.ai's Code Arena (WebDev), a 22-point jump put it in the top spot: per Arena.ai's own report, 3 points ahead of Claude Opus 5 (Max) and 17 ahead of Kimi K3 (Max). Blended pricing runs around $5 per million tokens.

First place didn't last a day. Arena.ai's changelog logs the addition of claude-fable-5.1-max to the same leaderboard on Sep 2, 2026 — the same day. As of today's pull (Sep 3, 2026), Fable 5.1 (Max) leads with 1,765 points; Qwen3.8-Max-0902 sits at 1,688 in second place, one point ahead of Claude Opus 5 (Max) at 1,687, with Kimi K3 (Max) behind at 1,674.

What an arena score is: Not a test with right answers. People see the same task solved by two anonymous models and vote on which solution they like better. Many such duels get turned into a number the way chess ratings work: beat strong opponents often, and you climb. That measures real judgment on real output — but it's judgment it's measuring, and the score wobbles with every new sample.

My take. A lead that a single new entry wipes out within 24 hours was never a lead — and the one point that still separates Qwen from Opus 5 today is a tie with the better press release. That doesn't make the news worthless — it just means something different than it claims: a model at around $5 per million tokens is playing in the same class as the most expensive thing the market has to offer. The story isn't the ranking, it's that the top is close enough that price decides. Base a tooling decision on an arena ranking, and you're basing it on a number that will look different tomorrow.

The open sibling. Try it Back on Aug 14, 2026, Qwen had already released Qwen3.8-27B under Apache 2.0: 27B parameters, 262,144 tokens of native context. That's the size class that runs quantized on a decent workstation — and here the license is the real story, since Apache 2.0 means commercial use with no questions asked. If you're testing something new this week anyway, this is the candidate that can't be taken away from you afterward.

Total vs. active parameters: The flagship of the line is called Qwen3.8-2.4T-A95B. "Mixture of Experts" means all the weights have to sit in memory, but only a slice of them computes for each generated word — like a law firm with a thousand lawyers, three of whom sit at the table for any given case. The naming convention decodes the numbers: 2.4 trillion parameters total, and the "A" stands for active, i.e., 95B per token. Caveat: one fetch of the GitHub description today read the two figures swapped, and the page that would settle it for good returned an empty body. I'm going with the naming convention and leaving the point open.
