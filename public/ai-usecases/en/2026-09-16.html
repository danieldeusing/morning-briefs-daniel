World's best speech model — and 35.1% in the banking workflow

Google released two models yesterday, on 2026-09-15: Gemini 3.8 Live and the stronger variant, 3.8 Live Extended Thinking. The announcement leads with a top ranking: first place on Artificial Analysis' Speech-to-Speech Quality Index, with 82.6 points.[1] An independent trade publication reported the same launch the same day.[2]

Speech-to-speech: the model listens to audio and responds directly with audio, without the detour through written text. Like a simultaneous interpreter who listens and speaks right away — instead of an interpreter who first writes everything down, reads the note, and then answers. The difference is audible: tone, interruptions, and pauses don't survive the detour.

More interesting than the top ranking is what else is in the same release. On Big Bench Audio, Extended Thinking scores 97.7%. On τ-Voice — voice-based agent tasks — 68.6%. And on Sierra's τ-Voice-banking, the banking-specific cut of the same test, 35.1%.[1]

τ-Voice: not a quiz, a work sample. The model has to actually get something done on the phone — ask follow-up questions, look things up, make changes, confirm them. What's graded isn't whether the answer sounded smart, but whether the end state is correct. Like a trainee at a service desk: what's measured is whether the request got resolved, not whether they were friendly.
Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking — the closer to a real workflow, the lower the score (as of 2026-09-15)

These three bars are today's story. Understanding: solved. Acting in general: two-thirds. Acting inside a specialized process: one-third. And this is the vendor's own number for the world's top-ranked model, in its own marketing copy, on launch day. The most honest line in the announcement isn't the 82.6 — it's the 35.1.

My take. This isn't an argument against voice agents, it's a price tag on scope. An agent that handles rescheduling, address changes, and invoice copies is buyable today. One for the full range of a bank service line is not — two out of three calls end wrong or incomplete. Anyone who doesn't measure that difference before buying measures it after rollout, in angry callers. That's exactly why the first twiceD idea sits below.

Distribution: Gemini API, AI Studio, Gemini Enterprise (closed preview), Search Live, the Gemini Live app, and Workspace inside Docs, Gmail, and Keep.[1] So as of yesterday, the model already sits inside tools millions of people have open anyway — distribution outruns any purchasing decision.

Nine in ten use AI, 37% see it on the bottom line [3]

McKinsey published its annual global AI survey — reaching us here via a wire pickup dated 2026-09-06, though the original report likely appeared in late August. Adoption keeps climbing: about 90% of respondents say their organization regularly uses AI in at least one function; 44% report enterprise-wide scaling, up from 38% a year earlier; 56% use AI in three or more functions, up from 51%.

The returns side is standing still. 37% say AI has contributed positively to EBIT — "broadly unchanged from 2025." Only 6% attribute at least 5% of their EBIT to AI and thereby qualify as "high performers." At the personal level, the picture is sunnier: 80% report better individual productivity, and half report better decisions.

EBIT: what's left of revenue before interest and taxes are deducted — the line the managing director actually looks at. "AI contributes positively to EBIT" doesn't mean "people like it," it means "it shows up in the income statement." Four out of five people feel like they're working faster — and just under two out of five companies see any of that in the number.

My take. The story isn't the gap between 90 and 37, it's that the 37 is holding still while adoption keeps rising. Buying is a decision; returns are a process change. Minutes saved only turn into money once someone actually touches cycle time, a vendor contract, or a headcount line — otherwise they drain away as convenience. The same gap as the 35.1% next door: capability on the test, non-completion in the workflow.

The deadline nobody postponed [8]

Brief, because the calendar ran in full here yesterday. The "Digital Omnibus" package is through: the European Parliament voted 423 to 57 with 174 abstentions on 2026-06-16, and the Council gave its approval on 2026-06-29. Result: the high-risk obligations for Annex III systems slide from 2026-08-02 to 2027-12-02, and for Annex I systems to 2028-08-02.

What gets lost in the noise: Article 50 was not postponed. The transparency obligations have applied since 2026-08-02 — for 45 days now. Users must be able to recognize that they're talking to an AI system, and synthetic content must be labeled.

My take. The discussion is upside down. What's being debated is the obligation that's 442 days away and will never hit most mid-sized companies; what's actually in force is the one that hits anyone running a chat widget on their website or an image generator in marketing. Anyone with an hour of compliance time today should spend it on the chat widget, not on an Annex III risk file.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · An acceptance test rig for voice agents — against the 35 percent cliff

ELI5 & value: a company buys a voice agent for its service hotline. Everything works in the demo. Six months later, callers are furious. Why is right there in today's numbers: the world's top-ranked model completes 68.6% of general voice tasks and 35.1% of banking-like workflows.[1] What it does with your specific workflows, nobody knows — nobody measured it. The product is a customer-owned test rig: 40 to 60 real conversation transcripts from the client's own service history, run against the candidate, scored with a pass rate, an error breakdown, and a clear yes/no. Value: a contact-center rollout costs six figures and takes months; the test rig costs a fraction of that and, at worst, is the cheapest "no" the client ever bought. At best, it's the sign-off document procurement needed anyway.
Prototype (V0): (1) With the service manager, pull 40–60 real transcripts from ticket and call history — especially the messy ones, since that's where it breaks. (2) Translate each transcript into a scripted dialogue with a defined end state: what has to be true in the system afterward? The pass criterion is the end state, not the phrasing — exactly how the τ-Bench-style tests the vendors themselves cite work. (3) A driver runs the candidate through its API (the Gemini Live API or the vendor's test access) and logs the transcript plus tool calls. (4) Two scores per run: the hard end-state check — the only one that's contract-worthy — and a rubric score for tone and escalation. (5) Report: pass rate by case class, error breakdown, a recommendation on scope. Deliberately not included: no building of the agent, no telephony, no production integration. Building blocks: vendor APIs, a τ-Bench-style test pattern, plain Python.
Market & gap: the market is very well funded — and conspicuously German. Parloa (Berlin/Munich) raised $350 million in January 2026 at a $3 billion valuation, over $560 million total, and names Allianz, Booking.com, and IKEA as customers.[9] Cognigy (Düsseldorf) sold to NICE for $955 million — closed September 2025 — and in April 2026 rolled out proactive agents at Openreach across 15 million customer interactions.[10] And Sierra doesn't just build agents, it also builds the τ-Voice test that Google cites:[1] the referee and a player on the field are the same company. That's the gap. The quality evidence a buyer gets today comes either from the vendor or from a competitor's benchmark — never from their own conversations. Nobody offers an independent, fixed-price acceptance test in German for a mid-sized company: it's consulting work, not a subscription, and structurally uninteresting for a platform valued at $3 billion.
Feasibility (solo + AI): High. No hosting, no on-call duty, no model costs beyond the test runs themselves. V0 buildable solo in three to five weeks, then five to ten days per client. Maintenance means: rerun it when the vendor swaps the model — half a day, and that's exactly the honest path to recurring revenue ("regression report per model change"). Scaling happens through an external developer per client, not headcount. Honest catches: you need real conversation data, which is a privacy conversation before it's a technical one — pseudonymize before the first run, or don't do it at all. And you have to be able to hand a client a "no" when they've already signed with the vendor. blog.google · siliconangle.com · cognigy.com

2 · The EBIT trail — the proof of value that 63% can't produce

ELI5 & value: 37% of companies say AI contributes positively to profit — that share hasn't moved in a year, while usage kept climbing.[3] The reason is rarely that AI doesn't work. The reason is that nobody measured the before state, so the after state has nothing to compare against. The product is a tightly scoped engagement that makes exactly one process measurable: four weeks of baseline measurement (cycle time, processing steps, error and rework rate, cost per case), then the same measurement with the AI step added, ending in one line the controller can actually read. Value: either the project can defend itself with a number — or you know that after eight weeks instead of after a two-year subscription.
Prototype (V0): (1) Pick exactly one process with a countable unit: an invoice, a ticket, a quote. (2) Set four metrics with the controller, not with IT — they have to recognize the number later. (3) Four weeks of baseline measurement from whatever's already logged (ERP or ticket export); where nothing is logged, count a sample by hand — inelegant, honest, cheap. (4) Introduce the AI step, keep measuring identically: same duration, same case types. (5) One page for management: before, after, delta — and explicitly, what the number doesn't cover. Deliberately not included: no attribution model, no company-wide metrics system, no dashboard product.
Market & gap: the measurement layer is only sold at the top end — by McKinsey, BCG, and the AI units of the big integrators, at day rates a mid-sized company won't pay for a single process; and those same firms write the studies that bemoan the gap.[3] Tool vendors deliver usage dashboards ("this many licenses, this many requests") — that measures use, not return. The pond is large: per the KI-Index Mittelstand 2026, 51.2% of surveyed German mid-sized companies use or are testing AI (+54% year over year), 16.6% deploy AI agents.[11] The majority has AI in-house, only a minority can name a number for it. This service sits exactly in that gap.
Feasibility (solo + AI): High as consulting, with one caveat: the value depends on data access. If the client logs nothing, the baseline measurement turns into hand-counting and gets more expensive than the savings it's meant to prove — that belongs in the first conversation, not in the proposal. Solo-manageable: six to eight weeks per client, of which ten days are real work, the rest is waiting out the measurement window. No maintenance, no operations. Scaling through a template and an external developer for the data pulls. The biggest catch: you're handing the boss a number that could kill his pet project — that only works if the baseline is locked in before twiceD builds anything on the same process. If the same hand measures and builds at the same time, the number is worthless — and so is the business that's supposed to follow from it. tribuneindia.com · mittelstandsbund.de
