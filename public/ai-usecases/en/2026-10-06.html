The AI that takes notes: named hospital numbers Health [1][2]

At HIMSS26, the big US healthcare-IT conference, two hospital systems put out numbers back in March 2026; a trade outlet recapped them on Aug 13, 2026, so the data itself is about seven months old, not seven weeks. But it's still the best named-case data available.[1]

  • West Virginia University Health System has expanded Abridge's software to more than 2,800 doctors and nurses across 25 hospitals. The reason: user surveys showing 61% less cognitive load and 77% higher job satisfaction.
  • AtlantiCare uses Oracle Health's Clinical AI Agent in its emergency departments and reports 41% less time spent on documentation.
Ambient clinical documentation ("ambient scribe"): A microphone listens in on the conversation between doctor and patient. The AI turns it into a draft for the patient's chart. The doctor reads it, corrects it, and signs off. It's like having a note-taker in the room who never gets tired. The doctor still makes every treatment decision.
Conversation→Transcript→Structured draft→Doctor reviews and approves→Patient chart
Methodology The 61% and 77% figures come from surveys of the users themselves. They measure how people feel, not minutes. AtlantiCare's 41% is a time figure, but the report names no baseline measurement and no sample size. Both numbers came out at a conference where the vendors exhibit. Read them as "the direction is right," not as a hard measurement.

The German counterpart already exists, in elder care. Berlin startup voize closed a $50 million funding round in November 2025, led by Balderton Capital. By its own account, more than 1,100 care facilities in Germany and Austria use the app, with over 75,000 caregivers. voize puts the documentation workload reduction at around 30%.[2] That, too, is a vendor figure.

My take: Three independent cases, three country contexts, and all point the same way: 30 to 40% less writing work. That's more credible than any survey on "productivity," precisely because the task is so narrow. The trick isn't the model. The trick is that a human signs off on the draft before it counts. That keeps liability clear, and the AI doesn't need to be perfect. This pattern fits any job that has to write a report after the work is done. More on that below, in the project idea.

7-day context: usage high, impact flat Study [3][4][5]

The full numbers ran in the brief from Oct 5. Here's the state of play in three lines:

  • Bitkom (Sep 14, 2026): 57% of German companies with 20+ employees use AI. In 2025 it was 36%, in 2024 it was 20%. For the first time, that's a majority.[3]
  • McKinsey "State of AI 2026" (August): 80% feel personally more productive. Only 37% see an effect on earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT), same as last year. Only about 6% get at least 5% of their EBIT from AI.[4]
  • Stanford HAI AI Index 2026 (Apr 13, 2026): 88% of organizations use AI, 10 percentage points more than in 2024.[5]
Methodology All three are self-reported surveys. Bitkom: 603 companies, by phone, weeks 28–33 of 2026. McKinsey sells AI consulting itself; we couldn't open the PDF directly today, the numbers are checked against three secondary sources. Stanford: the 88% figure is confirmed via a secondary source. In all three cases, "uses AI" doesn't mean "profits from it."

My take: This is exactly where the value of the hospital cases on the left comes in. McKinsey says the companies with a real profit effect rebuilt their workflows around AI. Ambient documentation is exactly that kind of rebuild, in miniature: one workflow, one metric, one sign-off step.

Brazil's banks: R$3 billion for AI and data Finance [6]

Per the technology study by banking association Febraban with Deloitte, presented on Aug 25, 2026 at Febraban Tech, Brazilian banks plan to put roughly R$3 billion into AI, analytics, and big data in 2026. That's 8% more than in 2025. It's part of a roughly R$50 billion tech budget. The money is meant to go toward efficiency, data analysis, faster customer responses, better service, and fraud prevention.[6]

My take: 8% growth is almost modest for an AI budget. That fits the McKinsey picture: the banks are investing steadily, not in a frenzy. In Brazil's financial sector, AI is already part of the regular budget, no longer a special project.

Digital Omnibus: An EU umbrella law that changes several digital rules at once. For the AI Act, it pushed back the strict obligations for high-risk AI. New systems still have had to add watermarks since Aug 2, 2026, though. It also bans apps that use AI to generate nude images of real people ("nudifiers"), and AI-generated child sexual abuse images.[7]

BaFin. In Germany, BaFin is responsible for AI in the financial sector and can impose fines. President Mark Branson says: "the responsibility for deploying AI rests with supervised companies and their management".[8]

ANPD. Brazil's data-protection authority has been a full federal agency since Law 15,352/2026 of Feb 25, 2026. It plans around 75 audits for 2026–2027. The roughly 20 audits on AI and new technologies fall mainly in 2027.[9]

My take: For a mid-size company, Dec 2, 2026 is the only date that matters this year, and only if it publishes AI-generated images or text externally. Everything else is 2027 or later. Anyone selling "AI Act panic" right now is selling too early. The same holds in Brazil: 2026 is the year to prepare, 2027 the year of audits.

Project ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals, with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Voice field-service reports for industrial service: Abridge for technicians

ELI5 & value: A service technician repairs equipment at a customer's site. Afterward, in the car or that evening, he has to type up a report: what was broken, what he replaced, how long it took. Often the report is short, hard to read, or arrives days later. Then the invoice is missing parts and hours, and the next technician doesn't know what happened. This idea carries over the hospital pattern: the technician talks into his phone for two minutes, the AI fills in the report's fixed fields, the technician checks it and signs off. In hospitals and elder care, exactly that brings 30 to 40% less writing time.[1][2] Value for the customer: same-day reports, complete parts and hours for billing, and a history that's searchable later.
Prototype (V0): (1) Pick one customer and one report type, for example the maintenance report for a machine family. (2) Nail down the required fields with the service manager: equipment, fault pattern, cause, replaced parts with part number, labor time, open items. (3) Speech recognition with the open-weights model Whisper, on an in-house server on request. (4) A language model fills the fields as structured JSON and flags anything missing or uncertain. For data sovereignty, an open-weights model, for example from Mistral; otherwise an API model. (5) A simple web app where the technician checks and approves the draft, plus an export as PDF or CSV into the existing system. Deliberately out of scope: a direct ERP write interface, dispatch scheduling, spare-parts ordering, languages other than German.
Market & gap: in the trades, the market is already taken. ToolTime has voice-to-docs for job-site reports with "Echo," Plancraft converts spoken measurements into material quantities (from about €59.90 per user per month on an annual plan), HERO has a phone assistant.[12] Benetics calls itself "the first AI voice assistant for the trades" and reports over 300 businesses.[13] Label Software has marketed since 2024 that AI smooths out illegible service-call reports.[14] In elder care, voize has over 1,100 facilities.[2] All of these products bundle the voice feature into their own industry suite. Anyone who doesn't use the suite doesn't get the feature. And a machinery or equipment manufacturer with 150 employees won't adopt trade software just to get voice reports. My assessment, not measured: that's exactly where the gap sits. Industrial field service has its own fields (part numbers, equipment types) and its own systems, and it often doesn't want the data in someone else's cloud. twiceD delivers the narrow reporting building block, adapted to the customer's fields, with in-house hosting on request. For price context: per an industry overview, AI consulting in the DACH region runs from about €5,000 for workshops to over €500,000 for platforms.[15] A fixed-price pilot in the low five figures sits at the lower end.
Feasibility (solo + AI): Medium to high. The V0 is an assembly of off-the-shelf parts and takes roughly three to five weeks solo. The hard work isn't the technology, it's the field schema worked out with the service manager, and testing against 30 real past reports. Honest note on upkeep: unlike pure consulting, this is an ongoing service. For the on-premise variant, twiceD runs the server, model updates, and monitoring. I'd budget two to four hours per week per customer in operations. At five customers, that's half a day a week, manageable for one person. Beyond that, it needs a contractor for operations. The growth lever: the field schema is reusable per industry, so the second machinery builder costs noticeably less than the first. Honest limit: workshop noise and technical jargon hurt recognition quality. The pilot has to measure that before there's a price. And the works council must approve voice recordings of employees before launch. findskill.ai · shyft.ai · softguide.de · startbase.de
