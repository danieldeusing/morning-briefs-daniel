Telekom: where AI already works, and what is only a target Telco [1]

On Monday, October 5, 2026, at its "AI Investor Day" in Bonn, Deutsche Telekom presented its AI plans with numbers.[1] I split the numbers into two groups, because they differ a lot in how solid they are.

Measured effects (Telekom's own figures):

  • The chatbot "Frag Magenta" handled about 2.6 million customer service calls in the first half of 2026.
  • In the US, customer service calls fell by 55%. AI agents there handle 40% of customer contacts.
  • The "RAN Guardian Agent" detects overload in the radio network. Handling of such events dropped from several hours to about 1 minute.
  • First cases show 30% fewer complaints. More than 100,000 employees are trained in working with AI.

Targets: by 2027 about €1.1 billion gross savings outside the US, by 2030 about €2.5 billion lower indirect costs, each compared with 2023. Plus AI revenue from business customers: about €250 million in 2026, about €800 million by 2030.[1]

RAN (Radio Access Network): This is the part of the mobile network between your phone and the core network, meaning cell towers and antennas. When a tower is overloaded, for example at a concert, customers notice right away. Before, a technician first had to see the problem and then act. The agent sees it on its own and proposes a fix.
Methodology All numbers come from Telekom, presented to investors. There is no independent check. "Indirect costs" is not explained in more detail. The release names no job cuts, but the risk notes mention "personnel-related restructuring measures". Whether part of the €2.5 billion comes from fewer staff remains open.

My take: This is one of the better AI reports from a DAX company, because it shows named systems with before-and-after numbers. The strongest case is not the chatbot but the network agent: a narrow task, a clear signal, a measurable time. The €2.5 billion, by contrast, is an intention, not a measurement. I would believe it only once it appears in an annual report as an achieved value.

  1. telekom.com, press release 10/2026. Primary source. Cross-read with artificialintelligence-news.com, Oct 6, 2026.

72% believe in their AI, 14% can show it Study [2]

The industry body TM Forum and Accenture launched a multi-year program for a "Trustworthy AI Governance Certification" on October 7, 2026. It aims to give network operators a common yardstick for how well they govern their AI, including self-acting agents.[2] The trigger is a survey of 130 AI leads at 130 network operators: 72% consider their AI trustworthy, only 14% can provide evidence for it.

Methodology Small sample, self-reported, and Accenture sells the consulting for the certification. The gap between 72 and 14 is still telling, because both values come from the same respondents.

My take: This fits the Telekom story exactly. Telekom shows numbers; most large companies cannot. "Being able to prove it" is the gap, and it is interesting for small service providers because it consists of documentation and measurement, not model research.

SAP buys TechWolf: the AI should know who can do what HR [4]

On October 6, 2026, at the SAP Connect conference, SAP announced it will buy TechWolf of Ghent. Neither company discloses the price. Closing is planned for the fourth quarter, and regulatory clearance is still pending.[4] TechWolf builds a continuously updated picture of employees' skills from HR and business systems. According to reports, customers include Booking.com, HSBC, PayPal, Ericsson and GSK.

In the same move, on Oct 7 SAP presented "Autonomous HCM": AI assistants for payroll, HR service, training, recruiting and onboarding, available from this month according to SAP.[5] SAP cites its own survey: 72% of executives believe that most "AI transformations" only automate existing processes.

Skill graph: A kind of map showing which person has which skill and which task needs which skill. Instead of someone maintaining profiles by hand, the software reads from projects, tickets and training what a person actually does. Then an AI can ask: "Who in the company knows SAP payroll and speaks Portuguese?"
Methodology The SAP survey comes from its own "Future of Work Research Lab". SAP names neither sample nor period. The SAP post names no customers and no impact numbers.

My take: For German mid-sized companies on SuccessFactors this means: the AI functions arrive in the existing system, whether you actively order them or not. Whoever switches on evaluating functions, such as a ranking of applicants or internal candidates, lands in the high-risk area of the AI Act (see below). Someone should check that before switching it on.

HackerRank: an AI conducts the job interview HR [6]

HackerRank released its AI interviewer "Chakra" to all customers on October 5, 2026.[6] The AI replaces three rounds (screening call, take-home task, follow-up with a specialist) with one interview. Candidates work on a real task, may use an AI assistant themselves, and Chakra asks why they made their choices. The AI assigns scores; a human decides on the hire.

  • In the test phase Chakra ran over 500,000 interviews, including customer tests and internal use.
  • According to HackerRank, signs of cheating were 70–80% rarer than in classic tests. The company's explanation: if people may use AI openly, they do not use it secretly.
  • Early test customers: Snowflake, Snorkel, Capgemini.
Methodology All numbers from the vendor. Fewer "cheating flags" does not automatically mean better hires. There is no figure on the quality of the people hired.

My take: The approach is smart because it flips the problem: instead of banning AI, it checks how someone works with AI. But an AI that evaluates applicants is, in the EU, exactly what Annex III of the AI Act means. A German customer of Capgemini, or a mid-sized company that buys such a tool, has obligations as a "deployer". The tool itself is a topic for developers; here the focus is its use in the HR process.

  1. techcrunch.com, Oct 5, 2026.

And in Germany? Recruiting AI is stagnating Study [7]

The survey "Jobcluster Recruiting Trends 2026" (report of Jul 6, 2026) shows the opposite of the hype: 68% of surveyed companies use no AI in recruiting, up from 66% last year. The share of active users fell from 34% to 31%. Where AI runs, it mostly writes texts, such as job ads.[7]

Methodology The report names neither the number of companies nor the period. It only says that more large companies took part this time. The direction is plausible, the percentages are not hard.

My take: Most mid-sized companies will not consciously "introduce" recruiting AI. They will get it as a new feature in Personio, SuccessFactors or Workday. That is exactly where the risk arises: a ranking function that someone switches on with one click, without the works council or data protection knowing about it.

AI Act: the omnibus, now checked in the Official Journal Reg [8]

So far I had cited the deadline change only through law-firm texts. Today I opened the text on EUR-Lex. It is Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of July 8, 2026, published in the Official Journal on July 24, 2026. According to recital 46, it entered into force on the third day after publication.[8]

  • Annex III (standalone high-risk AI, such as applicant selection, credit checks): from December 2, 2027.
  • Annex I (AI in products such as machinery or medical devices): from August 2, 2028.
  • Watermarking under Art. 50(2): anyone who already had their system on the market before August 2, 2026 gets a four-month transition. That gives December 2, 2026. This resolves a contradiction in the secondary sources (December 2026 or February 2027): December.
Provider and deployer: The AI Act distinguishes two roles. The provider builds and sells the AI, for example HackerRank or SAP. The deployer uses it, for example a company that selects applicants with it. Both have obligations. The deployer cannot hide behind the provider.

For employers as deployers, Art. 26 sets, among others, two concrete duties: keep the system's logs for at least six months, and inform employee representatives and affected employees before deployment.[9] In Germany, the works council's co-determination under § 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG applies as well. It applies today already, independent of the AI Act.[10]

Methodology I read the two new bans from December 2, 2026 (AI nude images without consent, abuse depictions) only in secondary sources. The EUR-Lex text was too long for a full retrieval; recitals 38, 40 and 46 are checked.

Compliance tools: the market is being consolidated M&A [11]

The US data governance vendor Collibra bought the Munich start-up trail ML on October 5, 2026. Its agents check which rules apply to an AI system and whether the controls work. Founded in 2023, price not disclosed. Collibra cites over 700 customers, including 78 of the Fortune 500.[11]

My take: A German AI Act tool moves into a corporate platform. That is good for the big players and bad for mid-sized companies: whoever has 300 employees and two AI functions in the HR system does not buy a Collibra license. More on this in the project idea.

  1. thenextweb.com, Oct 5, 2026.

Agents as customers: Meta and Sierra write the rules Retail [12]

Sierra and Meta are developing the "Personal Agent Protocol", an open standard for how a personal AI agent signs in to a shop and what it may do there. A version 0.1 is due in October. Payments are not included yet. Partners include Shopify, Stripe, Walmart and Genesys.[12] As a guest, the agent can query stock or return policies. After login, the customer chooses whether the agent may only read or also make changes.

OAuth: The method behind "Sign in with Google". You do not give an app your password, but a permission with limits that you can withdraw. Here your AI agent gets such a permission for a shop.

My take: For now this is a draft without payments, and Visa is pushing a second standard with Stripe and Shopify. For online retailers the question is already real: what happens when the "customer" on the site is a machine? Meta's enterprise platform (since Sep 28, prices unknown) is part of the same plan.[13]

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals, with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · HR AI Check: review recruiting functions before the works council asks

ELI5 & value: Many mid-sized companies have AI in HR without knowing it: applicant ranking in Personio, matching in SuccessFactors, a purchased interview test. The check collects all these functions and sorts them: what is harmless (scheduling, resume parsing), what is high-risk (evaluating, sorting, rejecting)? For each high-risk function a dossier is created: logs, employee information, a human's ability to intervene, documents for the works council. Value: a conflict with the works council or a discrimination lawsuit under the AGG costs more than the check. And there are still 14 months until 2027-12-02.[8]
Prototype (V0): (1) A questionnaire for HR and IT: which tools, which AI functions are switched on, who decides. (2) A classification table under Annex III, with the known exceptions for purely supporting functions. (3) A template per high-risk function with the deployer duties from Art. 26: keep logs at least six months, inform before deployment, human oversight.[9] (4) A short script that checks whether the tool exports logs at all and how long they are kept. (5) A meeting with HR and the works council where the dossier is presented. Deliberately out of scope: bias measurement with statistics, legal advice, a custom software platform.
Market & gap: According to P&S Intelligence, the AI governance software market was $310 million in 2025, with expected growth of 35.8% a year through 2032; other firms give clearly different values.[16] The known specialists for applicant AI, Holistic AI and Credo AI, come from the US and aim their audits at New York's Local Law 144.[17] The German tool trail ML has belonged to Collibra, a vendor for large customers, since this week.[11] Law firms offer the legal question, but not the technical check in the HR system.[10] My assessment, not measured: For a company with 200 to 2,000 employees, a platform is too big and a law firm alone too abstract. Gap for twiceD: a fixed-price check that combines technology, AI Act and German co-determination in one dossier. The market is smaller than the hype: only 31% use AI in recruiting knowingly.[7] So aim at companies with SuccessFactors or Workday and a works council, where the functions are already there.
Feasibility (solo + AI): High, with one clear limit. V0 is consulting plus templates, built solo in two to three weeks. One check takes about three to five days per customer. After that there is little maintenance, only an annual repeat and an update when the harmonized standards arrive. Honest limit: the legal assessment in an individual case is legal advice. twiceD needs a partner for that, a specialist employment lawyer who reviews the dossier. Scaling: one contractor with HR-IT experience per five parallel customers. Difference from the Oct 2 proposal (general AI inventory): this check is narrower, HR only, and has a fixed occasion in the works council meeting. eur-lex.europa.eu · artificialintelligenceact.eu · psmarketresearch.com · gov.uk · thenextweb.com
