An AI Chat Before the Doctor's Appointment: Safe Enough for a Test Health [1–2]

Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center (BIDMC) in Boston ran the first study with real patients on AMIE, together with Google. AMIE is a Google research system that asks questions like a doctor. The results are in The Lancet, and the announcement came on Oct 8, 2026.[1]

How it worked: patients of a primary care practice chatted with the AI by text before their appointment. Afterward they had the normal appointment with a human.

  • 114 patients were enrolled, 98 completed both chat and appointment.
  • In none of the 98 cases did a supervisor have to stop the conversation for safety reasons (0 stops).
  • The AI invented a statement once (1 hallucination). In 5 cases a clinical clarification was needed.
  • One reviewing provider called one conversation "somewhat harmful": the AI had named lymph node cancer as a possibility, and the patient became frightened.
Hallucination: The AI says something with full confidence that is not true or that nobody said. Before a doctor's appointment this can be dangerous, which is why the study counts every case.
Methodology A single-arm feasibility study: there was no comparison group without AI. The announcement itself says the goal was feasibility, "not to determine whether AI improves health". Alphabet funded the study, and researchers from Google Research and DeepMind are co-authors. Duration April to November 2025. The preprint gives 100 patients instead of 114/98; I could not open the Lancet text itself.[2] Hit rates (correct diagnosis among the top 7 in 90%, at rank 1 in 56%) I know only from a search summary. Not verified.

My take: This is the right first step, not the breakthrough that some headlines ("AI could improve emergency care") suggest. What was measured: can you let this run without harm? Answer: yes, within this small setting. The lymphoma case shows the real problem better than the zero stops. An AI that names every possibility is clinically thorough and humanly clumsy. In the EU, such a system would very likely be a medical device. That makes it expensive for small vendors, but solvable for hospitals with a partner.

Only One in Five Mittelstand Companies Uses AI, Mostly for Writing Study [3]

In its report Fokus Volkswirtschaft No. 554 (Jul 29, 2026, author Dr. Volker Zimmermann), KfW counted exactly which AI the German Mittelstand uses. I read the PDF myself today.[3] Result: 20% of companies use at least one AI technology. Use has grown strongly in six years, but it is shallow:

AI use in the Mittelstand by technology, share of companies (KfW Mittelstand Panel 2025, period 2022–2024)

Most common: generating text and speech (14%) and recognizing text (10%). Only 3% use data analysis, and 1% use AI for the autonomous movement of machines.

Methodology Good source: a large panel, extrapolated to all Mittelstand companies. But the data describe the years 2022 to 2024, so the time shortly after ChatGPT launched. Today the figure is surely higher. Another study (IW Consult for the eco association, per dpa of Jul 8, 2026) gives 40% for the whole German economy. I know that figure only from a search summary, and the definition is different. Do not compare the two figures directly.

My take: The distribution matters more than the 20%. The Mittelstand uses AI where it can be bought ready-made: writing texts, reading receipts. Data analysis at 3% is the real gap. It needs clean own data and someone who asks a concrete question. That is not delivered by a subscription, that is delivered by a project.

TCS: AI Is Already a Tenth of the Business B2B Services [4–5]

The Indian IT services firm Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) presented its figures for the second quarter of fiscal year 2027 on Oct 8, 2026. The most important number for this brief: annualized AI revenue is above $3.1B. That is more than 10% of total revenue.[4] One quarter earlier it was $2.6B.[5]

Q4 FY26: ~$2.3B→ Q1 FY27: $2.6B→ Q2 FY27: > $3.1B
Revenue "annualized": You take the AI revenue from one quarter and multiply it by four. That is an extrapolation, not an annual result. For comparison: TCS had $7.64B revenue in the quarter, times four that is about $30.6B. Of that, $3.1B is about a tenth.

Revenue rose only 0.5% versus the previous quarter (in constant currency), and profit was $1.45B. New orders worth $9.6B, excluding the two large deals:[4]

  • Porsche: a five-year partnership and the planned purchase of the consultancy MHP from the Porsche group. Several reports give €320 million enterprise value in cash, per TCS's exchange filing (read only in search results); Analytics India Magazine gives no price. Closing is still pending.
  • Best Buy: TCS takes over the US retailer's Indian development center and converts it into an AI center.
GCC (Global Capability Center): A Western company's own department abroad, usually in India, that does IT and back office for the group. When TCS takes over such a center, a group department turns into a services contract.
Methodology The TCS press release could not be retrieved (error 403). The AI figure comes from Analytics India Magazine, and two other reports also give $3.1B. What TCS counts as "AI revenue" is not openly defined. Probably classic data work is included too.

My take: This is the strongest signal of the week for service providers like twiceD. The AI share is growing fast, the overall business hardly. So AI work partly replaces old work at the same customers. The MHP purchase means for Germany: an Indian giant buys its way directly into German automotive consulting. Corporate projects become more fiercely contested. The small Mittelstand stays too small for TCS.

Google Brings an Agent for Office Work B2B SaaS [6–7]

Google Cloud introduced the "Gemini agent" on Oct 8, 2026, according to Google "a universal agent for work".[7] It is meant to handle multi-step tasks in the office on its own. It comes first for companies, later for consumers. Early test customers are the shoe maker On, Shopify and PayPal.[6]

  • Third-party models can also be chosen as the model, first the Claude models from Anthropic.
  • Companies can set spending limits in real time.
  • Google says almost 90% of Fortune 100 companies use Gemini Enterprise.
Open No price given. Whether the agent is already generally available or only runs in a closed preview, I could not clarify. The Google page does not say. The 90% is a marketing figure with no definition of "use".

My take: Two details matter more than the word "universal". First: Google lets Claude into its own product. The model becomes interchangeable, and the platform with the company data is the business. Second: spending limits. An agent that works on its own costs money per step. That Google advertises this as a feature shows that customers fear exactly that. The part on coding agents is in the ai-dev brief.

McDonald's: Lawsuit Over an AI Pricing Tool Retail [8]

A class action against McDonald's has been pending in a federal court in Chicago since Friday, according to Newsweek Oct 2, 2026. The plaintiff is Michael Thomas of DeKalb, Illinois.[8] The allegation: the AI-supported pricing tool that McDonald's gives to franchisees in the US shares non-public sales data of individual stores. That way stores that are actually competitors could coordinate their prices. This is said to have been going on since 2019.

Store A reports sales data→ Tool recommends prices→ Store B sees the same recommendation→ Prices converge (alleged)

McDonald's disagrees: "AI does not set menu prices at McDonald's, franchisees do." The tools do not automate, coordinate or set prices "in any way". According to McDonald's, the tool has been in use for more than ten years.[8]

Algorithmic price-fixing: Competitors used to meet in secret and agree on prices. That is illegal. The new allegation: you no longer need a meeting if everyone uses the same software, which computes with everyone's data. The software becomes the meeting place.
Status The lawsuit is at the very beginning, nothing is proven. According to a search summary, California has banned "common pricing algorithms" using competitor data as part of a collusion since January 2026. I did not check the law itself.

My take: For Germany the pattern matters, not the burger. Franchise systems, purchasing associations and platforms often give their members a shared AI price recommendation today. The legal question is always the same: which data flows from whom to whom? Whoever builds such a tool should separate this in the data model from the start.

AI Act: 54 Days Left to the First Omnibus Deadline Reg [9]

Yesterday I checked the deadlines of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 ("Digital Omnibus") against the Official Journal. Today the confirmation via a law-firm summary and the practical consequence.[9] The next deadline is close: anyone who already had an AI system that generates text, images, audio or video on the market before 08/02/2026 must implement the labeling under Art. 50(2) by 12/02/2026.

Labeling under Art. 50(2): An AI image or AI text should carry a kind of invisible label that a machine can read: "An AI made this." The duty falls on the provider, meaning whoever builds and offers the system. A company that only uses ChatGPT is usually not a provider.

The big deadlines remain: high-risk AI under Annex III from 12/02/2027, AI in products under Annex I from 08/02/2028. National sandboxes are due to be in place by 08/02/2027.[9]

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals, with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Labeling Check by 12/02: "Are We a Provider, and What Do We Have to Do?"

ELI5 & Value: Many Mittelstand companies have built, or had built, their own AI features since 2023: a chatbot on the website, a generator for product texts in the shop, a tool that creates images for customers. Anyone who offers something like this must label the outputs as AI content in machine-readable form from 12/02/2026.[9] Most do not know whether they are a provider or only a user. The check answers exactly this question in a few days and, if needed, implements the labeling technically. Value: many companies get a written all-clear, the rest a finished implementation before the deadline. For prohibited practices the law names fines up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide revenue.[12]
Prototype (V0): (1) A questionnaire with 15 questions: which AI features exist, who built them, who uses the outputs, under whose name do they run? (2) A decision table provider/deployer/user only under Art. 50, with reasoning per feature. (3) For each affected feature a technical proposal: metadata under the open standard C2PA for images and video, a notice field in the API response for text. (4) A small reference implementation for the most common case, the shop text generator. (5) One entry per feature in a simple AI register that connects to the record of processing activities under GDPR. Deliberately out of scope: high-risk assessment, own platform, legal advice.
Market & Gap: Dedicated AI Act tools cost roughly €5,000–30,000 per year according to the vendor Legiscope, enterprise governance platforms over €100,000 per year. Their core functions: AI register, risk classification, transparency evidence.[12] Named among others are OneTrust, TrustArc, Credo AI and IBM. The German tool trail ML has belonged to Collibra since this week (see brief of Oct 8). I know these names and prices only from search summaries and the text of a competitor. What these tools do not do: build the labeling technically into your own shop or chatbot. They document, they do not build. And according to KfW, 14% of Mittelstand companies use AI to generate text and speech.[3] Some of them built their own features, most are only users. For both groups an annual license is too much. Gap for twiceD: classification plus technical implementation from one source, at a fixed price, with a hard deadline as the sales trigger.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): High, but with a short window. Questionnaire, table and reference implementation can be built solo in about two weeks. A check with all-clear takes one to two days, with implementation three to ten days. After that there is hardly any maintenance. Honest limits: first, the window until 12/02 is short, after that it becomes a building block in the general AI inventory (proposal of Oct 2) and in the HR check (proposal of Oct 8). Second, classification in borderline cases is legal advice and needs a partner lawyer. Third, the technical standard for labeling may still change. The implementation then needs an update. Scaling: a contractor for the implementations, Daniel for classification and customers. cuatrecasas.com · legiscope.com · kfw.de
