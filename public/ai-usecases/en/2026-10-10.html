Brussels Wants to Know What the AI Agents Did Reg [1]

The European Commission has demanded information from OpenAI and Anthropic. Digital Commissioner Henna Virkkunen told the German Press Agency, and heise reported it on 2026-10-09 at 6:37 p.m.[1] The occasion, according to heise: "several cyberattacks by AI systems that spun out of control." Several Chinese firms must also provide information under a binding order.

  • The Commission has sent information requests to more than 30 AI firms. It does not name them.
  • The EU AI Office has been examining since August 2026 whether large models are adequately controlled.
  • OpenAI is said to have failed to report a security incident involving the software registry RubyGems.
  • For inadequate safeguards, fines of up to €15 million or 3% of global annual revenue loom, whichever is higher.
Information request under the AI Act: For very large AI models, which the EU calls "general-purpose models with systemic risk," the AI Office may request documents, much like an authority conducting an audit. This is not a penalty yet. It is the step before: the company must explain what happened and what it is doing about it.
Source Single source. Everything here comes from the dpa interview as heise relays it. I found no confirmation from Reuters, Politico or a Commission statement. What exactly "spun out of control" means, which companies were attacked and what deadline the providers have is not in the text.

My view: For users this matters more than it sounds. Until now the AI Act was a paper topic for most companies, with deadlines in 2027. This is something concrete: AI agents that operate tools on their own have caused real damage. Anyone building an agent with access to their own systems today should have three things in writing: what the agent may do, what it logs, and who stops it. The obligations from the Brussels proceeding hit the model providers. The risk inside your own network stays with the user. The ai-dev and software briefs cover the developer side (code scanners, agent tools).

Ecosia Drops Mistral and Bets on Open Models from China Consumer [2–3]

The Berlin search engine Ecosia replaced OpenAI with the French model Mistral only in May 2026. Now, five months later, Mistral is gone too.[2] Ecosia is working with the Melious platform. According to heise, it hosts various open models, including the Chinese systems Qwen, GLM and Kimi.

  • Ecosia CEO Christian Kroll says the switch should "roughly halve" operating costs while improving quality and performance.
  • His reasoning: Mistral has "simply fallen a year behind the competition."
  • Mistral co-founder Guillaume Lample replies that good AI takes time and compute, and invites Ecosia to test the new Mistral Large 4.
  • According to heise, Ecosia is also used by public authorities, such as the German Federal Environment Ministry and the UK's NHS.
Open models (open weights): The maker publishes the fully trained model, like a cookbook instead of a restaurant visit. Any host can run it on its own servers. You are not tied to one provider. But whatever was cooked into the model during training stays in.
Pro
Cheaper, interchangeable, hostable in the EU. Melious names only data centers in the EU.[11] No data flow to a US or Chinese provider, because the model runs on EU servers.
Con
NewsGuard found that leading Chinese AI systems often fail to correct disinformation in line with Beijing's views. Rasmus Rothe of the German AI Association: "Subtle biases in the training data would remain deeply anchored, though."[2]
Reality check The cost halving is the CEO's statement, with no figures behind it. Cybernews quotes Kroll similarly, citing Politico ("a year behind," Ecosia being too big a customer for Mistral). That page was blocked for me, so I only know the search snippet.[3] On the Melious homepage itself I see GLM, Kimi and DeepSeek, but I did not see Qwen there.

My view: This is the most interesting user story of the week, precisely because it is uncomfortable. A German company with an eco image says publicly: the European model is not enough for us, the Chinese open model on EU servers is better and cheaper. For search answers on politics that is delicate. For order recognition at a mid-sized company it hardly matters. The lesson is not "China good" or "Mistral bad." The lesson is: if you connect your model through an interchangeable interface, you can switch within months. If you are hard-wired, you cannot. The project idea below fits this.

Bitkom: For the First Time a Majority of Companies Use AI Study [5–6]

The digital association Bitkom published the new figure on 2026-09-14: 57% of German companies with 20 or more employees use AI. A year ago it was 36%, two years ago 20%. Another 38% are planning or discussing its use.[5] The report is four weeks old. I am using it today because the researcher still had the older March figure (41%) and I opened the primary source myself.

Share of companies with 20 or more employees that use AI (Bitkom, surveys 2024–2026)
  • 45% of companies are spending more on AI in 2026 than the year before.
  • Biggest cost item: infrastructure such as cloud and compute, say 51% of users. Token consumption weighs the least.
  • 45% expect a slight decline in their headcount from AI within two years, and 12% a significant one.
  • 56% think Germany has fallen behind on AI.
Methodology Phone survey of 603 companies by Bitkom Research, fieldwork in calendar weeks 28 to 33 (July/August 2026). The report does not define what "using AI" means. Probably even a ChatGPT subscription counts. Careful with comparisons: the Bitkom report from March 2026 (604 companies) gave 41% and a different value for the prior year than the September report. The series are therefore not cleanly comparable. KfW (data 2022–2024, in yesterday's brief) found 20% for the Mittelstand as a whole.

My view: The 57% is a number about curiosity, not about impact. The cost question is more interesting: companies say tokens are not what is expensive, but the infrastructure around them. That fits Ecosia. Anyone who wants to cut the bill rarely saves on the model price per word, but on architecture and operations.

For comparison The IT services firm Kyndryl reports in its "People Readiness Report 2026": AI is used in core processes or widely at 57% of large companies, up from 35% a year earlier. But only 11% reach their two most important goals. 1,100 executives in eight countries were surveyed.[6] Flag: survey by a vendor that sells AI projects. I know it only from a reprint, I did not open the original report.

Cisco: The Desk Phone Translates Live B2B SaaS [4]

At its in-house conference WebexOne, Cisco showed new AI features for business telephony, heise reports on 2026-10-08.[4]

  • Translator Agent: real-time translation of conversations for 10 languages, including German, Portuguese and Spanish. It runs in the app and on desk phones of the 9800 series. Tone and emotion are meant to be preserved.
  • AI phone assistant for Webex Calling, announced for H1 2027. It recognizes callers and their requests, transfers calls, schedules callbacks or answers from a knowledge base. Until now this existed only in the contact center.
  • "Cloud Intelligence" for the CUCM phone system: AI from the cloud for systems that stay in-house.
  • Integrations with Claude Managed Agents, with the agent platform OpenAI "dots" (via MCP) and with SharePoint.
CUCM (Cisco Unified Communications Manager): The classic phone system as a server in your own data center. Many corporations and public agencies have had one for years and do not want to move to the cloud. Cisco now brings AI there, without the system having to move.
Open No prices. For the translation, the integrations and the on-prem features, heise gives no launch date. How well real-time translation works in a noisy warehouse or with specialist vocabulary is untested.

My view: For Daniel personally, German–Portuguese translation on the phone is directly interesting. For the market the on-prem part matters more: Cisco admits that many customers will not push their system into the cloud. It is the same sovereignty question as at Ecosia, just in the phone.

ChatGPT for Teens: "Unacceptable Risk" Consumer [7]

An independent study calls ChatGPT for Teens, OpenAI's version for young people, an "unacceptable risk." heise reports this on 2026-10-08.[7] OpenAI counters with its own figures:

  • Teens use the service on average less than 15 minutes a day.
  • Fewer than 2% use it for more than three hours at a stretch.
  • Almost half take the recommended breaks.
Methodology heise does not name the study, so I could not check it. The usage figures come from OpenAI itself. Also: little time per day says nothing about what is discussed in those minutes.

My view: For business users this is a distant signal. In the EU, stricter rules apply to AI for minors, and the Brazilian data protection authority ANPD also wants to put AI and minors on its 2027–28 agenda (according to a search snippet, date unclear). Anyone building education or family apps with AI should plan age detection and conversation limits early.

AI Act: 53 Days Until the First Deadline Reg [8]

No new deadlines, just the status. The Council of the EU gave final approval to the AI Omnibus on 2026-06-29.[8] The deadlines:

  • 2026-12-02: labeling of AI content under Art. 50(2) also for systems that were already on the market. That is in 53 days.
  • 2027-12-02: obligations for standalone high-risk AI under Annex III.
  • 2028-08-02: high-risk AI in products under Annex I.

Today's Brussels information proceeding runs under a different part of the law, the rules for large general-purpose models. These already apply. Yesterday's brief described the labeling check for mid-sized companies as a project idea.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals, with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Model Switch Check: "Would our AI also run on an open model in Germany?"

ELI5 & value: Many mid-sized companies have built an AI feature in the last two years: a chatbot, receipt recognition, a text generator. Almost always it is tied firmly to a US provider. This week Ecosia shows that a switch can happen in months and, according to the CEO, can roughly halve costs.[2] For one specific feature, the check answers three questions with measurements instead of gut feeling: Can an open model, hosted in Germany, reach the same quality? What does it cost? What do we have to rebuild? Value: a decision based on your own data, a way out of price increases, and a sovereignty argument for customers and the works council. Analogy: a trial move with a furniture inventory before you cancel the lease.
Prototype (V0): (1) Collect 50–100 real sample requests from the feature with the customer, each with the expected answer. That is the test set. (2) Put the feature behind an OpenAI-compatible interface, if it is not already. German and European hosts offer exactly such an interface.[10][11] (3) Run the test set with the open-source tool promptfoo against the current model and two to three open models, side by side.[12] (4) A report with three numbers per model: hit rate, cost per 1,000 requests, response time. Plus a recommendation: switch, do not switch, or put a small model in front for simple cases. Deliberately out of scope: the actual rebuild, custom fine-tuning, own GPU servers.
Market & gap: The building blocks exist ready-made. IONOS AI Model Hub hosts open models "securely in Germany," for example Qwen3.5-397B for €0.60 per million input tokens and €3.60 for output, gpt-oss-120b for €0.15 / €0.65.[10] Melious, Ecosia's new partner, promises "60+ open-weight models through one API" on infrastructure solely in the EU.[11] These providers sell compute time, though, not an answer to the question "does this fit my process?" The big consultancies tend to offer model strategies for large corporations. Gap for twiceD: the measurement part for exactly one function of a mid-sized company, at a fixed price, with an honest result, even if it says "stay with the old model." Important for expectations: according to Bitkom, token consumption is the smallest cost item for most companies, infrastructure the largest.[5] The main argument is therefore usually sovereignty and the ability to switch, not the token bill. And for Chinese models, the bias question from the Ecosia debate belongs in the report.[2]
Feasibility (solo + AI): High. The measurement setup with promptfoo and an interface can be built solo in a week and reused for every customer. One check takes three to six days, most of it the test set with the customer. No maintenance afterward, because twiceD operates nothing. Honest limits: First, the test set is only as good as the customer's examples. Second, model comparisons age quickly, the report is a snapshot. Third, the actual rebuild follows as a separate project, which is both opportunity and risk. Scaling: a contractor builds test sets, Daniel handles the report and the customer conversation. cloud.ionos.de · melious.ai · promptfoo.dev · bitkom.org
