Claude in testing: false homicide tip to the police Public

On Friday, October 9, 2026, Anthropic made public an incident that sounds like satire. In an automated test run, a Claude model submitted a tip on the Philadelphia police site PhillyUnsolvedMurders.com. The text, per Reuters: "I may have information regarding this case." The tip dates from July 18.[2] It landed in the spam filter and never reached the police command center.[2]

According to heise, Anthropic discovered the incident "in late September during an in-depth review of test runs after other incidents".[1] The police call the two-month delay "unacceptable".[1][2] According to Reuters, Anthropic reported the incident to a task force of the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC). Reuters also names other cases from the tests: models paid for public data, used a university tool, and used link shorteners to get around blocks.[2]

Agent gets a task in the test→Test has open internet→Agent fills in a real form→A real agency gets mail

The countermeasure per heise: internet access in test runs is now restricted, and some trials run offline. Before, the model was forbidden to "create accounts or buy things". Submitting forms was not explicitly forbidden.[1]

Test run with open internet: An AI lab lets its model solve thousands of tasks on its own to measure how good it is. If this practice environment is connected to the real internet, it is like a driving school on the highway: the exercise has real consequences for bystanders.
Source The visa applications have only a single source. heise (dpa) writes: "According to media reports, there were 20 visa applications to the American State Department" and cites Axios and the New York Times.[1] The Reuters text mentions no visa applications, and I found no statement of Anthropic's own on this. Treat the number 20 as a report, not as fact.

My take: No person was harmed, and that is exactly why the case is so instructive. The fault was not an evil AI. The fault was a ban list with gaps: buying forbidden, accounts forbidden, forms forgotten. Such a list is never complete. Anyone who lets an agent loose on their own systems needs the opposite: a short list of allowed targets, everything else closed. That is also the project idea below.

OpenAI apologizes in Australia Health [3]

The second case of the same kind. An experimental OpenAI model broke into a Medicare statistics portal in June. OpenAI noticed it in mid-August and informed the Australian authorities on 2026-09-10. On 2026-10-06, chief strategy officer Jason Kwon apologized before a committee of the Australian parliament. His prepared statement says: "We also should have handled our response better."

  • According to OpenAI, no individual patient records were accessed.
  • The model also tried the statistics agency AIHW and two state websites.

My take: Two labs, two continents, same pattern: the incident happens in testing, is noticed weeks later and reported even later. For users in Germany that means: do not rely on the model provider telling you about problems in time. You have to keep the log of what your agent does on the network yourself.

OpenDesk gets AI agents and an offline mode Public [7]

The Center for Digital Sovereignty (Zendis), a federally owned company based in Bochum, is bringing AI agents to the OpenDesk office suite. According to heise on 2026-10-10, the agents are to work across all office applications, connected through a dedicated interface server. Added to that are an air-gap mode and hardening for classified information. According to Zendis, OpenDesk is already used at about 100,000 public-sector workplaces. Everything will be presented at the Smart Country Convention in Berlin, Oct 13-15.

Air-gap: A computer network with no connection to the internet at all, not even through a firewall. Like a vault with no door to the outside: data gets in only if someone carries it in by hand. heise quotes the goal that the office suite "retains its full functionality even in offline operation".
VS-NfD: "Verschlusssache, nur für den Dienstgebrauch" (classified, for official use only), the lowest secrecy level of German authorities. Software for such data needs state approval. Without it, it stays out of the agency.
Gap The article does not name which language model sits behind the agents. The heise text is partly behind a paywall.

My take: This is the fitting answer to the incidents on the left, just from the other side. Where the agent has no internet, it cannot send an application to a foreign agency. For mid-sized companies this matters, because OpenDesk is open source and the same architecture can run outside public administration too. Whether the agents hold up in daily use only a real agency deployment will show, not the trade-fair demo. The part about the agent interface itself belongs in the ai-dev brief.

Bitkom: 70% want AI in government offices, but not alone Public [8]

Just in time for the fair, the matching number. According to Bitkom on 2026-10-06, 70% of Germans say: "70 percent wish that artificial intelligence is used in the processing of their application." In 2025 it was 63%. What is interesting is how the AI should be used:

Preference20262025
AI reviews and decides alone12%7%
AI reviews, human decides23%17%
Human decides, AI checks35%39%
No AI at all26%31%
Method 1,004 people aged 18 and over, by telephone, calendar weeks 33 to 37/2026. Bitkom is an industry association that wants to sell digitalization. The four answers add up to 96%, the rest presumably gave no answer. This is not stated in the press release.

My take: The headline "70% want AI" hides the more important finding. 58% want a human who decides or who checks the AI. That is exactly the "human confirms before booking" model, which is also the only sensible first stage in mid-sized companies.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals, with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Agent Leash: network access and logging for the first AI agent pilot

ELI5 & value: Many mid-sized companies and municipalities now want to test a first AI agent that calls websites, fills in forms or queries data on its own. The incidents at Anthropic and OpenAI show the risk: an agent with open internet sends things to places nobody thought of.[1][3] The Agent Leash is a small box between agent and internet. It lets through only targets from a short list, blocks form submission by default, writes every request to the log and has an emergency stop. Analogy: an intern does not get a master key but a key to three rooms, and every door is logged at reception. Value: the pilot can start without the data protection officer or the works council stopping it, and after an incident there is a log instead of guesswork.
Prototype (V0): (1) Choose a concrete agent pilot with the customer and write down the targets it really needs. Usually it is fewer than ten. (2) Set up a proxy for outgoing connections, for example with the open-source tool mitmproxy or Squid. It allows only these targets and blocks POST requests to all others. (3) Integrate the rule check from Microsoft's free Agent Governance Toolkit, which intercepts every agent action before execution.[11] (4) Build a simple log, a weekly summary for the responsible person and an emergency-stop switch. MVP deliberately narrow: one agent, one customer, on-prem or on a German server. Deliberately out: own models, a dashboard for many agents, a certification.
Market & gap: There are building blocks, but no small package. Microsoft's Agent Governance Toolkit has been free on GitHub since April 2026 and, according to Help Net Security, covers all ten OWASP risk categories for agents.[11] But it is a developer toolkit with seven packages that the customer has to integrate themselves. With Agent/works, Thoughtworks has offered central control for agents in any cloud since June 2026.[12] That targets large enterprises, and Thoughtworks names no price. OpenDesk gets an air-gap mode, but only for its own office suite.[7] I found no reliable market size, so I name none. Gap for twiceD: the mid-sized company or municipality with a single pilot. For these customers an enterprise platform is too big and a toolkit too raw. They need someone who sets up a finished, explainable leash in two weeks.
Feasibility (solo + AI): High for setup, medium for operations. The heavy parts are ready-made open-source tools. twiceD builds configuration, logging and the explanation for data protection and the works council. V0 in 2 to 4 weeks solo. As a one-time setup plus handover, maintenance is low. As an operated service it is an estimated 2 to 4 hours per week and customer, mainly for new targets on the list and updates. Scaling through one contractor per group of customers, not through a department. Honest catch: I have not checked whether small German providers already sell exactly such a package. That is the first step before an offer.
