AI as a Reason for Layoffs Drops to No. 5 Work [1][2][3]

The US consultancy Challenger, Gray & Christmas counts every month how many jobs US companies plan to cut and what reason they give. In spring this was the number that every AI doom article cited. According to HCAMag (citing Challenger), 38,579 announced cuts in May were attributed to AI, which was 40% of all cuts that month.[3] Andrew Challenger said at the time: "AI is now the leading reason companies give for cutting jobs."

Since then the picture has turned. In August, AI fell to fourth place with 3,462 cuts, the lowest monthly figure since December 2025. According to Challenger, this ended a streak of five months starting in March in which AI was the No. 1 reason every month.[2] In September (report of 2026-10-01) AI ranks fifth: "Artificial Intelligence was the fifth-most cited reason with 3,961 cuts in September, about 9% of the month's total."[1]

Since January, AI has still been the most common reason: 120,136 cuts, roughly 21% of all announced cuts.[1] The overall total is low: 573,195 cuts through September, 39% fewer than a year earlier.[1] At 6,515, demand decline in September was the highest since February 2023.[1]

Challenger report: A count of announcements, not of jobs actually lost. A company says "we are cutting 2,000 jobs because of AI," and Challenger records it that way. Like a survey asking why people sell their car: the answer says a lot about what they want to say, and less about what really happened.
Method The reasons are the companies' own statements in press releases and filings. A cut counts in the month of the announcement, regardless of when or whether it is carried out. I checked the May figure only through HCAMag, not in the original report. August and September come from the Challenger PDFs.

My take: This is not proof that AI costs no jobs. It shows that in spring "AI" was the most convenient label for cuts. Cutting jobs and saying "AI" sounds like the future. Saying "demand decline" sounds like a problem. For users in Germany this means: a slide saying "AI replaces X jobs" is a claim, not a measurement. A solid business case counts hours in the process, not headlines. The next Challenger report comes in early November.

"SI" Instead of "AI": Trump Renames, Corporations Follow Policy [4]

According to heise of 2026-10-12, US President Trump has ordered federal agencies to say "Super Intelligence" instead of "Artificial Intelligence" from now on. Anyone who keeps saying "AI" is, per heise's headline, "THE ENEMY" for Trump.[4] Several corporations are following:

  • Salesforce has renamed its integration layer for AI agents from AIForce to SIForce.
  • Elon Musk has renamed SpaceXAI on X to SpaceXSI. According to heise, the domain is still x.ai.
  • Satya Nadella (Microsoft) now writes only "superintelligence," according to heise. Jensen Huang (Nvidia) and Jeff Bezos also speak almost only of "super intelligence."

The order is not binding for companies. heise writes: "For companies, the change of terms is a loyalty test" (translated from German).[4]

Superintelligence: So far a term for a hypothetical AI that outperforms humans at almost every task. That does not exist today. Using the word for every chatbot is like calling every bicycle a "race car": the bicycle does not go any faster afterward.
Gap I have not read the text of the order or its exact date in the original. heise gives no date for it. The statements about Nadella, Huang and Bezos come only from heise.

My take: For users nothing changes, and that is exactly the point. The software is the same on Monday as on Friday. Under German law the thing is still an "AI system" in the sense of the AI Act. If you get an offer from a US vendor that suddenly says "SI," ask the same question as always: what exactly does it do, and how is that measured? Together with the Challenger numbers, that is today's lesson: labels follow mood, not technology.

AI Act: What Is Fixed, and a Caveat Reg [5][6]

The amending regulation to the AI Act, the "Digital Omnibus on AI," appeared in the Official Journal on 2026-07-24 and entered into force three days later.[5] The major new dates according to the law firm Cuatrecasas:

  • 2027-12-02: high-risk AI under Annex III, for example AI for hiring or credit checks.[5]
  • 2028-08-02: high-risk AI built into regulated products (Annex I), such as machinery or medical devices.[5]
  • Two new prohibitions in Art. 5, according to law-firm commentaries on non-consensual intimate deepfakes and on depictions of child abuse.[5]
  • The AI literacy obligation (Art. 4) remains in modified form. According to Cuatrecasas, it explicitly does not require a particular level of competence from individuals.[5]
Annex III / Annex I: Two lists in the AI Act. Annex III names areas of use where AI decides about people, such as job applications. Annex I names products that already have their own safety rules, such as elevators. Like two doors into the same house: whoever comes through either one counts as "high-risk" and has to document more.
Correction I am pausing the countdown to 2026-12-02. The issues of Oct 10 and 11 gave this date as a firm deadline for labeling under Art. 50(2) for systems already on the market before 2026-08-02. The law firm Cuatrecasas states it that way.[5] Other commentaries give a later date. I could not load the text in the Official Journal (EUR-Lex) today. Until I have checked it, no date appears here.

Why this matters is shown by Bitkom: 66% of companies that do not yet use AI cite legal uncertainty as a hurdle.[6] The association writes: "The AI Act was supposed to create trust, but it creates widespread uncertainty" (translated from German).[6]

Method Bitkom survey, 603 companies with 20 or more employees, by phone, weeks 28 to 33/2026, release of 2026-09-14. Self-reported. Bitkom is an industry association that wants less regulation.

My take: If even the law firms argue over a date, uncertainty among mid-sized companies is no surprise. More important than the exact date: anyone who publishes AI texts or images externally today should be able to label them anyway. That is cheap and protects against trouble, whatever deadline ends up applying.

Apple Takes on the Huxe Team M&A [7]

According to heise of 2026-10-09, Apple is taking on the staff and technology of the AI startup Huxe, as an "acqui-hire" with a "non-exclusive" license for the intellectual property. Nobody names a price. Huxe was founded in late 2024 by former staff of Google's NotebookLM tool and shut down its AI podcast app in May 2026. The deal became known through a filing with the EU Commission under the Digital Markets Act (DMA).

Acqui-hire: A large company buys a small one mainly for the people, not the product. Like a soccer club taking over a whole amateur club just to get three players.

My take: For users this is small. Only the route is interesting: the EU notification duty for large platforms makes deals visible that would otherwise have passed quietly. A hint that Apple wants to catch up on audio summaries à la NotebookLM.

Power for AI: 150 Kilowatts per Rack Energy [8]

A c't background piece at heise of 2026-10-09 spells out what running AI means physically. A classic server rack needs 3 to 15 kilowatts. "AI racks already 150 kilowatts today," with "up to one megawatt per rack" conceivable (translated from German).[8] The federal government wants to quadruple data center capacity in Germany by 2030 compared with 2025. New data centers may have a PUE of at most 1.2.[8]

PUE: The data center's total power divided by the power that actually goes into the computers. 1.2 means: for every 100 watts of computing, 20 watts go to cooling and infrastructure. Like fuel in a car that goes to heat instead of motion.

My take: For a mid-sized company that wants to run an open-weight model in its own basement, this is the honest counter-calculation. A server with several GPUs does not fit into every equipment room, for power or for cooling. On-prem sovereignty is possible, but it starts at the power connection.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals, with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · AI-Act Ready: AI Inventory and Labeling Check at a Fixed Price

ELI5 & Value: Many mid-sized companies have long been using AI, often without knowing it: a copilot in the office suite, a translation service, a chatbot on the website, AI images in marketing. At the same time, 66% of companies without AI cite legal uncertainty as a hurdle.[6] The package answers three questions in two weeks: Which AI do we use? Does any of it count as high-risk? Do we label AI content correctly toward the outside? Analogy: like a fire-safety walkthrough. Nobody has to rebuild the building, but afterward you know where the fire extinguishers are missing. Value: management can decide again instead of doing nothing out of uncertainty.
Prototype (V0): (1) A German-language questionnaire plus a review of software invoices and admin consoles to find the AI tools in use. (2) Check each tool against a checklist: Annex III, Annex I, labeling under Art. 50, data protection. (3) A review of the website and marketing channels for AI content without labels. (4) A two-page report with a traffic-light rating and a one-hour training with a participant list as proof for Art. 4. MVP deliberately narrow: companies with 20 to 250 employees, no high-risk developers, no legal advice. Existing building blocks: a form tool, a spreadsheet as the inventory, a local open-weight model that searches invoice texts for AI services.
Market & Gap: The market exists, but mostly in English and Dutch. The Dutch company Embed AI (Praxikon) sells an AI governance scan for €2,950, a 30-day sprint for €9,900 and both together for €21,900. For Art. 4 training, the same overview names the platform LearnWize.[10] Caution: a vendor writes this overview itself, and it names no tools for inventory and GRC. I have not checked for a German-language fixed-price solution for small mid-sized companies. That is the first step before an offer. Gap for twiceD: a small German package priced below the Dutch scan, with hands-on work instead of a platform subscription. The data stays with the customer.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): High. It is consulting with a clear end, so almost no maintenance. V0 with questionnaire, checklist and report template in 2 to 3 weeks solo. Estimated 2 to 4 days of work per customer. Scaling through a contractor who does the walkthroughs, not through a department. Two honest catches: First, twiceD may not give legal advice, so edge cases need a partner law firm. Second, the labeling deadline is currently disputed (see above). The offer must point to the Official Journal text, not to a countdown.
