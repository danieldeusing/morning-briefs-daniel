Black Thursday: Asia doubts AI spending Market [1][2]

On Thursday, 2026-10-15, the Korean benchmark index KOSPI fell almost 8% in one day, according to heise. It now sits more than 40% below its mid-June high. Samsung lost 9.5%, SK Hynix more than 11%. On Wednesday the Nasdaq had already dropped about 2%, with weak prices for Oracle and CoreWeave.[1] Heise names three triggers: doubts about AI investments, Anthropic figures showing slower growth, and rising yields on US government bonds.[1]

The same day, TSMC presented the counter-evidence. The Taiwanese chipmaker reported a record third-quarter profit of NT$763.06 billion, up 68.7% from a year earlier. Revenue rose 41.2% to $46.72 billion.[2] For 2026, TSMC expects about 44% revenue growth, and for Q4 revenue of $52.3 to $54.3 billion. Capex for 2026 stays at $60 to $64 billion.[2] According to Exame, TSMC still cannot fully meet AI demand.[2]

Capex (capital expenditure): Money a company puts into things that last a long time: data centers, chip fabs, power lines. Like a baker buying a second oven. It only pays off if enough customers later buy bread. The market is asking right now: will customers for all those AI data centers show up fast enough?
Huge AI investments→ Doubts about returns + higher rates→ Memory stocks fall→ but: TSMC keeps delivering at the limit

A fund manager sums up the mood at heise: "In the end, the AI revolution will not be measured by how much computing power we install."[1]

Source The market figures come from a heise summary (citing Korea Times), not from price data. The TSMC figures were read via Exame, not in the TSMC report itself. The detailed market assessment belongs in the economy brief.

My view: This is not a contradiction but two questions in two places. TSMC sells the shovels and has full order books. The market asks whether the gold diggers, meaning the AI providers and their customers, will ever find enough gold. For small and mid-sized businesses this is good news: when investors press for returns, providers must show which use case really saves money. Anyone who wins a customer over with a measurable number today has a better standing in this climate than someone with a vision.

Anthropic: $16.8 billion quarterly revenue, IPO in sight M&A [3]

According to Exame, citing Reuters, Anthropic showed investors in San Francisco its figures on Wednesday, 2026-10-14. Third-quarter revenue was $16.8 billion, up from $11.6 billion in the second quarter. That is about 45% more. On an adjusted basis, Anthropic is said to be profitable.[3] In its May funding round, the company raised $65 billion at a valuation of $965 billion. For the IPO it targets more than $2 trillion, after the US midterm elections.[3] Against that stand commitments of at least $518 billion for cloud, compute and infrastructure over ten years.[3]

Source Single source: Exame, citing Reuters. The research could not load the Reuters text. Bloomberg-based reports put Q2 at about $11.5 billion, Exame at $11.6 billion. Heise only mentions "solid figures, but slower growth". So all values are "according to Exame/Reuters".

My view: The number is huge, and the market fell anyway. That shows how high expectations are. The other number is more interesting for users: $518 billion in fixed commitments for compute. Someone has to pay these costs through prices. I do not expect AI tokens for business customers to stay as cheap as today in the long run. Anyone building a product on a single provider should plan the switch to another model from the start. Anthropic's product news, for example on Claude Code, is in the ai-dev brief.

Eight US states sue OpenAI over child safety Reg [4]

Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia filed a lawsuit against OpenAI on Wednesday, 2026-10-14. Heise reported this on 10/15. The allegation: OpenAI downplayed the risks of ChatGPT for minors.[4] The states rely on consumer protection laws. They demand age verification, time limits and data deletion. Arkansas goes further and wants OpenAI to pause testing of advanced AI.[4]

Consumer protection lawsuit: Here it is not an individual victim suing, but the state on behalf of all citizens. It says: the product was sold as safer than it is. Like a state suing a toy maker because the packaging promises "child-safe" and it is not.

My view: Arkansas's demand for a testing halt will hardly succeed. Age verification, however, is realistic, and it affects every company that offers a chatbot to end customers. Anyone running a customer chatbot in Brazil or Europe should write down today: how do we recognize minors, and what happens then? That costs an afternoon now. After a lawsuit it costs much more.

Cologne court: Snapchat chatbot may not use children's data for advertising Reg [5]

The Cologne Regional Court ruled against Snapchat at the end of September (case no. 33 O 120/24), heise reports on 2026-10-15. The plaintiff was the Federation of German Consumer Organisations (vzbv). Snapchat may not use data from conversations with its AI chatbot for advertising if consent came through a pre-ticked box.[5] Violations risk fines of up to €250,000. The ruling is not yet final.[5]

Pre-ticked consent: The checkmark "Yes, you may use my data" is already set before you do anything. Under EU law this is not real consent. Like a contract someone signed for you while you were not looking.

My view: Together with the US lawsuits this forms a pattern: courts look at AI chatbots first for children and data, not for the technology. For a German shop or publisher with a chatbot, this means: conversation data is not free advertising data. Anyone who wants to reuse it needs clean, active consent.

ServiceNow gives AI agents a passport B2B SaaS [6]

On 2026-10-15, according to Exame, ServiceNow announced three building blocks. The AI Passport is a cryptographically signed proof of what rights an AI agent has. Nvidia is the first partner, the specification is meant to be open and to cover MCP servers too.[6] Added to that are shadow AI detection with more than 400 connectors and cost control for AI. All three are due to be available in Q4 2026.[6] ServiceNow's guiding line: "Você não consegue governar o que você não consegue identificar", meaning: what you cannot identify, you cannot govern.[6]

Agent identity: An AI agent acts on behalf of a company: it reads emails, books invoices, calls other programs. Today it often runs with an employee's password. A dedicated ID says: this is agent X, it belongs to department Y and may only do Z. Like a visitor badge at a factory instead of a borrowed master key.

My view: Yesterday I wrote about shadow AI in HR departments and about 1,500 self-built agents at Riachuelo. ServiceNow now sells the answer for large corporations. Small and mid-sized businesses have the same problem but no ServiceNow license. That becomes the project idea below. The technical side for developers, meaning MCP and agent tooling, belongs in the ai-dev brief.

Studies: Feels faster, trust stays with the team Study [8][9]

Gallup and JFF (USA): In the American Job Quality Study, 63% of employees who use AI say they finish tasks faster. 46% see better quality, 47% more time for work they enjoy.[8] 7,845 employees were surveyed from January to March 2026. Exame itself warns: "a pesquisa não comprova que a IA melhora automaticamente o desempenho profissional".[8]

Endeavor Brasil: Among 133 founders of 125 scale-ups, 59% almost always ask AI before deciding. On trust, though, the team wins: 46% trust the team more, only 20% trust AI.[9] And among those who work six hours or more a day with AI, 71% feel maximum pressure. For up to two hours it is 29%.[9]

Method Both studies measure self-reporting, not performance. Gallup only asks people who already use AI, which flatters the values. Endeavor has a small sample of 133 founders. The pressure figure shows a correlation, not a cause. Originals not checked, read via Exame.

My view: The pressure figure at Endeavor is the most interesting one of the day. Heavy AI use and heavy stress go together. Whether AI causes the stress or stressed founders use more AI, the study does not say. But nobody should write "more AI makes you calmer" on a sales slide anymore.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals, with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Agent registry for small and mid-sized businesses: who may do what?

ELI5 & Value: In many companies, AI tools and small agents now run that nobody knows centrally. One employee connects ChatGPT to their mailbox, another builds an automation flow that reads invoices. Each has access to data, often with a human's password. twiceD builds a lean registry: which agents exist, who owns them, what data they access, what rights they have, and who switches them off? Analogy: the caretaker's key book. Value: less data protection risk, evidence for auditors and the works council, and a basis for AI Act classification from 2027.[10]
Prototype (V0): (1) One-day inventory: interviews with three to five departments and a look into the Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace admin console to see which apps have access to accounts. (2) A registry as a small web app or structured spreadsheet: agent, owner, data sources, rights, risk class. (3) For Microsoft customers: use the existing Entra Agent ID instead of building something custom.[12] (4) One simple rule per agent: own account instead of a borrowed password, as few rights as possible. (5) One review per quarter. MVP deliberately narrow: no custom detection software, no real-time blocker, only inventory, rules and review.
Market & Gap: The big players are building this right now. ServiceNow brings the AI Passport and shadow AI detection in Q4 2026, for customers of its own platform.[6] Microsoft Entra Agent ID is available to all Entra customers. The security features for agents, however, need a Microsoft Agent 365 license, included in M365 E7 or as an add-on to E5 or Business Premium.[12] Okta offers, with Agent Gateway and Auth0, tools to detect agents and check rights per tool call.[13] All three are platforms for companies with their own identity team. I did not find a reliable market size for "agent governance for small and mid-sized businesses" today, so I name none. Gap for twiceD: the company with 50 to 500 employees, a Business Premium license and an IT lead who does everything alone. It does not need a new platform, but someone who sets up the existing tools and maintains a list, in German and with a GDPR focus.
Feasibility (solo + AI): High for the inventory, medium for the ongoing service. The inventory is consulting without operational load, estimated 2 to 4 person-days per customer. The quarterly review costs an estimated 1 to 2 hours per week and customer. Custom software is hardly needed, the heavy technology comes from Microsoft, Google or Okta. Scaling via one contractor per three to four customers with a fixed template. Two honest catches: without the customer's admin access the inventory stays incomplete, and new agents appear faster than a quarterly review finds them. That is why a clear rule "new agent only with an entry" belongs in the contract.
