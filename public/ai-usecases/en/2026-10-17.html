Belo Monte: Out of the Cloud Bill, Into Its Own Server Energy [1]

Norte Energia operates the Belo Monte hydropower plant on the Xingu River. The company had a document chatbot based on OpenAI. According to Exame (2026-10-16), it hit its limits. The successor is called IANE. It runs open models on its own hardware: a Dell PowerEdge R7625 with two AMD EPYC 9354 processors and one AMD Instinct MI210 card.[1] At first only one device is available for testing. The open-source tool n8n orchestrates the workflows.[1]

The split is hybrid. Contracts and operating records stay in-house, public data may go to the cloud. Real-time control of power generation is deliberately excluded.[1] CFO Júlio Freitas states the reason openly: expanding AI across the whole company would create "custos variáveis crescentes e difíceis de prever", meaning rising costs that are hard to predict.[1]

The benefit: in social and environmental monitoring, meaning the plant's environmental and social obligations, IANE saves an estimated two hours a day per specialist. That is "cerca de 500 horas anuais".[1]

Cloud chatbot for some users→ Rollout to the whole company→ variable costs hard to plan→ open models on own server, cloud only for public data
On-prem and hybrid: "On-prem" means the computer sits in your own building, not at Amazon or Microsoft. Hybrid means part runs in-house and part in the cloud. Like a restaurant that runs its own kitchen but buys bread from the bakery. With the cloud you pay per use, like a taxi. With your own server you pay once, like owning a car. The car pays off if you drive a lot.
Method The 500 hours are an internal estimate, not a measurement. Freitas himself says: "A Norte Energia ainda não trabalha com uma métrica de ROI financeiro rígida ou consolidada para a IANE." So there is no return figure. The article leans heavily on Dell and AMD as suppliers. Dell itself says: "Não vemos necessariamente um movimento de saída da nuvem, mas de construção de ambientes híbridos."[1]

My take: This is the best story of the week for twiceD, precisely because it is unspectacular. Not a hype project, but a CFO who can no longer plan the cloud bill. The pattern fits every municipal utility, every law firm and every mid-sized company with many contracts. I would do two things differently. First: a baseline measurement before buying, of how long a task takes today. Without it, "500 hours" stays a claim. Second: a single test device is fine for a start, but operations need a failover reserve. This leads to the project idea below.

Federal Government and Nvidia: "Physical AI" for German Factories? Mfg [2]

According to heise (2026-10-16, citing Handelsblatt), the German federal government plans a collaboration with Nvidia. It covers AI models for the factories of the future. "The federal government is said to be ready to invest a three-digit million amount."[2] German companies such as Siemens also want to take part.[2] An investment summit takes place in Berlin on Monday and Tuesday, that is Oct 19 and 20. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is expected at the kickoff on Monday evening with Chancellor Merz. "The collaboration could then be announced […]."[2]

Physical AI: AI that does not only write text but understands the real world and acts in it: robots, gripper arms, autonomous vehicles in the plant hall. It is often trained first in a simulation, a "digital twin" of the factory. Like a learner driver who practices in a simulator before going on the road.
Source Not officially confirmed. heise relays Handelsblatt, and the research could not load the Handelsblatt text. There is no exact sum, only "three-digit". The calendar dates Oct 19 and 20 are derived from "Monday and Tuesday" in the article from Friday.

My take: A few hundred million euros is little for Nvidia, but a lot for German industrial policy. On Monday I mainly expect a photo of Merz and Huang with few details. For mid-sized companies the question that follows matters: is there compute time or simulation tooling that a company with 200 people can also use? Or does it remain a project for Siemens and co.? Only when that is clear does an offer make sense. Until then: wait, and read the official release on Tuesday.

Anti-Money-Laundering Checks: EU Authority Wants More AI, Privacy Advocates Warn Finance Reg [3]

The new EU anti-money-laundering authority AMLA has presented draft guidelines on ongoing customer monitoring. According to heise (2026-10-16), it asks banks to consider algorithms, machine learning and AI. It stresses "technological neutrality" and provides for human oversight and appeal options for customers.[3] The money-laundering regulation behind it is to "apply from July 2027".[3]

The Dutch organization Privacy First, on the other hand, demands "a clear ban on automated risk profiles".[3] The figures from the Netherlands show why banks want to automate anyway:

NetherlandsValue
Unusual transactions reported, 2020just under 250,000
Unusual transactions reported, 2024more than 530,000
Full-time staff for this (8 largest banks)about 13,000
Costsabout €1.6 bn

The downside: according to the Dutch Court of Audit, in a sample of several hundred reports "in more than 60 percent of cases, people with foreign-sounding surnames were affected".[3]

AML / KYC: AML means "Anti-Money-Laundering". KYC means "Know Your Customer": the bank must know who its customer is. The bank constantly watches transfers and reports anything that looks odd. Like a bouncer who checks every guest for anything off. The problem: a badly trained bouncer mostly stops people with foreign-sounding names.

My take: The numbers are a textbook case. Reports more than doubled in four years, and 13,000 people review them by hand. That AI is coming here is certain. But the 60-percent figure shows that in the audited sample, today's rules already hit certain groups harder. A model that learns from those old cases takes over the pattern. Anyone advising in this field therefore sells not "more automation" but "demonstrably fair and explainable". The appeal path for customers is already in the draft. From 2027 it becomes mandatory.

Anthropic Pays $100 Million for People, Not Chips Policy [4][5]

Anthropic announced the Claude Frontier Academy on 2026-10-02. Exame put it in context on Oct 16: "US$ 100 milhões para treinar 10 mil engenheiros até o fim de 2027".[4] Each participant works for twelve weeks on a real project at their own company. The first cohorts sit in San Francisco, New York and London, including people from Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk.[4] Through its partner network, Anthropic cites 46,000 companies and more than 175,000 Claude certificates.[4]

Source The research could not load the Anthropic announcement itself. As early as March 2026, Anthropic had committed $100 million to the partner network. Whether the new money is additional or the same sum is unclear.[5]

My take: The Exame title hits the mark: there is a shortage of people who can build AI into a company. A model vendor is now paying to train deployment engineers, because otherwise its product sits on the shelf. For twiceD this is a confirmation. This exact work, understanding the process and fitting AI to it, is the business. But the competition is coming too: 10,000 certified people, many at big consultancies. The German Mittelstand is not at the top of their list. Anthropic's product side, such as Claude Code and the independent evaluators, is in the ai-dev brief.

Studies: Who Sees Their AI Costs? Study [6][7][8]

KPMG (June 2026): "42% dos líderes consultados tinham apenas visibilidade parcial dos gastos com IA."[6] According to Exame, 23% also have problems with usage-based costs, and 33% do not understand the billing, tokens included.[6]

Method The Exame headline says 42% of companies "blow" their AI budget. The text only shows that 42% see their spending only partially. That is not the same. Name, sample and method of the survey are missing, and I did not find the KPMG original. The article ends with an ad for a course.

AI salary premium (UK): A study by Bone, González Ehlinger and Stephany in Technological Forecasting and Social Change (2025) finds 23% higher pay in ads that require AI skills. It analyzed "aproximadamente 11 milhões de anúncios de emprego britânicos entre 2018 e meados de 2024".[7]

Method These are salaries in ads, not wages paid. It is a correlation: those who ask for AI skills often seek more expensive roles anyway. The figure does not automatically apply to Brazil.

Publicis Sapient (2026): Among 1,550 AI decision-makers in six markets, 73% use AI regularly or in most processes. Only 10% call it core to operations.[8] A consultancy study, read only through a summary.

My take: The KPMG figure fits Belo Monte exactly. Whoever pays per token and does not understand the billing cannot plan costs. That is a problem for the CFO, not for IT. And the gap between 73% usage and 10% "core" is the real story: almost everyone is trying, almost nobody has AI at the true core of the business.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals, with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · "IANE for Municipal Utilities": sovereign records assistant with a fixed cost cap

ELI5 & Value: A municipal utility, a grid operator or a law firm has thousands of contracts, permits and inspection reports. People search them by hand every day. A records assistant answers questions like "What conditions does permit X have, and when does it expire?" and shows the source passage. The core of the offer is not the AI but the promise: the data does not leave the building, and the costs are fixed in advance. That is exactly what Norte Energia wanted.[1] Analogy: your own car instead of a taxi for the daily route. Value: time in the specialist department, predictable costs for the CFO, less data-protection risk.
Prototype (V0): (1) Baseline measurement before the start: how long do five typical searches take today? Without this number there is no proof of benefit later. (2) Load a document set, for example 500 permits, into a search with source passages (RAG). (3) Run an open model locally, for example via Ollama or vLLM, with n8n as workflow orchestration as at Norte Energia.[1] (4) Give every answer a link to the original paragraph, so the specialist can verify. (5) After four weeks, measure the same five searches again. Keep the MVP deliberately narrow: one document type, one department, read-only, no control of plants. Norte Energia also excludes real-time control.[1]
Market & Gap: Ready-made devices exist. Pyramid Computer sells AKHET Local AI, an AI box for in-house operation, with software from co-mind.ai, document search, tenant separation and rights management, "without sensitive data ever leaving your own infrastructure". Prices and technical data are not published.[10] Dell and AMD supply the hardware, as in Belo Monte.[1] Aleph Alpha offers German models, and heise is currently testing Kolibri-1.[11] I did not find a reliable market size for on-prem AI in the German Mittelstand, so I name none. Gap for twiceD: all three sell one piece, box, server or model. None delivers the whole for a concrete process, with before-and-after measurement and a fixed monthly price. The KPMG figure on unclear AI costs shows that this promise resonates.[6]
Feasibility (solo + AI): Medium. V0 is doable solo in 3 to 5 weeks, because model, search and n8n are ready-made building blocks. The catch is operations: a server at the customer needs updates, monitoring and a failover reserve. An estimated 2 to 3 hours per week per customer, more during incidents. Two paths make this sustainable. Either the customer's IT runs the hardware and twiceD handles only the software, or twiceD uses a ready-made box like AKHET and handles only fitting and measurement. Scale through one contractor per four to five customers with a fixed template. Without this division of labor, it becomes too much on-call duty for a small team.
