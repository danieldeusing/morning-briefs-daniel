Vercel index: open models do the work B2B SaaS [1]

New since Sat Vercel runs an AI Gateway, a pass-through station that lets software teams reach many AI models through a single connection. Vercel has now analyzed the figures for September 2026, and heise reported on 2026-10-19.[1] The result is clear:

Vercel AI Gateway, September 2026Value
Open models' share of tokens71.5%
Open models' share of estimated spend~23% (June: ~5%)
Anthropic's share of spend63.7% → 43.9%
Teams with a new main model in September48%
Teams with a new main provider in September28.6%
Models per team, from 1 billion tokens42 on average

More details: spend on open models has risen more than 70-fold since December 2025, while spend on the expensive closed frontier models rose about 10-fold.[1] Of the teams that spend more on open models, 68% also spent more on closed ones.[1] A single model, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, generates about half of all open tokens but only 2.6% of open spend. More than two thirds of spend on Chinese models runs through US providers.[1] The ai-dev brief discusses the model itself.

Open models (open weight): An AI model whose "brain", meaning the weights, you may download and run yourself. A closed model exists only as a service from its maker. Like a recipe you may cook at home versus a restaurant that keeps its recipe secret. At home it is cheaper, while the restaurant cooks some dishes better.
Method "The Vercel data reflect only the traffic routed through AI Gateway."[1] That means mostly software teams that already use Vercel, not the market. Spend is estimated at list prices, not from real invoices. Discounts and direct contracts with OpenAI or Anthropic are missing entirely. The research did not open the Vercel index itself, only the heise report.

My view: This is the pattern Norte Energia showed at Belo Monte on Saturday, only in numbers. The bulk of the work runs on cheap open models, while the money flows to the expensive models for the hard cases. The 68% figure matters: open models do not replace the expensive ones, they come on top. So the question for a company is not "which model" but "which model for which task". Anyone who does not manage this pays restaurant prices for sandwiches. That 48% of teams switch their main model within a single month also shows that lock-in to one provider is a risk, not a strategy. The project idea below grows out of this.

Berlin: photo with Huang, letter of intent, no sum Mfg Policy [2][3]

Update since Sat On Monday evening Chancellor Merz opened the "Invest in Germany" investment summit in Berlin. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was there. According to heise (2026-10-19, 23:50), the research ministry BMFTR signed a letter of intent with Nvidia.[2] The content: German industrial data is to be usable with Nvidia's model families Nemotron and Cosmos, for "physical AI". Huang said: "The next AI wave is physical AI." Merz said: "Germany is ready to grow at a higher speed."[2]

heise is critical. According to media reports the summit is said to have cost around €5 million. Merz also hinted that machinery could be exempted from the AI Act. heise questions that.[2]

Unconfirmed On 10/16, citing Handelsblatt, heise had reported a "three-digit million amount", with Siemens as a possible partner.[3] The heise report on the summit names no sum. Whether and how much money flows is therefore open. The quotes above come from a summary of the article, not the original wording. The research had no ministry statement.
Letter of intent: A paper in which two sides write down what they plan to do together. It is not a contract and promises no money. Like two neighbors saying "we should grill together sometime". Whether there are sausages gets decided later.

My view: On Saturday I wrote: lots of photo, few details. That is how it played out. Nothing tangible for the Mittelstand (mid-sized companies) today. There is no funding program, no access to compute, and no application deadline. The one useful piece of information is the direction: industrial data plus simulation. Anyone advising in manufacturing should know the names Cosmos and Nemotron. An offer on this is only worthwhile once the ministry says who may take part. I consider the hint on the AI Act summit rhetoric. An exemption for machinery would have to be decided by the EU, not Berlin.

When the AI agent pulls out the credit card Retail Finance [4][5]

Stripe reports that purchases by AI agents through its payment service Link "se multiplicaram por 38 em um mês". In other words, they rose 38-fold in one month. Exame reported on 2026-10-19; the figure itself dates from 2026-09-29.[4] Link is active at more than one million merchants. The agent wallet exists only in the US. The article names Meta's Muse, Grok Bot and Instinct as assistants. Every purchase needs the user's approval.[4]

Method Stripe gives no baseline and no absolute totals. Going from 10 to 380 purchases is also 38-fold. Without a base the figure is a signal of growth, but not a size. Exame also cites Brazilian surveys: 46% of AI users search for products with AI, 41% want to confirm before an AI purchase. Sponsors and samples are missing.[4]

A second Exame article from the same day gives a Bain figure (Bain's own published US forecast is $300-500 billion by 2030; whether Exame means global or US-only is unclear): agent purchases could move "até US$ 6,2 trilhões em vendas" by 2030, that is, up to $6.2 trillion in merchant sales.[5] In Brazil, Banco do Brasil is testing with Visa Intelligent Commerce. The agent gets no real card number, but a substitute code. According to the article, one agent payment ran in controlled production in March 2026.[5]

Agentic commerce and tokenization: An AI agent searches, compares and buys on the user's behalf. So that it does not know the real card number, it gets a "token", a substitute code that is valid only for this purpose. Like a hotel key that opens only your room and stops working after you check out.
Method The Bain figure is an upper bound ("up to") from press material. The research has not seen the Bain report. It is merchant sales, not profit.

My view: The growth is real, the size is unknown. Something practical matters for merchants now: can an agent read my shop? Clear product data, prices and delivery times in a machine-readable form will become as important as search engine optimization used to be. This is more a 2027 topic than a tomorrow topic, and in Germany the agent wallet does not exist yet. The Brazilian test with Banco do Brasil is closer to home for Daniel than the US case.

China wants AI exports to need approval Policy [6]

According to heise (2026-10-19, citing Reuters), China has presented a draft for a new list of export controls. It covers 40 technologies. "For 26 of them, licenses are to become necessary, and for 14 of them the export is to be banned outright."[6] 23 items relate to AI, including AI agents and methods for large models. Comments are possible until 2026-11-15. Nothing has been decided yet.[6]

Source Further details, such as a threshold for models of two trillion parameters and up, appear only in a secondary source from the web search. I therefore do not present them as fact.

My view: This sits badly with the Vercel figure. Half of the open tokens there come from one Chinese model. Companies betting on cheap open models from China should now have a Plan B. That does not mean leaving, but having a second model you can deploy without a rebuild. Whether the rules affect weights that have already been downloaded is open. New versions could, however, arrive more slowly or no longer be free.

ISO/IEC 27090: a guideline, not a seal Reg [7]

On 2026-10-19 the first edition of the standard ISO/IEC 27090 was published. Across 56 pages it describes how to secure AI systems against attacks. It costs 204 CHF.[7] It is a guideline, and you cannot be certified against it. heise stresses: "27090 does not create a presumption of conformity under the AI Regulation or the Cyber Resilience Act."[7] It treats AI agents only in passing. The European standard prEN 18282 is still a draft, and ISO/IEC 27091 is in the final draft stage.[7]

Presumption of conformity: If a company meets a specific, officially recognized standard, the authority assumes it also meets the law. Like an inspection sticker: with the sticker, the police do not ask further. 27090 is not such a sticker, but more of a good textbook.

My view: For consultants it is useful anyway. An AI security check "according to ISO/IEC 27090" sounds more robust to a buyer than "according to our own checklist". You just must not promise that it meets the obligations under the AI Act. Only the European standard fills that gap, and it is still missing.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals, with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · AI cost check with a model switch for the Mittelstand

ELI5 & Value: Many companies today send every AI request to the same expensive model, whether it is a contract or a spell check. A "model switch" (router) sorts the requests: simple tasks to a cheap open model, hard ones to a strong one, sensitive ones to a model in-house. The Vercel numbers show this works: in September 71.5% of tokens there ran on open models, for around 23% of estimated spend.[1] Analogy: a shipper who sends letters by post and furniture by truck, instead of everything by courier. Value: predictable and usually lower costs, no outage when a provider drops out, and clear rules on which data goes where.
Prototype (V0): (1) Cost check: for four weeks, route all AI requests of one department through a gateway and log them. What does each task cost today? (2) Sort the requests into three classes: simple, hard, confidential. (3) Set two models for each class, one of them open, so a switch works without a rebuild. (4) Build a small test set from real tasks and check whether the cheaper model is good enough. (5) After four weeks, compare cost and quality with the baseline. MVP deliberately narrow: one department, one gateway, no custom model training. Existing building blocks: LiteLLM, an open gateway with cost tracking per project and user.[10] Alternatively OpenRouter as a ready-made service.[11]
Market & Gap: The technology exists. LiteLLM describes itself as an "open source AI Gateway that gives you a single, unified interface to call 100+ LLM providers", with "multi-tenant cost tracking and spend management per project/user".[10] OpenRouter offers "One endpoint, hundreds of models, with routing, fallbacks, and cost tracking across providers".[11] Vercel runs the AI Gateway the index comes from.[1] I found no reliable market size for this field, so I cite none. What the providers leave out: they are tools for software teams that decide for themselves which model suits which job. A Mittelstand company does not have those people. On top come two German questions no US service answers: which data may go into a Chinese model, and what happens if China restricts the export of new models?[6] Gap for twiceD: cost check, data rules and switch plan as a fixed package, in German, with measurement before and after.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): High. The cost check is consulting work with little operations. Daniel can do the V0 solo in 2 to 4 weeks per client, because gateway and models are ready-made. Ongoing effort is about 1 to 2 hours per week and client, mainly to re-run the test set on new models. The gateway is best operated by the client's IT, so twiceD carries no on-call duty. Scaling via a fixed template and a contractor from about five clients. Limit: anyone who wants their own models in-house needs hardware operations. Then the more cautious assessment from the 10/17 idea applies.
