Walmart builds a door for shopping agents, Amazon shuts its own Retail Deploy

New since Oct. 21 Walmart CEO John Furner unveiled the Agent Gateway on Wednesday, Oct. 21, 2026, at the Masters of Scale Summit in San Francisco. With it, an AI agent can "search across Walmart's online inventory and make purchases on behalf of the agent's user".[1] Technically it runs through an MCP server that approved agents connect to.[1]

With the Agent Profiles, due "next month", the customer decides how purchases get approved. Either they confirm each purchase individually, or they allow purchases up to a spending limit they set themselves.[1] Walmart also cites a partnership with Meta's Muse, "a leading agent in the space".[1] The report names no revenue figures or targets.

Shopping agent: An AI assistant that does not just suggest products but searches, compares and pays on its own. Like a courier you hand your shopping list and your wallet. The store then no longer sees the customer at the checkout, but the courier.
MCP server: A standardized socket through which an AI agent talks to someone else's system. Instead of clicking through a web page like a human, the agent asks directly: "Which detergents do you have under $10?" The technical side of MCP belongs in the ai-dev brief.

Amazon takes the opposite path. According to TechCrunch, it "began blocking AI agents from making purchases on behalf of online shoppers".[1] According to Retail Gazette, Amazon has blocked Meta's agent Muse since Sunday night, Sept. 20, 2026.[2] Muse launched in the US on Sept. 8 and, per TechCrunch, reached 10 million US downloads in 40 days.[3]

Source We know the Gateway only from the TechCrunch report. The research found no statement from Walmart itself. The date of the Amazon block comes from a search snippet, the article was not opened. The 10 million downloads appear only in a headline.

My take: This is the most important retail conflict of the year, and it is an honest one. Amazon earns money from advertising on its own site. An agent sees no ads and buys the cheapest offer. So Amazon blocks. Walmart is number two and wins if agents bring it customers who would otherwise be at Amazon. So Walmart opens up. I like the spending limit. It is the first answer from a large retailer to the question of who is liable when an agent buys the wrong thing: the customer sets the limit beforehand. German shops have to answer exactly this question too, and before agents arrive in volume.

Agent commerce in Germany: lots of forecasts, little practice Retail Study

In Germany, almost nobody shops in an AI chat today. That is the state of the research, without a hard number. What does exist:

  • Forecast: The consultancy Strategy& expects €109 billion of e-commerce revenue via agents in Europe by 2030, of which €17 billion in Germany.[4]
  • Start-up: Shopagentic from Hanover wants to have AI agents handle "a large part of the daily work in online retail" (quoted from the German). t3n reports 60 agents for one web shop and a first fashion brand as a customer, unnamed.[5]
  • Standards: OpenAI and Stripe built the Agentic Commerce Protocol, open under the Apache 2.0 license. According to the project site, ChatGPT is the first platform to use it.[6] With the Trusted Agent Protocol, Visa has presented a way for merchants to recognize verified agents.[7]
Method The Strategy& figure is a forecast from a consultancy that sells agent projects. It comes from a search snippet of a press release, assumptions and method are unchecked. For planning it works as an order of magnitude, not as a target value.

My take: €17 billion in 2030 is a bet, not demand. The real news lies elsewhere. The standards are being set in the US now, and German shops will have to adopt them, just as they once had to adopt Google Shopping feeds. The project idea below follows from this.

Mistral and Nvidia: physics AI for aircraft, cars and chips Mfg Deploy

New since Oct. 21 According to the-decoder, Mistral and Nvidia want to jointly offer physics AI for aviation, the auto industry and semiconductors.[8] The report also names Mistral Large 4 with about one trillion parameters, trained on 3,800 Grace Blackwell GPUs in Europe. Open weights are said to be planned for the end of October. By 2030, Mistral reportedly wants to build 1 GW of compute capacity. The report names no customers.[8]

The foundation is older. On May 27, 2026, Mistral presented its physics AI, with ASML, Airbus, Safran and Siemens Energy as partners. According to Mistral, a classic simulation needs "hours to weeks of compute time per design variant". The AI model delivers an estimate "on the order of seconds, on a single GPU".[9]

Physics AI (surrogate model): Engineers compute on a computer how air flows around a wing or where a component breaks. That takes hours to weeks per design. An AI model learns from many past calculations and estimates the result in seconds. Like an experienced master craftsman who looks at a workpiece and says "that will hold" without measuring it. For final sign-off you still calculate exactly.
Source The partnership and the Large 4 details appear only at the-decoder. The research found no second source. Mistral's May page mentions neither Nvidia nor Large 4. "Seconds instead of weeks" is a vendor claim, with no independent test and no accuracy figure. The Large 4 model page is a matter for the ai-dev brief.

My take: This is one of the few European AI stories with a real industrial connection. I believe the direction, not the number. Seconds instead of weeks applies to a pre-selection: you test 1,000 designs roughly and calculate the best ten exactly. For a Mittelstand machine builder this is interesting, but only with their own simulation data. Without past calculations, the model has nothing to learn from.

AI proteins against scorpion venom, and OpenAI buys its way into pharma Health Study M&A

Anthropic reports a lab experiment with Claude Science. The system designed 318,484 protein candidates against five venoms from four scorpion species. 211 of them were tested in the lab, 17 met the criteria for a binder. The two strongest bind the venom Cn2. Two independent labs confirmed the results.[10] According to the report, scorpion venoms kill over 3,250 people per year.[10]

Binder: A protein that attaches to a specific other molecule, here the venom. Like a key that fits exactly into a lock and blocks it. A binder is not yet a drug: whether it works in the body and is tolerated only a later test will show.
Method A vendor study about its own product. The hit rate is about 8% (17 of 211, own calculation). Nothing has been tested yet as an antidote. Confirmation by two external labs is a good sign, but does not replace a peer-reviewed publication.

The same day, the-decoder reported: OpenAI buys Orchestra Bio. The start-up builds AI agents for drug development and is to be folded into OpenAI's Rosalind platform. The companies do not disclose the price. According to the report, Orchestra works with Novo Nordisk and Moderna. Drug development in the US takes 10 to 15 years there.[11]

My take: An 8% hit rate sounds low, but that is the point. In the past, you tested thousands of candidates for a handful of hits. If the AI pre-sorts the list so far that 211 trials suffice, that saves months of lab work. Both big players now want into pharma. For twiceD this is not a market, access requires domain expertise and labs.

Manager survey: excited, but afraid of losing control Study B2B-SaaS [12]

Nielsen surveyed 1,700 directors and senior managers in seven countries on behalf of Monday.com. TechCrunch reports on Oct. 21:

  • 94% are excited about AI.
  • 78% have changed how they evaluate employees. 39% let AI draft performance reviews.
  • 58% expect to manage more AI agents than humans within a year.
  • 46% fear losing control. Only 29% consider their AI infrastructure fully built out.
Method A commissioned survey: Monday.com sells work-organization software, including with agents. The report gives no margin of error, time period or list of countries. "Excited" is a mood, not a result.

My take: I ignore the 94%. The 39% is the interesting part: more than one in three managers lets AI judge people, at least as a draft. In Germany that would be delicate. An AI system for evaluations can count as a high-risk system under the AI Act, and the works council has a say in performance monitoring. That 58% want to manage more agents than humans while only 29% have the technology for it describes the gap in which consultants earn money.

US kicks DeepSeek out of government agencies, China plans export controls Public Policy

The US Department of Homeland Security (DHS) ordered on Oct. 20, 2026: all civilian federal agencies must exclude and remove DeepSeek products. This also applies to products from other vendors built on DeepSeek technology. The report names no deadline, exceptions are possible. China protests.[13] At the same time, the-decoder reports that China wants to tighten control over the export of open models and 23 key AI technologies.[14]

Open weights: The vendor releases the finished model for download. Anyone can run it on their own machines. Like a recipe you cook at home instead of eating at the restaurant. That is why many products are invisibly built on DeepSeek.

My take: The important part is "including products built on it". Many companies do not know which model sits inside an AI tool they bought. Anyone supplying US agencies has to know that now. German companies are not directly hit yet. But the question "which model is inside your product?" will end up in procurement questionnaires. On the China item I have only the headline, details are missing.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Agent check for Mittelstand web shops: let in, lock out or limit

ELI5 & Value: Soon it will not only be humans shopping in a web shop, but also their AI assistants. A German merchant needs to know three things. First: does my shop recognize an agent at all, or does the bot protection block it by chance? Second: can an agent read my products, prices and delivery times cleanly? Third: who is liable when an agent orders wrongly? The check answers this in a fixed package and ends with a clear rule: in, out, or in with limits. Like a safety inspection for the shop door before the new customers arrive. Value: the merchant decides deliberately, instead of chance or bot protection deciding for them, and does not lose early agent purchases to the competition.
Prototype (V0): (1) Test the shop with a real agent: search for a product, add to cart, go up to just before payment. Log where it gets stuck. (2) Check the bot protection and the access rules that today block or let through agents unintentionally. (3) Check the product feed: are price, stock and delivery time machine-readable and current? (4) Define an agent rule with the merchant, modeled on Walmart's approach: approval per purchase or spending limit, plus text for the terms and conditions and right of withdrawal, to be approved by a lawyer. (5) A report with a list of changes ranked by effort. MVP deliberately narrow: one shop, no custom agent interface, no payment integration. Existing building blocks: the open Agentic Commerce Protocol from OpenAI and Stripe (Apache 2.0) as the target picture for checkout[6] and Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol for recognizing verified agents.[7]
Market & Gap: The demand side is moving. Walmart has been opening its own agent access since Oct. 21, Amazon is locking agents out.[1] Strategy& expects €17 billion of agent revenue for Germany by 2030, a forecast to treat with caution.[4] The tooling side is taken: OpenAI and Stripe supply the protocol[6], Visa the recognition.[7] In Hanover, Shopagentic builds agents that take over shop operations, with a fashion brand as first customer.[5] That is a product for running the shop, not an independent check of the door. I did not find a market size for agent consulting, so I name none. Gap for twiceD: a neutral, short check in German that brings together technology, bot protection and contract questions. The protocol vendors sell their own solution, and a large consultancy is not worth it for a 50-person merchant.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): Medium to high. The check is short-duration consulting: about 3 to 5 days per shop, no maintenance afterward. An agent for the test purchases and a check script for the feed can be reused, which makes each further check faster. Scale with a fixed template and a contractor from about four checks per month. Two honest risks: the market in Germany is early, many merchants do not see the problem yet. And the standards change fast, the template needs about 2 to 3 hours of upkeep per week. Limit: liability and terms and conditions belong with a lawyer, twiceD supplies the technical groundwork. A follow-up engagement would be implementing the changes, which is normal shop-project business. techcrunch.com · agenticcommerce.dev · t3n.de · visa.com · pressefeuer.at
