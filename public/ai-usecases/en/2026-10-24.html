Anthropic: Juniors Are Becoming Rare, Meetings Are Increasing Work Study

Anthropic has published a report on work inside the company. According to t3n (Oct 18):[1]

  • The share of junior staff in the technical departments fell from 21% to 14% since early 2025.
  • Among new technical hires, it fell from 27% to 9%.
  • Technical teams are now only 56% of the workforce, up from 61% before.
  • Time in meetings rose by 20% per person year over year, based on calendar data.
  • In August, the interactive AI agents worked six times as many hours as the humans.
Agent hours: An AI agent works through a task on its own, such as writing and testing code. If it runs for one hour, that counts as one agent hour. One person can run several agents at the same time. Like a cook with five pots on the stove: they stand in the kitchen for one hour, and the pots cook for five hours together.
Method An AI maker reports on itself. According to t3n, the report does not say what "junior" means. Nobody has checked the numbers independently. I did not open Anthropic's original report; all numbers come from t3n. Anthropic is not a normal employer: the company builds exactly the tools that replace work here, and has an interest in the message "agents work."

My take: The 9% is the number to remember. If a company that uses AI more intensively than anyone hardly hires beginners in technical roles anymore, other companies will follow in two to three years. The problem comes later: whoever trains no juniors today will have no seniors in 2030. The 20% increase in meetings fits this. Anyone who steers agents spends more time coordinating and reviewing and less on their own work. For German companies this means: the question is not only "How many jobs does AI save?" but "How do new people learn the craft when agents take over the learning tasks?"

Mistral and Nvidia: AI for Industrial Simulation Mfg EU

Mistral and Nvidia want to build AI together for engineering simulation: for aerospace, the auto industry and semiconductors. According to the-decoder (Oct 21), the project combines Mistral Large 4 and physics AI with Nvidia software (CUDA-X, Warp, Omniverse).[2] Mistral Large 4 "has about one trillion parameters and was trained on 3,800 Grace Blackwell GPUs in Europe."[2] Open benchmarks and datasets have been announced, as has a release of the model weights at the end of October. By 2030, Mistral wants to expand its compute capacity to one gigawatt.[2]

Engineering simulation: Before an airplane wing is built, a computer calculates how air flows along it and where the metal breaks. That often takes hours to days per variant. An AI trained on many such calculations estimates the result in seconds. Like an experienced engineer who says "that will hold" at a glance, before the exact calculation runs.
Open weights: The company releases the finished model for download. Anyone can run it on their own servers without sending data to the maker.
Status An announcement, not a deployment at a named customer. The release of the open weights is planned but not yet confirmed. There are no benchmarks yet.

My take: This is the most interesting industry news of the week, because it hits the German core exactly: machine building, automotive, suppliers. Simulation data is company secret. An open model, trained in Europe and able to run on your own servers, solves the problem "We don't put our design data into a US cloud." Whether it is technically good will only show with the promised benchmarks. I would wait until the weights are actually available.

Google Cloud: Three Model Families, One Bill B2B-SaaS Deploy

Google Cloud is the first provider to offer models from Google (Gemini), Anthropic (Claude) and OpenAI on one platform. From OpenAI, according to the-decoder, come GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna.[3] "The models will initially launch as a private preview for select enterprise customers." And: "All costs run through a single Google Cloud bill."[3] The costs can be offset against existing cloud contracts. Google does not state prices.

Offsetting against the cloud contract: Large companies often promise Google to spend a fixed amount over several years, and get a discount for it. If they now also buy Claude or GPT through Google, that counts toward this amount. Like a coupon book that suddenly works in other shops too.

My take: For procurement and administration this is good news: one contract, one bill, one data protection framework. The flip side is a new dependency. The company no longer ties itself to one model, but to one cloud. That fits a number from the heise update of Oct 21: through Vercel's AI Gateway, more than 70% of traffic runs over open models, and in September almost every second team switched its most-used model.[4] Models become interchangeable. Who decides which model is used for which task? See the project idea below.

Audit at OpenAI: Gaps in Agent Monitoring Governance Study

The independent audit organizations METR and Redwood Research were on site at OpenAI from 07/21 to 08/11. They were to examine how OpenAI monitors its own AI agents. According to the-decoder (Oct 23), "an estimated 6 to 25 percent of internal agentic Responses API traffic" ran without monitoring.[5] The full report has 61 pages; METR and Redwood received a shortened version of 38 pages. "Only a short summary is publicly available."[5]

Agent monitoring: A second AI or a program reads along with what an agent does and raises the alarm on dangerous steps. Like a camera in a warehouse: it only helps if it sees all the aisles. Here, depending on the estimate, one aisle in four to one in sixteen was out of view.
Method The range of 6 to 25% is wide, so this is a rough estimate. The public sees only a summary; the details stay with OpenAI. On the positive side: OpenAI let external auditors in at all. The part about Codex is in the ai-dev brief.

My take: If even OpenAI does not see every agent in its own house, a mid-sized company certainly does not see its agents. That fits this week's bank freeze: agents are deployed faster than companies can control them. For companies that operate high-risk systems under the AI Act, complete logging becomes mandatory from December 2027. Whoever starts today has time. Whoever starts in 2027 has a problem.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Model Portfolio Check: which AI model for which task, with an EU option

ELI5 & value: Many companies use one AI model for everything, often the most expensive one, because it was there first. Now there are models from three big makers on one cloud, plus open models such as the planned Mistral Large 4 that you can run yourself. The check takes a company's real AI tasks (for example sorting emails, writing quotes, summarizing documents) and tests them with three to four models. Result: one recommended model per task, with cost, quality and the question of whether the data leaves the EU. Like comparing electricity tariffs: not every appliance needs the most expensive tariff. Value: lower running costs, and for sensitive data a path without a US cloud.
Prototype (V0): (1) Pick 3 to 5 real tasks with the customer and collect 20 to 50 real examples for each. (2) Define a simple rating for each task: right, wrong, needs rework. (3) Send all examples through 3 to 4 models, including one open model on your own server. (4) Put results, cost per 1,000 transactions and data location into a table. (5) A report with one recommendation per task. MVP deliberately narrow: one customer, three tasks, no custom software, no ongoing operation. Existing building blocks: the clouds' model catalogs, an open-source evaluation tool, Ollama or a similar server for the open model.
Market & gap: Google Cloud now bundles Gemini, Claude and GPT-6 on one platform, but only as a private preview for select large customers and without prices.[3] At Vercel, more than 70% of traffic runs over open models, and almost every second team switched its main model in September.[4] This shows: models are becoming interchangeable, and the choice changes often. I did not research other providers of model comparisons or a market size today, so I name none. What the clouds leave out: they recommend their own platform, not the cheapest model, and no open model on the customer's server. Gap for twiceD: a neutral comparison with the customer's real data, in German, with an honest EU or on-prem option.
Feasibility (solo + AI): High. This is short-term consulting, about 3 to 5 days per company, with no maintenance afterwards. Test scripts and report template are reusable. About 2 hours per week go into maintaining the model list, because new models arrive quickly. Scaling through a contractor from about three companies per month. Honest risk: the customer must hand over real examples, which needs a clean data processing agreement. The market research on competitors is still missing and must be done before an offer. Follow-up work: migrating to the recommended model or operating an open model. the-decoder.de · heise.de · the-decoder.de
