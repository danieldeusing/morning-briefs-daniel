American Express: $336 million for the restructuring, AI appears only in a quote Finance Deploy

American Express reported third-quarter results on Friday, Oct 23. According to the filing with the US securities regulator SEC:[1]

  • Restructuring charge: $336 million pre-tax, $253 million after tax, $0.37 per share. The release gives only "efficiency initiatives" as the reason.
  • In the adjusted figures, the charge is offset against the gain from the sale of the stake in GBTG (business travel).
  • Profit: $3.7 billion, a year earlier $2.9 billion. Revenue: $20.1 billion, up 9%.
  • AI appears only once in the release: under risks, as "the adoption of artificial intelligence (AI)". No headcount figures are given.

The AI story comes from a secondary source. The outlet BERI writes on Oct 25: "American Express is cutting about 5% of its workforce." It quotes the CFO from the earnings call as saying the company wants to "reduce layers and increase spans of controls, so that we can automate".[2]

Source I know the 5% and the quote only from BERI. I did not find the earnings-call transcript, and my own search turned up only older Amex cut rounds from 2009, 2013 and 2015. How many jobs exactly are meant is open. Amex does not officially say that AI is the reason for the cuts.
Span of control: How many employees report directly to one manager. If a company removes a layer of middle managers, each remaining manager has more people under them. Like a school that abolishes the grade-level coordinator: the classroom teachers then report directly to the principal.
Cut layers→ more people per manager→ standardize processes→ then automate

My take: A company with profit up 28% restructures and offsets the costs against a one-off gain. That is classic cost management in good times. As far as the evidence goes, AI here is the story for analysts, not the proven reason. The order in the quote is still interesting and honest: first reorganize, then automate. I know this from projects. A process with five approval stages does not get faster with AI, only the waiting queue in front of it gets longer. But be careful with the reverse conclusion: whoever cuts managers also cuts people who approve things. That is exactly where Anthropic sees the bottleneck (right).

Anthropic: agents wait 80% of the time for humans Work Study

New look since Oct 21 The study "How employment is changing at Anthropic" from Oct 16 appeared here already on Oct 21, with the junior share of technical new hires (27% → 9%) and the ratio of interactive agent hours to human hours as of August (6 : 1).[3] On Oct 19, Fortune put a different figure from it in the foreground, under the keyword "botsitting", meaning babysitting bots.[4] Anthropic writes: "Claude sessions amount to around 80% idly waiting for human input."[3]

Botsitting: A person starts several AI agents at once and spends their time checking and approving the results. Like a cook with six pots on the stove: the pots cook on their own, but none is finished until the cook stirs and tastes.
Method A self-study by a single AI company about its own people: 192 agent users against 74 non-users, plus 17 interviews. Anthropic itself writes that adoption was not random and the results could be biased.[3] The work is mostly programming. The figures apply to this company, not to a Mittelstand firm. I know the Fortune article only as a headline.

My take: In practice the 80% matter more than the 6 : 1. They say: more agents do not make a company faster if the human who checks and approves remains the bottleneck. For companies outside the tech industry this is good news. The lever is not the best model, but the question of where in the process a human must say yes, and whether they do it in bundles or one by one. The coding side of the study is in the ai-dev brief.

Three companies, three ways of talking about AI and jobs Work 7 days

Putting the cases of recent weeks side by side, you see three different languages:

  • American Express (Oct 23): cuts with a dollar amount on the balance sheet, AI only in the spoken quote.[1][2]
  • Anthropic (Oct 16): no layoffs, but clearly fewer junior new hires.[3]
  • Deutsche Telekom (investor day Oct 5, brief of Oct 8): target of about €2.5 billion lower indirect costs by 2030 versus 2023, about €1.1 billion gross already in 2027. The chatbot "Frag Magenta" handled about 2.6 million calls in the first half. Telekom gives no figure on jobs.[5]
Method All figures come from the companies themselves. According to Mobiflip, the Telekom billions are "mostly targets", not savings already achieved.[5] The three cases are not comparable: a bank, an AI company, a network operator.

My take: None of the three companies says clearly "AI replaces X jobs". That is not only caution. It is hard to separate the effect cleanly, and it is politically expensive to name it. For consultants this means: a client rarely asks "How many people do I save?". They ask "Where does the process get stuck, and who has to approve it?". The project idea below builds on that.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Approval-bottleneck check: where humans slow down the process, before AI comes in

ELI5 & Value: Many companies want to speed up a process with AI, such as invoice review or quote creation. Often, though, the time is not spent on the work itself but on waiting for a signature. Anthropic measures internally that agents wait about 80% of their time for humans.[3] And according to a secondary source, the Amex CFO names the same order: first restructure layers, then automate.[2] The check measures, for one process, where the time gets stuck and which approvals can be bundled, tied to a value threshold, or dropped. Like a traffic jam: a faster car does not help if the traffic light is the problem. Value: afterwards the client knows whether AI will speed anything up at all, and does not spend money on an agent that then just waits.
Prototype (V0): (1) Pick a process with many approvals together with the client, for example incoming invoices. (2) Export the timestamps from the ERP or ticket system: when created, when reviewed, when approved. (3) Use the open-source library PM4Py to compute cycle times and waiting times per step.[11] (4) Two hours of conversation with the people who approve: why does it take so long? (5) Result: one page with the three biggest waiting points and one suggestion each, such as "no second signature under €500" or "review once a day in a bundle". Only after that, the question of where AI helps. MVP deliberately narrow: one process, one client, no custom software, no continuous monitoring.
Market & Gap: The field is called process mining. The best-known vendor is Celonis, alongside SAP Signavio and UiPath. Celonis does not publish prices. According to a user comparison site (as of October 2026), entry projects start at "$100K to $250K annual", and "data volume scaling can get costly quickly".[10] These are user statements, not from the vendor. I found no reliable market size: the estimates in search were off from one another by a large multiple, so I cite none. What the platforms leave out: they measure data traces, but they do not ask why a person hesitates. They pay off for large corporations with many processes, not for a company of 150 people with one painful process. Gap for twiceD: a fixed-price analysis of a single process, with an open tool instead of a license, in German, with a concrete recommendation instead of a dashboard.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): High. This is short-term consulting: about 3 to 5 days per process, depending on how clean the data is. No maintenance afterwards. A language model helps with cleaning up the exports and writing the report. About 1 to 2 hours per week to maintain the analysis scripts, which get better with every client. Scaling via a contractor who prepares the data exports. Honest risks: some ERP systems do not store usable timestamps, and then only the conversation is left. And the finding "your boss is slowing things down" is uncomfortable. That takes tact in the report. A follow-up engagement is obvious: build the first agent at exactly the spot the check found. anthropic.com · peerspot.com · pm4py.fit.fraunhofer.de
