Microsoft: 60,000 offers to leave, and the data centers help foot the bill Work US

On Monday, Oct. 26, Microsoft announced a new program for voluntary departures. According to Fox Business, it applies to "about 60,000 eligible employees". These people can leave with money and help finding a new job.[1] The key facts from the article:

  • As of Jun. 30, 2026, Microsoft had around 223,000 full-time employees. So the offer reaches more than a quarter.
  • It applies in the US, the UK, Canada and India, only in some business units.
  • Employees up to internal career level 70 are eligible. The offer is time-limited.
  • It follows a voluntary early retirement program from earlier this year.

HR chief Amy Coleman writes in a memo that the program "reflects an important part of how we're evolving our approach to change". A voluntary exit is new for Microsoft.[1] Fox puts it in context: Microsoft and the other tech giants face expensive investments in AI data centers and chips, and that triggers cost-cutting rounds in other areas.[1]

Voluntary buyout program: The company fires no one. It offers a large group money if they leave of their own accord. Each person decides for themselves whether to accept. Like an overbooked flight: at the gate, the airline asks who will take the later flight in exchange for a voucher.
Billions for AI data centers→ Pressure on the remaining costs→ Offer to 60,000 people→ lower personnel costs
Source I read only the Fox article itself. Other reports (according to a search snippet, citing Reuters, Bloomberg and NBC) mention a deadline in early December, an exit on Dec. 31 and an investment figure for 2026. Those pages were blocked or paywalled. Fox itself names no deadline, so I do not state the numbers as fact. How high the severance is stated nowhere I have read.

My view: This story is often told as "AI replaces jobs." What is documented is something else: AI costs so much money that Microsoft saves on people. AI is the cost driver here, not the replacement. A voluntary offer also has a known catch: the company does not decide who leaves. Often the people who accept are exactly those who can easily find a new job, meaning the good ones. With them goes the knowledge that is written down nowhere. And for the Mittelstand the indirect consequence matters more: anyone investing this much in data centers wants the money back through cloud and AI prices. The project idea below builds on this, on the knowledge side, not the pricing side.

AI Act: 36 days left until the next deadline Reg EU DE

The amendment to the AI Act (the "Omnibus") has been law since July: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in the Official Journal on Jul. 24, 2026, in force since Jul. 27, 2026.[4] What comes next:

  • Wed Dec 2, 2026: new prohibitions on AI that generates nude images of real people and on child abuse material: "These prohibitions apply from 2 December 2026."[4]
  • Same period: Generators that were already on the market before Aug. 2, 2026 have a four-month grace period for the machine-readable labeling of their output. Other transparency obligations, such as the notice on deepfakes, have applied since Aug. 2, 2026.[4]
  • Thu Dec 2, 2027: high-risk obligations under Annex III (such as personnel selection and credit checks), previously Aug. 2, 2026. Annex I (AI in regulated products) follows on Aug. 2, 2028.[4]

In Germany, the KI-Marktüberwachungsgesetz (KI-MIG, AI market surveillance act) has been in force since Jul. 29, 2026. It makes the Bundesnetzagentur the "Marktüberwachungsbehörde, Anlaufstelle und Beschwerdestelle für die KI-Verordnung" (market surveillance authority, contact point and complaints office for the AI regulation).[5] For small and mid-sized companies there is an AI service desk with "niederschwellige[n] Informationen und Orientierung" (low-threshold information and guidance). An AI regulatory sandbox, in which companies can test AI systems, is planned.[5]

Machine-readable labeling: An invisible label in an image, text or video that a program can detect: "An AI made this." Like the watermark in a banknote, only for software instead of the eye.

My view: For the typical Mittelstand company, Dec. 2 is not a big day. It mainly concerns the providers of generators. More important is the Bundesnetzagentur as a contact: there is now a German body you can ask and where customers can complain. Anyone selling their own generator feature, such as text drafts in industry software, should clarify labeling by December.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Knowledge handover before departure: a Q&A assistant from an expert's head

ELI5 & Value: When an experienced employee leaves, into retirement or with a severance package as now at Microsoft, their knowledge leaves with them. Most of it is in no document: why the plant is set up differently in winter, which supplier helps with rush orders. The idea: in the last weeks before departure, you hold structured conversations with this person, transcribe them automatically and link them to their documents. The result is a Q&A assistant that answers with a source: "According to Mr. M., conversation of Nov. 12". Like grandma's recipe book, which you start before it is too late. The need is large: according to the IW, another 16.5 million baby boomers will reach retirement age by 2036, and only around 12.5 million working-age people are coming up behind them.[10] Value: fewer costly mistakes and a shorter onboarding for the successor.
Prototype (V0): (1) With the customer, pick one person and one knowledge area, such as the maintenance of a plant. (2) Five to eight conversations of one hour each following a guide, recorded and transcribed locally with the open speech model Whisper. (3) Transcripts and existing documents into a search index, with an open model via Ollama on a machine at the customer's site. (4) The successor tests the assistant with real questions, gaps go back as questions into the next conversation. (5) Handover: index, transcripts and a list of open points. MVP deliberately narrow: one person, one area, no connection to the intranet or ERP, no permanent platform.
Market & Gap: Enterprise search with AI already exists, the best-known vendor is Glean. Glean does not publish prices. According to competitor Workativ (February 2026), there is usually a minimum of a "50 to 100 seat range", and entry contracts start "around $60,000".[11] These are a competitor's claims, so take them with caution. Such tools search what is already written down. They do not extract the knowledge in people's heads, and for a company with 150 people and a single departing expert they are too big. I found no reliable market size for knowledge handover, so I name none. Gap for twiceD: a fixed-price package per person, with conversation guidance in German, transcript and index on the customer's own hardware. The conversations contain trade secrets, so "runs only on your side" is a real argument.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): Medium to high. The technology is ready to use: Whisper, an open model, a search index. The work lies in guiding the conversations and cleaning up the transcripts, where a language model helps. Roughly two to three weeks per person, spread across the notice period. After that hardly any maintenance, as long as the customer runs the index themselves. Hosted continuous operation would mean upkeep, so twiceD should only offer it as an add-on. Scaling via a contractor who conducts the conversations. Honest risks: a departing employee is not always keen to help, especially after a dismissal. And the works council must approve recordings. Both are settled before the first conversation. iwkoeln.de · workativ.com · github.com/openai/whisper · ollama.com
