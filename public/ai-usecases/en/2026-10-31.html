Anthropic: Experts adopt the AI's opinion and take it for their own Study Global

On Oct 27, Anthropic published a study with 50 experts in causal statistics. Each expert solved an analysis task about a fictional agricultural cooperative, with an AI agent as a helper. The trick: on four decisions, the agent was assigned its position at random. Result: the experts chose the agent's option in 86% of cases. Without influence, 50% would be expected.[1]

The more important part: 77% of the experts (37 of 48) adopted at least one argument from the agent. But only 15% mentioned in their reasoning that an AI was involved. So the experts took the thoughts for their own.[1]

There is also a good side. With AI, only 6% of the papers (3 of 50) had serious errors; without AI it was 55% (6 of 11). With AI, the experts needed 65 minutes on average instead of 90.[1] Anthropic itself writes: "There may be no single right division of labor between people and AI."[1]

Anchoring effect: If you hear a number or opinion first, you stay close to it, even if it was arbitrary. Like at a flea market: if the seller names $50 first, you end up closer to $40 than to $10. The study shows this effect in experts working with an AI agent.
Methodology The study comes from an AI vendor. 50 participants is few; the group without AI had only 11 people and selected itself. The task was fictional. Who paid for the study is not stated in the summary. I know the figures from The Decoder's report; I did not open Anthropic's original text.

My take: This is the most important report of the week for anyone rolling out AI in operations. Almost every concept for lending, claims settlement or candidate selection ends with the sentence "A human decides in the end". The study says: the human often decides only formally. They nod the proposal through and write it down as their own reasoning. The benefit (fewer errors) is real, but it holds only as long as the agent is right. Anyone planning a "human in the loop" must also measure how often that human disagrees. If the rate is close to zero, the human is a signature, not a reviewer.

Reddit: Google writes the answer, the visitors stay away Consumer US

Reddit's stock fell more than 25% on Oct 30, even though the quarterly results were good. The reason lies in a statement from CEO Steve Huffman: in October, Reddit lost more than 1 million visitors per week from Google Search. He blames Google's AI summaries and AI Mode: "AI Mode does not drive traffic. Not for us, and not to anybody as far as I know."[2] Outside the US, Reddit is now testing login requirements.[2]

Google writes the answer itself→Users don't click through→Fewer visitors to the source→Less advertising, fewer new users
AI Mode / AI Overviews: Google shows a ready-made answer above the search results, written by an AI from many pages. Like a tour guide who reads you the restaurant's menu: you no longer need to go there. That is bad for the restaurant.
Source The figures "over 25%" and "over 1 million per week" come from a single source (The Decoder). I did not check revenue and profit for the third quarter. Older articles in search results concern the second quarter, with different figures.

My take: Reddit is one of the sites Google likes to cite most. If even Reddit loses visitors, smaller sites are hit even harder. For a mid-sized company that finds customers via Google, this means: the question is no longer just "Do I rank first?" but "Does the AI answer name me at all?". The project idea below follows from this.

An AI agent chooses the therapy and operates on a mouse Health UK

Gabriel Ocana-Santero, a doctoral student at Oxford and head of the start-up Exin Therapeutics, reports on an experiment with Claude Sonnet 5. The agent was given 19 possible gene therapies, a mouse with epilepsy and a motorized device for brain procedures. It chose the active agent, target region and coordinates itself, based on three research papers, and checked the procedure through 32 camera images. When one target lay outside the device's reach, the agent stopped.[3]

A veterinarian approved every step that cannot be undone and had an emergency stop. The larger models Opus 5 and Opus 5.5 declined the task. The mouse is said to have recovered.[3] Ocana-Santero: "I am not aware of an agent previously choosing a treatment and a brain target".[3]

Stereotactic procedure: The brain is mapped with coordinates like a city map. A device guides a fine needle exactly to one point, for example "3 mm back, 2 mm to the side, 4 mm deep". Like a GPS for the needle.
Source & methodology A single source: The Decoder summarizes an X post by the researcher.[4] There is no paper and no independent review; a technical report is only announced. One mouse is not a result, it is a demonstration.

My take: Medically, this is not yet anything. What matters is the pattern: the agent plans, a human approves every risky step. That is exactly the setup the Anthropic study above calls into question. A veterinarian with an emergency stop is good. But whether they really review the plan or just nod it through, nobody measures.

US banks: Who is in the account? Finance US

Update since Oct 28 According to Axios on Oct 22, large US banks have found third-party AI agents in customer accounts. The agents mostly log in with the customer's password. Banks react differently: some block, others limit.[5] Bank of America CEO Moynihan told CNBC: "We strongly believe there should be a human in the loop to authorise money moving out of your account".[5] A summary from Oct 29 notes that details on access and on the agents' actions "haven't been fully disclosed".[6]

Source The Axios article is behind a paywall. I know it only from summaries. Which bank does exactly what contradicts itself between sources, so I name no attribution. There are no figures on cases or damage.

My take: Here too, the answer is "human in the loop". After today's study, I ask: which human, with how much time, and how often do they say no? More in the brief of Oct 28.

Insurers: AI in the claims office, but more staff Finance US

On October earnings calls, W. R. Berkley reported that AI had improved efficiency in intake of new cases "by more than 20%" (Insurance Journal, Oct 21).[7] At the same time, State Farm plans to hire around 3,000 additional people for claims handling by 2027, around 5,500 with natural attrition, at about 11 million claims per year. State Farm does not mention AI in this context.[7]

Source Both statements come from a vendor's summary (bevaya.ai); I did not open the original articles. "Efficiency in intake" is not defined more precisely.

My take: A good counter-image to the replacement narrative. AI makes intake faster, but the largest insurer in the US is still building up staff. The bottleneck stays where a human talks to the customer and decides.

AI Act: 32 days to go Reg EU

Nothing new on regulation today. The amendment to the AI Act (the "Omnibus") has been in force since 2026-07-27.[8] The deadlines:

  • Wed 2026-12-02: The disclosure obligations under Art. 50 have applied since 2026-08-02. For systems that were on the market before, the transition period for machine-readable labeling ends on 2026-12-02.[8]
  • Thu 2027-12-02: High-risk AI under Annex III, such as candidate selection and lending.[8][9]
  • Wed 2028-08-02: High-risk AI in products under Annex I.[8][9]
Machine-readable labeling: An invisible label in the image, video or text that programs can read: "An AI made this." Like the watermark in a banknote, but for computers.

In Germany, the Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur) is the central supervisor, and BaFin for high-risk AI in finance.[10] When exactly the German implementing law took effect is open: one source gives 2026-07-29, while the federal government only writes "on the day after promulgation".

Source Deadlines from law-firm summaries (Lewis Silkin, Gibson Dunn); I did not read the text in the EU Official Journal. Many SME guides online still give 2026-08-02 as the upcoming deadline for high-risk AI. That has been outdated since the Omnibus.

Nvidia and Hugging Face: The antitrust watchdogs take a look M&A US

According to Reuters (Oct 28), the US Department of Justice is reviewing Nvidia's planned purchase of Hugging Face. The deal is worth $12.93 billion and is due to close in the first half of 2027. The French competition authority is separately concerned about dependence on Nvidia's CUDA software.[11]

Hugging Face: The largest open library for AI models, a kind of GitHub for models. Many companies download open models there to run on their own servers.

My take: For companies betting on open models to stay independent of US cloud providers, this is a risk. If the maker of the chips also owns the library of models, "sovereign" gets harder to explain. One more reason to mirror open models locally as well.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · AI Visibility Check: Does ChatGPT name our business when a customer asks?

ELI5 & value: Many buyers no longer ask only Google today, but ChatGPT, Gemini or Google's AI Mode: "Who makes custom stainless-steel containers in southern Germany?" The AI names three companies. Whoever is not among them does not get the inquiry. Reddit shows that the click to your own site drops out in the process.[2] The idea: a fixed-price check for a mid-sized company. twiceD puts 30 to 50 typical customer questions to several AI systems, counts how often the company is named and whether the details are correct, and delivers a list of changes. Like a mystery shopper, but at the AI instead of in the store. Value: the customer sees whether they exist in the new search, and gets concrete steps before inquiries fall further.
Prototype (V0): (1) Collect 30 to 50 real questions from the customer's buyers, in German and English. (2) Put the questions to several models programmatically, through their official interfaces, several times per question because the answers vary. (3) Evaluate: Is the company named, at which position, with which facts, which competitors appear next to it, which sources does the AI cite? (4) Check the company's own site: are products, locations and certificates clearly and machine-readably stated? (5) Report with a traffic light and ten concrete changes, re-measurement after three months. MVP deliberately narrow: one customer, one product group, text questions. No permanent dashboard, no advertising in AI answers.
Market & gap: Tools for this exist, and one comes from Berlin: Peec AI received funding of $21 million in November 2025 and, according to tech.eu, has more than 1,300 brands and agencies as customers.[13] Prices start at $95 per month according to the Peec page and go through $245 and $495 up to "Custom". The plans measure ChatGPT, AI Mode, AI Overviews, Copilot, Gemini and Naver AI.[14] Such tools measure; they change nothing. They target marketing teams and agencies that read the dashboard themselves and turn it into work. A mid-sized company with 80 people and no marketing department does not have that person. I found no reliable market size, so I name none. Gap for twiceD: do the measurement once with a ready-made tool or own script, and then deliver the work on the site and on the product data, in German and for one niche.
Feasibility (solo + AI): High. The query script is built in a few days, the model costs per check are small, and a language model does the first pass of the evaluation. About three to five days per customer, then no maintenance except the paid re-measurement. Scaling via a contractor who implements the site changes. Honest limits: the AI's answers vary, so show only tendencies and promise no guarantee of first place. And the market is young, many agencies already offer "GEO"; twiceD wins only with a niche and implementation, not with the report alone. tech.eu · peec.ai · the-decoder.com
