Waters: Lab measurement software talks to AI agents Deploy Health US

Lab equipment maker Waters announced "AI Link" on Oct 13. It is a bridge between its lab software waters_connect and AI agents such as Claude, built on Anthropic's open MCP standard. According to the release, it is "available now for early access, with general availability in November".[1] A lab worker is supposed to be able to retrieve and analyze measurement data by asking a question instead of clicking through menus.

LC-MS (liquid chromatography with mass spectrometry): A device that first separates a sample, such as a drug or blood, into its components and then "weighs" each part. That shows what is in it and how much. Pharma companies use it to check every batch. The software for it is powerful but hard to use.
Source A press release from the manufacturer. It names no customer and no measured impact. The claim that an integration shrinks from "weeks or months" to "hours or minutes" is tied by the release to the Model Hardware Standard (MHS) research program in which Waters participates. It is an expectation, not a result.[1] The technical side (MCP) belongs in the ai-dev brief.

My take: This is the most interesting pattern of the week, precisely because it is so unspectacular. Many companies have expensive specialist software that only two people in the building can use properly. A bridge through which an AI agent can read that software makes the knowledge usable more widely. In the pharma world, though, every change to a measurement workflow requires validation. So I expect use first in research and analysis, not in batch release. The project idea below follows from this.

Persona: Agents check IDs, says the vendor Finance US

Identity-verification company Persona says, according to a news aggregator from Oct 30, that its "Case Review Agents" "already support hundreds of thousands of identity decisions in production".[2] The agents look at cases where the automatic check was uncertain, such as a blurry ID photo. According to the Persona blog, early access started on May 7, 2026. The customer chooses whether the agent decides on its own or only writes a recommendation for a human.[3]

KYC (Know Your Customer): Banks and payment services are legally required to know who their customer is. That's why you upload a photo of your ID and a selfie when you join a new bank. A program checks most cases. The unclear ones land with a human, and those are exactly the cases an agent is now supposed to pre-sort.
Source "Hundreds of thousands" is a vendor claim, passed on by a news aggregator (aiagentstore.ai). I did not find it on Persona's own pages. There is no figure on errors, cost or time. The claim of "more than 30 customers" in early access could not be confirmed, so I don't state it.

My take: "Hundreds of thousands of decisions" sounds big but says nothing about quality. The more important number is missing: in how many cases does the agent decide alone, and how often does the human disagree when it only recommends? According to the Anthropic study from the Oct 31 brief (experts followed a random agent in 86% of cases), the "recommend only" option is less protection than it sounds.

DoorDash: The agent customers are barely there yet Consumer US

According to a news aggregator from Oct 31, DoorDash so far receives only "several hundred" orders via third-party AI agents. The claim comes only from this one aggregator.[4] An analyst note from Citizens is also said to show that the first agent shopping carts are a third of the size. I know this note only from another summary.

Source The figure "several hundred" appears in no original source I found. Whether it comes from a conversation with analysts and what period it covers is open. I therefore don't calculate a ratio to DoorDash's total volume. Please read it as an unconfirmed single claim, not a measured value.

My take: This is the most useful number of the week, precisely because it is so small. For months we've heard that AI agents will soon shop for us. If one of the largest delivery services in the US sees almost none of it, "agent commerce" is a test today, not a market. For a mid-sized retailer that means: clean up your own product data first, then think about agents. A dedicated agent channel isn't worth it yet.

Bitkom: 41% in March, 57% in September Study DE

The checked figure: according to a Bitkom release from Sep 14, 2026, 57% of companies with 20 or more employees in Germany use AI. A year earlier it was 36%, two years earlier 20%. Another 38% plan or discuss use.[5] Of the companies that use, plan or discuss AI, 11% already use AI agents.[5]

The earlier survey: according to a Bitkom release from Mar 11, 2026, 41% of companies with 20 or more employees use AI, and another 48% plan or discuss use (604 companies, surveyed by phone in calendar weeks 2 to 6). 19% say they cut jobs because of AI. The release does not say which companies the 19% refers to, presumably the AI users.[6] Both figures are therefore real. The catch: the September release gives 36% for "a year ago", and that doesn't fit the 41% from the start of the year.

Contradiction & Methodology Two Bitkom surveys, both read directly at Bitkom: March with 604 companies (calendar weeks 2 to 6)[6], September with 603 companies (calendar weeks 28 to 33)[5], each by phone, commissioned by Bitkom, a digital-industry association. The jump from 41% to 57% in six months is large, and the prior-year value of 36% in September doesn't fit the March figure. Bitkom doesn't explain this. The 19% job cuts have no clearly stated base. And heads up: "uses AI" can also mean that an employee opens a chatbot now and then.

My take: For twiceD, the direction matters more than the decimal place. More than half of companies with 20+ people use AI, but only about one in nine of the AI-interested companies uses agents. In between lies the work a small service provider can deliver: turning "we use ChatGPT" into a concrete, measured workflow.

McKinsey: Everyone feels it, few profit from it Study Global

Two figures are circulating from McKinsey's "State of AI 2026": 80% of respondents say AI makes them personally more productive. But the share of companies that see an impact on operating profit (EBIT) is stuck at 37%. The survey ran from May 4 to Jun 8, 2026.[7] A survey by the IT auditors' association ISACA fits this: only 22% say AI has met or exceeded their expectations for return.[8]

Methodology I did not open the McKinsey report itself; the figures come from two summaries. I therefore don't know the sample or the definition of "productivity". It is self-reporting, not measurement. ISACA likewise only as a search excerpt.

My take: "I feel faster" and "the company earns more" are two different things. The week fits that: many agent announcements, not a single measured impact at a named customer.

AI Act: 31 days to go Reg EU

Nothing new, just the clock running. The amendment to the AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, appeared in the Official Journal on Jul 24, 2026 and has applied since Jul 27, 2026.[9] The deadlines:

  • Wed Dec 2, 2026: The disclosure obligations under Art. 50 have applied since Aug 2, 2026. For systems that were on the market before, the transition period for machine-readable labeling ends on Dec 2.[10]
  • Thu Dec 2, 2027: High-risk AI under Annex III, such as hiring and credit granting (previously Aug 2, 2026).[9]
  • Wed Aug 2, 2028: High-risk AI in products under Annex I.[9]
Omnibus: A law that changes several older laws at once, like a bus that takes many passengers along. Here it mainly pushes deadlines back.
Source Deadlines from law-firm texts (National Law Review, Lewis Silkin); I did not read the text in the Official Journal. Many guides still give Aug 2, 2026 for high-risk AI, which is outdated. Whether the wording of the training obligation in Art. 4 has changed is disputed between the sources, so no statement here.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Machine Inquiry: AI agent reads the plant data but writes nothing

ELI5 & Value: Waters demonstrates it in the lab: a bridge lets an AI agent query the data of specialist software.[1] Mid-sized manufacturers have the same situation on the shop floor. Machines report temperatures, unit counts and faults via the industry standard OPC UA, but only the maintenance technician knows where to look. The idea: twiceD builds a bridge through which a shift supervisor asks "Why was Line 3 down last night?" and gets an answer from the real machine data. Like a colleague who has all the displays in view at once. Value: less time spent searching for fault causes, knowledge no longer held by just one person.
Prototype (V0): (1) Pick one machine or line at the customer with an existing OPC UA server. (2) Take an existing open bridge as a starting point, such as the MIT-licensed package opcua-mcp.[12] (3) Remove the write function and make only approved data points readable. (4) Connect a language model, running locally where needed, and test 20 real questions from the shift supervisors. (5) Back every answer with the raw values so the human can verify. MVP deliberately narrow: one line, read only, no control, no dashboard.
Market & Gap: The building blocks exist openly. opcua-mcp reads and writes OPC UA values, but sits at version 0.1.0 from March 2025.[12] A directory notes that this package has no safeguards of its own beyond OPC UA.[13] I did not find an official Siemens offering in a short search, but that doesn't mean there isn't one. I found no reliable market size, so I don't state one. Gap for twiceD: the hobby bridges are too raw for a plant, and the big vendors sell entire platforms. In between, what's missing is the secure, read-only connection of one specific line, in German, cloud-free on request.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): Medium. The bridge can be built solo in two to three weeks because the parts are open. The rest is hard: access to the plant network, the customer's IT security, and data points that are often poorly named. Operation takes about two to four hours per week and customer as long as it only reads. Scaling via a contractor with automation experience per customer. Honest limit: as soon as the customer also wants to control, it becomes a safety project with liability. That doesn't fit a solo operation, so the offering stays read-only. kyodonewsprwire.jp · pypi.org
