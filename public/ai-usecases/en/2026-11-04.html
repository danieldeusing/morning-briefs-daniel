EU Court Opinion: Training Free, Copying Not Reg Consumer EU

The Hungarian press publisher Like Company operates several news portals. It sued Google in Budapest because Gemini allegedly uses its texts. The court in Budapest referred questions on EU law to the EU Court of Justice. According to heise (report of 04.11., 05:33; we could not confirm the date of the opinion itself), the opinion of Advocate General Maciej Szpunar is now available.[1]

His line has two parts:

  • Training: Using protected works for training is "freely permitted", "within a statutory exception", namely the exception for text and data mining.[1]
  • Answers: If a chatbot retrieves press articles and uses them for its answer, then "this could affect the publishers' rights of reproduction and communication to the public".[1]

He draws the line at the subject matter of protection: the publisher's right protects "not the reported facts, but only the 'specific form of expression'". Publishers are therefore not meant to get a monopoly on news. According to heise, Google argues that Gemini learns "linguistic patterns in order to generate original content rather than reproduce content".[1]

Training on press text→ per the opinion: TDM exception, free→ Answer reproduces the wording→ Publisher's right may be infringed
Advocate General and opinion: Before an EU Court ruling, an Advocate General writes an opinion for the judges, a kind of recommendation. The judges do not have to follow it, but often do. The opinion is therefore a strong signal, but not law.
Text and data mining (TDM): An exception in copyright law. Under certain conditions it allows "texts and data to be read and analyzed automatically".[1] Like a student who reads a thousand newspapers to learn to write: allowed. If he then copies out an article: not allowed.
Ancillary copyright for press publishers: A separate right of publishers, alongside the copyright of the journalists. It is meant to stop other platforms from earning money with publishers' texts without paying.
Source I know the opinion only from heise, not from the original at the EU Court of Justice. The heise text names neither the case number nor a date for the opinion, only the time of the report. I have not checked either and therefore do not state them. The opinion is not binding.[1]

My take: For companies that only use AI, this is the more important half. Training is Google's and OpenAI's problem. The answer, however, is every operator's problem. Anyone who builds a chatbot over press content, for example over a press digest or over trade journals in their own archive, may be liable for what the bot outputs verbatim. If the EU Court follows the opinion, the rule of thumb is simple: summarizing and linking is safe, outputting long passages verbatim is not. That can be checked technically, and that is exactly where today's project idea comes from.

Cisco: a 27-Year-Old, Unlimited Tokens and Two Numbers Deploy B2B SaaS US

Cisco executive Jeetu Patel gave Padmanabhan Krishnamurthy an unusual assignment: modernize old code with AI. "He has unlimited AI tokens at his disposal and the instruction 'Go nuts'". Krishnamurthy is 27, sits five levels below Patel and came to Cisco in 2025 through the acquisition of his startup EzDubs. The project runs 24 months. "By the end of 2027, 70 percent of products are supposed to be renewed", meaning rewritten or rebuilt.[2]

Number Conflict Fortune had first reported that 98% of the code would be completely rewritten. Cisco told iX that this is not the goal; it meant 70% of products, rewritten or rebuilt. That is a big difference: "code" and "products" are different measures. I have not read the Fortune article. I know its date (10/30) only from secondary sources.[2]

What is missing: Cisco does not say which product line the pilot is renewing. It is also unclear who the second person on the team is, whether customers will be told, and what this means for updates, stability and security.[2]

Refactoring: Cleaning up code without changing what the program does. Like renovating a kitchen in which cooking continues: the plumbing gets replaced, but the food still has to reach the table every day.

My take: This is a bet, and Cisco is making it publicly before there is a result. That is bold or marketing, probably both. The scale is what is interesting: two people and AI instead of a whole department. If it works, "legacy code modernization with AI" becomes a standard offering, including for mid-sized companies with their 20-year-old specialist applications. I will believe the 70% only when Cisco shows the first modernized product line. The tools behind it, coding agents and new models, are covered in the ai-dev brief.

Digital Summit: Who Loses Their Job? Policy Retail DE

The second day of the Digital Summit in Berlin (11/03) was about AI and work. According to heise, Digital Minister Karsten Wildberger spoke about retail: "There's 20-30 percent margin in there, that is profit." As a counter-example to job fears he named radiology, where the number of radiologists has grown by 17% in ten years. heise reports this only in paraphrase.[3]

Labor Minister Alexander Schweitzer (SPD, in office since mid-October according to heise) said: "The jobs of the future do not exist without AI." Irene Bertschek (ZEW) and Enzo Weber (IAB) described a gap: many people use generative AI, but fixed AI workflows are much rarer. Weber on this: "If the grunt jobs go away, we should not make a crisis out of it." On the other side: an open letter from more than 230 economists warning of bigger consequences than during the Industrial Revolution, and an Adaptavist survey in which "almost three quarters of knowledge workers" want to go back to work before ChatGPT.[3]

Methodology All numbers here are quotes from a stage, not studies. I know the radiology figure and the economists' letter only from heise, with no source of their own. The Adaptavist survey is a vendor survey; I do not know the sample or the question.

My take: The most interesting point is the sentence from Bertschek and Weber: lots of use, little integration into work. The US numbers further right say exactly the same. The debate about job losses is therefore premature. First, companies would have to really build AI into their processes, and that is where it falls short.

India: The Models Should Come to the Data Policy Public IN

According to heise (11/03), India's IT ministry MeitY has presented a discussion paper. Instead of AI companies scraping data from the web, there would be a government data platform. "Training Data Providers" supply data, "Training Data Consumers" train with it, and do so "on the platform's terms, not their own". The paper names health, schools and banks as areas. It builds on India's DEPA framework.[5]

"Model to the data": The data never leaves the vault. The AI company brings its model inside, trains there under fixed rules and takes only the result with it. Like an archive where you may read but not copy anything.

My take: A discussion paper, not a law. But it fits the EU Court opinion: both look for a path between "everything allowed" and "everything forbidden". For Europe, with its health data plans, this is a model to watch.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · The Quote Guard: Checking Chatbot Answers for Overly Long Press Copying

ELI5 & Value: Many companies are currently building internal chatbots over their own documents. These often contain third-party text: the press digest, trade journals, industry services. The EU Court opinion says the facts in them are free, the wording is not.[1] The quote guard checks every answer before it goes out: does it take a long passage verbatim or nearly verbatim from a protected text? Then it shortens it to a summary with a link or flags it. Value: less legal risk, an audit log for the data protection and legal departments, and an argument for approving the chatbot at all. Like an editor who proofreads every text for plagiarism, only in milliseconds.
Prototype (V0): (1) Mark the protected sources in the customer's chatbot index, for example everything from the press digest. (2) Check every answer against these sources with classic text matching (overlapping word sequences, so-called shingles). This needs no AI model. (3) Additionally find near-verbatim passages through a comparison with embeddings, using an open model on the customer's server. (4) A simple rule: above a threshold, the passage is replaced by a summary with a source link, and everything is logged. MVP deliberately narrow: text only, German only, only the sources the customer marks. No legal advice, no assessment of whether a quotation is permitted. Existing building blocks: the index the chatbot already has, a Python library for text matching, an open embedding model.
Market & Gap: The pattern already exists. Microsoft offers a "protected material detection" in Azure AI Content Safety. It "scans the output of large language models" and flags, among other things, news texts, starting at "More than 200 characters taken verbatim from any news article".[8] But: "Protected material detection works with English only."[8] It also checks against a Microsoft list of known content, not against the customer's own licensed sources. On the rights side, the collecting society Corint Media in Germany manages the ancillary copyright of press publishers and broadcasters and commented in July 2026 on AI offerings from Google and Perplexity.[9] The rights holders are therefore organized. I found no market size for such filters, so I cite none. Gap for twiceD: German texts, the customer's own sources, operation on their own server, and someone who attaches this to the existing chatbot.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): High. Text matching is a known, simple method; the hard part is the connection to the customer's chatbot. V0 in 2 to 4 weeks solo, if the chatbot has an index that can be accessed. Low maintenance: about 1 to 2 hours per month and customer for thresholds and new sources. Honest limits: The opinion is not a ruling, and twiceD cannot set the "how long is too long" threshold legally. The customer's lawyer has to do that. Demand depends on whether the EU Court follows the opinion. Therefore sell it as a small add-on package to a chatbot project, not as a product of its own. Scale through one contractor per customer. learn.microsoft.com · corint-media.com · heise.de
