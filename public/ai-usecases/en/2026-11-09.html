ADNOC: AI agents for planning oil fields Deploy Energy UAE

The state oil group ADNOC from Abu Dhabi signed an agreement at the ADIPEC trade show on Nov 2. TNW writes: "ADNOC has signed a multi-year agreement with OpenAI and G42".[1] The basis is OpenAI Frontier, which TNW calls "a platform launched in February that allows businesses to build and manage AI agents". By its own account, ADNOC is the first energy company on this platform. G42 supplies the compute infrastructure in the Emirates. Security work also runs through OpenAI's "Daybreak Blue" program.[1]

The first use case is planning new fields. According to ADNOC, the system could "produce a complete development plan in hours, a process that currently takes months".[1] Humans are still meant to review major decisions. The starting point is not small: ADNOC says it has rolled out ChatGPT Enterprise "to more than 40,000 employees" and runs more than 200 AI applications and 70 robotics projects, in use or in development.[1]

Field development plan: The document that sets out how an oil or gas field is developed: how many wells, where, with what facilities, at what cost. Like the blueprint for a house, except that geologists, engineers and finance people spend months calculating it. An agent is supposed to pull together the data from all these departments and write a first draft.
Open "Months to hours" is a claim by ADNOC, not a measurement. TNW itself writes: "The companies have not disclosed the value of the agreement or when the system will be rolled out."[1] The 40,000 users and 200 applications are company figures. I read only TNW, not ADNOC's announcement.

My take: What is interesting is not the number but the kind of work. A development plan is a large document that pulls together knowledge from many departments. Writing it does not take months. The months go into coordinating, running variants and reviewing. An agent can produce the first draft quickly. Whether the review gets faster is the real question. The same pattern exists in small form in the Mittelstand: bids on tenders. The idea below came from that.

Anthropic: What the filing means for AI customers Market B2B SaaS US

Anthropic has updated its IPO filing. CNBC reported the figures on Nov 2: "roughly $17 billion in revenue" in the third quarter of 2026 and a net loss of "$23.29 billion".[2] Over the first nine months, revenue was $33.24 billion, according to CNBC. On top of that come purchase commitments and leases of about $895.4 billion, according to the filing almost entirely for compute and infrastructure.[2] Growth over the prior quarter was about 47%, according to CNBC, after 144% in the second quarter.

S-1 (IPO filing): The document a company files with the US regulator SEC before going public. In it, it must disclose its numbers, including the uncomfortable ones. Like the disclosure when buying a house: the seller has to mention the damp basement.

My take: For companies that use Claude or another large model, the most important number is not revenue. It is the almost $900 billion in data center commitments. Someone has to pay for them, and in the end that means customers. Today's prices per token are not a law of nature. Anyone who builds a process firmly on one model should calculate costs with a buffer and have a plan B. Growth is also slowing, even if it stays high.

Source All figures come from the CNBC report. I have not read the SEC filing. According to CNBC, the loss is heavily shaped by valuation effects on convertible notes, not just by ongoing operations.

Bitkom: AI from Germany, but please not more expensive Study B2B SaaS DE

Bitkom published new figures on Nov 2, at the Digital Summit. "96 percent of companies would prefer to use AI applications from German providers."[3] For 87%, the country of origin matters in the selection. The catch follows immediately: "However, at 64 percent almost two thirds are not willing to accept compromises for it", for example higher prices or fewer features.[3]

Preferred origin of AI providers, share of companies, Bitkom, weeks 28–33/2026

The shift from the previous year is clear. France rises from 38 to 46%, other EU countries from 40 to 43%. The US falls: "Only 41 percent would still prefer AI from the United States", a year ago it was 51%. China falls from 18 to 12%.[3] 64% think Germany needs more hyperscalers.

Methodology Telephone survey by Bitkom Research, 603 companies with 20 or more employees, weeks 28 to 33/2026, representative according to Bitkom.[3] The question was about preferences, not purchases. Bitkom is an industry association whose members also sell AI.

My take: This is the most important number of the week for twiceD, and it is uncomfortable. "Sovereignty" is not a price argument. Almost everyone wants it, but two thirds will not pay a premium. An offer with a German or open model therefore wins only if it is equally good and not more expensive. Origin is a bonus, not a selling point on its own.

Citizens use AI against bureaucratese Public Consumer DE

A second Bitkom survey from Nov 4 concerns public administration. "At 11 percent, one in nine has already used artificial intelligence for this", meaning in dealings with government offices. Another 27% can imagine doing so.[4] For what? 35% of users look up their rights, 25% have letters from government offices explained, 19% have their own letters checked before sending.[4]

Openness is growing fast. 51% would rather talk to an AI assistant than to a person, to avoid waiting times and office hours. A year ago it was 43%. 44% believe AI helps against staff shortages in government offices (2025: 35%).[4]

Methodology Telephone survey of 1,004 people aged 18 and over, weeks 33 to 37/2026. Only 109 of them have used AI in dealings with government offices.[4] The percentages on purposes are therefore based on about 100 people.

My take: This is AI from the bottom up. Citizens do not wait for the government chatbot, they take ChatGPT and translate the notice themselves. For government offices that means: their letters are already being read by machines today. Anyone who writes clearly saves follow-up questions. The same goes for insurers and energy suppliers.

Study corner: lots of usage, little rebuilding Study Global

A fitting perspective alongside ADNOC. In June, Publicis Sapient surveyed 1,550 AI decision-makers in six countries. "73% of respondents say AI is used regularly or across most of their business processes." But: "only 10% describe AI as core to how their business actually operates."[6]

Methodology Publicis Sapient sells AI transformation as consulting, so the study supports its own market. Fieldwork by Protégé, Apr 29 to May 14, 2026. The article does not say who paid for it.[6] "Used regularly" is loosely defined. I read only MarketScale's report, not the study.

My take: The gap between 73 and 10 is the most honest number. Almost everyone uses AI for individual tasks. Hardly anyone has rebuilt workflows around it. ADNOC wants to take exactly this step. Whether it works, we will know at the earliest once a first plan has been reviewed.

AI Act: 23 days to go Reg EU

Nothing new since yesterday, just the countdown. According to Legal 500, the AI Omnibus package, as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, was "published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026" and "entered into force on 27 July 2026".[7] From Wed Dec 2, two new bans apply. On the same day, a transition period ends: generative AI systems already on the market before Aug 2, 2026 must then label their outputs in a machine-readable way (Art. 50(2), transitional rule in Art. 111(4)).[8] This comes from a tracker, not from the Official Journal text on EUR-Lex. The high-risk obligations under Annex III move to Dec 2, 2027, those under Annex I to Aug 2, 2028.[7]

For the Mittelstand: Anyone who only uses AI and does not offer it themselves has little to do on Dec 2. Anyone who already gave customers their own tool with generative AI before Aug 2, 2026 should check the labeling now.

Project ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Proposal workshop: tender responses built from a company's own past proposals

ELI5 & value: A Mittelstand company bids on public or private tenders. Each time, someone gathers old proposals, references, certificates and résumés and writes a new response from them. That is ADNOC's development plan in miniature: pulling knowledge from many corners into one large document. The workshop is like an assistant who knows all the old proposals. It lays out a first draft, with a source for every paragraph. Value: Sales saves days per proposal, and the company can answer more tenders instead of turning down good ones.
Prototype (V0): (1) Collect 20 to 50 of the client's old proposals, references and certificates and load them into a searchable archive. (2) Read in a new tender and pull the requirements out as a list, with knockout criteria. (3) For each requirement, search the archive for the matching text and write a draft, every paragraph with a source (RAG). (4) A review view: what is still missing, what has no evidence? MVP deliberately narrow: one client, one proposal type, no pricing section, a human writes the final version. Existing building blocks: a search archive like pgvector, a tool for reading PDFs, an open or European model.
Market & gap: The German market is split among many small tools. A comparison by KI-Syndikat (as of September/November 2026) lists, among others, VergabePilot (match alerts and "AI summary of the tender documents", €0 to €125 a month), TendiGo (up to €230 a month), Patterno ("flags knockout criteria with page reference") and tendric.ai ("extract requirements from extensive specifications").[11] The prices come from the vendors; the comparison tested nothing. These tools find and read tenders. According to the comparison, none of them handles the actual writing from the company's own knowledge. Internationally, AutogenAI does that, a platform for proposals and grant applications with "270+ companies" as customers. The success figures there are vendor claims. A focus on Germany is not visible on the site.[12] Gap for twiceD: the last mile. Setting up and maintaining the client's archive, in German, with data in Germany. But Bitkom also shows: origin alone does not carry a premium.[3] The price has to be measured against the time saved, not against the "sovereign" label.
Feasibility (solo + AI): Medium to high. The V0 is doable for Daniel in four to six weeks, the building blocks are ready. The work lies in tidying up the old proposals, and that is a one-time job per client. After that, roughly two to four hours per week and client for upkeep and new documents. With more than five clients, a contractor is needed for setup. The risk: if it turns into a SaaS for many, twiceD competes with the platforms above. So it is better sold as setup plus operation per client, on request in the client's own data center. ki-syndikat.de · autogenai.com · bitkom.org · thenextweb.com
