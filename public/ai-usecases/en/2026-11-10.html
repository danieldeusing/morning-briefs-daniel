Ramp: More companies pay, but they hardly use more Study B2B SaaS US

Ramp sells corporate credit cards and invoicing software. From its customers' payments, Ramp builds an AI index every month. The new reading: "In September 2026, AI adoption reached 55.9% of U.S. businesses on Ramp, up 0.6 percentage points from August."[1] Year over year, that is 12.1 points more. 43.8% of companies paid for Anthropic, 40.6% for OpenAI.[1]

The second half of the data is more interesting. TechCrunch analyzed token spending, meaning spending on the actual use of the models. In July it rose 42%. After that: "token spend only grew 1.5% across August and September".[2] From Sep 23 to Oct 23, another 8.9% came on top. In token spending, Anthropic is far ahead: "Anthropic captured the largest share of AI token spending among businesses on Ramp, at 60.5%."[1] OpenAI has 35.1%.

The per-head sums are small. "Median firms spend $12-$15 per employee per month", writes TechCrunch. The top 10% pay $600 to $1,000, the top 1% just under $10,000.[2]

Token spending: A subscription like ChatGPT Team costs a fixed amount per head. Someone who builds a model into their own software through the API instead pays per piece of text the model reads or writes. These pieces are called tokens. It is like electricity: the base fee says who is connected. The meter says how much is really running.
Methodology The basis is more than 85,000 US companies that use Ramp cards or invoicing software. A user is anyone with at least one payment to an AI product. Ramp itself writes: "It's likely our results underestimate actual AI adoption and spend", because free tools and personal accounts are missing.[1] Ramp's customers are not a random sample, and Ramp itself sells spend-management software. According to TechCrunch, part of the dip comes from price cuts at Anthropic. The article puts that at roughly $600 million less revenue growth. That is a second-hand estimate.[2]

My take: More than every second company is connected, but the meter is turning more slowly. That fits yesterday's study: lots of usage, little restructuring. For consultants this is good news. The tool is not what is missing; what is missing is someone to move a process onto it. Claude Fable 5.2 and its pricing are covered in the ai-dev brief.

CoCounsel: A legal agent for Brazilian law firms Deploy Legal BR

Thomson Reuters is bringing its CoCounsel Legal tool to Brazil, in Portuguese. Brazil is the first market in Latin America. The first law firms have been testing it since the Fenalaw trade fair in São Paulo (Oct 27 to 29). According to Exame, the general launch is planned for November, with no exact date.[3] The idea: "o advogado descreve o caso em linguagem comum", the lawyer describes the case in plain language. The system then plans the research itself and writes drafts of legal briefs.[3]

The performance data all comes from Thomson Reuters: a table analysis across up to 10,000 documents with up to 100 questions at once, an ISO/IEC 42001 certification, one million users in 107 countries. $40 million is said to have gone into a proprietary model called "Thomson". According to Exame, the global version now runs on Anthropic's Claude Agent SDK. The company does not name a price.[3]

Table analysis across documents: You load in 500 contracts and ask 20 questions, for example: when does the contract end? The system fills a table, one row per contract, one column per question. In the past, junior lawyers spent weeks on the pile.
Vendor claims All figures are self-reported, not independent measurement. The Exame page shows no fixed date, only "three days ago", so Nov 6, 2026. Responsibility clearly stays with the human. A Thomson Reuters manager says: "É o advogado quem assina", the lawyer signs.[3]

My take: A global vendor is building a Portuguese version with local law, instead of just translating the interface. Brazil has many court cases and a lot of paper, so this pays off here. A gap remains for small law firms: contract review in a narrow specialty can also be built by a small vendor.

Telekom: Munich AI factory full, now twice as big Deploy Mfg DE

Telekom runs an "Industrial AI Cloud" at Tucherpark in Munich, a data center just for AI. According to heise, demand is higher than planned: "Statt zu Beginn 10.000 Blackwell-GPUs von Nvidia sollen in Kürze 20.000" run, that is, instead of 10,000 Nvidia Blackwell GPUs at the start, 20,000 are to run soon. The reason: "Nach sieben Monaten war alle dann installierte Rechenleistung belegt", after seven months all the computing power installed by then was in use.[4] As users, heise names Siemens and Daimler Truck.

A much larger EU "gigafactory" remains open. Telekom hesitates because of one rule: an operator would have to "60 Prozent der Rechenleistung einer Gigafactory für die öffentliche Hand vorhalten", reserve 60 percent of a gigafactory's computing power for the public sector. According to heise, Telekom fears high costs for machines that then sit idle. There is no decision.[4]

GPU: A graphics chip. It computes very many small tasks at the same time, which is exactly what AI needs. Anyone without their own room full of chips rents computing time, like a hotel kitchen instead of your own.

My take: This is the counter-check to the Bitkom survey from yesterday. There, almost all companies wanted AI from Germany, but two thirds did not want to pay more for it. Here, large industrial customers pay for German computing power, and the capacity is gone. The difference: Siemens and Daimler Truck have data that must not leave the country, and their own teams for training. For mid-sized companies the question remains: who builds an application out of rented computing time?

Vale: AI turns SAP workshops into requirements Deploy Mfg BR

The mining group Vale is moving its ERP to SAP S/4HANA. The project is called "Neo Vale". According to Exame, the system has "mais de 30 mil usuários", meaning more than 30,000 users, and more than 350 connected systems.[5] Accenture uses its own AI tool here. It analyzes the workshops with the business departments and turns them into requirements, specifications and code. More than 270 requirements were analyzed this way. The definition was finished "até três semanas", up to three weeks, earlier.[5]

According to Exame, the project is on schedule and on budget. Neither Vale nor Accenture says what it costs or how much the AI saves. Accenture says it uses the tool at around 40 clients in Latin America.[5]

SAP S/4HANA and ECC: SAP is the software many large and mid-sized companies use to run purchasing, warehouse, accounting and HR. ECC is the old version, S/4HANA the new one. SAP maintains ECC as standard "until the end of 2027". After that there is an extension to the end of 2030, for "a premium of two percentage points" on the maintenance fee.[6] So many companies will have to switch soon. The most expensive part is not the technology but the question: what exactly does each department need?

My take: One of the best user stories of the week, because it is boring. No agent doing everything alone: the AI listens and writes a structured record. Three weeks sounds small, but an SAP project has many expensive consultants. Mid-sized companies face the same 2027 deadline, without an Accenture budget. The idea below grew out of that.

Grok calls the cable provider, the customer is liable Consumer US

xAI offers an agent in its subscription from $30 a month that makes phone calls for the user. PYMNTS describes a test: the agent called Comcast for about 25 minutes. The bill dropped from $84 to $45 a month.[7] The catch is in the terms of use. According to PYMNTS, the customer is "the caller of record and solely responsible", so solely responsible for what the agent says.[7]

There are also limits. On Oct 31 and Nov 1, usage meters jumped from 50 to 100% of the quota, according to the report. Musk is said to have said the cost per call is too high for the revenue. And some companies simply hang up on AI callers. So far this applies only to US numbers.[7]

My take: This is a single story, not a measurement. But the pattern matters: anyone who runs a hotline will soon get calls from machines that negotiate hard and never get tired. Companies with customer phone lines should work out how to recognize an AI caller and what their staff may promise it.

Airbnb and Meta: Agents for travelers and small businesses Deploy Consumer US

Airbnb wants to launch an AI travel agent in the first half of 2027. It is meant to answer questions, handle support tasks and build its own interface: it "draws from a library of hundreds of travel-specific interface components".[8] The backdrop is good: revenue rose 19% in the third quarter to $4.87 billion. "Q3 was our fastest revenue growth in more than three years."[8] Airbnb does not say how much support the AI already handles today.

Meta is building a unit to sell AI agents to small businesses, according to The Information, led by former MongoDB chief CJ Desai. Large retailers were reluctant.[9]

My take: At Airbnb the interface is what counts. The agent assembles ready-made, vetted building blocks instead of free text. A good pattern for any agent facing customers.

AI Act: 22 days left, and one clarification Reg EU

The dates stand as follows, according to the law firm Garrigues: the AI Omnibus package was "published today, July 24, in the Official Journal" and entered into force on Jul 27, 2026.[10] The transparency obligations under Art. 50 have applied since Aug 2, 2026. For systems that were on the market before, labeling has "a transitional period... until December 2, 2026". On the same day two new bans arrive: AI for intimate images without consent and for depictions of child abuse.[10] High-risk AI under Annex III follows on Dec 2, 2027, under Annex I on Aug 2, 2028.

Note All dates come from a law firm and other secondary sources, not from the Official Journal on EUR-Lex. The sources agree: new generative systems placed on the market after Aug 2, 2026 get no grace period for machine-readable labeling (Art. 50 para. 2). They must label immediately. The grace period until Dec 2, 2026 covers only systems already on the market before Aug 2, 2026. On its voluntary code of practice for labeling, the EU Commission only writes that it "will apply from 2 August 2026". The page does not mention Dec 2. That is silence, not a contradiction.[11]

For mid-sized companies: Anyone who gives their own generative AI to customers should not count on the grace period. The safe assumption: label now. The code describes two layers, metadata in the file and a watermark.[11]

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · Requirements workshop tool for mid-sized companies moving to new SAP

ELI5 & Value: Before every SAP migration, consultants sit for weeks with purchasing, warehouse and accounting. They ask: how do you work today, what do you need tomorrow? In the end there should be a list of requirements. Today someone writes it from notes, and a lot gets lost. The workshop tool is a note-taker that listens and links every requirement to the spot in the recording. That way you can check later who said what. Value: Vale reports a definition up to three weeks faster.[5] The bigger gain is less arguing: "We never said that" can be checked with one click.
Prototype (V0): (1) Convert workshop recordings to text locally, with an open speech model like Whisper. (2) Pull a list of requirements per workshop, each with timestamp, speaker role and department. (3) Flag duplicate and contradictory requirements across all workshops. (4) Export as a table or into the consultant's tool, for example Jira. MVP deliberately narrow: German, one module (such as purchasing), no code and no SAP configuration, a human confirms every requirement. Existing building blocks: Whisper for speech, an open or European model for extraction, a simple review interface.
Market & Gap: SAP itself sets the pressure. Regular maintenance for the old suite runs "until the end of 2027", after that the extension to 2030 costs "a premium of two percentage points".[6] Accenture has its own tool for exactly this step and uses it at around 40 clients in Latin America, so at large corporations.[5] SAP offers consultants "Joule for Consultants". According to SAP, this is an assistant where "consultants can ask targeted questions and receive contextual, SAP-specific answers". It knows SAP's knowledge, not the client's knowledge from the workshop. SAP reports up to 14% faster delivery from pilot projects, a vendor claim.[16] A search for a tool specifically for workshop recordings in the mid-sized SAP market found no clear vendor. That does not prove none exists. Gap for twiceD: small SAP partners and mid-sized companies without Accenture. When employees are recorded in Germany, the works council often has a say. A solution in the company's own data center is a real argument here, not just a label.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): Medium to high. The V0 is doable solo in four to six weeks, all building blocks are ready. The real work is the quality of extraction in German, with technical jargon and dialect. Daniel is not an SAP consultant. So the best route is a partner: a small SAP service provider who uses the tool in their projects. Operation per project is about two to three hours a week, and a project lasts months. Maintenance is low if it runs locally at the partner. The time window is limited: after 2030 at the latest, demand drops. But the pattern fits any large software migration. exame.com · news.sap.com (maintenance) · news.sap.com (Joule)
