Mercedes-Benz: A robot sorts brackets, 96% correct Deploy Mfg DE

Google DeepMind has unveiled Gemini Robotics 2.5, its strongest model for robots so far. The real-world test ran at Mercedes-Benz. Humanoid Apollo-2 robots from Apptronik pre-sorted metal vehicle brackets there for assembly.[1] Heise writes: "According to Google DeepMind, the pilot trial achieved a success rate of 96 percent."[1]

The other figures also come from the vendor. More than 200 tasks were tested. Compared with the previous version, the model is said to achieve "a 30 to 100 percent higher success rate, depending on the task type".[1] It works 20 to 30% faster but remains slower than a human. DeepMind's head of robotics, Kanishka Rao, calls the path from 96% to human reliability a "steep path".[1]

VLA model (Vision-Language-Action): A model that sees a camera image, understands an instruction in words, and turns that into movements for arms and hands. Like an apprentice you tell "put the brackets in the crate": he looks, understands, and grabs them, without anyone programming every single move.
Source situation All figures come from Google DeepMind itself, as reported by heise (heise+, visible text read). It is a pilot, not series production. The article does not name the plant. We found no statement from Mercedes-Benz itself. How many parts per hour the robot manages is stated nowhere.

My take: 96% sounds high, but on the line it is low. Out of 100 grips, four go wrong, and someone has to find them. A human is also faster. The task itself is what is interesting: picking is monotonous, poorly paid, and hard to staff. That fits the demographics figure from Nahles further below. For mid-sized companies this is not yet something to buy. But it is a sign of where robotics is heading: less programming, more instructing.

Anthropic rebuilds Excel, sort of Deploy B2B SaaS Global

Anthropic is extending Claude with office tools. Claude Sheets is a spreadsheet: you describe in words what you want, and Claude builds tables with formulas and multiple sheets.[2] Claude Whiteboards collects a team's ideas and arranges them into flow diagrams. "Both launch as a beta for paying users."[2] The analysis tool from October leaves beta and is now called Claude Data. Claude Meetings launches as a beta for business customers and at first works only with Google Meet.

Heise states the goal openly: "In the long run, Claude is meant to become the central user interface for everyday office work."[2] According to the article, Microsoft's stock fell on the day of the announcement. The product manager himself tempers expectations: Claude Sheets is "still quite simple", and for complex financial models the established programs are better.[2] The article names neither prices nor countries.

Connector: A bridge between Claude and another program, for example accounting software or Adobe. Like a plug adapter when traveling: it does nothing by itself, but without it nothing fits together. According to heise there are more than 9,000 of them, and only some already open directly inside Claude.[2]

My take: The question for a company is not whether Claude Sheets is better than Excel. It is not, as Anthropic itself says. The question is where work will begin in the future: in the chat window or in the program. Whoever pulls their data into Claude through connectors hands Anthropic the workplace. For mid-sized companies this means: settle the rules for data access beforehand, not after the first month.

McKinsey: Lots of agents, little profit Study Global

McKinsey has published its "State of AI 2026" report, titled "On the road to ROI". The core figure is sobering. According to McKinsey partner Dan Tinkoff, "only 37% of organizations report any positive EBIT contribution, essentially flat compared with last year".[3] The group of real winners, the "high performers", stays at about 6%.[3]

With agents, by contrast, a lot is moving. Among companies with more than $1 billion in revenue, 40% are scaling agents, up from 27% the year before. Among smaller companies the figure is 22%.[3][4] 39% expect AI to shrink their workforce, versus 32% the year before. 43% expect no change.[3]

McKinsey State of AI 2026, share of companies in %, per diginomica
EBIT: Earnings before interest and taxes, that is, what the business itself earns. Like a vegetable stand: revenue minus purchasing, rent, and wages, before the bank and the tax office come. "Positive EBIT contribution" means: AI measurably increased that amount.
Methodology The McKinsey page was unreachable at retrieval. The figures come from diginomica's report of Nov 4 and a search snippet from mckinsey.com for the 27 → 40%. We do not know the sample or the questions. "Any positive EBIT contribution" means: any contribution, however small. So the 37% is an upper bound, not a success rate.

For comparison, three other surveys. Zapier reports that 88% of business leaders use or test agents, up from 72% in 2025 (513 respondents, September 2026).[5] That is a vendor survey, and we saw only the search snippet. Deloitte counts 23% who deploy agents "at least moderately", among more than 3,000 respondents. That too is only secondhand.[6]

My take: 88%, 40%, 23%, 37%: the figures do not contradict each other, they measure different things. "Testing" is cheap, "scaling" is hard, and "profit" is hardest. The gap between agent euphoria and flat profit is the most honest signal of the year. Anyone selling AI will have to show where the money lands from now on. That applies to twiceD as well.

Nahles: 800,000 jobs gone, a similar number new Study Public DE

Andrea Nahles, head of the Federal Employment Agency, gave Handelsblatt a forecast. Over the next 15 years, "around 800,000 jobs in Germany would disappear because of AI, but a similar number would also be created".[7] Business service providers and analytical tasks at insurers and banks would be hit hardest. The skilled trades are more robust because AI replaces them less easily.[7] At the same time: "Because of demographics, Germany loses a net of around 500,000 workers per year."[7]

Methodology The article names no study behind the number. It is a forecast by the agency, reproduced in an interview (dpa via heise). Whether "disappear" means "position cut" or "position not refilled" remains open.

My take: The two numbers belong together. 800,000 jobs in 15 years is on average about 53,000 per year. Demographics takes away around 500,000 workers every year. Germany's problem is therefore more that people are missing, not that AI displaces them. For mid-sized companies this means: AI is less a cost-cutting program than a replacement for positions that stay empty anyway. That is how it should be sold.

ChatGPT becomes a business directory Deploy Consumer US

OpenAI lets businesses create their own profile in ChatGPT, similar to Google Maps. Businesses confirm they are the owners and enter phone, website, and opening hours. They can also list products with price and availability.[8] If ChatGPT names a business in a conversation, the user reaches its page via a link and can chat with a "Business Agent". It answers using the company's details.

In a "Business Hub", the business sees on which topics ChatGPT suggests it. According to OpenAI, "these are only anonymous statistics".[8] The limits: "The Business Agents feature is available in beta for selected companies in the US."[8] According to heise, the profile itself can apparently already be claimed anywhere. The article does not explain cost or ranking.

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization): SEO for chatbots. Before, a company wanted to rank at the top of Google. Now it wants ChatGPT to name it when someone asks "who repairs heating systems around here?". Like the difference between a phone-book listing and a neighbor's recommendation.

My take: For small businesses, this matters more than any new model. When customers ask chatbots instead of search engines, ChatGPT decides who is even considered. And whoever maintains no profile still gets talked about by the chatbot, only with old or wrong data. The idea below builds on that.

DeepL Aure: translates in your own voice Deploy B2B SaaS DE

DeepL has unveiled the speech model Aure. It translates spoken language while keeping the speaker's voice, tone, and emphasis. To do this, "Aure continuously analyzes the audio source" instead of taking a voice sample only at the start.[9] "Aure can initially speak English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese, and Korean."[9] It is available "starting now" in online meetings, for in-person conversations, and via the DeepL API.

My take: For Daniel, with clients in Germany and Brazil, this is directly useful, because German and Portuguese are both included. The article names no customers, prices, or error rates, though. So test internally before a client conversation.

AI Act: 20 days to go, and the GDPR waits Reg EU

As a reminder: the AI omnibus package was published in the Official Journal on 2026-07-24 and entered into force on 2026-07-27.[10] High-risk AI under Annex III thus moved "from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027", with Annex I following on 2028-08-02.[10] On 2026-12-02, the labeling grace period ends for systems that were already on the market before 2026-08-02 (details in the Nov 10 brief). Heise also confirms: the ban on "nudify apps" applies from the end of 2026 (I could not confirm the exact date).[11]

The second omnibus, which is meant to amend the GDPR, is not as far along. According to heise, the Council has adopted changes, and Parliament is not yet negotiating the data protection part. "The trilogue with the Council and Commission is likely to begin only in 2027."[11]

Note The AI Act dates come from law firms and heise, not from the Official Journal on EUR-Lex. The exact date for the new bans (2026-12-02) and the regulation number were not confirmed from a primary source today.

For mid-sized companies: With the AI Act, the dates are clear; with the GDPR, the old law stays in place until 2027 at least. Anyone who wants to train AI on customer data should plan with today's rules.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · AI Visibility Check: What does ChatGPT say about my company?

ELI5 & Value: More and more customers ask a chatbot instead of Google: "Which business nearby supplies stainless steel cuts?" The chatbot names three companies. Whoever is not among them loses the order without noticing. Whoever is among them may have old prices or wrong opening hours attributed to them. The check is like a mystery shop: someone asks the chatbot the questions real customers ask and writes down what comes out. Then what is wrong gets fixed. Since yesterday this has a lever: ChatGPT lets businesses maintain their own profile with products and prices.[8]
Prototype (V0): (1) Collect 30 to 50 typical customer questions with the client, in German and regional. (2) Put the questions regularly to ChatGPT, Gemini, and Copilot through an existing measurement tool like Peec AI, instead of building a measurement service yourself.[17] (3) Results report: Is the company named? Next to whom? Are prices, address, and offering correct? (4) Repair: claim and fill the profile in ChatGPT, structured data on the website, keep the product list current.[8] (5) Measure again after four weeks. MVP deliberately narrow: one client, one region, one product group. No software product of its own, no paid chatbot ads.
Market & Gap: The market is there and well funded. According to SiliconANGLE, Profound raised in September "$180 million in funding at a $1.8 billion valuation" and targets large brands.[16] Berlin-based Peec AI closed a $21 million Series A in November 2025 and reports more than 2,500 customers, including TUI and Hugo Boss (company statement, single source).[17] According to third-party listings, Peec starts at around $95 per month, not confirmed by Peec itself.[17] These tools measure. They do not repair: the customer has to fix profile, website, and product data on their own. That is exactly what a trades business or a supplier with 40 people cannot do. It has no marketing department that reads measurement reports. We did not search today for a German agency offering exactly this fixed-price check for small B2B companies. That is the next research step. Gap for twiceD: measuring plus repairing as one package, in German, at a fixed price, for companies that do not pay an ad agency.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): High. The measurement tool is bought in, twiceD does questions, analysis, and repair. One check takes about two to four days solo. After that, monthly re-measurement is possible, with a few hours per client. Maintenance: low, because no software of its own runs. Scaling through a template and a contractor. Two risks open: Business Agents exist only in the US so far,[8] and chatbot answers fluctuate from day to day. The check must not promise a place in the answers, only clean data and an honest measurement. heise.de (ChatGPT profiles) · siliconangle.com (Profound) · everything-pr.com (Peec AI)
