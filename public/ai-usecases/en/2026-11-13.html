Santander: €194 million from AI, says Santander Deploy Finance EU

Spanish banking giant Santander released its third-quarter results on Oct 28. They contain a sentence rarely read at banks. The bank says it deploys AI "at scale" across its businesses: "This generated €194 million of business value".[1] According to the release, this means higher revenue, lower costs and lower provisions for loan losses. The routes there: better customer service, more productivity, more business.[1]

AI in service, sales and lending→more revenue + lower costs + fewer loan losses→€194 million "business value"
Provision for loan losses: Money a bank sets aside because it expects some loans not to be repaid. Like a landlord who saves a little each month because now and then a tenant doesn't pay. If an AI spots bad loans earlier, the bank has to set aside less, and that counts as profit.
Methodology The figure is the bank's own claim, unaudited. How Santander calculates it is not in the release. There is no comparison value without AI. The period is only given as "during the year". The release covers the first nine months of 2026, but that is our reading. Figures on a target for the coming years appear only in secondary sources that we did not open.

My take: €194 million is not much for a bank of this size. More important is that Santander names a euro figure with a definition at all: three levers, one amount. That is the pattern others will be measured against. The weakness is just as clear: without the calculation method, nobody can check whether the same euros would have come without AI. Still, it is a better start than "80% feel more productive".

NAB: "Ruby" for 40,000 people, numbers without a baseline Deploy Finance AU

National Australia Bank (NAB) presented an internal AI assistant with its annual results in early November. It is called Ruby and runs on Anthropic's platform. "Over 40,000 of our colleagues will have access to Ruby," itnews quotes the bank.[2] In addition, about 50 teams from the business units are working on AI workflows with partners such as Anthropic and AWS.[2]

The bank names two effects: "a 20 percent reduction in the time needed to approve business loans" and 18% faster delivery of changes.[2] According to itnews, the loan figure is tied to the AI agents and teams, not to Ruby itself. Over the same period, NAB plans, per itnews, "a planned reduction of about 2500 staff".[2][3]

Squad (business team): A small, mixed team that owns a problem from start to finish, for example "make business loans faster". Like a soccer team for exactly one game: business experts, developers and data people together, instead of everyone waiting in their own department.
Methodology Both percentages are self-reported with no baseline. 20% faster means a lot if a loan used to take four weeks, and little if it took two days. According to our research, the source draws no connection between the job cuts and Ruby. Second source: letsdatascience.com, a news aggregator.[3]

My take: NAB shows how it usually goes. Many people get access, and the success figure comes from another, smaller project. Access is easy to count, the effect is not. Anyone who promises a client "percent faster" should first measure the before number. Otherwise nobody will believe you later.

The counter-check: little profit, high costs Study Global DE

Two surveys put the bank figures in context. In "State of AI 2026," McKinsey reports that "only 37% of organizations report any positive EBIT contribution, essentially flat compared with last year".[4] We covered the report in detail yesterday. New on top: 80% of respondents say AI has improved their own productivity.[4] So the benefit feels big, but is small on the company balance sheet.

In Germany, according to a report by drweb.de, Bitkom counts 41% of companies with 20 or more employees that use AI. Last year it was 17%.[5] The flip side: "33 Prozent der KI-einsetzenden Unternehmen sagen, dass KI teurer ist als anfangs angenommen" (33% of AI-using companies say AI is more expensive than first assumed).[5] And "19 Prozent der KI-einsetzenden Unternehmen haben bereits Stellen abgebaut" (19% of AI-using companies have already cut jobs).[5] (According to drweb; the base is not clear in the Bitkom release.) 604 companies were surveyed by phone in early 2026.

Methodology McKinsey: read only via diginomica, the McKinsey site was unreachable. Sample and period are unconfirmed. Bitkom: the study is from March 2026 (Bitkom release 11.03.2026; drweb reported it in May), not new. The Bitkom release confirms 41% (up from 17% a year earlier) and 604 companies; the 33% and 19% come via drweb. The jump from 17% to 41% in one year is large. We could not check whether the question stayed the same. According to drweb, the 33% refers to companies that already use AI; for the 19% (job cuts due to AI), the base is not clear in the Bitkom release.

My take: Together with Santander, a clear picture emerges. Usage is growing fast. Costs surprise every third company. And hardly anyone can prove the profit. The problem is not the technology but the accounting of the benefit. Whoever solves that has a selling point. The idea below builds on this.

AI Act: 19 days left Reg EU

On Dec 2, 2026, a grace period ends. Generative AI systems that were already on the market before Aug 2, 2026 must then label their outputs in a machine-readable way. This is governed by Art. 50(2) of the AI Act. The law firm Cuatrecasas gives a transition period "until December 2, 2026".[6] The related omnibus package is Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, published in the Official Journal on Jul 24, 2026.[6]

The high-risk rules come later. For standalone systems under Annex III, Cuatrecasas says "December 2, 2027" applies, and for AI in products under Annex I, Aug 2, 2028.[6] Pinsent Masons confirmed the framework back in May: high-risk rules "will take effect from late 2027".[7] The omnibus also adds a ban on apps that artificially undress people in photos.[6]

Machine-readable labeling: An invisible label in an AI image, text or audio that a program can read out. Like the watermark in a banknote: you can barely see it with the naked eye, but a checking device immediately recognizes where the note comes from.
Note All dates come from law firms, not directly from EUR-Lex. The entry-into-force date (Jul 27, 2026) is calculated: three days after publication.

For mid-sized firms: This mainly affects providers of AI systems, not every company that uses ChatGPT. But anyone who builds their own tool that generates images or text should check now whether it can already do the labeling.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · AI-benefit audit: the euro amount per use case

ELI5 & value: Many mid-sized firms pay for Copilot, ChatGPT or an agent. When the boss asks "What do we get for it?", there is usually only a feeling. The audit does what Santander demonstrates publicly: for each AI deployment, an amount is calculated, split into more revenue, lower costs and lower risk.[1] Like a household budget book for AI. Value: the company sees which subscriptions pay off and which do not. That matters when every third company finds AI more expensive than expected.[5]
Prototype (V0): (1) Pick three to five AI deployments with the client, for example proposal texts or invoice checking. (2) For each deployment, measure the before number: duration, errors, count per week. This is exactly what is missing at NAB.[2] (3) Collect the costs: licenses, token bills, onboarding hours. (4) After six to eight weeks, re-measure and convert to euros, with an open calculation formula. (5) A two-page report for management. MVP deliberately narrow: one spreadsheet and one report, no custom software, no permanent dashboard. Existing building blocks: the vendors' usage reports and a spreadsheet.
Market & gap: Measurement tools exist, but they target large enterprises. Worklytics connects data from "Slack, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini, Zoom and more" and mainly measures how many employees use the tools.[10] Larridin promises to connect usage, spend and "business outcomes".[11] Larridin names further vendors such as Ascerta, Jellyfish and GetDX, the latter two focused on software teams.[11] Neither side lists prices. We found no reliable market-size figure today. The gap: these tools measure usage. The customer has to calculate the euro amount themselves, and a company with 80 people lacks the controlling department for that. Gap for twiceD: measuring before and after, plus the euro calculation, as a fixed-price package in German. We did not search today for a German agency with exactly this offer. That is the next research step.
Feasibility (solo + AI): High. It is consulting with a clear end, not ongoing software. One audit takes about three to five working days solo, spread over two months. Maintenance: almost zero. Quarterly re-measuring is a few hours per client. Scaling via a template and a contractor. The honest risk: the results can be bad. The report must then also say "this subscription is not worth it". That is exactly what makes it credible, but it is awkward if twiceD also advises the same company on adopting AI. santander.com · worklytics.co · larridin.com
