PwC: German Mid-Sized Firms Have a Navigation Problem Study DE B2B

On Nov 17, 2026, PwC published a study titled "From Pilot to Value Creation." According to the press release, it surveyed 400 mid-sized companies with more than €50 million in annual revenue.[1] The result is sobering. Almost 70% see no impact of AI on their costs over the past year. 87% see no clear effect on revenue, and 83% no effect on employees.[1]

PwC partner Nico Reichen sums it up: "So when it comes to AI, the Mittelstand has not an investment problem but a navigation problem."[1] According to the study page, the main gap is integration into internal processes; in finance and controlling, much potential remains untapped.

Methodology The PwC pages returned an error (403) twice. All numbers come from search snippets, not from the report. Even the sample is unclear: the press release says 400 companies, the methodology page says 400 people, mostly from finance departments and senior management. These are self-reports. PwC itself sells AI consulting for the Mittelstand; a "navigation problem" is also a sales pitch. We treat the numbers as a direction, not a measurement.

For comparison, the figure we read at the source: according to Bitkom, 57% of companies with 20 or more employees use AI, up from 36% the year before and 20% two years earlier. Bitkom surveyed 603 companies by phone, in calendar weeks 28 to 33.[2]

"Using" versus "impact": Bitkom asks whether a company uses AI. A chat login for a few people is enough for that. PwC asks whether costs or revenue have moved. It is like the difference between "we have a gym membership" and "we lost weight." Both numbers can be true.

My take: I believe the direction, even without having read the report. It fits what we have seen for weeks: many pilots, little production use. But "sees no effect" often means "did not measure." Anyone who took no baseline number before the pilot cannot see an effect afterward, even if there is one. That is the real gap, and it is small enough for a small service provider: not an AI strategy for the whole house, but one workflow with a number before and a number after.

AI Act: 9 Days to Go, and a Dispute Over the Date Reg EU

The AI Omnibus package appeared in the Official Journal on Jul 24, 2026, and has applied since Jul 27, 2026.[3] On Wed Dec 2, 2026, two things happen. First, the transition period for machine-readable labeling of AI content in older systems ends, according to CMS "until 2 December 2026". Second, new bans apply: "This ban is set to take effect from 2 December 2026."[3] The high-risk rules come later: Dec 2, 2027 for standalone systems, Aug 2, 2028 for AI in regulated products.[3]

Contradiction According to our research, an overview table from the law firm DLA Piper gives Dec 2, 2027 for labeling under Art. 50(2) in legacy systems. We have not seen the table ourselves. CMS, Cuatrecasas and Pinsent Masons give Dec 2, 2026; yesterday we read the same date at Garrigues and Lewis Silkin. We assume 2026. Anyone who offers a product with AI text or AI images should check the date against the statute with their law firm. This is not legal advice.

My take: A one-year difference sounds like a lawyers' detail. For a software company with a text feature in its own product, it decides whether the project comes now or in 2027. Anyone who relies on the later date and is wrong has no label from Dec 2. The cheap safeguard is an inventory now.

Defense: France Plans an AI Center, the UN No Ban Policy Public EU

On Nov 12, 2026, France announced a center for AI resilience in defense. It is to be built in Rennes, start in 2027 and seek NATO recognition. The ministry did not say which states take part; it "could not, at present, reveal the list of partner nations".[4] On Nov 21, heise ran the headline "Five EU states want to found a center for AI in defense."[5] We do not know whether this is the same center; we have not read the article.

The same day, heise reported: "Killer robots: UN conference ends without mandate for a binding ban."[5] We know this only as a headline, too. For background: in September, according to Human Rights Watch, 76 states supported negotiations on a treaty; the review conference in November was to decide on the mandate.[6]

Autonomous weapons (LAWS): Weapon systems that select and attack a target without human approval. The question at the UN is whether a human must always press the final button. Without a mandate, there are no negotiations on a binding treaty.

My take: Not directly relevant for twiceD, but relevant for Europe's AI market. Defense will become a big customer for AI, with its own rules and its own buyers. Small providers working in supply chains should know that "dual use" will soon appear more often in contracts.

Three patterns. First: "We use AI" is the majority; "AI moves our numbers" is not, if PwC is even roughly right. Second: the calendar drives more than the technology. Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · The Impact Proof: One Finance Workflow, One Number Before, One Number After

ELI5 & Value: According to PwC, the large majority of mid-sized companies see no cost effect from AI, and finance and controlling are considered untapped.[1] The impact proof takes exactly one workflow, for example incoming invoices. It first measures how long an invoice takes today and how often it is corrected. Then it introduces a ready-made AI solution and measures again after six to eight weeks. Like a doctor who takes blood pressure before treatment so that afterward they can say whether the pill works. Value: management gets a euro figure it can use to decide on the next workflow, instead of yet another pilot report with no result.
Prototype (V0): (1) Baseline measurement: time per document, number of corrections and lead time to approval, from a sample of 100–200 real invoices. (2) Selection and setup of an existing solution for pre-coding, for example Candis with DATEV handover; no custom text recognition. (3) Wait out a learning phase of about 30 days, which the vendor itself states.[8] (4) Second measurement with the same method, plus a two-page report: hours saved, error rate, license cost, result in euros per month. MVP deliberately narrow: one workflow, one company, no strategy workshop, no custom software.
Market & Gap: The tools exist. Candis advertises "up to 91% correctly captured information" and a learning phase of "about 30 days," both vendor claims.[8] Consulting exists too: PwC offers an "AI Kickstart Workshop" for the Mittelstand on its website, and on Jan 1, 2027 the government launches a network of 17 Mittelstand-Digital Zentren, "free of charge and vendor-neutral."[9] The centers advise and train, but they do not set up a solution in operations and do not measure afterward. The software vendors measure their own accuracy, not the euro impact at the customer. Gap for twiceD: set up and measure neutrally, at a fixed price, with a result that is allowed to be "not worth it." Honestly open: we found no well-documented market size for such proofs, and we have not read the PwC numbers at the source.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): High. The measurement template, sampling script and report can be built solo in about a week. One project needs roughly 3–4 days of work, spread over eight to ten weeks because the learning phase has to run. Ongoing load after the project: none, the vendor operates the solution. Scaling: one contractor per parallel project; the second workflow at the same customer (for example dunning) is the natural follow-up job. Honest catch: the free centers create price pressure; twiceD must make clear that it sets up and measures, not just advises. A large team is not needed. candis.io · datev.de · pwc.de
