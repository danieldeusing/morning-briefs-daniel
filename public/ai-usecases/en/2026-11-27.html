SAP: "AI first", 10,000 fewer jobs Work B2B SaaS DE

"SAP is working out plans to cut at least 10,000 employees," writes Daniel AJ Sokolov at heise on Thu 11/26/2026 at 9:05 pm.[1] The source is Bloomberg: "Bloomberg reports this, citing insiders who cannot be named."[1] The reason is already in the headline: "AI first". The sentence that goes with it: "AI is meant to make at least 10,000 redundant."[1]

The scale. "10,000 employees would be nearly a tenth of the workforce of 110,730 worldwide (as of the end of September)."[1] According to heise's Friday daily roundup, "all SAP departments have been tasked with stating where they can cut jobs." Management wants to "fundamentally change how SAP programs and sells software."[2]

Who is hit. "Accordingly, jobs are to be cut especially in programming, product support and sales."[1] These are the three fields where AI tools promise the most today.

What SAP says. Almost nothing. "SAP did not deny the plans to Bloomberg" and spoke of "continuous reviews of the organization."[1] SAP gives no figure.

Source status According to Bloomberg/heise, unconfirmed. We did not read the Bloomberg text itself, and we found no second independent source. The figure of 10,000 comes from unnamed insiders. SAP has neither confirmed nor denied it.

The comparison with 2024. "SAP already cut 10,000 jobs in 2024, mostly through voluntary departures." Then came the counter-movement: "The company has since added about 15,000 jobs again."[1] On balance, SAP therefore employed more people after the last restructuring than before.

The German part. "A German works agreement on job security at SAP expires at the end of the year."[1] In September, SAP concluded a new agreement with the works council of the German parent company. The union IG Metall criticizes this agreement. heise writes: "An explicit exclusion of dismissals for operational reasons is still missing." And: "The agreement mainly regulates the amount of severance pay."[1]

Works agreement (Betriebsvereinbarung): A contract between the company and the works council (the elected employee body) that applies to all employees at the site. Like house rules that the landlord and the tenants' representatives sign together: they apply to every apartment, not just to the signatories.
Dismissal for operational reasons (betriebsbedingte Kündigung): The company dismisses someone because it no longer needs the position, not because of the person. Like a restaurant that cancels the lunch shift: the cook did nothing wrong, the shift simply no longer exists.

My view: This is the first big German company where "AI" is reportedly named directly as the reason for a cut of this size. I would still be careful. In 2024 the number was also 10,000, and two years later 15,000 new people were on board. "AI first" can be real automation, or a friendlier label for a normal restructuring. Anyone who wants to know needs an answer to one hard question: which tasks does the AI concretely take over, and who measures whether it manages them? This very question now lands on the table of many works councils, not just at SAP. The idea for this is below.

NRW: AI advises urgent applications, social courts fill up Public Legal DE

Jens Blüggel, president of the NRW state social court (Landessozialgericht), proposes "a 15-euro entry fee for urgent proceedings," according to dpa. heise reports this on Thu 11/26/2026.[3] His court has "already submitted a concrete draft bill to the Federal Ministry of Labor and Social Affairs."[3]

The numbers. The number of urgent proceedings at the NRW social courts has "literally exploded from about 1,400 in the first quarter of 2025 to 3,600 in the same period of 2026."[3] That is more than two and a half times as many. Today, "the judges of the eight NRW social courts alone receive around 1,600 urgent applications per month."[3] Regular lawsuits recently took "almost 16 months."[3]

Decision stalls→ AI advises urgent application→ Urgent proceedings ×2.5→ Judges overloaded→ Proposal: €15 fee

The role of AI. Blüggel says: "As soon as something stalls somewhere, the AI advises an urgent application."[3] According to the article, the applications are partly drafted with AI and are often needlessly long. Many concern basic income support, social assistance or health benefits, before the agency has even decided. A working group of the justice ministers' conference has been examining, since 11/19, a cost share for urgent proceedings as well. Recommendations are due in spring 2027.[3]

Urgent proceedings (Eilverfahren / Eilantrag): An application asking the court to make a provisional decision quickly, because the normal procedure takes too long. Like the emergency room instead of a family doctor appointment in three months.
Methodology The link to AI is the court president's assessment, not a study. Nobody counts how many applications really come from an AI. The source is a dpa report via heise.

My view: This shows a side of consumer AI that hardly anyone talks about. A chatbot that advises every citizen to file an urgent application shifts the work from the citizen to the judge. For the individual it may even be the right call. For the system it is a spam wave with a legal entitlement. A fee is the bluntest tool against it. Smarter would be for providers to have their models ask about the status of the proceedings first, before advising to go to court.

A sketchbook from 1808, solved in 22 minutes Research Culture Global

The historian Mark Humphries (Wilfrid Laurier University, Canada) gave an AI a riddle from the Beinecke Library at Yale University. Who made the drawings of a journey from Montreal to Fort William in 1808? Martin Holland reports this at heise on 11/25/2026.[4] GPT-6.2 Astra answered: "After exactly 22 minutes and one second." The artist was probably Louis-Guy-Charles-Guillaume de Rohan-Chabot, Vicomte de Chabot (1780–1875).[4]

The evidence is a diary entry by Chabot from June 18, 1808: "I made a sketch." According to Humphries, the description matches a drawing from that day. Fable 5.2 reached "the same conclusion after 35 minutes, but without finding the diary."[4] Humphries thinks: "A human would probably never have figured it out."[4]

Source status One expert, his own blog, no independent check. Humphries himself says it is unclear how the model found the diary. The hint came from a 2014 doctoral thesis. He also concedes that he initially fed the model two wrong names.[4]

My view: The pattern fits patent research, due diligence or government archives: read scattered sources, find a lead, deliver evidence. But anyone who cannot trace the path to the result has to check every piece of evidence themselves. The model details and Astra's safety card are in the ai-dev brief.

BSI: AI agents as burglars Security Public EU

The BSI sees a "heightened threat level." It warns about "Agentic Offensive Frameworks (AOFs) such as Artex, which have meanwhile reached a high level of maturity." Olivia von Westernhagen reports this at heise on 11/26/2026 from a heise security PRO event.[5]

  • Cairn: Gambit Security uncovered "attacks on hundreds of online shops," built on this framework.[5]
  • Artex: At the EU Commission, an attack with this tool led to the "shutdown of central internal administration platforms." CERT-EU and the FBI had already warned about the tool.[5]
  • Timing: According to security researcher Florian Roth, the attacks picked up speed "in August and September of this year."[5]
Agentic Offensive Framework (AOF): A toolbox in which several AI agents carry out an attack on their own: find targets, test for weaknesses, break in. Like a burglar team that never sleeps and tries a thousand doors at once.

The defense is boring. The BSI mainly insists on the basics. Roth recommends multi-factor authentication (MFA) for all access and "a clear separation of network areas." His rule of thumb for "defensive minimalism": "Anyone who wants to enable a feature should be able to explain" why the effort is worth it.[5]

Source status Everything comes from one heise report about an event. We did not read the BSI paper itself. How many shops exactly were affected and how long the Commission's systems were down is not in the excerpt.

My view: For mid-sized shop operators this means: the attacker is now cheap and patient. An open admin access without a second factor will not be found someday, but soon. This week's technical security holes are in the software brief.

Cloudera: AI with Mistral, even without internet Deploy B2B SaaS EU

The data platform vendor Cloudera presented the "Anywhere Cloud" at its conference in Madrid. It "is set to be generally available from November 30," writes Harald Weiss at heise on 11/26/2026.[6] The control layer manages services in "company-owned data centers and fully isolated environments (air gap)." Added to this is "the partnership with Mistral AI announced back in September," and the new partners are the European cloud providers Exoscale and Aruba.[6] Cloudera says it has "invested around one billion US dollars through in-house development and acquisitions."[6]

Air gap: A computer network with no connection to the internet at all. Like a vault without windows: nobody can see from outside what is inside, but new goods have to be carried in by hand.

The Gartner figure. "By 2028, according to a Gartner forecast, 60 percent of regulated companies" will have moved their AI applications to local infrastructure, according to Cloudera as reported by heise. In addition, "31 percent of companies face uncertainty about where their data is stored."[6]

Methodology Both Gartner figures come from a report about the vendor's conference. We have not seen the Gartner study itself, its sample or its date. A forecast is not a measurement.

My view: Sovereign AI is becoming a product shelf for the big players. The gap lies with the mid-sized company that has no data center for a platform of this size. This supports the sovereignty bill of materials from the Nov. 26 issue.

AI Act: 5 days left Reg EU

The AI omnibus package appeared in the EU Official Journal on 07/24/2026 and has applied since 07/27/2026. On Wed 12/02/2026, two items kick in. First, a new ban in Art. 5 on AI images of an intimate nature without consent and on depictions of child abuse: "This ban is set to take effect from 2 December 2026." Second, the grace period ends for generative AI that came to market before 08/02/2026: it has "until 2 December 2026 to comply with transparency and labelling requirements."[7] The high-risk rules follow on 12/02/2027 for stand-alone systems and on 08/02/2028 for AI in regulated products.[7]

Open A law-firm overview gives 12/02/2027 instead for part of the labelling duty (Art. 50 (2)) for legacy systems. We could not resolve this. Anyone who generates AI content and was on the market before August should have the Official Journal text checked themselves. The parliament quotes are in the Nov. 26 issue.

Project Ideas for twiceD

Researched proposals — with market gap and honest solo feasibility

1 · AI expert for works councils: What does the system really do?

ELI5 & Value: When a company introduces AI, for example an assistant in support or a tool that evaluates work output, the works council (Betriebsrat) has a say. But it often cannot judge what data the system collects and who sees it. The law helps: if the works council has to assess the introduction or use of AI, then "the engagement of an expert is deemed necessary to that extent."[10] twiceD would be that expert, but for the technology, not for the law. Value for the works council: a clear answer instead of a gut feeling. Value for the company: a works agreement that is finished faster, because both sides talk about the same facts. The occasion is current: if SAP wants to cut jobs under "AI first,"[1] works councils in many companies face the same question.
Prototype (V0): (1) Build a fixed question catalog: What data does the system capture? Who has access to usage data? Where does the model run? Does it make decisions about people? (2) At the client, check a test account and the admin interface: what evaluations of individual employees can the employer pull up there? (3) Write a five-to-ten-page expert report in works-council language, with a traffic light per question. (4) Deliver clause suggestions for the works agreement, for example on access to logs. The works council's lawyer checks the legal text. MVP deliberately narrow: one AI system, one report, no legal advice. Building blocks: the vendor documentation, the admin console, the network capture from the sovereignty bill of materials of Nov. 26.
Market & Gap: Law firms such as CMS describe the legal framework in detail. Under § 90 BetrVG (German Works Constitution Act), the employer must inform the works council "in good time about the planned use of AI, presenting the necessary documents." Software- or web-based AI is therefore in principle a technical device in the sense of co-determination (§ 87 (1) no. 6 BetrVG).[11] On costs, CMS cites the legislator's assumption: "a daily rate of an expert of EUR 833.00 (incl. VAT)," as a starting point for negotiations.[11] Law firms deliver the law, and this CMS text is clearly aimed at employers. Gap for twiceD: the technician who opens the admin console and says what is really stored. Honestly open: we did not research which technical consultants already work for works councils today, for example union-affiliated advisory bodies. We also have no market size. €833 per day including tax is a negotiating anchor, not a market price, and rather low for consulting.
Feasibility (Solo + AI): High for the report, with three catches. First: pure consulting without operations and maintenance. The question catalog takes one to two weeks solo, a report takes three to six working days. Second: the works council cannot commission alone. It needs "an agreement between the parties of the works constitution on the person of the expert,"[11] and the employer can negotiate over the person. Third: independence. Anyone who audits for works councils cannot also sell AI projects to the same company. twiceD must pick one side per client. Scale through a contractor with labor-law experience. Video sessions work from Brazil, an on-site appointment in Germany needs planning. gesetze-im-internet.de · cms.law · heise.de
