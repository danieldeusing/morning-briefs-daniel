Standing still isn't calm here

FX in detail — where we stand and why

ECB reference rate ("fix"): every business day around 14:15 Central European Time — about 09:15 in Brasília — the European Central Bank polls the major banks for a rate and publishes it as the official daily anchor. Like a class photo: one moment, captured for the whole day. The market keeps moving, the anchor stays put. Every number here is set against the last fix and settled against the next one — never against a morning spot.

Update since Mon Since Monday's fix, Daniel's rate has moved 0.08%: 5.8454 → 5.8503. That looks like an uneventful market, but it's the opposite — the move was there, it just canceled itself out:

EUR/USD 1.1535 → 1.1515 (−0.17%)× USD/BRL 5.0675 → 5.0806 (+0.26%)= EUR/BRL +0.08%

Both currencies lost ground against the dollar, the real a bit more. When both legs weaken at the same time, Daniel's cross rate is largely immune to the dollar cycle. It only gets dangerous when they diverge — say, if Copom moves the real tonight without anything happening in Frankfurt or Washington.

In practical exchange terms: €100 bought R$584.54 at Monday's fix, R$585.03 at Tuesday's — 49 centavos more. The 12-month low of the daily series is 5.7449 (May 13); the current rate sits 1.83% above it. A note on the chart above: the 12-month curve only shows start-of-month readings, so its May point (5.8103 on May 4) isn't the actual low — the real bottom came nine days later. Even so, the level stays in the bottom eighth of the 12-month range of 5.7449 to 6.5851 (Dec 23) — not a buying opportunity, a waiting room.

The risk premium falls for a second day

Positioning, sentiment & Brazilian yields

CDS (Credit Default Swap): insurance against a country defaulting on its debt. The price in basis points shows what the market charges annually for that protection — 119.42 means 1.1942% of the insured amount per year. Like bike insurance: if the neighborhood gets rougher, the premium rises before the first bike is even stolen.

Brazil's five-year CDS closed on Aug 4 at 119.42 basis points, −3.32 bp (−2.71%). The implied previous-day close of 122.74 is exactly the value this brief independently pulled for Aug 3 yesterday — the series is clean: 125.90 (Jul 31) → 122.74 (Aug 3) → 119.42 (Aug 4). In early July, the same premium stood at around 126 basis points, per MacroMicro.

Source note The same page lists 190.23 as "previous-day close" — arithmetically incompatible with −2.71% (that would be a 37% plunge). Not used; the internally consistent pair 119.42 / −3.32 is the reliable one.

My read: on the eve of a rate decision, three months before an election, with a debt ratio at its highest level since October 2021, Brazil's default insurance is getting cheaper. If the real were election-jittery, this exact price would have to rise. It's falling for a second straight day. The real is giving ground because the dollar bloc is pushing it — not because the market sees Brazil as riskier.

NTN-B real yield: a Brazilian bond that strips out inflation — it guarantees IPCA plus X%, no matter how high inflation jumps. The X shows how much real return the market demands from the government. When X falls, the government is trusted more.

That fits with the NTN-B 2035: IPCA + 7.98% against 8.30% on Jul 27. Caveat: the source carries no timestamp — the value is good for direction, not for a day-to-day comparison.

Copom tonight: the number isn't the story

Market-implied probability: options on the Copom decision trade on Brazil's B3 exchange. Their prices can be worked backward to show how likely traders consider each outcome — like betting odds in soccer. "75.5%" means: three out of four real staked sit on "a quarter-point cut."

The Selic stands at 14.25%, and Copom decides tonight after the market close — it would be the fourth straight cut. The consensus is clear: 38 of 42 in the Reuters poll (taken Jul 27–31) expect −25 basis points to 14.00%, and B3 options price it at 75.5%.

Why the number still isn't the story: an outcome that's three-quarters priced in barely moves the rate — it's already baked in. Movement comes from what isn't priced in: the tone of the communiqué. That matters especially today, because the expectation around the path has shifted. In the Focus survey, the year-end Selic forecast slid from 14.00% to 13.75%, the 2026 IPCA forecast from 5.12% to 5.03% — a fifth straight decline.

Focus survey: Brazil's central bank asks over a hundred banks and fund managers every week for their forecasts on interest rates, inflation, and the exchange rate, and publishes the median. Like a class average before the final exam: not the result itself, but the best available read on what everyone is expecting.

Oil: correction — the crash kept going

Yesterday this space said "the crash is holding only halfway," based on a Brent price of $85.01 (+1.48%). That was a mid-morning tick that didn't survive the day. This morning, the same contract trades at $78.99 a barrel, in its third straight losing session — roughly 7% below the value of 24 hours ago.

Cleanliness note: the source page ticks live and shows two slightly different readings in the header and body text ($78.99 / −0.47% versus $78.86 / −0.63%). What's reliable is only this: Brent is below $79 and falling. The drop is confirmed by the same page's reference figures — the monthly gain melted from +18.1% to +9.7%, the annual gain from +25.7% to +18.1%. That doesn't happen without a double-digit dollar loss.

Terms of trade: the price ratio between what a country sells and what it buys. Like trading collectible cards: if your own cards lose value, you get less back for the same amount. Brazil exports crude oil — when Brent falls, foreign-currency earnings drop, and the real loses a support.

My take: I overweighted yesterday's countermove. The oil price is now pushing twice — it takes pressure off inflation (and thus off Copom's brake) and worsens Brazil's export earnings. Both channels end at a weaker real. That's why my monthly range stays tilted higher, even though the risk premium is falling.

It's Wednesday, August fifth. Brazil's central bank makes its rate call tonight, and this brief goes out hours before that decision — everything you're about to hear about it is expectation, not outcome.

The exchange rate itself is barely moving. In the European Central Bank's fixing for Tuesday, EUR/BRL sits at five point eight five zero three. Against Monday's five point eight four five four, that's zero point zero eight percent — next to nothing across two trading days.

But that stillness isn't calm, it's a cancellation. The real lost zero point two six percent against the dollar, the euro zero point one seven percent. Both currencies weakened, the real a bit more. Because those two moves nearly offset each other, Daniel's cross rate barely budged. A hundred euros bought five hundred eighty-five point zero three reais at Tuesday's fix, forty-nine centavos more than Monday.

Now to the decision itself. The policy rate stands at fourteen point two five percent, and a cut tonight would be the fourth in a row. In the Reuters poll, thirty-eight of forty-two analysts expect a quarter-point cut, to fourteen percent, and on Brazil's stock exchange, options price that cut at seventy-five point five percent. That's the only real market price in this brief; every other percentage is my own judgment.

That's exactly why the number itself isn't the headline tonight. An outcome that's three-quarters priced in barely moves the rate anymore. Movement comes from the tone of the communiqué. And something has shifted there: in the weekly Focus survey, the year-end rate forecast slid from fourteen to thirteen point seven five percent, the inflation forecast for twenty twenty-six from five point one two to five point zero three percent — the fifth straight decline. Those numbers come from Bloomberg's reporting on August third; a direct check with the central bank failed today, the site returned only an empty shell.

Now for today's correction, and it's an uncomfortable one. Yesterday, this brief said the oil crash was holding only halfway, based on a Brent price of eighty-five point zero one dollars. That was a morning tick that didn't survive the day. This morning, Brent trades below seventy-nine dollars, its third straight losing session, roughly seven percent below the value I printed yesterday. The same source's reference figures confirm it: the monthly gain shrank from eighteen to just under ten percent.

That changes the read, not just the number. Cheap oil takes pressure off inflation and gives the central bank room for further cuts — which narrows the rate gap the real depends on. At the same time, it worsens Brazil's terms of trade, since the country exports crude oil. Both paths land in the same place: a weaker real.

Against that stands today's strongest counter-signal. The market's insurance against a Brazilian sovereign default fell on Tuesday to one hundred nineteen point four two basis points, a second straight day of declines, down from one hundred twenty-two point seven four on Monday. On the eve of a rate decision, three months before an election, with debt at its highest level since October twenty twenty-one, that insurance is getting cheaper. If the real were election-jittery, this exact price would have to rise. It's falling instead. Fitting that picture, the real yield on the ten-year inflation-linked bond also eased, from eight point three to seven point nine eight percent.

The counter-move comes from the United States. The Fed held at three point five to three point seven five percent in late July, but three members wanted to hike. Brazil is cutting while three voices in Washington openly back higher rates — that gap is the structural threat to the real. The Dollar Index itself trades at ninety-nine point eight four, near a seven-week low.

For today, I expect a range of five point eighty-two to five point eighty-nine, most likely five point eighty-five. For the week, five point seventy-nine to five point ninety-four — the reaction to tonight's decision won't land until Thursday's trading. So tomorrow's brief will be the first to actually see the Copom announcement in the rate; that's what I'll be watching first.
