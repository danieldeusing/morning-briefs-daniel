The Real Fell Before Copom Spoke

FX in detail — where we stand and why

ECB reference rate ("fix"): Every business day around 14:15 Central European time — about 09:15 in Brasília — the European Central Bank polls major banks for a rate and publishes it as the official daily anchor. Think of a class photo: one moment, frozen for the whole day. What matters today: that moment came before Wednesday evening's Copom announcement.

Update since Wed Between Tuesday's and Wednesday's fix, Daniel's rate rose from 5.8503 to 5.9102 — +1.02%. After nine trading days between 5.78 and 5.86, that's a breakout, not noise. The breakdown:

EUR/USD 1.1515 → 1.1554 (+0.34%)× USD/BRL 5.0806 → 5.1153 (+0.68%)= EUR/BRL +1.02%

Both legs pulled in the same direction this time — exactly the setup this brief flagged as the dangerous one yesterday. And the decisive part is the sign: the dollar was weaker (EUR/USD rose), and the real still lost against it. Losing against a currency that's giving ground means you're not losing because of it. Confirmed by the dollar index, sitting still at 99.72: there was no dollar wave on Wednesday to blame this on.

In practical exchange terms: 100 euros bought 585.03 reais at Tuesday's fix, and 591.02 reais at Wednesday's fix — nearly six more reais in a single day. The trailing 12-month low sits at 5.7449 (May 13), the high at 6.5851 (Dec 23). The current fix stands 2.88% above the low, in the bottom fifth of the range — yesterday it was still the bottom eighth. Still favorable ground for a euro seller, but the cushion shrank by a third in one day.

And today? Thursday's trading has produced no reversal so far: XE reported 5.9268 at 01:48 BRT, and the TradingEconomics cross implies 5.9344. A cleanliness note: the fix and the spot are different measurement points from different sources — only the direction is reliable, and it points up.

Forecast Test: the Range Didn't Hold

Yesterday this page said, for Wednesday, "5.82–5.89 (65%), most likely 5.85 (~32%)." The fix came in at 5.9102 — 0.34% above the upper bound, 1.03% off the point estimate. That's a clean miss, not a near hit.

What went wrong The error wasn't the direction, it was the width. I set the range at ±0.6% around the last fix — the usual daily width of a calm market — and did that for the fix of a decision day, of all days. On top of that, the assumption that the two legs would offset each other as they had on prior days. They did the opposite. Methodology fix: on central-bank decision days, the daily range will now be opened to at least ±1.0%, and the "offsetting legs" assumption only applies as long as it's actually held over the last three fixes.

What did hold: the weekly range of 5.79–5.94 — barely, the rate sits at its upper edge. And the conditional path I weighted highest yesterday: "cut plus open door → toward 5.92." That's exactly what happened. The mechanism was right; the timing wasn't — the move came before the decision, not after.

The Risk Premium Is Turning Too

Positioning, sentiment & BR yields

CDS (Credit Default Swap): Insurance against a country defaulting on its debt. The price in basis points shows what the market charges annually for that protection — 121.26 means 1.2126% of the insured amount per year. Like bike insurance: as the neighborhood gets riskier, the premium rises before the first bike is even stolen.

Brazil's five-year CDS stands at 121.26 basis points, +1.84 bp (+1.54%). That ends the brief easing streak: 125.90 (Jul 31) → 122.74 (Aug 3) → 119.42 (Aug 4) → 121.26. The premium rises on the exact day the real loses 0.68% against a weakening dollar. My read: that's confirmation the move is Brazilian in origin. Had it been a dollar wave, default insurance wouldn't have had to move with it.

Dating — disclosed The source page lists "08/05" as its date, which is ambiguous on its own. We read it as August 5, 2026, because the daily change shown there, +1.84 bp, lands exactly on 119.42 — the closing level this brief independently pulled for Aug 4 yesterday (119.42 + 1.84 = 121.26; 1.84/119.42 = 1.54%). The math checks out with no remainder. Anyone unwilling to follow this derivation should ignore the figure — the rest of the argument holds without it.

Not pulled today: BCB swap positions, CFTC COT positioning, the DI futures curve, EWZ fund flows, and NTN-B real yields. The fetch budget went to the Copom decision and the FX anchors. No estimate stands in their place here.

Copom: Cut, Door Open, Currency Named as a Risk

The Selic has stood at 14.00% since last night — down 25 basis points, unanimous across all seven members, the fourth straight cut. That was expected: B3 options had priced this outcome in beforehand at around 75%. The news is in the text.

Forward guidance: the part of a central-bank statement that doesn't say what's happening today, but what might happen next. Like a coach who hints, right after the game, at who'll start next week — the team adjusts even though nothing's decided yet. Markets price in that hint immediately.

The key sentence: the total extent of the cutting cycle will depend on "new information" that confirms inflation's return to target. That's not an end to the cycle — it's an open door. And the list of risks is notably specific: elevated inflation risks "with upside asymmetry," including longer-unanchored expectations, sticky services inflation, a more depreciated currency, and demand running above potential. Per the statement, inflation expectations sit at 5.0% for 2026 and 4.2% for 2027 — both above target.

Why it matters for Daniel: Copom has just described, in its own words, the feedback loop his exchange rate is caught in.

The loop closes: the further the cuts go, the weaker the real; the weaker the real, the bigger the inflation risk that should slow Copom down. My position: this feedback is the best insurance against the real sliding out of control — it caps how deep the cycle can go. But it works late. The currency has to fall first before the brake kicks in. For the coming weeks, that means: a weaker real first, and a floor from the central bank itself only after.

The Fiscal Gap, Calculated Two Ways

Primary balance: government revenue minus spending, but excluding interest on existing debt. Like a household budget that leaves out the loan payment: it shows whether day-to-day operations pay for themselves. A deficit means the country is taking on new debt before the first interest payment is even due.

The government officially expects R$22.6 billion in extra spending for 2026 (roughly US$4.4 billion). A second official tally, which strips out certain line items and the tolerance band, stood at R$60.3 billion in mid-July — nearly three times as much. The government's bimonthly budget report on Jul 24 revised that down to R$52 billion (R$12.3bn higher expected revenue, R$5.7bn of unblocked spending). The path already booked is unambiguous: from January through May, a primary deficit of R$44.4 billion built up, against a surplus of R$32.9 billion over the same period in 2025.

My read: it's not the size of the gap that's the problem, it's the spread. When two official tallies from the same government are three times apart, the market doesn't buy the smaller number — it prices in the uncertainty. Currencies punish a bad framework harder than a bad number. That's why my 3–6-month range stays tilted upward above, even though the real interest rate, at roughly nine percent (Selic 14.00% minus 5.0% expected inflation, roughly calculated), is hard to beat internationally. Dating: the fiscal figures come from reporting dated Jul 20 — not critical for an annual projection, unusable for a day-to-day comparison.

It's Thursday, August sixth. Last night, Brazil's central bank rate-setting committee made its call, and today, for the first time, we have a rate that actually reflects that decision. But the more interesting story happened before that.

Copom cut its benchmark rate by a quarter point to fourteen percent. Unanimous, across all seven members, and it was the fourth straight cut. That part was expected — Brazil's stock exchange had priced this outcome in beforehand at around seventy-five percent. So the real news isn't in the number, it's in the text of the statement.

And that text leaves the door open. The total extent of the cutting cycle, it says, depends on new information. That's not a signal that it's over. What stands out is the list of inflation risks the committee spells out: unanchored expectations, sticky services inflation, demand running above potential — and explicitly, a more depreciated currency too. The central bank is describing, in its own words, the loop that Daniel's exchange rate is caught in.

Now to the actual move. In the European Central Bank's Wednesday fixing, EUR/BRL stands at five point nine one zero two. Against Tuesday's five point eight five zero three, that's a jump of one point zero two percent in a single day. After nine trading days between five point seventy-eight and five point eighty-six, that's a breakout. And this fix is taken around a quarter past nine Brasília time — hours before Copom had even spoken.

Broken down, it looks like this: the real lost zero point six eight percent against the dollar. The euro gained zero point three four percent against that same dollar in the same window. That's the key point. The dollar was weaker, and the real still lost against it. Losing against a currency that's giving ground means you're not losing because of it. The dollar index confirms this: it's sitting at ninety-nine point seven two, essentially unchanged. There was no dollar wave to blame here. This move is Brazilian.

That fits with the signal from the risk premium. The market's insurance against a Brazilian default rose to one hundred twenty-one point two six basis points, up one point five four percent. That ends a two-day easing streak — on the exact day the real drops.

Now for the uncomfortable reckoning. Yesterday this brief said Wednesday would land between five point eighty-two and five point eighty-nine, most likely five point eighty-five. The fix came in at five point nine one zero two. That's zero point three four percent above the upper bound — a clean miss. The error wasn't the direction, it was the width: I used the typical daily range of a calm market, for a decision day of all things. From now on, decision days get at least double the usual range.

What did hold up was the weekly range and the conditional path. I'd written that a cut plus an open door meant heading toward five point ninety-two. That's exactly what happened. The mechanism was right, just not the timing — the move came before the decision, not after.

In the background sits the fiscal situation, and it has a framing problem. The government officially expects twenty-two point six billion reais in extra spending this year. A second official tally stood at sixty point three billion in mid-July — almost three times as much; the government's July twenty-fourth report revised that down to fifty-two billion. From January through May, a primary deficit of forty-four point four billion already built up, against a surplus of thirty-two point nine billion in the same period last year. When two official numbers from the same government are three times apart, the market doesn't buy the smaller figure. It prices in the uncertainty.

For today, I expect a range of five point eighty-nine to five point ninety-six, most likely five point ninety-three. For the week, a plateau between five point eighty-six and six even. A hundred euros bought five hundred ninety-one point zero two reais at Wednesday's fix, nearly six more reais than Tuesday. Tomorrow's brief will show whether the first fix after the decision confirms this jump or gives some of it back. That's what I'm watching first.
