FX in Detail: The Slip Came After the Fix

Yesterday morning the market was boring, and this brief wrote it up that way. By the afternoon it wasn't. The path, in numbers:

The decisive step is the third one. If the dollar had strengthened globally, the Dollar Index would have to follow suit — it stands at 99.794, 0.02% lower, and EUR/USD at 1.15413 is likewise almost unmoved. If the dollar stands still against half the world and gains 0.42% against the real, the market has a real problem, not a dollar story.

Fix vs. Spot: The ECB fix is a single snapshot taken once a day, set at 14:15 CEST (09:15 BRT). The spot rate is the running film. Like a class photo taken at nine in the morning: anyone who changes their hairstyle afterward still shows up with the old one in the picture. That's why today's 0.34% gap between the fix and the spot isn't a data glitch — it's simply what happened after the shutter clicked.

What that means for exchanging money. The monthly range (Jul 10–Aug 10, ECB fixes) runs from 5.7775 to 5.9138; today's spot of 5.8962 sits at 87% of it. Concretely: €100 buys R$589.62 instead of R$577.75 at the monthly low, and a €5,000 exchange comes to roughly R$593 more than at the worst moment of the month. Not a rate to celebrate, but a good one. Anyone who needs to exchange this month anyway has a decent window this morning — with the caveat of the two data points ahead of the fix.

Forecast check. For yesterday's fix, this page had 5.84–5.91 with a point estimate of 5.87. What came in was 5.8764 — inside the range, 0.1% off the point estimate. Not proof of anything: on quiet days, a tight range is cheap to hit.

Today in Brazil: Minutes at Eight, Inflation at Nine

Two releases, both ahead of the ECB fix, both with a direct bearing on today's exchange rate.

08:00 BRT — Copom minutes. The central bank cut the policy rate, the Selic, unanimously (7:0) by 25 basis points to 14.00% on Aug 5 — the fourth cut of that size in a row. The minutes provide the reasoning, and the market reads them for how many more cuts are coming.

09:00 BRT — July IPCA. The prior reading came in at 4.64% year over year (June, down from 4.72% in May, below the 4.80% forecast) and 0.16% month over month. Above target, but heading in the right direction for months now.

Rate advantage (carry): Brazil pays 14% on safe money, the US 3.5–3.75%. Whoever borrows dollars and invests in reais earns the difference — a good ten points a year. Like a savings account that pays ten times as much as the one next door: as long as the currency doesn't crash, that's a very good deal. That's exactly why the real holds up despite poor debt numbers. Every Selic cut shaves a piece off that cushion.

My expectation: the minutes will sound more cautious than the unanimous vote suggests — anyone cutting into a rising debt ratio builds themselves a verbal exit. That would be real-friendly and push the spot toward 5.88.

Fed: I'm Flipping the Sign

Update since Mon On Jul 29 the Fed held the policy rate at 3.50–3.75%. What's interesting isn't the outcome, but the vote: 9 to 3 — and the three dissenters (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) wanted to raise the rate, not cut it.

Dissent at the FOMC: Anyone on the Fed's committee who votes differently is named publicly. Like a family where everyone usually says "yes" to the vacation spot: if three suddenly object loudly, the next decision is pretty likely to go a different way. Three dissents pointing the same direction are the strongest signal short of an actual rate change.

Until now, this brief had priced the 12-month path on the assumption that the Fed was more likely to cut. That's no longer tenable after this vote. Per coverage of the meeting, futures markets are now pricing in two 25-basis-point hikes for September and December — I couldn't confirm that market pricing against a primary, dated source today, so it appears here without a percentage. The vote itself is documented; the direction is enough to draw the consequence.

Why it matters: a hiking Fed lifts the dollar and trims the rate advantage from the US side. For EUR/BRL that works in two stages — first it weakens the real against the dollar, then EUR/BRL follows, muted, because a strong dollar also weighs on the euro.

Fed hike risk→US yields up→BR carry edge shrinks→BRL weaker→EUR/BRL higher

Fiscal: 81.9% — the Headwind to Rate Cuts

Brazil's general-government gross debt stood at 81.9% of GDP in June (around R$10.8 trillion), up from 81.0% in May — the highest level since the 2021 pandemic peak. The public sector's primary deficit came to R$55.3 billion in June, cumulatively R$157.2 billion over twelve months, or 1.19% of GDP.

Primary deficit: What the government spends minus what it takes in — without interest on existing debt. Like a household that sets aside the monthly loan interest and just asks: does the paycheck cover rent and groceries? If even that part is in the red, the debt pile grows even if not a single interest payment were due.

That's the uncomfortable tension of this week: the central bank is cutting while the debt ratio climbs and the interest burden is the main driver. Together, that's a country that needs cheaper money to carry its debt — exactly the setup in which investors eventually demand a risk premium instead of just a higher yield. As long as the advantage stays this large, the yield wins. That's the reason for my upward tilt on the 3–6 month horizon.

Tariffs and Oil: the Two Quiet Channels

[BR/USA] The US Section 301 tariff of 25% on most Brazilian goods has been in effect since Jul 22; nothing new is moving politically. Washington accuses Brasília of not negotiating in good faith, while Vice President Alckmin keeps betting on negotiation over retaliation. For the exchange rate, that's the friendlier line: retaliatory tariffs would damage the trade-balance channel that supports the real.

[Global] Brent is trading at $87.73 a barrel, a good 5% higher than a month ago and around 33% above a year ago. The effect cuts both ways: as a net exporter, Brazil benefits on the trade surplus, while at the same time expensive oil pushes inflation up through fuel and transport prices — exactly the number due at 09:00 BRT today. Lately, the export channel has dominated.

Why this belongs in an FX brief: a P/E of 150 means the market is paying for 150 years of earnings up front. Valuations like that only hold up when risk appetite is high — and risk appetite is exactly the fuel that sends money into high-rate emerging markets like Brazil. If the US AI segment tips over, the real often tips with it on the very same day, without anything having happened in Brazil. Not an investment thesis here, just an early-warning sensor.

It's Tuesday, August eleventh. The currency market looked quiet yesterday morning, and by afternoon it wasn't — and the one number that's supposed to capture the day's move never even saw it happen.

The European Central Bank's reference rate for the euro against the real sits at five point eight seven six four. It was set yesterday at a quarter past nine in the morning, Brasília time. The real weakened after that. This morning the spot rate is running at five point eight nine six two, roughly a third of a percent above the reference rate.

A cross-check through the two underlying legs backs that up. Dollar-to-real times euro-to-dollar comes out to five point eight nine four three — just three hundredths of a percent off the traded rate. So the anchors line up cleanly; the gap versus the reference rate is real movement, not noise.

What's worth noting is where that movement came from. The Dollar Index is essentially flat today, and euro-dollar barely budged either. Yet the dollar still gained about four tenths of a percent against the real. When the dollar sits still against half the world and only climbs against the real, the problem is sitting in Brazil, not in the dollar.

This morning should settle whether that holds. At eight o'clock, Brazil's central bank publishes the minutes from its August fifth meeting, where it cut the policy rate unanimously to fourteen percent. At nine, July's inflation reading follows; June came in at four point six four percent year over year. Both land before the next reference rate is set.

For anyone exchanging money, today's rate sits in the top sixth of the monthly range. A hundred euros currently buy around five hundred eighty-nine reais, versus just under five hundred seventy-eight at the monthly low. Anyone who needs to exchange this month has a solid window this morning.

One correction on my own work. This brief had been assuming the Federal Reserve was leaning toward cuts. After the July twenty-ninth meeting, I no longer treat that as the base case: the rate held at three and a half to three and three-quarters percent, but three members voted against it — pushing for a hike, not a cut. Three dissents pointing the same direction is the strongest signal short of an actual rate change, so I've pulled my three-to-six-month range upward.

In the background, Brazil's fiscal picture keeps working against the real. Gross debt hit eighty-one point nine percent of GDP in June, the highest level since the pandemic, and the public sector ran a primary deficit of fifty-five point three billion reais for the month. As long as the rate advantage of a good ten percentage points holds, the yield wins out over that worry. It won't hold forever.
