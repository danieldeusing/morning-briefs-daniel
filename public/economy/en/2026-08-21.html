FX in Detail: the Euro Carries, the Real Pushes Back

Thursday's ECB fix stands at 6.0666, +0.45% versus the previous day — the highest reference rate since March 24. The information lies in the breakdown.

Two legs: EUR/BRL isn't its own market. Nobody trades euros directly against reais in any meaningful volume; your rate emerges as the product of euro-to-dollar times dollar-to-real. Like a flight with a layover: euros into dollars first, then dollars into reais. That's why every move in your rate has exactly two possible sources — Europe or Brazil.

On Thursday, the euro leg rose 0.65% (1.1605 → 1.1681), while the real leg fell 0.20% (5.2041 → 5.1936): the euro accounted for roughly 145% of the move, with the real working against it.

EUR/USD +0.65%− USD/BRL 0.20%→ EUR/BRL +0.45%

This isn't a one-day story. On the week (08/13 → 08/20, +1.52%), 1.27 percentage points came from the euro and only 0.24 from the real — 84% Europe. On the month (07/20 → 08/20, +4.02%), the split is more balanced: 2.23 points euro against 1.75 points real. The real weakened all month; the past week belongs to the euro alone.

For today, that means: the euro keeps running (1.1693 this morning, +0.12%), while Brazil hasn't traded yet. The market cross of 6.0730 is therefore, at its core, a euro carry-forward onto yesterday's real, not a fully new rate. This exact setup misled yesterday: the 6.0444 cross undershot the fix by 0.37%, because the real gave back its overnight gain during Brazilian trading.

In concrete terms: exchanging €5,000 comes to R$30,333 at yesterday's fix. At the year-to-date low on 05/13 (5.7449), it would have been R$28,725 — R$1,608 less; a month ago, R$29,161. The exchange window isn't just open — it opened a bit wider still yesterday.

The Dollar Is Losing Its Rate Argument

On Wednesday, the US Treasury announced it would at least double its buybacks of long-dated government bonds: from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation, across the 10-20 and 20-30 year maturity bands, effective 09/09 through 11/04/2026.

Bond buyback: the government repurchases its own IOUs in the market. Not to pay down debt, but to guarantee a reliable buyer is always there. Like a concert promoter who buys back tickets himself so the resale price doesn't collapse — anyone holding a ticket then worries less about getting stuck with it, and demands a smaller discount.

The effect was immediately measurable: the ten-year yield fell 6 basis points to 4.647%, the thirty-year fell 9 to 5.196%. The Dollar Index slipped below 99 for the first time since late May — the data provider explicitly names the announcement as the trigger.

Buybacks doubled→ 30y −9bp→ DXY below 99→ EUR/USD 1.1681

The bigger half of the story is the Fed. After the July meeting — held at 3.50–3.75%, but with three dissents in favor of a hike — the market on 08/05 was still pricing roughly 65% odds of a September move up. That bet now sits around one-third. Goldman goes further: Fed unchanged through year-end, first cut in 2027, citing softer retail sales, a cooling labor market, and easing inflation data.

Why this reaches you indirectly: the dollar's rate advantage over the euro is shrinking in expectations — a Fed that's no longer hiking on one side, an ECB that might be on the other. That's the fuel behind 1.1681. The same soft dollar is positive for the real too, but Brazil faces an election headwind that Europe doesn't have. So net, something is left over for you — unlike yesterday morning, when both legs canceled each other out.

Why is the market pricing an ECB hike at 79 to 83%, while unwinding the Fed's? The answer runs through the oil price — and it works in both directions for you.

Brent is quoted at $93.13, a second straight winning week and up more than 5% this week alone — driven by US-Iran tensions over the Strait of Hormuz and Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy infrastructure. On August 3, the same price stood at $87.38: +6.6% in under three weeks.

That's flowing through into European inflation: the energy component jumped to 10.0% year over year in July (from 8.5%), and the headline rate to 2.9% against a 2.0% target. The ECB's June staff projection sees a 2026 average of 3.0%, not returning to 2.0% until 2028. A central bank facing that picture with a 2.25% deposit rate has little room to sit still.

Priced in: an event that nearly everyone expects is already baked into today's rate. Like a surprise party the birthday person already knows about — the party still happens, but the surprise is gone. Only a deviation can then move the rate: a hike that doesn't happen, a different tone, a revised projection.

That's why the 83% pricing is more downside risk than upside fuel. If the ECB hikes as expected on 09/10, little happens to the euro; what matters then are the staff projections and the question of a second step. If the expectation flips — because today's PMI disappoints or oil pulls back — the euro could give back one to two percent. That's the single biggest risk in my monthly range. And the lower branch in the diagram is pulling against you regardless: Brazil is a net oil exporter, so expensive oil supports the real and weighs on EUR/BRL. Both paths spring from the same source; so far, the European one is winning by a wide margin.
