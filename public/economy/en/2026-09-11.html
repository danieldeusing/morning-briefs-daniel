The Cushion That Isn't Working Today

FX in detail — where we stand and why

Cross rate: the euro is never traded directly against the real — the rate is built from two steps: euro → dollar, dollar → real. Like a connecting flight: never direct, always through a hub. The dollar is that hub, which is why your rate has two legs that can stumble independently.

That's normally your protection: if the dollar strengthens globally, EUR/USD falls — and USD/BRL rises. The two effects multiply, and a good part cancels out.

Normal dollar shock→ EUR/USD −0.2%+ USD/BRL +0.2%= EUR/BRL ≈ 0.0%

Not today. The dollar is firmer against the euro — the US producer price index came in yesterday at 5.4% y/y, up from 4.8% in July, hotter than expected, and investors are positioning ahead of today's consumer-price print: EUR/USD 1.15910 (−0.18%). By the textbook, the real should have fallen with it. It hasn't, because it has a homegrown story: USD/BRL 5.1024 (−0.06%), a three-week high.[1][2]

Today: dollar firm→ EUR/USD −0.18%+ Election poll → USD/BRL −0.06%= EUR/BRL −0.65% vs. fix

This is the most expensive setup for you: a dollar impulse without the offsetting real-side effect. The math is 5.1024 × 1.15910 = 5.9142 against yesterday's fix of 5.9528 — 0.65% fewer reais per euro, about R$193 of difference from yesterday on €5,000.

Cross-check Yesterday's ECB fix and the cross computed from the two ECB legs (5.1247 × 1.1616) sit 0.001% apart — 5.9528 versus 5.9529, no flag. Today's 0.65% is real market movement, not a measurement gap between two clocks.[3]

The Poll Carrying the Real — and Why I Think It's Thin

The trigger is easy to name: on Sep 9, Gerp published a runoff scenario with Flávio Bolsonaro at 47% against Lula at 40% — 2,400 respondents, fieldwork Sep 3–8. The market reads a Bolsonaro win as more fiscally conservative and therefore currency-supportive; the real jumped to a three-week high.[4][5]

My objection: this is a bet on a cross-tabulation, not on an election outcome. The first-round numbers look different: Quaest (Sep 2) had Lula at 37% against Flávio Bolsonaro's 29%, Futura/Apex (Sep 3) 38.7% against 33.6%. A runoff has to materialize at all first — and within it, a lead has to flip that the first round shows running the other way.[6]

Positions that all rest on the same spreadsheet leave the room through the same door. A single divergent poll before Oct 4 is enough to reverse the move — and unlike a rate or oil shock, there's no economic counterforce holding the rate up.

ECB: Your Euro Leg Is Getting Structurally Heavier [7][8]

Deposit rate: the rate the ECB pays banks for parking money with it overnight. Think of it as the savings account every bank has access to — and since no rational lender lends for less, this one rate drags every other euro rate up with it. A higher deposit rate makes the euro more attractive to investors.

Yesterday the ECB raised all three key rates by 25 basis points: deposit rate to 2.50%, main refinancing 2.65%, marginal lending 2.90% — effective Sep 16. The reasoning names the Middle East conflict as an inflation driver; inflation will stay "well above target for some time yet." Staff projections: headline inflation 3.0 / 2.5 / 2.1% for 2026/27/28, growth 0.9 / 1.4 / 1.5% — both revised up.[7]

More important than the decision itself is what the market makes of it: three more hikes through March 2027, a fourth by June. Lagarde said the return to 2%, projected for late 2027, "could take further time."[8]

Why this isn't visible in your rate yet: a rate path doesn't act on decision day, it works through capital flows over months — in the short run, every day's headlines bury it. In the medium term, it's the one factor pushing your euro leg up, and none of the real-side forecasts cited elsewhere price it in: they're all USD/BRL calls, your risk has a second leg.

Contradiction A survey of bank views lists MUFG and ING as houses that see September as the end of the cycle, against Goldman, Wells Fargo, and UniCredit who see room for further tightening. Yesterday's pricing says something else — three more hikes. I'm weighting that higher: it's fresh, some of those house views aren't.

Positioning & Brazilian Bonds: Quiet Today

Empty out of honesty, not laziness: nothing was pulled today — no BCB swap stock, no CFTC positioning in BRL futures, no DI curve (DI1F27/DI1F29), no five-year CDS, no NTN-B real yield, no EWZ flows. The budget went entirely into the dated analyst anchors. So there's no stale number here dressed up as freshly checked.

It's Friday, September eleventh, and today is one of the rare days when your exchange rate's built-in shock absorber just isn't working.

Normally your rate is pretty well insulated against dollar news. The reason is simple: euro and real are never traded directly against each other, the rate always runs through the dollar as a stopover. When the dollar gets stronger, one leg shrinks while the other grows, and the two mostly cancel out. Today the dollar has actually gotten firmer against the euro, because American producer prices came in yesterday at five point four percent year over year, much hotter than expected. The real should have fallen right along with it. It didn't.

The reason is a poll. The pollster Gerp published a runoff scenario on September ninth showing Flávio Bolsonaro at forty-seven percent, ahead of Lula's forty. The market reads that as more fiscally conservative and is buying the real, which is now sitting at a three-week high. For you, that means the derived cross this morning stands at five point nine one four two, about zero point six five percent below yesterday's European Central Bank reference fix. On five thousand euros, that's roughly a hundred ninety-three reais of difference from yesterday.

I think this real rally is thin, though. It's a bet on a cross-tabulation, not on an election outcome. The first-round polls tell a different story: Quaest had Lula at thirty-seven percent on September second, with Flávio Bolsonaro at twenty-nine. A single divergent poll could flip the whole move, and there's no economic counterforce out there to catch the rate if it does.

The much bigger story happened yesterday and hasn't even shown up in your rate yet. The European Central Bank raised all three of its key rates by twenty-five basis points, and the deposit rate will sit at two point five percent starting September sixteenth. What matters more than the decision itself is what the market is now pricing in: three more hikes through March twenty twenty-seven, and a fourth by June. Christine Lagarde said the return to target inflation could take further time. That's the one factor that's going to keep pushing your euro leg higher over the medium run.

And that points to an asymmetry worth keeping in mind. Every bank forecast I quote here is a forecast for dollar against real. The Focus survey sees five point two zero by year-end, Rabobank five point three five, BTG five point four zero. None of them factor in a stronger euro. If the central bank actually delivers its priced-in path, your real-world rate will end up above every one of those numbers.

Next Wednesday, September sixteenth, gets interesting. The Federal Reserve and Brazil's Copom decide on the same day, in opposite directions. In Washington, the market treats a hike as the more likely next move; in Brasília, a cut to thirteen point seven five percent is the consensus call. Your rate cushion could shrink by fifty basis points in a single day.

As for the numbers I'm giving you today: for today, I expect five point eight seven to five point nine seven, most likely five point nine two. For this week, five point eight six to six point zero eight. Over twelve months, I see five point eight oh to six point six oh, most likely six point two five. Those probabilities are my own judgment, not what the market is pricing in.

One timing detail that could matter today. The European Central Bank's fixing window falls around a quarter past nine Brazilian time, and US consumer prices land at nine thirty. So the rate the central bank publishes around eleven o'clock today is a pre-inflation-data rate. Whatever the numbers do won't show up until Monday's fix.
