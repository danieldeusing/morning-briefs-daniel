The Fix That Stopped Too Early

FX in detail — where we stand and why

Reference fix: every business day around 14:15 CET (09:15 BRT), the ECB polls what a currency costs and publishes one official daily rate from it. Like a class photo: a single frozen moment. The market keeps moving — the photo ages.

Friday's photo was taken early. During the fixing window, both legs pulled down and added up cleanly: EUR/USD from 1.1616 to 1.1592 (−0.21%), the ECB-consistent USD/BRL from 5.1247 to 5.1108 (−0.27%). That's −0.48% on the cross, i.e. 5.9244.[1]

EUR/USD −0.21%× USD/BRL −0.27%= EUR/BRL −0.48%

After that, something happened that the fix never caught: Supreme Court Chief Justice Edson Fachin ordered Justice André Mendonça to unseal the files in the "Master" case within 24 hours — amid concern it could expose more of the "Dark Horse" complex, which touches Senator Flávio Bolsonaro. Desks bought dollar protection into the weekend; the B3 dollar closed +0.44% at R$5.1254.[2]

Fix 09:15 BRT · 5.9244→ Supreme Court order, hedging buys→ B3 close 5.1254→ Cross 5.941
Cross-check 5.1254 × 1.1592 = 5.9414. TradingEconomics independently prints 5.94106 for later Friday trading — a 0.005% gap, two separate sources, the same number. Against the ECB fix, +0.28%: below the 0.5% flag, but not noise — on €5,000, roughly R$85.[3]

Takeaway: the rate you look up over the weekend is the pre-repricing rate. If the political premium holds, Monday's fix will open closer to 5.94 than to 5.92 — not because of new news, but because the photo is catching up.

Wednesday: Two Central Banks, One Day, Two Directions

Rate differential (carry): the gap between Brazilian and European interest rates — Selic 14.00% against the ECB deposit rate 2.50%, or 11.50 points. Like a savings account paying eleven times more than the one next door: as long as the exchange rate doesn't run away, the difference is your profit. This gap is the whole reason global money holds reais at all.

On Wednesday, Sep 16, both ends get pulled. The Fed meets Sep 15–16; the decision and projections land Wednesday, with a 25-basis-point hike to 3.75–4.00% expected. Copom decides that same evening — consensus: −25 basis points to 13.75%, minutes on Sep 22.[4][5]

Fed +25 bps+ Copom −25 bps= Carry −50 bps in one evening
Conflicting prints How firmly the hike is priced in depends on when you look: CME FedWatch itself reported roughly 90% on Sep 11, after the US CPI print. Write-ups from earlier put it at 66–69%. Hence the range instead of a single number; the CME primary page wouldn't load today.[6][7]

My take — against the obvious reading: for your EUR/BRL, the Fed is the less important of the two dates. A Fed move makes the dollar globally stronger and hits both legs in opposite directions: EUR/USD falls (costs you reais), USD/BRL rises (gives you some back). That's the built-in buffer of your cross rate — the euro and the real are never traded directly against each other, the dollar is always the hub, and a pure dollar impulse largely cancels out inside it. The Copom move has no such buffer: a Selic cut makes holding reais cheaper only, it doesn't make the euro cheaper. It acts one-sided — upward.

What the Fed really delivers isn't the decision, it's the projections — the dot plot, where each member anonymously marks where they see the policy rate heading. If that shows more tightening penciled in for 2027, it works over months, and then the buffer stops helping you: over those timeframes, capital flows shift rather than rates twitching.

The Inflation Number That Gives Copom Cover [8][9]

Cutting rates while the world's biggest central bank is hiking is risky — capital moves to wherever it gets more. Brazil got its justification on Friday: August's IPCA fell −0.32% m/m, against an expected −0.29%. That's the first monthly decline since August 2025 and the sharpest in four years; the twelve-month rate slipped to 4.22% instead of 4.26%. Four groups were in deflation: housing −1.87%, transport −0.86%, food −0.34%, communication −0.09% — not one outlier item, but breadth.

Why this matters to you: a cut against a tightening Fed is either a credibility problem or an earned move. With this number, it's earned — data-backed, not politically forced. That spares the real the risk premium an "independent" rate cut would otherwise cost. The carry still shrinks, just without a breach of trust on top.

Positioning & Brazilian Bonds: Thin, Not Empty

DI futures: Brazilian interest-rate futures contracts. Each one shows the average rate the market expects through a given date — like betting odds on where the Selic stands a year and a half out, except backed by real money and updated by the second.

The January 2027 contract (DI1F27) closed on Sep 11 at 13.585% against 13.586% at the prior day's settlement — practically unchanged, with the curve ending slightly higher after a weak start. Translated: the market is sticking with exactly one more cut through early 2027, unmoved by the IPCA decline or the Fed's expected move. That supports the Copom consensus.[18]

What's missing today — as a gap, not an estimate: no BCB swap stock, no CFTC positioning, no NTN-B real yield, no EWZ flows. For the five-year CDS (the price of default insurance on Brazilian government bonds — the nervousness thermometer), the last verified reading is 126 bps from Jul 10, 2026; the current pages are JS-rendered. Two months old means: useful as an order of magnitude, worthless as a daily signal.

It's Saturday, September twelfth. Nothing trades today and there's no new reference rate — but there's a number worth double-checking before you take it at face value.

The European Central Bank's latest reference fix sits at five point nine two four four, set Friday morning at a quarter past nine Brazilian time. That's a snapshot, not a live feed. Friday afternoon, something happened that snapshot never caught: Brazil's chief justice, Edson Fachin, ordered a colleague to unseal the files in the so-called Master case within twenty-four hours, over concern it could implicate Senator Flávio Bolsonaro. Traders bought dollar protection heading into the weekend, and Brazil's dollar closed zero point four four percent higher, at five point one two five four reais.

Run that through to your rate and you land at five point nine four one. TradingEconomics prints an independent five point nine four one oh six — basically the same number from a different source. That means the fix you'd look up this weekend is running about zero point two eight percent below the market you'd actually trade in. On five thousand euros, that's roughly eighty-five reais. If that political premium holds, expect Monday's fix to open closer to five point nine four than to five point nine two.

The bigger story is next Wednesday, September sixteenth, when the Federal Reserve and Brazil's Copom both decide — on the same day, in opposite directions. The Fed is expected to raise its rate a quarter point, to a range of three point seven five to four percent. Copom is expected to cut a quarter point, to thirteen point seven five percent. That's fifty basis points shaved off your rate cushion in one evening.

How sure the market is about that Fed hike depends on when you check. CME FedWatch itself put the odds at around ninety percent on Friday, measured after that morning's US inflation report. Earlier readings had it closer to two-thirds. So I'm giving you a range instead of a false-precision number.

Here's my contrarian take: for your euro-real rate, the Fed is actually the less important of the two meetings. A Fed hike strengthens the dollar against everything, which means it pushes your two legs in opposite directions and they largely cancel out. Copom's cut doesn't have that built-in offset — it only makes holding reais less attractive, so it pushes your rate one way, and one way only. If you're marking your calendar for Wednesday, mark the Brazilian decision.

And Brazil earned that cut. August consumer prices fell zero point three two percent from July — the first monthly drop since August of last year and the sharpest in four years. The twelve-month rate came down to four point two two percent, with four entire spending categories now in outright deflation. That makes this a data-driven cut, not a political one, which spares the real some of the risk premium an independent-looking rate move would otherwise cost.

Here's the blind spot in almost everything you'll read about the real: it's all a dollar forecast. Brazil's own analyst survey sees five point two zero reais per dollar by year-end, Itaú five point three zero, and BTG just raised its call from four point nine zero to five point four zero. None of that tells you what the euro itself is doing. On that front, Wall Street's year-end targets cluster around one point two two to one point two five dollars per euro — but those targets assume a Fed that's cutting and a European Central Bank on hold at two percent. Both assumptions died this week, in opposite directions, which means those targets are now a scenario, not a base case.

So here are my numbers. For Monday's fix, I expect five point nine zero to five point nine eight, most likely five point nine four. For this week, five point eight eight to six point zero eight. For the next thirty days, which includes Brazil's first election round on October fourth, five point eight five to six point two zero. And over twelve months, a wide range of five point eight zero to six point six zero, most likely six point one five. Those are my own judgment calls, not market-implied odds.
