The Last Session: Stocks Up, Real Steady

FX in detail · what happened Friday

Friday had two drivers: weak US jobs data at midday and Thursday evening's Datafolha poll. The two chains run different distances.

Chain 1 — US jobs to the euro.

Payrolls +29,000 instead of 90,000→ fewer bets on Fed hikes→ DXY −0.17%→ EUR/USD 1.12556 (+0.27% above the ECB fix)→ cross-rate 5.8707

The ECB fix was set at ~09:15 BRT; the payrolls data came out at 09:30 BRT. So the fix doesn't know the jobs numbers — the closing rate does. Broken down: the euro leg +0.27%, the real leg −0.11% (5.2158 instead of 5.2214), together +0.17%. That's the number the market starts from on Monday, before the election gets priced in.

Chain 2 — the same data reaching the B3.

DI futures: bets on Brazil's future interbank rate. Like a fixed-price power contract: the price reveals what everyone expects for the coming years. When the rate falls, the market expects less rate pressure — or less fiscal fear.
US rate pressure eases→ DI curve falls across the board (DI1F29 −11 bp)→ stocks cheaper to finance→ Ibovespa +2.63%⇢ USD/BRL only −0.08%

The last step is where it breaks down. When stocks and rates ease this clearly, the dollar usually gives ground too. It didn't. My read: FX investors are keeping their hedges on over the weekend — nobody wants to carry open real risk into an election. Like packing umbrellas despite the sun, because storms are forecast for the evening. That also means part of that hedge could unwind on Monday if the election passes without a shock.

The week: −0.97% from the high, almost entirely driven by the euro (EUR/USD month −3.19%, near a one-year low); the real itself lost a little ground (USD/BRL week +0.65%). The pacesetter was Washington, not Brasília.

Tomorrow: Round 1 and What Comes After

The month's most important date

Winning in the first round takes more than half of valid votes. Datafolha (Sep 28–Oct 1, 2,506 respondents, ±2) puts Lula at 45%, Flávio Bolsonaro at 40%. That makes the runoff on Oct 25 the clear base case. For the runoff, Datafolha shows Lula ahead 48 to 45, while Atlas/Bloomberg in late September had Flávio ahead 47.7 to 47.6 — a dead heat. Today's seven polls could still shift the picture; their numbers weren't available at press time. The TV Globo debate was cancelled after last-minute court rulings.

Risk premium: the markup investors demand because they aren't sure Brazil's public finances will hold up. Like a higher interest rate for a borrower with a shaky job: as worry rises, so does the markup — and foreign money pulls out, the real falls.

The banks agree: it's not the name but the fiscal path afterward that moves the real. Goldman (Oct 1) says so explicitly. The scenarios, converted at EUR/USD 1.1256 (own calculation):

For you it works in reverse: a result that looks like a strict budget strengthens the real and lowers your rate. A result that looks like more spending raises it. Morgan Stanley's base case (5.25) is deliberately a 50/50 blend of the two paths — not a level the market would linger at for long. The bank expects "premium in Q3, moderate recovery in Q4."

How big could the swing get? Options prices imply about 6.5% of movement across both rounds, per JPMorgan. On €1,000, that's roughly R$380. The first round alone would likely trigger only part of that, since it just postpones the decision.

Where I Disagree With the Consensus

Commerzbank looks too optimistic on the real to me

Commerzbank (Aug 19) expects the real to "keep appreciating" after the election — supported by real interest rates around 14%, to USD/BRL 4.80 by end-2027. The argument holds up as long as rates stay high. But Focus sees the Selic falling to 12.00% by end-2027, and Itaú expects a rising debt ratio. Less rate advantage plus more debt isn't a recipe for a durably stronger real.

For your rate, the euro leg adds to that. This week it explained more than the entire EUR/BRL move. An election result that's "good" for you could get swallowed by a further-falling euro — and a softer Fed could lift the euro even if the real firms up. I could be wrong: a single weak jobs report has misled before.

Positioning & Brazilian Risk

Credit eases, FX markets stay hedged

CDS (Credit Default Swap): insurance against a sovereign default; 131 basis points means a 1.31% annual premium. Like homeowner's insurance that gets pricier when break-ins become more common on the street — it measures nervousness, not the default itself.
  • 5-year CDS 131.04 bp (close Oct 2), −0.63%; down from 131.88 on Oct 1 and up from 121.12 on Sep 21. Election jitters are priced in but not growing further. 52-week range 110.62–159.67.
  • DI futures (close Oct 2): DI1F27 13.51% (−4.9 bp), DI1F29 13.69% (−11 bp). The longer maturity fell more — that points more toward less fiscal markup than toward just different rate expectations.
  • Ibovespa 192,114.55 (+2.63%), week +4.71% after two red weeks. On Sep 28, the exchange's volatility index had hit a record of 31.84.
  • BCB swaps: the roll of the November maturities begins Mon, Oct 5 — the central bank is extending existing hedges; no new net swaps were reported.

My read: credit and the rate curve say "no fiscal shock expected." The FX market says "but I'm still paying for the insurance anyway." That gap is the room for Monday to move.

Practical Notes for Converting

Understanding, not a recommendation

There's no market rate over the weekend, only the frozen Friday level. The first rate that knows the result comes early Monday. Anyone converting from Monday on is converting into a world where round 1 is known but the runoff is still open. The full swing (roughly ±R$380 per €1,000 across both rounds) is more likely to land around Oct 26 than Oct 5. What you do with that is your call.

It's Saturday, October third. Tomorrow Brazil votes for president.
