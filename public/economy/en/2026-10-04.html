The Final Polls: Tighter Than Thursday

What's new since yesterday · as of Saturday evening

Saturday brought the last polls before the election. Yesterday their numbers weren't in yet at press time; now they are. Datafolha and Quaest point the same direction: Flávio Bolsonaro is gaining ground.

Valid votes: all votes minus blank and invalid ballots. Only these count toward the 50% threshold. Like a classroom election where blank ballots just get tossed out — whoever has more than half of what's left wins outright.

For comparison: Datafolha had Thursday's numbers at 45 to 40 in round 1 and 48 to 45 in the runoff. In Quaest, Flávio rose among all respondents from 34 to 38%, while Lula climbed only from 39 to 40%. The smaller candidates — Caiado (4%), Cury and Renan Santos (3% each, Datafolha) — together are enough to keep Lula under 50%.

Margin of error (±2): a poll asks only a few thousand people, not everyone. So the true value can be off by around two points in either direction. Like sampling a bag of candy: ten pieces tell you roughly how many are red, but not exactly. When two candidates fall within this range, pollsters call it a "technical tie."

What follows: a decision in the first round is very unlikely, and the runoff on Oct 25 is the expected outcome. And that runoff is wide open — all four polls put it within the margin of error. Atlas had Lula narrowly behind in late September, now narrowly ahead. That's noise, not a trend.

How Election Night Could Move Your Rate

FX in detail · the causal chain

Nothing in the market has moved since Friday; only the polls are new. The banks agree: it's not the name that matters, but the fiscal path the market reads into the result. Goldman says so explicitly on Oct 1. That produces two chains.

Risk premium: the markup investors demand because they aren't sure Brazil's public finances will hold up. Like a higher interest rate for a borrower with a shaky job: as worry rises, so does the markup — and foreign money pulls out, the real falls.

Chain 1 — Flávio strong.

Flávio tied or ahead→ market expects a stricter budget→ risk premium falls→ USD/BRL falls→ EUR/BRL falls (bad for you)

Chain 2 — Lula strong.

Lula clearly ahead or above 50%→ market expects continued high spending→ risk premium rises→ USD/BRL rises→ EUR/BRL rises (good for you)

For you, it's mirrored: what the market celebrates as "good for Brazil" lowers your rate. Here's my reading guide for tonight. The USD/BRL ranges are my own estimate, not a bank forecast; converted at Friday's euro rate of 1.1256.

Where I Disagree With the Consensus

A Flávio result helps the real less than many think

The convenient reading is: Flávio is gaining, so the real should rally sharply on Monday. I think the effect will be smaller — and the risk unevenly distributed. Goldman wrote on Sep 23 that investors are positioned in the real about as heavily as in 2022. Back then, the dollar jumped 4.5% after the runoff.

Positioning: how many investors have already bet on one direction. Like a boat where everyone's already sitting on one side: it can barely tilt further that way, but a wave from the other side rocks it hard.

Anyone who already holds a lot of real buys only a little more on good news. On bad news, though, everyone sells at once. And the stock market already celebrated some of that hope on Friday (+2.63%). For your rate, that means: the way down is likely short, while the way up can get steep if Lula outperforms the polls. Goldman itself stresses that concrete policy moves markets more than the result does. I could be wrong: if Flávio is clearly ahead, the relief rally could carry on past Monday too.

Practical Notes for Converting

Understanding, not a recommendation

Anyone converting from Monday on is converting into a world where round 1 is known but the runoff is still open. The bigger swing is more likely to land around Oct 26 than on Oct 5. By my read, the risk to your rate is steeper on the upside than the downside. What you do with that is your call.

It's Sunday, October fourth, and Brazil is voting for president today. Polling stations are open from eight in the morning until five in the afternoon, Brasília time, and the electoral court won't publish results until after that — so there's still no result as this brief goes out. Your rate stays frozen at Friday's European Central Bank reference rate until Monday: five point eight six one zero. And that runoff itself is wide open — all four major polls put it within the margin of error.

Here's what that means for you. The market reads a strong Flávio result as a signal for a stricter budget, which strengthens the real and lowers your rate. A strong Lula result does the opposite. If Flávio is tied or ahead tonight, I'd expect Monday's rate around five point seven four to five point eight three. If Lula is narrowly ahead, as the polls suggest, more likely five point eight three to five point nine two.
