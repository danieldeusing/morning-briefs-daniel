[BR] Round 1 · as of Monday morning, 99.96–99.99% counted

Runoff arithmetic: only two names remain in round 2. The eliminated candidates' votes have to redistribute. Like a class election where the third- and fourth-place candidates drop out — their fans decide who becomes class president.

Let's run the numbers: besides the top two, 7.81% of the valid vote remains. Lula is 4.85 points short of 50% — so he needs a good six in ten of those votes. Flávio needs just under four in ten. That's a simplification: yesterday's non-voters could also show up on Oct 25. But the direction is clear. Flávio is now the favorite, not a certain winner.

Then there's Congress. The PL leads with 19 of 54 Senate seats, MDB with 7, PT with 5. The Senate confirms Supreme Court justices and the central bank's directors. The market reads the rightward shift as a chance for a budget reform to pass starting in 2027.

Where We Stand & Why: Two Forces, One Direction

FX in detail · the causal chain

Your EUR/BRL rate is the product of two rates: EUR/USD × USD/BRL. Today both are moving against you. That's rare.

Chain 1 — the election (USD/BRL).

Flávio beats the polls→ market expects a stricter budget→ risk premium falls→ money flows into Brazil, USD/BRL falls→ EUR/BRL falls
Risk premium: the markup investors demand because they aren't sure Brazil's public finances will hold up. Like a higher interest rate for a borrower with a shaky job: as the job gets more secure, the markup falls — and more money flows into the country.

How big is the jump? The EWZ, a New York-traded fund holding Brazilian stocks, rose 8.72% Sunday evening to $41.52. Because the EWZ is priced in dollars, it captures both effects at once: higher stock prices and a stronger real. In 2022 — when Jair Bolsonaro beat the polls — the dollar fell 4.09% on the Monday to R$5.174, and the stock market rose 5.54%. Together, that's roughly today's EWZ move. Bloomberg calls the result "far better" than investors were positioned for. ASA, AZ Quest, Manchester, and Nomos expect a stronger real and lower interest rates.

Chain 2 — the euro (EUR/USD).

Spain's government loses a vote→ snap-election rumors + France's budget worries→ investors pull out of the euro→ EUR/USD −0.63% to 1.1185→ EUR/BRL −0.6%

On Friday, the Spanish parliament rejected two of the government's housing decrees (178 to 172 and 184 to 166). According to TradingEconomics, government and party circles are now backing an early election. On top of that come the familiar worries about France's budget. Rabobank, per a press report, cut its 12-month EUR/USD forecast from 1.18 to 1.12 — the original source wasn't accessible, so take this with caution.

Put together: if USD/BRL falls 4.09% as in 2022 and the euro stays at 1.1185, your rate lands around 5.60 — about 4.5% below Friday's fix. That's a calculation, not a forecast. My forecast sits to the right and runs a bit higher, for one reason: positioning.

Where I Disagree With the Consensus

Monday is loud, the runoff is quieter — and the boat is full

The mood is euphoric: Nomos talks about 230,000 Ibovespa points if Flávio stays ahead through the runoff. I think the first jump is justified; the follow-through is less certain. Three reasons.

Positioning: how many investors have already bet on one direction. Like a boat where everyone's already sitting on one side: it can barely tilt further that way, but a wave from the other side rocks it hard.
  • The boat is full. Fund managers kept adding real bets through Sep 29 (details on the right). Goldman already called the positioning comparable to 2022 back on Sep 23.
  • Round 1 isn't a win. 47% isn't 50%, and polls can also be wrong in the other direction.
  • The market is buying hope. Goldman stresses that post-election policy matters more than the result. Nomos calls the inflows "speculative."

I could be wrong: if Flávio leads clearly in the first runoff polls, the rally could carry through to the election, and the floor would sit closer to 5.50 than 5.60.

Practical Notes for Converting

Understanding, not a recommendation

The event most likely to push your rate down has happened, and the euro is weakening on top of it. Anyone converting today converts close to the first move. Part of that could come back by Oct 25; a clear Flávio win could also push the rate lower still. Waiting buys information, and spreading conversions across several dates spreads the risk. The decision is yours.

It's Monday, October fifth, and Brazil woke up to a surprise. Flávio Bolsonaro won the first round of the presidential election with about forty-seven percent of the valid vote. Lula came in just over forty-five. Every last poll had shown Lula ahead. The runoff is set for October twenty-fifth.

The market is celebrating. The Brazil fund EWZ jumped almost nine percent in New York on Sunday evening. Investors are reading the result as a signal for a stricter budget. That makes the real stronger. For you, that's bad news: your rate falls when the real rises.

On top of that comes the euro. It fell early Monday to one point one one eight five dollars. The reason is talk of a snap election in Spain and worries about France's budget. That's pulling your rate down too.

As this brief goes out, there's still no new reference rate from the European Central Bank. Friday's, the last one, stands at five point eight six one. Back in twenty twenty-two, the dollar fell by a bit over four percent on the Monday after a similar surprise. The same move today, with the weak euro, would put the rate around five point six zero.

Here's my own call, and the probabilities throughout are my confidence, not a market number. For today's fix: five point five five to five point seven eight, most likely five point six four. At a thousand euros, that's roughly two hundred twenty reais less than Friday. For this week: five point five two to five point eight zero. Over thirty days: five point four zero to six point one zero, with two peaks depending on the runoff. Over three to six months, most likely five point seven zero. Over twelve months, most likely five point eight zero.

Where I disagree: the mood is euphoric, but the boat is full. Per the US regulator CFTC, big asset managers already held very large bets on the real before the election. When you already hold a lot, you buy little more. And the first round isn't a win — Lula needs a good six in ten votes from the eliminated candidates. So I think it's possible that part of today's drop comes back before the runoff.
