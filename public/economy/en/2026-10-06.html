Where we stand & why: one day, one jump

FX in detail · the causal path · Update since Mon

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL. From Friday to Monday it fell 4.71%. Breaking that down on an ECB basis, almost all of it comes from Brazil: USD/BRL −4.53% (5.2214 → 4.9847), EUR/USD only −0.19% (1.1225 → 1.1204). The euro had already fallen on Thursday and Friday.

Chain 1 — the election.

Flávio 47.03%, Lula 45.16%→ Market expects a stricter budget→ Risk premium falls: CDS −12 bp, DI1F29 −104 bp→ Money flows into Brazil, USD/BRL −4.5%→ EUR/BRL −4.7%
Risk premium: the extra return investors demand because they're not sure Brazil's public finances will hold up. Like a higher interest rate for a borrower with a shaky job: if the job gets safer, the extra charge drops — and more money flows into the country.

How much of this was expected? An XP survey of 116 professional investors had predicted that a Flávio lead would send the dollar about 4% lower, stocks +6%, and rates down at least 40 bp.[1] So the dollar moved exactly as expected. Stocks (+7.7%) and long rates (−104 bp) overshot that. André Matos of MA7 Capital pushes back: the dollar went from 5.22 to below 5.00 in a single day, "but nothing in the real economy changed between Friday and Monday."[2]

Chain 2 — the euro.

Sánchez reportedly called snap elections→ plus France's budget worries→ EUR/USD briefly ~1.117, lowest since May 2025→ EUR/BRL pressured down

Two outlets report that Sánchez has called a snap election, but no election date is confirmed. Nov 29 would be the earliest possible date.[3] Per a press report, Rabobank cut its 12-month EUR/USD forecast from 1.18 to 1.12 (snippet only).

Looking back at yesterday: my 5.55–5.78 range held, but the fix landed at the lower end, not at my 5.64 call. The reason: the fix fell within the first trading hour, when euphoria peaked. USD/BRL then closed at 5.001 — above the fix value of 4.9847. That's the first small pullback.

The runoff: a favorite, not a winner yet

[BR] Sun Oct 25 · no poll yet after round 1

Final round-1 result (TSE): Flávio 47.03% (56.1 million votes), Lula 45.16% (53.9 million). Behind them: Cury 2.89%, Renan Santos 2.24%, Caiado 2.18%. In a letter, Cury congratulated both and forbade anyone from announcing an endorsement in his name.

Prediction market: an exchange where people bet on events. A price of 83 cents for "Flávio wins" means bettors see that as roughly 83% likely. Like horse-racing odds — they show what bettors think, not the truth.

Polymarket has Flávio at 83.5% ($174 million traded), Kalshi at 84%. On election morning it was still 63%. JPMorgan writes: "the election is Flávio's to lose." Goldman expects a polarized runoff with a "slight edge" for Flávio.

My view: I put Flávio at around 75%, below the markets. The last Datafolha poll still had Lula ahead (42 to 40). But the fact that the polls missed doesn't tell us how the 7.8% of eliminated candidates' voters will swing.

My dissent: the risk is now skewed

The best case is priced in — the worst case isn't

The consensus is celebrating: UBS sees over 30% upside for stocks, JPMorgan 22% for MSCI Brazil. That may be right for equities. For your exchange rate, something else matters: the Flávio case is already almost fully priced into the rate.

  • The target has been hit. BTG saw ~5.00 for a Flávio win, and so did JPMorgan. The dollar is at 5.00. A win on Oct 25 brings little new information.
  • The alternative case is large. A Lula win would mean 5.70–6.00 per BTG — for you, EUR/BRL above 6.39. Even at only 25% probability, that's the bigger swing.
  • The market is buying hope. Goldman stresses that post-election policy matters more than the result itself. JPMorgan's own scenario: on fiscal disappointment, the real loses about 6%.

I could be wrong: if the first polls show Flávio clearly ahead and he quickly names a market-friendly finance minister, it could head toward Goldman's 4.80 — putting 5.40 in reach.

Practical for your exchange

For understanding, not a recommendation

The rate already reflects almost the best-case runoff outcome. If you don't have to exchange now, you might wait for a pullback or for the runoff; the risk: a clear Flávio win with fast reforms could push the rate toward 5.40. If you do have to exchange, you could spread smaller amounts across several dates. The decision is yours.

It's Tuesday, October sixth. The European Central Bank's first reference rate since Brazil's election is in, and it's a rough one. The euro cost five point five eight four nine reais on Monday. On Friday it was five point eight six one. That's a drop of almost five percent in a single day.

Here's what that means for you: on one thousand euros, you'd get about two hundred seventy-six fewer reais than Friday. The real is at its strongest in seventeen months. The dollar closed at five reais, and São Paulo's stock market jumped nearly eight percent to a record high.

The reason is the election. Flávio Bolsonaro narrowly led Lula in the first round. Investors are betting on a stricter budget, so long-term interest rates dropped more than a full percentage point, and the cost of insuring against a Brazilian default got cheaper. The euro barely helped — it had already weakened on worries about a possible snap election in Spain.

The runoff is October twenty-fifth. Betting markets give Flávio about eighty-three percent odds. I'm putting him closer to seventy-five, since there's no fresh polling yet.

My core call: the Flávio win is already almost fully priced into the exchange rate. JPMorgan and BTG both saw the dollar around five reais if he wins, and that's exactly where it is. A Lula win, though, could push the euro above six point three reais, according to BTG. So the risk here is skewed — and skewed in your favor.

These probabilities are my own confidence levels, not a market price. For today's fix, I expect five point five four to five point six eight, most likely five point six one.
