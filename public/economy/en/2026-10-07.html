Where we stand & why: two gears

FX in detail · the causal path · Update since Mon

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears turning independently. On Monday, almost only the Brazilian gear turned (USD/BRL −4.53%). On Tuesday both turned, but against each other: EUR/USD +0.58% (1.1204 → 1.1269), USD/BRL −0.32% (4.9847 → 4.9686, ECB basis). Net result: EUR/BRL +0.25% to 5.5991.

Chain 1 — the euro lifted the fix.

Weak US data (PCE, jobs)→ Fed officials less hawkish, ~80% pause odds for Oct 28→ DXY −0.22%→ EUR/USD +0.58%→ EUR/BRL +0.25%

This morning the euro is already back at 1.1231 (−0.25%), near its low since May 2025 — per TradingEconomics, on worries about France's budget. Spain votes on Nov 29, Italy next year.[1]

Chain 2 — the real strengthened further.

Market believes in a stricter budget→ Risk premium keeps falling: CDS −9.9 bp, DI1F29 −20.5 bp→ USD/BRL below 5→ EUR/BRL pressured down
Risk premium: the extra return investors demand because they're not sure Brazil's public finances will hold up. Like a higher interest rate for a borrower with a shaky job: if the job gets safer, the extra charge drops — and more money flows into the country.

Notably, stocks took profits (Ibovespa −0.52%), the real didn't. InfoMoney quotes an analyst calling it just "a correction toward the averages," with the trend still pointing up.[2]

Looking back at yesterday: the 5.54–5.68 range held, the 5.61 point estimate was 0.2% too high. The direction was right, the reason wasn't: I expected profit-taking in the real, but it was the euro that lifted the rate. Lesson: after the election jump, the euro gear matters more.

My dissent: the euro is the underrated gear

Everyone's watching Brasília — your rate now also hinges on Paris

For your rate, the runoff is only half the equation. The real has largely priced in the Flávio case: JPMorgan saw ~5.00, the dollar is at 4.98. The open variable is the euro.

  • Euro down, targets up. EUR/USD is at 1.12 — near its low since May 2025. BofA expects 1.15 by year-end, TradingEconomics 1.13 in Q4. Every cent the euro recovers lifts your rate by roughly 0.05, real held constant.
  • The euro has support. The ECB already hiked in June and September. In late September, futures priced about 60% odds of another hike on Oct 29 (as of Sep 24, search-summary only). Higher rates pull money into the euro.
  • The counter-risk is political. France's budget today, Spain's election on Nov 29. If both stay noisy, the euro stays weak.

I could be wrong: if US yields keep rising (the 10-year is already at 5.316%, near a 24-year high), that strengthens the dollar against both the euro and the real. Then your rate stays stuck around 5.60.
