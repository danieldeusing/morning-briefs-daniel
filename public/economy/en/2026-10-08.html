Where we stand & why: both gears against the dollar

FX in detail · the causal path · Update since Wed

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. On Wednesday both turned in the same direction: the dollar got stronger against everything. EUR/USD fell 0.82% (1.1269 → 1.1177), USD/BRL rose 0.75% (4.9686 → 5.0061, ECB basis). A weaker euro means your rate falls. A weaker real means your rate rises. Net result: EUR/BRL −0.07% to 5.5953.

Chain 1 — the dollar gets stronger against everyone.

Fed minutes: "most participants" see another hike by year-end→ US yields stay high (10-year 5.31%)→ DXY above 102→ EUR/USD −0.82%, USD/BRL +0.75%→ EUR/BRL −0.07%
Fed minutes: the transcript of the US central bank's last rate meeting, published three weeks later. It shows how the members really think. Like the minutes of a parents' meeting: you already know the decision, but only here do you read who is in favor of the next rule.

The Fed raised its policy rate to 3.75–4.00% on 09/16. The minutes now say: most members consider another hike "by year end" likely.[1] ING puts it in perspective: the market already prices a December hike firmly, so the bar for another dollar jump is high.[2]

Chain 2 — the euro carries its own burden.

Doubts about the French budget→ France–Germany spread 152 bp→ ECB can hike less (March: 80 → 45 bp priced in)→ Euro at 1.116–1.12
Spread: the yield gap between two government bonds, here France versus Germany. The bigger it is, the more investors fear France. Like two neighbors who want a loan: the one with the shakier household books pays more interest.

France's 10-year yield stood at 4.89% on 10/07, and the gap to Germany at 152 bp on 10/05 — the largest since 2011.[3] According to ING, this holds the ECB back: only 45 bp of tightening is still priced in through March, versus 80 bp on 09/24. ING sees "Risks … firmly on the downside for EUR/USD" and room down to 1.110 or 1.100 if bond stress grows.[2]

Chain 3 — Brazil's own risk stayed calm. The 5-year CDS dipped slightly to 108.81 bp, the DI1F29 by 1.0 bp. Had the real fallen because of Brazil, these numbers would have risen. So the cause was the global dollar. There is also caution: according to InfoMoney, Flávio is the favorite, but the market knows little about his budget plans.[4]

Correction with caveat Yesterday I wrote that France would present its 2027 budget "today" (10/07). That was probably wrong. TradingEconomics reported the formal submission for 10/06, and a search summary puts the presentation as early as 10/01 (deficit target 5.0% of GDP for 2027).[3] I have not checked a primary source.

Looking back at yesterday: range 5.54–5.66 held, point forecast 5.59 was 0.1% too low. The euro risk came as named; I overlooked that the same dollar also weakens the real.

My dissent: two skewed gears

The consensus says "election decided" — I see risks on both sides

  • The real gear is skewed upward (good for you). The Flávio case is priced in (JPMorgan ~5.00, dollar at 5.02), a Lula comeback is not. With my 75% instead of 84%, the risk of a weaker real is bigger than the price says.
  • The euro gear is skewed downward (bad for you). French bonds under stress, falling ECB expectations, ING sees 1.110 or 1.100. The banks' targets of 1.15 need a turnaround that I do not yet see.

So in the short term I see a sideways zone around 5.60, with two possible breakouts: upward via the runoff, downward via Paris. I may be wrong: if Flávio quickly delivers a clear budget plan, the real's edge disappears — then the rate heads toward 5.40.

It's Thursday, October eighth. The European Central Bank's reference rate on Wednesday came in at five point five nine five three reais per euro. That's almost exactly where it was on Tuesday. But compared with the Friday before the election, you get about two hundred sixty-six fewer reais on a thousand euros.

Behind the calm rate was a noisy day. Minutes from the US Federal Reserve showed that most members expect one more rate hike by the end of the year. The dollar strengthened against nearly everything. The euro dropped by a little over eight tenths of a percent, and the real fell almost as much. Because your rate is the product of the two, the losses nearly canceled out.

What matters for Brazil: its own risk gauges stayed quiet. Insurance against a Brazilian default actually got slightly cheaper, and longer-term rates edged down. So the real fell because of the dollar, not because of Brazil. The dollar closed in São Paulo at five point zero one one reais, just back above five.

The euro has a burden of its own. French government bonds are under pressure, and the gap to Germany is the widest since twenty eleven. ING sees room down to one point one one zero, or even one point one zero zero. One correction: yesterday I wrote that France would present its budget on Wednesday. That was probably wrong, since it came out earlier.

For the October twenty-fifth runoff, there is still no new poll. TV campaigning starts Friday. The first debate has moved to Sunday, October eighteenth. Prediction markets give Flávio Bolsonaro about eighty-four percent. I'm staying at about seventy-five.
