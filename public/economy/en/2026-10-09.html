Where we stand & why: caution ahead of the poll

FX in detail · the causal path · Update since Thu

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. On Wednesday they turned against each other, on Thursday almost only one moved. The euro stayed flat (+0.08%), the real lost 0.21% (ECB basis). Result: EUR/BRL +0.29% to 5.6118.

Chain 1 — the real waits for the poll.

Datafolha only expected in the evening→ Traders hedge with dollars→ USD/BRL ~5.02, second rise in a row→ EUR/BRL +0.29%

BPMoney described the close like this: "Dólar fecha acima de R$ 5,02 à espera de pesquisa Datafolha pós-primeiro turno".[1] The contradiction in the day's picture is interesting. Oil rose 4%, which normally helps Brazil as an exporter. Long DI rates fell by up to 20.5 bp, the stock market rose. According to Manchester Investimentos, the rates market was already pricing a Flávio government with a credible fiscal rule.[1] Only the FX market stayed cautious. That suggests the real catches up today.

Chain 2 — the poll after the close.

Datafolha: Flávio 52 : 48→ less fear of a fiscal relapse→ smaller risk premium on the real→ USD/BRL lower→ EUR/BRL lower (expected, not observed)
Risk premium: the surcharge investors demand for an uncertain country. Like a tenant with unclear income: the landlord asks for a bigger deposit. When the income becomes clearer, the deposit shrinks — and the real gets stronger.

Important: the market has largely priced in Flávio already (Polymarket 84% on Oct 5, 85.75% today). So the poll confirms more than it surprises. That is why I expect a move of tenths, not a jump like on Oct 5. Overnight USD/BRL stood at 5.0176, only 0.11% lower.[2]

Chain 3 — inflation today at 09:00 BRT.

End of the Itaipu bonus→ IPCA September 0.62–0.82%→ 12 months above 4.50%→ Central bank cuts more slowly→ Carry stays high → real supported
IPCA & Itaipu bonus: the IPCA is Brazil's official inflation rate. In August there was a one-time discount on the electricity bill from the Itaipu power plant, so the index fell 0.32%. In September the discount ends. Like a supermarket that ran a discount for a week: afterward the normal price feels like a price jump.

The estimates: XP and ASA 0.75%, JPMorgan 0.81%, C6 0.82%, Inter 0.62%. Over 12 months XP expects about 4.50%, C6 4.58% — after 4.22% in August.[3] If the figure is above 0.80%, the pressure on the central bank to cut slowly rises. That supports the real through the interest rate. XP still expects cuts from November, in steps of 0.25 points.[4]

Yesterday in review: range 5.57–5.67 held, point 5.62 was 0.15% too high. The euro stayed calmer than feared, and the real slipped further after fix time.

The runoff: Flávio turns the polls

[BR] Sun Oct 25 · the first numbers after round 1

Datafolha (Oct 6–8, 2,520 respondents, ±2 points): Flávio 52%, Lula 48% of valid votes. Among all votes 49 to 45 — Valor calls it an "empate técnico", a statistical tie. PoderData (Oct 5–7, 3,000 interviews, ±1.8 points): 53 to 47.[5] Before round 1 Lula still led Datafolha 47 to 46. So the picture has flipped.

Valid votes: all votes without blank and spoiled ballots. Brazil counts the official result this way. That is why the gap looks bigger there — like a class election where you don't count the abstentions.

Polymarket gives Flávio 85.75%. The margin market most likely sees a Flávio win by 4–8 points (47%).[6] My view rises from 75% to about 80%. Two pollsters show the same, and round 1 already went his way. I stay below the betting market because the gap among all votes is within the margin of error and the debate on Oct 18 is still ahead.[7]

My dissent: the market is buying the fiscal promise too early

The consensus says "Flávio = discipline" — I see two open questions

  • What Flávio promised costs money. According to Folha (via InfoMoney), his team is examining cuts of about 2% of GDP. At the same time he promises pensions above inflation. Together that is not yet a plan. Yet the rates market already prices in the plan (DI1F35 −20.5 bp in one day).
  • The CDS believes it less. The insurance against a Brazilian default got more expensive on Thursday (+1.49 to 110.30 bp) while DI rates fell. A small signal, but a contradiction.

That is why I see JPMorgan's 4.70–4.80 as a target for the most favorable case, not as the base. I may be wrong: if Flávio names a credible finance minister before Oct 25, the real can run there quickly — then your rate heads toward 5.30.

Practical for the exchange

Understanding, not a recommendation

The situation has turned. Until yesterday, waiting was a bet on both sides: a Lula surprise would have lifted your rate toward 6. After Datafolha that edge has become smaller, and JPMorgan openly bets on a firmer real. The risk of waiting now lies more on the downside (toward 5.30–5.50). The counterweight remains the euro: if it recovers to 1.13, that offsets part of it. Whoever has to exchange spreads smaller amounts over several dates until the runoff. The decision is yours.

It's Friday, October ninth. Thursday's European Central Bank reference rate stood at five point six one one eight reais per euro, about three tenths of a percent higher than Wednesday. Compared with the Friday before the election, you still get roughly two hundred forty-nine reais less per thousand euros.

The euro barely moved. The real gave up a little, and the dollar closed in São Paulo at around five point oh two. Traders waited for the first poll since the first round and hedged. Long-term interest rates in Brazil, on the other hand, fell sharply, and the stock market rose almost one percent.

The poll arrived in the evening, after the market close. According to Datafolha, Flávio Bolsonaro has fifty-two percent of valid votes and Lula forty-eight. The PoderData survey has it at fifty-three to forty-seven. Before the election, Lula still held a narrow lead. The betting market Polymarket now gives Flávio almost eighty-six percent. I see about eighty.

Today at nine a.m. Brasília time, September inflation comes out. Banks expect between point six and point eight percent for the month. Over twelve months, the rate is likely to reach or exceed the upper bound of four point five percent. The reason is the end of an electricity discount from the Itaipu power plant. Higher inflation means the central bank cuts more slowly, and that supports the real.

Globally, the dollar stays firm. Fed Governor Waller says more rate hikes are needed. Oil jumped above one hundred four dollars after attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. JPMorgan is now openly betting on a stronger real and sees the dollar at four point seven to four point eight reais in the favorable case.

The probabilities are my own confidence, not a market figure. For today's fix, I expect five point five six to five point six six, most likely five point six oh. For the next seven days, five point four eight to five point seven two. Over thirty days, five point three oh to five point nine five, depending on the runoff. For three to six months, most likely five point six five, and for twelve months, most likely five point eight oh.
