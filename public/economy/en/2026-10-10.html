Where we stand & why: the polls beat inflation

FX in detail · the causal path · Update since Fri

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. At the time of Friday's fix almost everything stood still: euro +0.18%, real +0.24% (ECB basis), result EUR/BRL −0.06% at 5.6086. The real move came afterward.

Chain 1 — the polls prevail.

Datafolha, PoderData, AtlasIntel: Flávio ahead→ less fear of a fiscal relapse→ foreigners buy reais and stocks→ USD/BRL 4.985 (−0.78%)→ EUR/BRL ~5.58 in the evening

On Friday AtlasIntel joined in: Flávio 52.8%, Lula 47.2% of valid votes (Oct 3–8, 5,026 respondents, ±1 point). That puts three pollsters on the same picture.[1] The dollar closed at R$ 4.985, and the stock market rose 1.38% to a record.[2] Over the week the dollar lost 4.44% — about 23 centavos in five days.

Chain 2 — inflation surprises, but supports the real.

IPCA 0.82% instead of ~0.76%→ 12 months 4.58% > ceiling→ Selic cuts slower or none at all→ interest-rate advantage stays high longer→ real supported
Core inflation: inflation without the prices that swing wildly (electricity, food). Like body weight without the water you just drank — it shows the real trend. When it rises (here 0.23 → 0.36%), inflation is not just an electricity effect.

Without electricity the IPCA would have been 0.52% according to IBGE (Valor headline) — so a large part is the end of the Itaipu discount. But core inflation also rose.[3] The odd part: long DI rates fell anyway, DI1F35 by 20.5 bp. The polls outweighed inflation.[2]

Chain 3 — why the fix misleads you.

ECB fix 09:15 BRT: 5.6086→ PTAX 13:07 BRT: 5.5844→ Close ~17:00 BRT: ~5.581

The fix is a snapshot from the morning. Whoever exchanges on Monday starts from ~5.58, not 5.61. On Monday B3 is closed for the holiday; the ECB fix still comes, but the real then trades only thinly abroad.

Look back: daily range 5.56–5.66 held, point estimate 5.60 was 0.15% too low. The euro did not rise further, and the real reacted only after the fix.

My dissent: the discount is already in the price

The consensus says "dollar toward 4.50" — I see it getting there only later

  • The market is running ahead of the result. USD/BRL 4.985 already sits on BTG's target for a Flávio win and only 1.7% above JPMorgan's old Flávio target of 4.90. Further gains need more than a win: they need a plan.
  • Morgan Stanley and UBS say so themselves. Both tie 4.50 to a "plano fiscal mais credível". That does not exist yet.
  • The funds have already sold. The CFTC data show that speculators cut their real bets in election week (net −8,275 contracts).

That is why I see 4.50 as a target for 2027, not for October. I may be wrong: if Flávio names a credible finance minister before Oct 25, the real can keep running quickly — then your rate points toward 5.30.

Practical for the exchange

Understanding, not a recommendation

Your rate lost about 5% in one week — roughly R$ 280 per €1,000. The cause is political, not a euro problem: the euro barely moved. The risk of waiting remains tilted downward, because the banks point toward 4.50–4.80. At the same time much is already priced in, and a Lula comeback remains a small but costly tail on the upside. Whoever has to exchange spreads smaller amounts over several dates until the runoff. The decision is yours.

It's Saturday, October tenth. Markets are closed. The European Central Bank's last reference rate, from Friday, was five point six zero eight six reais per euro. But that number is already out of date: it was taken in the morning, before the dollar dropped in São Paulo.

On Friday the dollar closed at four point nine eight five reais, below the five-real mark. Over the whole week it lost more than four percent. Using closing prices, your euro stood at about five point five eight in the evening. On a thousand euros, that's roughly two hundred eighty reais less than a week ago.

The reason is the polls. Datafolha, PoderData and AtlasIntel have Flávio Bolsonaro at fifty-two to fifty-three percent of valid votes. Only Vox Brasil has Lula narrowly ahead. The prediction market Polymarket gives Flávio almost eighty-nine percent; I see about eighty-two.

September inflation came in at zero point eight two percent, above expectations. Over twelve months it's four point five eight percent, above the ceiling of the target band. That slows the central bank's rate cuts. For the real, that's rather good news, because high interest rates attract foreign money.

Globally, the dollar stays firm; the dollar index rose for the fourth week in a row. The euro recovered slightly from a seventeen-month low. According to Morgan Stanley and UBS, the dollar could fall to four point five in Brazil, but only with a credible budget plan.

The probabilities are my own confidence, not a market figure. For Monday's fix I expect five point five three to five point six three, most likely five point five eight. For the next seven days, five point four two to five point six eight. Over thirty days, five point two five to five point nine zero, depending on the runoff. For three to six months, most likely five point five five, and for twelve months, most likely five point seven five.
