Where we stand & why: Monday without São Paulo

FX in detail · the causal path · Monday = fresh start

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. The real gear has stood still since Friday evening. The euro gear is turning slightly this morning in Asia and Europe. Three chains explain where Monday lands.

Chain 1 — the starting point is below the last fix.

ECB fix Fri 09:15 BRT: 5.6086→ Dollar falls to 4.985 by the close→ Closing cross rate ~5.581→ Euro today 1.1206 → ~5.586

The fix is a snapshot taken in the morning. After it, the dollar fell to R$ 4.985, and the week brought −4.44%.[1] Since the Oct 2 fix (5.8610) your euro has lost 4.31%: about R$ 252 less per €1,000.[2]

Chain 2 — a triple holiday makes the fix random.

Holiday in Brazil + US + Japan→ Real traded only abroad→ low volume→ Fix with random noise
Thin trading: few buyers and sellers in the market. Like a flea market in the rain — few people, and a single big buyer visibly moves the price. So today's fix says little about the trend. The real test comes on Tuesday, when São Paulo trades again.

Chain 3 — weekend election signals pull slightly in both directions.

Eurasia 70 → 80%, DataTrends 53.1%→ Reform hope confirmed↔ Polymarket −2.7 points since Sat→ Net: hardly any new pressure on the real

Eurasia sells political assessments to funds; its higher number only confirms what was already almost priced in. The betting market even eased slightly.[3] I read the weekend as neutral to slightly real-friendly.

Look back: the range for Monday's fix (5.54–5.62 in Sunday's brief) can only be checked after 09:15 BRT.

My dissent: the discount is already in the price

The consensus says "dollar toward 4.50" — I see it getting there later

  • The market is running ahead of the result. USD/BRL 4.985 is already at BTG's target for a Flávio win and below the Focus median of 5.20 for the end of 2026.
  • The condition is still missing. Morgan Stanley ties 4.50 to a credible fiscal plan, UBS to a runoff like round 1. On Sunday Folha quotes economists with the same warning: the euphoria only lasts with fiscal adjustment (headline).
  • A first counter-signal: on his website Flávio promises to index the minimum wage to inflation (Folha, headline). That sounds like thrift, but leaves open how the deficit falls.
  • No bank has republished its year-end target since round 1. For Itaú, Bradesco, XP, Santander and BTG the research found no new dollar figure.

That is why I see 4.50 as a target for 2027, not for October. If Flávio names a credible finance minister with a plan before Oct 25, I am wrong — then your rate heads toward 5.30.
