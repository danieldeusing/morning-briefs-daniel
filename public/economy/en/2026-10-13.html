Where we stand & why: the fix catches up with Friday

FX in detail · the causal path · Tuesday = diff vs. Monday

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. On Monday the real gear stood still, only the euro turned. Today São Paulo starts moving again. Three chains explain where we are.

Chain 1 — the fix catches up with Friday.

Fri 09:15 BRT fix 5.6086 (before the slide)→ Dollar falls to 4.985 by the close→ Mon: no B3 trading, euro 1.1181→ Fix 5.5819 (−0.48%)

Monday's drop is not a new push. The fix only shows what had already happened on Friday after 09:15 BRT.[1] Since the Oct 2 fix (5.8610), the last one before round 1, your euro has lost 4.76%: about R$ 279 less on €1,000.[2]

Look back: Monday's brief expected 5.55–5.62 for the fix, most likely 5.59. It came in at 5.5819 — inside the range, just below the point estimate.

Chain 2 — two dollar forces cancel out.

US rate fear: Fed hike in Dec. ~88%→ DXY 102.44, near high since April 2025→ Euro 1.117 ↓ and real pressure ↑→ EUR/BRL: both sides fall, net small
Why a strong dollar often does little to you: picture two kids on a seesaw, euro and real, both against the dollar. When the dollar gets stronger, it pushes both down. For your rate, only who sinks deeper counts. This week the euro sinks (French debt) slightly more than the real (election hopes). That is why your rate falls slightly even though the dollar is strong overall.

According to TradingEconomics, rate expectations and safe-haven buying over the Middle East support the dollar.[3] France's record debt and doubts about whether the divided parliament will pass cuts weigh on the euro.[4]

Chain 3 — the betting market gains, the fiscal plan is still missing.

Polymarket Flávio 85.95 → 87.35%→ Team weighs cuts ~2% of GDP↔ no details, no finance minister→ Real firm, but no new push

The betting market rose 1.4 points overnight.[5] TradingEconomics reports that Flávio's team is considering spending cuts of around 2% of GDP — a single source, with no list. DGABC writes the opposite: market euphoria "esbarra na falta de compromissos concretos" — it runs into a lack of concrete commitments. An adviser says Bolsa Família and BPC stay untouched.[6] Those are two of the largest social items. Without them, 2% of GDP will be hard.

My dissent: 2% of GDP is a number, not a plan

The consensus says "dollar toward 4.50" — I only see it there in 2027

  • The market is running ahead of the result. USD/BRL 4.987 is already at BTG's target for a Flávio win and below all Focus values for end-2026 — also below the top-5 median of 5.25.
  • The new number has gaps. Cuts of ~2% of GDP sound big. But Bolsa Família and BPC are meant to stay, and only TradingEconomics reports the figure.
  • The condition is still missing. Morgan Stanley ties 4.50 to a credible fiscal plan. Without a finance minister, nobody can present one.
  • No new bank figure since Oct 7. Research found nothing newer at Itaú, Bradesco, XP, Santander, BTG and Citi.

That is why I keep 4.50 as a target for 2027, not for October. If Flávio names a credible finance minister with a list before Oct 25, I am wrong — then your rate points toward 5.30.
