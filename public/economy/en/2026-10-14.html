Where we stand & why: the poll costs the real one percent

FX in detail · the causal path · Wednesday = diff vs. Monday

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. On Tuesday it was mainly the real gear that turned, and in your favor. Two chains explain where we are.

Chain 1 — a tie in the poll, and the election premium shrinks.

08:04 BRT: BTG/Nexus 48–45, technical tie→ Traders doubt a sure Flávio win→ Real purchases get unwound→ USD/BRL 5.020 (+0.71%), fix 5.6125 (+0.55%)
Technical tie: a poll never measures exactly; it has a margin of error — here ±2 points. If two candidates are closer together than that margin allows, you cannot say for sure who leads. Like two kids who look the same height as long as you only measure by eye.

BTG/Nexus had Flávio at 48% (before: 44%) and Lula at 45% (before: 46%).[1] So Flávio gained — yet the real fell. That shows how much the market had already priced in: after round 1 it traded as if the election were decided. A result within the margin of error was enough for a retreat. InfoMoney names the poll as the trigger.[1] The Futura poll the same day (Flávio 50%, Lula 46.6%) no longer helped the real.

Look back, honestly: Monday's brief expected the fix at 5.53–5.61, most likely 5.57. It came in at 5.6125 — just above the range. I had not planned for a new poll before the fix; it arrived at 08:04 BRT, an hour earlier.

Chain 2 — the election-risk premium also shows up in rates.

Doubt about the election outcome→ DI1F29 +28.5 bp to 12.505%→ Ibovespa −1.04%→ Foreigners pull out a little, real weaker
DI future: a bet on the B3 about how high the policy rate will be on average until a given date — DI1F29 runs to January 2029. When the value rises, investors demand more interest for more risk. Like a lender who suddenly wants more interest from an uncertain customer.

The long rate jumped more than the short one (DI1F27 only +2.8 bp to 13.467%).[1] That fits the election story: the fear is about the coming years — a new government's budget — not the next Copom meeting. The euro also contributed little: EUR/USD per the ECB 1.1188 on Tuesday (Monday 1.1181).[2]

My dissent: the Focus lags the market

The consensus says "dollar toward 4.50" — Tuesday shows how thin the ice is

  • The market already sits below the consensus. The Focus cut to 5.1535 — the dollar trades at 5.02. The economists are only catching up with what the market has traded since round 1.
  • The Focus contradicts itself. IPCA 2026 up to 5.22%, Selic 2026 down to 13.25%. More inflation and still another cut — that only adds up if the economists expect a new government with austerity. The plan for it is still missing.
  • The asymmetry has flipped. A tie within the margin of error cost the real 0.7%. Good news for Flávio, by contrast, brings little, because it is already paid for.
  • No new bank figure since Oct 7. The big targets (4.50–4.80) date from the euphoria right after round 1.

That is why I keep 4.50 as a target for 2027, not for October. If Flávio names a credible finance minister with a plan before Oct 25, I am wrong — then your rate points toward 5.30.
