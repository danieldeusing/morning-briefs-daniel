Where we stand & why: the real gear turns on its own

FX in detail · the causal path · Friday = diff vs Monday

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. On Wednesday both turned your way. Since Thursday almost only the real gear turns: of the +0.96% since the fix, about +0.81% comes from the weaker real and only +0.16% from the euro.

Chain 1 — the election opens up again, foreigners sell the real.

Quaest 46–44 (Wed 18:06 BRT) + Senate approves end of the 6x1 week (Wed evening)→ Polymarket Flávio 86.1 → 82.95 → 79.25%→ B3: dollar +1.17% to 5.087, DI1F29 +25 bp→ USD/BRL early 5.0978 (+0.81% vs the ECB value)→ EUR/BRL cross 5.7225 (+0.96% vs the fix)
Why the fix does not show the slide: the ECB takes only one photo a day, at 14:15 CEST = 09:15 BRT. That is shortly after the B3 opens. Whatever happens afterwards only shows up in the next day's photo — like a class photo missing the boy who arrived after recess.

The day's numbers: PTAX (official midday) 5.0521, close 5.087, daily high 5.099.[1][2] InfoMoney gives two reasons: at 13:13 BRT "Dollar rises and Ibovespa falls after poll with technical tie," plus general caution ahead of the runoff. The closing coverage adds the Senate: according to InfoMoney it approved the constitutional amendment ending the 6x1 week on Wednesday evening with 75 votes.[1] Flagged honestly: the vote result appears in this one source only; the research found no second one.

6x1 week and PEC: "6x1" means six days of work, one day off — normal for many jobs in Brazil. A PEC is a constitutional amendment. My reading of why the market is nervous: shorter weeks can raise payroll costs for companies, and the project shows Congress waving through popular laws before the election. Like a household buying gifts before payday.
DI rate (DI1F29): a bet on the B3 about how high the interest rate will be in January 2029. When it rises 25 bp (0.25 percentage points) in a day, investors demand more compensation for risk — like insurance that gets pricier after a storm.

Chain 2 — hot US producer prices, but the dollar stays calm.

PPI September +1.3% instead of ~0.5–0.6%→ Fed hike December 88 → 92% (TE)→ DXY still 102.06 → 101.91→ EUR/USD 1.1225 (+0.16% vs the fix)→ barely any effect on EUR/BRL
Producer prices (PPI): what companies get for their goods at the factory gate. They run ahead of shop prices — like the price of flour that later shows up in bread.

US producer prices rose 1.3% in September from the prior month and 6.3% on the year. Excluding food, energy and trade margins: +0.5% and 5.0%.[3] About 0.5–0.6% was expected (the previews at InfoMoney give both values). TradingEconomics now sees a Fed hike in December priced at 92%, and a pause in October at ~72%.[4] Normally that strengthens the dollar. It fell slightly anyway; TradingEconomics cites worry about less AI investment as a counterweight. For you that means: the euro gear stands still.

Look back, honestly: yesterday I expected the fix at 5.62–5.70, most likely 5.66. It came in at 5.6679 — inside the range, almost on the dot. The asymmetry also played out: a dead heat in the polls lifts your rate fast. What I did not expect was the strength in the afternoon.

My dissent: the betting market is still too sure

79% does not fit a two-point dead heat

  • The polls say "close." Quaest 46–44 with a ±2 point margin of error. The Datafolha of Oct 8 had a four-point gap. A race with a two- to four-point lead is open, not a four-to-one favorite.
  • The betting market lags. It fell from 87 to 79% in three days while the polls tightened. I expect it to keep falling if the next big poll brings no clear picture.
  • The bank targets hang on the betting market. Goldman cut to 4.70 on Oct 13, when Polymarket still stood at 87.35%. JPMorgan's 4.70–4.80 dates from Oct 7. Both need Flávio and a fiscal plan.

That is why I see the risk for your rate through Oct 25 as more to the upside. I am wrong if Datafolha and the debates show Flávio clearly ahead — then your rate falls toward 5.60.
