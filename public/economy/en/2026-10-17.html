Where we stand & why: a snapshot in the morning, a recovery in the afternoon

FX in detail · the causal path · Saturday = diff to Monday

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. On Friday at fix time both turned against the real: of the +0.91% versus Thursday, about +0.51% comes from the real (ECB value 5.0570 → 5.0828) and +0.40% from the euro (1.1208 → 1.1253). Afterwards both turned back. Over the week: fix Oct 9 5.6086 → 5.7197 = +1.98%, of which ~+1.55% real and ~+0.42% euro (own calculation from ECB fixes).

Chain 1 — the fix catches up with Thursday and hits the peak.

Thursday: real falls only in the afternoon (close 5.087)→ Friday ~09:15 BRT: dollar still near the day's high (5.096)→ ECB fix 5.7197 (+0.91%)→ afterwards: Datafolha 47–45 (InfoMoney 09:58 BRT), PTAX 13:04 BRT 5.0826
Why the fix and the closing rate are so far apart: the ECB takes only one snapshot a day, at 14:15 CEST = 09:15 BRT. On Friday that snapshot fell in the expensive hours. Like a class photo where everyone happens to have their eyes closed — by the afternoon the class looked different. The control at the same time of day (PTAX × ECB euro = 5.7194) shows: the fix is correct, just early.

Chain 2 — oil and a weak dollar save the afternoon.

Brent +3.72% to $109.86→ Petrobras lifts Ibovespa +0.54%→ DXY weaker, US tech falls→ USD/BRL close 5.073 (−0.28%), DI rates fall→ EUR/BRL cross 5.69–5.70
Why expensive oil pushes your rate down: Brazil sells oil, Europe buys it. When the oil price rises, more dollars flow into Brazil and more euros flow out of Europe. Like two neighbors: one sells firewood, the other has to buy it at a higher price in winter. Both work against your rate.

InfoMoney writes: "Por aqui, a alta foi sustentada basicamente por Petrobras". Brent and WTI "dispararam nesta sexta-feira".[1] TradingEconomics cites "tightening supply" — no second source confirms a specific trigger, so I name none.[2] At the same time DI rates fell across the whole curve, DI1F29 to 12.685% (−4.5 bp).[1]

DI rate (DI1F29): a bet on the B3 about how high the interest rate will be in January 2029. When it falls, investors demand less compensation for risk — like an insurance policy that gets cheaper after a quiet year.

Looking back, honestly: yesterday I expected the fix at 5.66–5.77, most likely 5.72. It came in at 5.7197 — on the dot. What I did not see: the recovery afterwards. For Monday this means: the starting point is not 5.72 but around 5.69–5.70.

My dissent: 77% is too high for a two-point race

The betting market is falling — but not far enough yet

  • The polls say "tight". Datafolha 47–45, Quaest 46–44, each with a ±2-point margin of error. A race with a two-point gap is open, not a three-to-one favorite.
  • The betting market lags. It fell from 87 to 77% in four days, and the polls tightened in that time. I expect a few more points off if the debates bring no clear picture.
  • The bank targets hang on the betting market. Goldman cut to 4.70 on Oct 13, when Polymarket stood at 87.35%. No new bank figure has come since. Both assumptions — Flávio and fiscal plan — have become more uncertain since then.

That is why I see the risk for your rate until Oct 25 as more to the upside. I am wrong if the debates show Flávio clearly ahead — then your rate falls toward 5.60.
