Where we stand & why: the Friday spike is gone

FX in detail · the causal path · Tuesday = diff, with fresh anchors for all horizons

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. On Monday both turned in the same direction: of the −0.99%, about −0.74% comes from the real (ECB value 5.0828 → 5.0453) and −0.25% from the euro (1.1253 → 1.1225), my own calculation from ECB fixes. Over seven days: fix 10/09 5.6086 → 10/19 5.6633 = +0.98%.

Chain 1 — the fix catches up with Friday afternoon.

Fri 10/16: fix at the morning high (5.7197)→ Fri afternoon: real recovers (B3 5.073)→ Mon: dollar falls further to 5.048→ ECB fix 5.6633 (−0.99%)
Why Monday catches up so much: the ECB takes only one snapshot each business day, at 09:15 BRT. On Friday the snapshot fell in the expensive morning hours; afterward the real firmed up further. Like a thermometer you read only once a day — Monday's reading also shows what happened on Friday afternoon.

Chain 2 — polls in the morning, unrest in Europe.

AtlasIntel: Flávio 2.2 points ahead→ less fear of a Lula win→ Real firmer+ Protests in France, euro slightly weaker→ EUR/BRL lower

The polls came out Monday morning (08:00–10:21 BRT), before the fix.[1] Over the weekend, protests against rising prices escalated in France; the Carrefour CEO reported 50 looted stores, and Europe's stock markets fell.[1] The euro eased only slightly and was back up +0.18% early Tuesday (1.12332).[2]

Why a restless Europe pushes your rate down: investors dislike unrest. When things get loud in France, some pull money out of the euro. Less demand for euros means your euro buys fewer reais. Like a store with police at the door — customers go somewhere else for now.

A warning sign: the real firmed up, but the stock market did not. Ibovespa −0.17%, Petrobras −2.82% despite expensive oil.[1] The DI curve steepened: short rates fell (DI1F28 −5 bp), long ones rose (DI1F34 +4.5 bp). That means the market believes in falling rates soon, but demands more for the risk eight years out.

Steeper DI curve: DI contracts are bets on B3 about how high the interest rate will be on a given date (DI1F28 = January 2028). When the long ones rise and the short ones fall, it is like a loan that gets cheaper in the short term but more expensive in the long term — the bank trusts the distant future less.

Look back, honestly: on Saturday I expected Monday's fix at 5.62–5.78, most likely 5.69. It came in at 5.6633 — within the range, but 2.7 centavos below the point estimate. The real got stronger on Monday than Friday's close suggested.
