Where we stand & why: the real moves alone

FX in detail · the causal path · Wednesday = diff, with fresh anchors for all horizons

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. On Tuesday almost only the Brazilian one turned: of the −0.52%, about −0.43% comes from the real (ECB value 5.0453 → 5.0235) and only −0.09% from the euro (1.1225 → 1.1215), my own calculation from ECB fixes. Over seven days: fix Oct 13 5.6125 → Oct 20 5.6339 = +0.38% — the Friday peak (5.7197) is fully gone.

Chain 1 — the election bet gets bigger.

Poll: Flávio 52% valid votes→ Polymarket 77% → 81%→ Ibovespa +1.57%, long DI −7 to −10 bp→ Dollar 5.048 → 5.019→ ECB fix 5.6339

InfoMoney links the rally to Flávio's poll lead; an economist at Suno Research cites the expectation of a "deeper and faster" fiscal consolidation under Flávio.[1] Important compared with Monday: stocks joined in this time. On Monday the real was firmer but stocks were not — yesterday equities and long rates confirmed the same bet.

Valid votes: in Brazil, blank and invalid ballots do not count. The "votos válidos" split only the votes for candidates, so they always add up to 100% and sit higher than the totals among all respondents. 52 to 48 valid corresponds to a gap of about three to four points among all respondents.

Chain 2 — oil helps the real and slows the euro.

Houthi attacks, 18 ships in Hormuz→ Brent above $110→ Brazil exports oil, Europe imports→ Real supported, euro held back→ EUR/BRL lower

Brent December closed Tuesday at $108.79 after a daily high near $111 (five-month high); early Wednesday $110.08.[1][2] On top of that comes France: protests turned into clashes with the police. Handelsbanken writes that "much more euro weakness is to be priced in".[3]

Why expensive oil pushes your rate down: Brazil sells oil to the world, Europe buys it. When the price rises, more dollars flow into Brazil and more euros flow out of Europe. Like two neighbors, one selling firewood and the other forced to buy it — in a cold winter one gets richer, the other poorer.

Look back, honestly: yesterday I expected the fix at 5.61–5.72, most likely 5.66. It came in at 5.6339 — within the range, but 2.6 centavos below the point. That is the second day in a row that I underestimate the real. So I set today's point lower.

My dissent: 81% for 52 to 48

The betting market runs ahead of the polls

  • The polls say "close". The last three major rounds show 51 to 52% of valid votes. The strongest of them appears in only one source.
  • The betting market jumps on one number. +4 points overnight on a poll with no published field dates. That is sentiment, not new information.
  • The bank targets hang on two assumptions. JPMorgan and Morgan Stanley assume that Flávio wins and delivers a fiscal plan. A win alone is not enough.

So I see the risk for your rate until Oct 25 as slightly upward. I am wrong if Quaest tonight and Datafolha tomorrow confirm the 52% — then your rate falls toward 5.55–5.60.
