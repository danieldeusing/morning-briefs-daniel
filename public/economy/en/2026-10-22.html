Where we stand & why: this time the euro turned

FX in detail · the causal path · Thursday = diff, with fresh anchors for all horizons

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL — two gears. On Wednesday at fix time only the European one turned: EUR/USD rose from 1.1215 to 1.1234 (+0.17%), the dollar in reais stayed practically flat at 5.0233 (Tuesday 5.0235). My own calculation from ECB fixes. The rise of the dollar in São Paulo to 5.027 came only in the afternoon, so after the fix. It lands in today's fix.

Chain 1 — stocks tip, rates don't.

WEG misses profit forecast→ Ibovespa −0.86%→ Dollar 5.019 → 5.027→ but DI −1 to −6 bp, CDS −2%→ no fiscal scare

WEG reported third-quarter profit of R$1.67 billion, against an expected R$1.71 billion; the stock fell 7.34%. According to InfoMoney, sentiment turned after an early gain.[1] What matters: the rate curve fell across all maturities, and the state's credit insurance got cheaper. A company problem, not a confidence problem.

Why the rate curve tells you more than the stock market: DI rates show what the market demands for lending to the Brazilian state. When they fall, the market trusts the state more. Like a baker whose cake sold badly today — that says nothing about whether the bank still gives him credit.

Chain 2 — oil helps the real and slows the euro.

Drone hits Aramco field Shaybah→ Brent $111 (+2%)→ Brazil exports oil, Europe imports→ Real supported, euro held back→ EUR/BRL lower

According to TradingEconomics, the US is pulling part of its embassy staff out of Saudi Arabia. Rising exports from the Middle East slow the climb.[2] Add France: the 10-year yield is above 5%, the highest since 2002. TE describes the euro as "under pressure around 1.122".[3] The euro at fix time (1.1234) was firmer anyway — this brake works slowly.

Why expensive oil pushes your rate down: Brazil sells oil to the world, Europe buys it. When the price rises, more dollars flow into Brazil and more euros flow out of Europe. Like two neighbors, one selling firewood and the other having to buy it — in a cold winter one gets richer, the other poorer.

Look back, honestly: yesterday I expected the fix at 5.59–5.69, most likely 5.64. The result was 5.6432 — a hit. After two days in which I had underestimated the real, I keep today's point close to the early value.

[BR] Sun 10/25 · 3 days to go

Quaest (published Wed 10/21 evening): Flávio 45%, Lula 44% of all respondents, margin of error ±2 points. A week earlier it was 46 to 44. Two sources give the same numbers; I did not find the field period, sample or valid votes.[1] The same day: Meio/Ideia 51.1 to 48.9 and Gerp 53 to 47, both in valid votes (one source each, InfoMoney; the 51.1 to 48.9 split is identical to the Oct. 14 Quaest round, so a mix-up is possible and I could not confirm it). "Within the margin of error": a poll asks only a few thousand people, so every value can be off by ±2 points. 45 to 44 therefore means: it could just as well be 43 to 46. Like two children who look equally tall — without an exact tape measure you don't know who is taller.
