Where we stand & why: the rally came after the snapshot

FX in detail · the causal path · Saturday = diff to Monday

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL. At fix time on Friday little moved: the dollar in reais fell from 5.0490 to 5.0465 (−0.05%), the euro in dollars from 1.1205 to 1.1193 (−0.11%). That makes the −0.16% at the fix. The big move only came afterward. All my own calculation from ECB fixes.

Chain 1 — the market is betting on Flávio.

Betting market Flávio 77% → 80%→ Hope for a stricter budget→ DI rates −3 to −16 bp→ Ibovespa +0.47%→ Dollar 5.056 → 4.993

InfoMoney calls it the "Rali Flávio": investors bought the win they expect ahead of Sunday.[1] According to Agência Brasil, the real was the best major currency of the day, even though the dollar rose slightly worldwide.[2] At the same time, foreign investors bought more protection against falling prices (EWZ options). So they are betting on Flávio, but hedging.

"Buying the rumor": traders buy before the event happens because they think it is likely. If it then really happens, part of the move has already taken place. Like concert tickets whose price rises as soon as the band is merely announced.

Chain 2 — the time gap.

ECB snapshot 09:15 BRT: 5.6485→ PTAX 13:07 BRT: dollar 5.0377→ Rally in the afternoon→ Close 4.993 × 1.1193→ your rate ~5.59

The fix is a snapshot. Anyone who compares the new fix on Monday with 5.6485 overstates the move by about 1%. The right comparison point for Monday is ~5.59. Rule of thumb: 1% on USD/BRL shifts your rate by about 0.056.

Chain 3 — oil and France, the small gears. Brent rose to $116.72 (+3.13%), according to TE because of the expectation of new US strikes on Iran.[3] Expensive oil helps the real, because Brazil exports oil. According to TE, political uncertainty in France weighed on the euro.[4] Moody's left France at Aa3 with a negative outlook on Oct 23 — no downgrade, but no all-clear either (headlines only).

Looking back, honestly: yesterday I expected the fix at 5.62–5.72, most likely 5.67. It came in at 5.6485 — within the range, but 0.02 below the point. The Datafolha tie did not weigh on the real as I thought; the betting market even rose. The week range 5.40–5.95 holds.

My dissent: the market already prices in the Flávio world

Asymmetry before Sunday

  • Polls and betting market diverge. The polls show a tie, yet Polymarket rose to almost 80%.
  • The rate follows the betting market. At 4.993, a good part of the Flávio gain is already in the price.

That is why the risk is lopsided: in the Flávio case your rate falls only about 2% more, in the Lula case it rises by 4–12%. I am wrong if the Saturday polls show Flávio clearly ahead — then the market was right.
