The runoff: nine polls, a tie with a tilt

[BR] Sun Oct 25 · result this evening · as of Oct 25, ~02:45 BRT

The two big pollsters show the same picture. Datafolha: Flávio 51%, Lula 49% of valid votes, after 50/50 on Oct 22. Datafolha surveyed 4,050 people on Oct 23 and 24, margin ±2 points, registration BR-09540/2026.[3] Quaest: also 51/49, 3,702 respondents, ±2 points, BR-09158/2026.[4] The other finals (headlines only): AtlasIntel 50.8/49.2, CNT/MDA 50.7/49.3, Nexus/BTG 50.5/49.5, Futura 50.1/49.9, Gerp 52/48 — all for Flávio. Colectta is the only pollster with Lula ahead, 50.3/49.7.[5]

Margin of error ("empate técnico"): a poll asks only a small share of voters. The margin says how far the result can be from the true value — here 2 points in either direction. So at 51 to 49, 49 to 51 can also be the truth. Like a spoonful of soup: it tastes almost like the pot, but not exactly.

Polymarket: Flávio 79.75%, Lula 20.5% (Oct 25, 05:12 UTC). Trend: 87.35% on Oct 13, 77.3% on Oct 23.[6] In the first round on Oct 4, Flávio had 47.03% and Lula 45.16%.

My view: ~70% for Flávio (yesterday ~65%). Yesterday I wrote that I would change the number at a gap of three points. No major pollster showed one. I still raise it slightly: eight of nine finals point the same way, Datafolha moved from 50/50 to 51/49, and among the most likely voters Datafolha sees 52/48 (headline only). I stay below the betting market. Every single number is within the margin, and pollster errors are often biased in the same direction. At ~30%, a Lula win is no edge case.

Where we stand & why: the market has already voted

FX in detail · the causal path · Sunday = diff to Monday

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL. There have been no new quotes since Friday. So what matters is what Friday already priced in.[1][2]

Chain 1 — the advance purchase.

Betting market Flávio ~80%→ Hope for a stricter budget→ Dollar 4.993 (Fri)→ your rate ~5.59→ little room to fall

The dollar is already at 4.993. JPMorgan's Flávio target of 4.90 (Oct 3) is only 1.9% away, the ING target of 4.85 just under 3%. Société Générale wrote on Oct 21 that the hurdle "below 5.00" is low[13] — that has already happened.

"Buy the rumor, sell the news": traders buy before the event because they expect it. When it happens, part of the move is already over, and some take profits. Like concert tickets whose price rises as soon as the band is announced.

Chain 2 — the time-of-day gap.

ECB snapshot 09:15 BRT: 5.6485→ Afternoon rally→ Close 4.993 × 1.1193→ ~5.59

Anyone who compares Monday's new fix with 5.6485 overstates every move by about 1%. The reference point is ~5.59. Rule of thumb: 1% in USD/BRL shifts your rate by about 0.056.

Chain 3 — oil and Iran, two directions. According to several outlets, Trump said the US could attack Iran "maybe tomorrow" — or strike a "big, beautiful" deal. Iran reports hits on two tankers in the Strait of Hormuz.[23] Only a small source reports a US attack that was carried out; NYT, Bloomberg and WSJ report threats and preparations. For you, oil works two ways: expensive oil helps the real (Brazil exports oil), and according to UBS an Iran escalation weighs on the euro.[16] Both lower your rate. A flight into the dollar works the other way.

My dissent: the risk is skewed

Asymmetry on election night

The consensus reads 51/49 and 80% in the betting market as "Flávio, and the real keeps rising." I read it differently. In the Flávio case your rate falls only about 2% more, because Friday has already paid for the win. In the Lula case it rises by 4–13%. I am wrong if a clear Flávio win triggers new foreign buying — then Goldman's 4.70 is right, and your rate falls toward 5.30 by year-end.

Understanding, not a recommendation

There is no rate today. On €1,000, the Friday close brought about R$ 5,589. In the Flávio case (~5.49) it would be about R$ 5,490 on Monday, so R$ 100 less. In the Lula case (5.84–6.34) it would be R$ 5,840–6,340, so R$ 250–750 more. The more likely case costs little, the rarer one brings a lot. It's Sunday, October twenty-fifth. Brazil elects its president today, and the result was still open at press time.

Saturday's final polls put Flávio Bolsonaro narrowly ahead in nearly every case. Datafolha and Quaest each have him at fifty-one to forty-nine percent of valid votes, with a margin of error of two points. Only one smaller pollster has Lula in front. The Polymarket betting market gives Flávio just under eighty percent. I put him at about seventy percent. So a Lula win is no edge case.

There's no exchange rate today. The last European Central Bank reference rate, from Friday, was five point six four eight five. But at the market close, your rate was already around five point five nine, because the dollar fell below five reais. So the market has already paid part of the price for a Flávio win.

For Monday's first fix, I see two worlds. If Flávio wins, Bloomberg Economics and ING expect your rate at about five point four five to five point five zero. If Lula wins, Bloomberg Economics expects the real to plunge eleven percent. Your rate would then be around six point two six to six point three four. Most likely, I expect five point five zero.

One important point: no bank published a new target over the weekend. The anchors are older, for example JPMorgan from October third and Goldman from October thirteenth. What's new is a euro path from UBS: the euro is expected to climb to one point two zero by mid twenty twenty-seven. That lifts your rate even if the real holds firm. Over twelve months, I therefore see five point four zero to six point three zero, most likely five point eight zero. The probabilities are my confidence, not a market figure.
