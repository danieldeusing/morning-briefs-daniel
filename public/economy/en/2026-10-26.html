The upset: Lula turns the runoff

[BR] Sun Oct 25 · 100% counted · as of Oct 26, ~02:50 BRT

Lula received 51.79% of valid votes, Flávio Bolsonaro 48.21%, with 100% of ballot boxes counted.[1] That is roughly 64 million against 59 million votes. In the first round Flávio was still ahead, 47.03% to 45.16%. According to Bloomberg Línea, this is the first time since the 1988 constitution that a first-round loser wins the runoff.[2] Lula turns 81 on Tuesday and begins his fourth term.

I was wrong. Yesterday I had Flávio at ~70%, Polymarket at 79.75%, and eight of nine final polls showed him ahead. Datafolha and Quaest had Flávio at 51%; he got 48.21%, about 3 points less. The errors all went in the same direction, as I warned yesterday. My brief called the Lula world "not an edge case" and calculated 6.16–6.34 for it. That is exactly where the CME night stands now.

The challenge. Goldman wrote before the election: under a 1-point margin the risk of a challenge rises, over 4 points there is a clear mandate.[4] 3.58 sits in between. Flávio does not recognize the result and wants to file for annulment at the electoral court TSE, because Justice Zanin extended voting until 7 p.m. TSE ministers rule out an annulment, according to SBT News and Folha.[3] BTG sees "no room" for a challenge.[5]

My view: the challenge is background noise for the rate as long as the TSE decides quickly. The main point is economic policy. According to BTG, the agenda and team are open; the current team wants to present austerity measures "this year," and one scenario is Alckmin as finance minister (one source).[5]

Where we stand & why: the night of the shock

FX in detail · the causal path · Monday = full rewrite

Your rate is the product EUR/USD × USD/BRL. The euro has barely moved (1.12205, +0.16%).[11] The whole move comes from the real.

Chain 1 — the bet goes wrong.

Market prices ~80% Flávio→ Dollar falls to 4.993 (Fri)→ Lula wins→ Rally must reverse→ CME dollar 5.56→ your rate ~6.24

The real future for November fell 10.02% by 22:20 BRT; the implied dollar for today stands at R$ 5.56.[6] Bloomberg Línea cites the same number, from a 5.01 settlement, with a peak at 5.68 and 75,100 contracts by 22:14 BRT.[2] Before the first round the dollar stood at R$ 5.22 (GT Capital).[8] So the night wiped out the entire election rally and more.

CME future overnight: a contract in Chicago that fixes the real's rate for a later date. It trades when the B3 is closed. At night only a few trade, so prices swing harder. Like a flea market at 5 a.m.: there is a price, but few buyers, and by 10 a.m. it often looks different.

Chain 2 — the traders have to get out.

CFTC: net +32,518 long real (Oct 20)→ Loss on a Lula win→ Forced selling of real→ Overshoot

Many funds bet on a rising real. When everyone has to get out at once, it falls harder than the news alone justifies — like a full hall with only one door. In a Citi survey before the election, 63% expected a real loss of more than 5% on a Lula win.[8] The night shows double that.

Chain 3 — stocks follow. EWZ after hours −18.53% (21:16 BRT), Petrobras ADR −11.96%, Itaú −13.65%, Vale only −4.19%: Vale sells in dollars, a weak real helps it.[7] Bradesco BBI cut Brazilian equities to "neutral."[8] GT Capital expects more than −5% at the B3 open.[10]

What counts today The night is a hint. The three real numbers: Focus ~08:30 BRT (data from Oct 23, does not show the shock yet), ECB fix ~12:00 BRT (your reference rate; measured ~10:10 BRT, about 70 minutes after the B3 open) and PTAX ~13:00 BRT. Compare the fix with ~5.59 (Friday close), not with 5.6485.[9][10]

What history says: four elections in the PTAX

BCB PTAX (selling rate) · official series

PTAX is the central bank's official daily rate — an average of four morning measurements. It is the "referee rate" for contracts.[12]

ElectionDay 1Afterward
2014 Dilma, 12 years ago today+2.16%, day 2 −1.86%+7.1% by year-end, +29% by March 2015 — despite finance minister Levy
2022 Lula III (expected)−1.65%+6.1% from the Nov 1 low (5.15) to Nov 17 (5.4655), spending dispute; against the pre-election level (Oct 28) only +2.2%, April 2023 below the pre-election level
2002 Lula IPeak 3.9552 already before the election (Oct 22)−27% by June 2003 after clear commitments
2018 Bolsonaro−1.05%+5.4% by year-end

Two patterns. First: the first jump is often partly reversed on day two (2014). Second: the direction in the following months is decided by the budget, not by election night. In 2022 the pressure came two to three weeks later with the spending dispute and disappeared with the new fiscal rule.[13] In 2014 a recession came on top, and even an orthodox minister did not stop the trend.[14] My reading: 2026 resembles 2014 most — surprise, incumbent, doubts about the budget. The difference: this time the bets were one-sidedly on the real, so the first blow is much bigger than in 2014.

My dissent: the night overshoots, the direction does not

What the consensus reads this morning

The level of the night overshoots: thin market, forced selling, and in 2014 almost everything came back on day two. The direction is right: without Flávio the reason for the 4.50–4.80 targets is gone, and the banks' euro path points up. The 5.59 from Friday will not come back so quickly. I am wrong if Lula names a clearly market-friendly team this week — then a 2022 path toward 5.80 in three months is possible.

It's Monday, October twenty-sixth. Lula has been re-elected, and it was an upset. He took fifty-one point seven nine percent of the valid votes, Flávio Bolsonaro forty-eight point two one. The betting market had given Flávio almost eighty percent, and I had him at about seventy. I was wrong.

Overnight, the real fell about ten percent on the futures exchange in Chicago. The dollar implied there for today stands at five point five six reais, up from five point oh one on Friday. For your rate, that means roughly six point two four, compared with about five point five nine at Friday's close. That's a hint from a thin overnight market, not a real rate.

Brazilian stocks plunged as well. The Brazil fund EWZ dropped more than eighteen percent in after-hours trading in New York. Flávio does not accept the result and wants the election annulled. Ministers on the electoral court rule that out, according to reports.

What matters for the forecast: every bank target from Goldman, UBS BB and JPMorgan for the coming months assumed a Flávio win. They no longer apply. No major bank has published new targets yet. For the Lula case, there's JPMorgan at five point five zero reais per dollar from October third, and Suno Research at about five point four zero for twenty twenty-seven.

My take: the fix today between five point nine five and six point four zero, most likely six point one eight. This week, I expect part of the jump to come back, most likely six point one zero. Over twelve months, I see five point eight zero to six point nine zero, most likely six point three zero. The reason: the real stays weaker, and the banks expect a stronger euro. The probabilities are my own confidence, not a market figure.

History helps. In twenty fourteen, the dollar jumped on the first day after Dilma's re-election and gave back almost all of it on the second. In the months after, though, it rose almost thirty percent as the budget got out of hand. In twenty twenty-two, the market only calmed down with a new fiscal rule.

Three numbers count today: the Focus survey at about eight thirty, the European Central Bank fix at about ten fifteen, and the official PTAX rate at about one in the afternoon, all Brazilian time. Later in the week, the Fed and the European Central Bank decide. The next brief will show the first real rate after the election and the first new bank targets.
