Where we stand & why: the day of the shock

[BR] FX in detail · Mon Oct 26 · Update since Mon real rates instead of CME overnight

Now there are real closing rates instead of the CME overnight. The dollar closed at R$ 5.403, +8.21%.[1][2] Folha and Valor call it the biggest one-day rise since 1999, that is, since the real was floated.[3] In the morning it stood at 5.605.[1] The official PTAX was 5.5007.[4]

Chain 1 — the fix catches the peak.

Morning panic: dollar ~5.60→ ECB measures ~10:15 BRT→ Fix 6.2523→ Afternoon: dollar falls to 5.40→ Cross ~6.00–6.05

The ECB measures once a day, around 2:15 pm CET = 10:15 BRT. The fix is a snapshot, not a daily average — and the snapshot was taken at the worst hour.[5] At the B3 close the cross came to 6.045, this morning 6.001 (5.3739 × 1.11666).[6][7] The fix is thus 3.4–4.2% above the market. O Povo reported a euro peak of R$ 6.27 in the late morning (~11:50 BRT) — that fits the time of the fix.[3]

For your exchange Compare an offer today with ~6.00, not with 6.25. 6.05 is not a decline, but the market after a peak.

Chain 2 — from the election result to the fiscal premium.

Market priced in Flávio→ Lula wins→ Doubts about the budget→ Yield curve +250 bp→ Real −8%

According to InfoMoney, Goldman sees a higher fiscal premium that Brazilian assets must price in.[14] Brazil has 83% gross debt to GDP and a deficit of around 10%; Commerzbank: Lula has "little room to disappoint".[18][19]

Fiscal premium (risk premium): the extra interest investors demand because they fear the state is spending too much. Like a landlord who takes a higher deposit from a tenant with a shaky job.

Chain 3 — the state steps in, the peak crumbles.

Tesouro cancels auctions→ buys back ~R$ 15 billion in bonds→ Yields fall from the peak→ Dollar 5.60 → 5.40

The Finance Ministry canceled the week's auctions for fixed-rate and IPCA+ bonds. It bought back around R$ 7.1 billion in fixed-rate and R$ 7.7 billion in inflation-linked bonds.[9] The DI Jan/31 fell from 15.94% (11:02 BRT) to 15.185%.[9] No BCB FX auction confirmed. O Globo and JC headline "BC e Tesouro atuam", but InfoMoney and Agência Brasil name only the Tesouro.[1][9][3]

Buyback: the state buys back its own bonds that nobody wants right now. Like a shop owner who buys back his own goods in a clearance sale so the price does not fall through the floor.

Fiscal dominance: the state is so indebted that higher interest rates no longer help, but only make the debt more expensive. Like a credit card customer whose interest grows faster than he can pay off. Then the central bank loses its weapon, and the currency falls.

Structural view, 2–3 years: gross debt in August was 83% of GDP, the highest level since 2021 (BBH, Commerzbank).[18][19] Morgan Stanley's bear scenario (August, before the election) sees it above 100% in 2033.[30] The lever according to BBH and Deutsche Bank: the Congress, which has shifted to the right, can curb Lula's spending.[19][21] My center of gravity for 2028–2029 remains ~6.2–6.6 (own calculation: dollar ~5.30–5.50 × euro ~1.18–1.20). Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000 the fix 6.2523 yields on paper R$ 6,252. The market this morning gives more like R$ 6,001 — about R$ 250 less. Against Friday's close (cross ~5.59) it is still about R$ 410 more. Short term everything hangs on the name of the finance minister: a market-friendly name can push the rate toward 5.90, a spending signal can lift it back above 6.25. Medium term I see little path back below 5.80. The real daily rate is shown by the fix ~12:00 BRT. The decision is yours.

It's Tuesday, October twenty-seventh. Monday was the worst day for the Brazilian real since January nineteen ninety-nine. The dollar closed on the B3 at five point four oh three reais, up eight point two percent. In the morning it was even at five point six oh.

One point matters for your rate. The European Central Bank set its fix at six point two five. But it measures only once a day, around ten fifteen Brazilian time, and that was right at the worst hour. Last night and this morning the market was at about six point oh oh. So the fix sits three to four percent above the real market.

Stocks collapsed too. The Ibovespa fell almost fourteen percent, with the first trading halt since twenty twenty. Yields on long-term government bonds jumped by about two and a half percentage points. The Finance Ministry canceled its auctions and bought back about fifteen billion reais in bonds. I did not find an FX auction by the central bank.

On the risk gauge known as CDS, there are two numbers. In the morning it stood at about one hundred fifty-two points, at the close at about one hundred thirty-seven. These are two separate series; the closing number has only one source.

The big banks see the dollar between five point four oh and five point six oh in the next few days. But Goldman and Morgan Stanley wrote their numbers on Monday morning in the middle of the panic, JPMorgan and UBS only in the afternoon. The market is already below them. For three to twelve months, no bank has published a new target yet.

My own outlook, as confidence and not a market number: today between five point eight eight and six point one eight, most likely six point oh oh. This week most likely six point oh five. Over twelve months I see five point eight oh to six point nine oh, most likely six point three oh. That's my own calculation, because bank targets are missing.

One question matters most right now: who becomes finance minister, and what Lula's plan for the budget looks like. On top of that come the Fed on Wednesday and the European Central Bank on Thursday.
