Where we stand & why: day two

[BR] FX in detail · Tue Oct 27 · Diff vs. Mon peak crumbling

The dollar fell 1.07% on Tuesday to R$ 5.3488; the low was 5.316 around 10:45 AM BRT.[1][2] The PTAX came in at 5.3346, after 5.5007 on Monday.[3] That means the real won back about a third of Monday's jump (PTAX before the election: 5.0377).

Chain 1 — this time the fix measures a calm moment.

Dollar falls in the morning toward the low 5.316→ ECB measures ~10:15 AM BRT→ Fix 5.9624→ B3 close 5.3488 × 1.115 = 5.964

On Monday the ECB photographed the panic peak, which is why the fix sat 3–4% above the market. On Tuesday the measurement fell in the calm phase, and fix and cross are only 0.03% apart.[4][5][6] You can use the fix again as a benchmark for a quote.

Chain 2 — the Tesouro buys calm, but not confidence.

Tesouro buys back R$ 16.8 bn NTN-B→ Fixed rates fall up to −47 bp→ less panic, dollar −1%→ but IPCA+ 2050 rises +15 bp

On the second day the Tesouro bought back inflation-linked bonds (NTN-B) worth R$ 16.8 bn, after more than R$ 14.8 bn on Monday; the next LFT auction is cancelled.[10] The DI Jan/28 fell to 14.41% (−41 bp), the Prefixado 2029 from 15.26% to 14.79%.[8][9] The catch: the IPCA+ 2050 rose from 7.03% to 7.18%.[9]

Real yield (IPCA+): the rate above inflation that the government has to pay on very long bonds. Like the premium a landlord asks for a 25-year lease when he does not trust the tenant. When it rises, the market distrusts the government over the long run — no matter how calm the day looks.

Chain 3 — two ministers, two messages.

Durigan: measures "prontas"→ Relief in the morning→ Guimarães: "não foi discutido"→ Market waits for the name

Finance Minister Dario Durigan said the government has "várias medidas já sendo estudadas e prontas", including a law aimed at the center of the fiscal target. He called the price crash "não é de fundamento".[12][13] Two hours later José Guimarães, minister for relations with Congress, said: "Esse assunto não foi discutido, e nem está sendo discutido".[14] Durigan also says: first Lula picks his team, then the team presents the measures.[12] SocGen and TradingEconomics see the finance minister's name as the next big signal.[18][5]

My dissent: the recovery is borrowed

What the consensus reads this morning

TD Securities expects short-term swings "around 5.60" — written Tuesday morning, before the dollar fell to 5.35.[17] Commerzbank considers a counter-move "in the coming days" possible, but writes itself: "Recent history gives us some cause for doubt".[16] SocGen puts the support at 5.34 — exactly where the dollar closed.[18] My view: the market takes the recovery too seriously. It comes from the Tesouro buyback, not from new confidence. The proof: the long real yield rose the same day, and Citigroup and BCA Research downgraded Brazilian assets.[9][41] Until a finance minister is named, I see the risk more to the upside (dollar 5.45–5.60, EUR/BRL ~6.06–6.23). I am wrong if Lula names a market-friendly figure this week — then the support at 5.24 (EUR/BRL ~5.83) is reached quickly.

Practical for the exchange

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000 the fix of 5.9624 yields R$ 5,962 on paper. That is about R$ 290 less than at Monday's peak fix (6.2523), but still about R$ 314 more than before the runoff (5.6485). The fix is a fair comparison for a quote again. Short term, the finance minister's name decides: a market-friendly name could push the rate toward 5.83, a spending signal lift it above 6.20. Today the Fed does not influence the fix, because it decides only after the measurement. Medium term I see little path back below 5.75. The decision is yours.

It's Wednesday, October twenty-eighth. On the second day after the election shock, the real has recovered somewhat. The dollar closed on the B3 at five point three five reais, down a little over one percent. The European Central Bank's fix fell to five point nine six.

For your rate, that's good news on one count. On Monday the central bank measured right at the panic peak, and the fix sat three to four percent above the market. On Tuesday, the fix and the market line up almost exactly again. So you can use the fix once more as a benchmark for a quote. Compared with the time before the runoff, though, your rate is still about five and a half percent higher.

The recovery comes with a catch. It comes mostly from the Treasury, which on the second day bought back nearly seventeen billion reais of bonds. Short-term rates fell sharply. But the rate on very long, inflation-linked bonds kept rising. In other words, the market still doesn't trust the government over the long run.

Two messages came out of the government. Finance Minister Durigan said the budget measures are ready. Two hours later, another minister said a savings package hadn't even been discussed. Who the new finance minister will be is still open; the names floated are Alckmin and Fonteles. Central bank chief Galípolo says he'll step into the currency market only at a real disruption, and that's not the case now.

Among the banks, since the result only Commerzbank has confirmed a target with a longer horizon: five point two zero on the dollar by year-end, but only with credible fiscal reform. All the other twelve-month targets date from before the runoff and are stale. There are no direct targets for euro to real; my numbers are my own calculation.

My outlook, as confidence and not a market figure: today between five point eight six and six point zero six, most likely five point nine five. This week, most likely five point nine seven. Over the next thirty days, most likely six point zero zero. Over three to six months, most likely six point one zero, and over twelve months, most likely six point two five.

This evening at three o'clock Brazil time, the Fed decides, so only after today's fix is measured. On Thursday the European Central Bank follows, right at fix time. The first Focus survey after the election comes Tuesday, November third, because Monday is a holiday. The Wednesday after that, the Copom meets. In the Polymarket bets, a rate pause leads, but that's a betting market, not an official forecast.

The next brief will show the effect of the Fed and, if there is one, the name of the new finance minister.
