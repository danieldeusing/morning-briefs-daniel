Where we stand & why: day three

[BR] FX in detail · Wed Oct 28 · Diff vs. Mon Real calm, rest nervous

The dollar closed Wednesday at R$ 5.3466 (−0.04%, InfoMoney); Agência Brasil reports R$ 5.34 (−0.19%).[1][2] The difference comes from two rate variants (spot and commercial). Both say the same: the real held Tuesday's recovery. That puts it right on the SocGen support at 5.34.[14]

Chain 1 — the fix photographs a calm moment, then the peak comes.

ECB measures ~10:15 BRT, dollar ~5.33→ Fix 5.9307→ Dollar rises to 5.4005 by 13:36→ Close 5.3466, TE close EUR/BRL 5.9665

The ECB series implies a dollar of about 5.327 at 10:15 BRT (own calculation: fix ÷ EUR/USD).[4][6] After that it rose to 5.4005 by 13:36 (+0.97% vs the prior close).[1] That is why the fix sits below the closing level: 0.36% lower against the InfoMoney close, 0.60% against the TE close.[5] On Monday it was the other way round. What this means for you: the fix is a one-minute snapshot. On volatile days, an afternoon quote can be 0.5% better or worse.

Chain 2 — the Fed pulls long rates up, not the real.

Fed: pause, 2 votes for +25 bp→ US 10-year rate ~5.02%→ DI Jan/35 +21 bp to 15.12%→ short end flat, real flat

The Fed left its policy rate at 3.75–4.00%. Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack wanted to raise it by 0.25 points.[10] The US 10-year rate stood at 5.02% according to Rio Times (one source).[11] In Brazil, the DI for January 2035 rose to 15.12%; the DI for January 2028 stayed at 14.385%.[8] The curve is getting steeper.

Steep yield curve: long loans cost much more than short ones. Like a dealer who gives you a fair price for tomorrow, but wants a hefty markup on a ten-year contract — because he doesn't trust the future. For the real, the short end matters most: that is where the rate comes from that investors earn on a few months in reais. It stayed stable, so the real stayed stable too.

Chain 3 — banks drag the stock market to a two-month low.

Itaú BBA drops buy rating on Bradesco→ Bank stocks lead the losses→ Ibovespa −2.68%, 171,006→ EWZ in New York −1.98%

The Ibovespa closed at 171,006 points, its lowest level since Aug 20. Since the last close before the election, that is −16.6% (own calculation).[2] According to an InfoMoney headline, Itaú BBA dropped its buy rating on Bradesco and sees a worse 2027.[12] Bank stocks led the losses.[11] On Oct 26, foreigners pulled R$ 2.846 billion out of B3 equities.[2]

What is missing: a name. Fonteles, governor of Piauí, leads the headlines; Alckmin and Renan Filho are also mentioned. According to PlatôBR, Lula picks his ministers only after "miniférias"; O Globo writes that he is weighing whether the savings package or the name comes first. These are headlines, not an official announcement.[12]

My dissent: the real is too calm

Three markets, two opinions

Stocks fall, long rates rise, credit insurance (CDS) gets pricier — only the real acts as if nothing happened. The reason is the rate: the short end of the DI curve stays high, and that keeps investors in the real. My view: the FX market is the most optimistic of the three, and that won't last long if Lula takes his time. Every day without a name but with new costs (debt relief for micro-entrepreneurs R$ 18.7 billion, loan to the postal service R$ 7 billion) supports the skeptics. Until a finance minister is named, I see the risk to the upside: dollar 5.45–5.60, EUR/BRL ~6.07–6.23 (own calculation). I am wrong if Fonteles comes quickly and presents a fiscal law — then the SocGen support at 5.24 (EUR/BRL ~5.83) is realistic.[14]

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000, the 5.9307 fix computes to R$ 5,931. That is about R$ 322 less than at Monday's peak fix (6.2523), but about R$ 282 more than before the runoff (5.6485). Yesterday the fix was below the closing level; on a day like that, an afternoon quote can be slightly better than the fix. Today the ECB acts directly on your rate via the euro. It's Thursday, October twenty-ninth. On the third day after the runoff, the real stayed calm. The dollar closed on the B3 at about five point three four reais, nearly unchanged. The European Central Bank's fix slipped slightly to five point nine three. Compared with before the election, though, your rate is still about five percent higher.

Yesterday the fix came in below the closing level. The central bank measures in the morning, Brazilian time. After that, the dollar climbed almost one percent by early afternoon and only partly came back. The lesson: the fix is just a snapshot, and on volatile days an afternoon quote can look a bit different.

The rest of the market was jittery. The stock exchange in São Paulo lost almost three percent and closed at its lowest level since August 20. Since the election, that's about seventeen percent. Bank stocks led the losses. Long-term rates kept rising, partly because the U.S. Federal Reserve paused, but two members voted for a hike.

So why did the real stay so calm? Short-term interest in Brazil is high and stable, and that keeps investors in the real. My view: right now, the currency market is the most optimistic of the three. As long as Lula doesn't name a finance minister and keeps signing off on new costs, I see the risk leaning toward a higher rate.

There is still no finance minister. Rafael Fonteles, the governor of Piauí, leads the headlines. According to reports, Lula wants to choose his team only after a short vacation. That is not yet officially confirmed.

Among the banks, there has been no new, verifiable target since Tuesday. Commerzbank stays at five point two zero on the dollar for year-end, but only with a credible fiscal reform. I couldn't verify headlines about targets from Bank of America and Deutsche Bank, so I'm not weighting them.

My outlook, as confidence and not as a market number: today between five point nine zero and six point zero three, most likely five point nine five. Over the next seven days, most likely five point nine seven. In thirty days, most likely six point zero zero. Over three to six months, most likely six point one zero, and over twelve months, most likely six point two five.

Today at ten fifteen Brazilian time, the European Central Bank makes its decision, almost exactly when the fix is measured. A pause is expected. On Friday, the debt data for September comes out. The first Focus survey after the election arrives Tuesday, November third, and the day after, Copom decides.
