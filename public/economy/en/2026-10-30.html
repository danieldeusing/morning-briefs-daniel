Where we stand & why: fourth trading day

[BR] FX in detail · Thu Oct 29 · Diff to Mon the real gives up its calm

The dollar closed on Thursday at R$ 5.3875 (+0.76%). InfoMoney and Agência Brasil report the same number.[1][2] That puts the dollar back above the SocGen support at 5.34.[15] Yesterday I wrote the real was "too calm". It has made up part of that.

Chain 1 — the fix photographs the morning, the evening is more expensive.

ECB measures ~10:15 BRT, dollar ~5.352→ Fix 5.9563→ Dollar climbs to 5.4302 by 15:26→ Close 5.3875 → cross 5.996

The ECB series shows a dollar of 5.3516 at the measurement time (fix ÷ EUR/USD).[4][6] After that it climbed to 5.4302 by 15:26.[1] Calculated with the close, your rate was at ~6.00 in the evening, 0.67% above the fix — over my 0.5% warning line. That is not a data error, but two photos at two times of day. What this means for you: the starting point for today is closer to 5.99 than 5.96.

Chain 2 — the dollar falls worldwide, but rises against the real.

No finance minister + IFI: R$ 40 bn unfunded→ Fiscal premium rises→ Real −0.76%, even though DXY falls→ EUR/BRL rises, euro flat

The US data were strong: GDP grew 3.4% in the third quarter (expected 3.2%), consumer prices (PCE) rose 0.5% in September, core 0.3%.[1] Still, the DXY stood at ~101.8 on Friday morning (Thursday 17:07, per InfoMoney: 101.96, −0.16%).[7] InfoMoney names fiscal worry and a government that is "in no hurry" with its new economic team as the reason for the real's weakness.[1] The independent fiscal institute IFI expects R$ 40 bn for campaign promises that are in neither the 2026 nor the 2027 budget.[14]

Fiscal premium: the part of the dollar rate that investors demand on top because they fear excessive government spending. Like a deposit a landlord raises when the tenant pays bills late. Yesterday the deposit went up, even though the dollar got cheaper everywhere else.

Chain 3 — the ECB holds, Lagarde leaves, the euro doesn't flinch.

ECB: pause at 2.50%→ Lagarde resigns effective Feb 7, 2027→ EUR/USD fix 1.1130 (previous day 1.1133)→ your rate moves only via the real

The ECB left the deposit rate, main refinancing rate and marginal lending rate at 2.50%, 2.65% and 2.90%. It writes that energy prices keep rising, which raises inflation risk and lowers growth; it sets no rate path.[10] Already at 11:00 CET it announced that Lagarde will resign on Feb 7, 2027.[11] The euro stayed "virtually unchanged" at 1.1150 afterward; Reuters reports a slightly weaker euro after the decision.[12] What this means for you: the euro was no factor yesterday. It becomes one once names for Lagarde's successor start circulating.

What is missing: a finance minister. Money Times reports Lula wants to invite Fonteles and give an address to the nation next week. Broadcast reports Lula will decide on ministries only from December. Fonteles says he "never spoke with Lula or anyone" about posts. These are headlines, not an announcement.[13]

It's Friday, October thirtieth. On the fourth trading day after the runoff, the Brazilian real gave ground again. The dollar closed at B3 at five point three eight eight reais, up almost three-quarters of a percent. The European Central Bank's fix rose to five point nine five six.

But the fix only captures the morning. After that, the dollar kept climbing into the afternoon. Calculated with the closing rate, your rate was about six point zero zero in the evening. That's more than half a percent above the fix. The numbers aren't wrong; they're two snapshots taken at two different times.

The reason is what makes this interesting. The American data were strong, yet the dollar fell worldwide for the third day running. Only against the real did it rise. That means the premium is coming from Brazil. There's still no finance minister, and the independent fiscal institute puts campaign promises that aren't in the budget at forty billion reais. Stocks fell for the fourth day in a row, down about seventeen percent since the election.

In Frankfurt, the European Central Bank left interest rates unchanged. Earlier in the morning, it announced that Christine Lagarde will step down in February 2027. The euro barely moved anyway. So your rate moved only through the real.

On the finance minister, there are two conflicting reports. Money Times says Lula wants to invite Rafael Fonteles next week and give an address to the nation. Broadcast says Lula won't decide until December. Fonteles himself says he hasn't spoken with anyone about posts. I think a longer wait is more likely, and that points to a higher rate.

Among the banks, there's no new, verifiable target. I still couldn't confirm a headline about a Bank of America target for 2027, so I'm not giving it any weight.

Here's my outlook, as confidence and not as a market number: today between five point nine three and six point zero seven, most likely five point nine nine. Over the next seven days, most likely six point zero zero. In thirty days, most likely six point zero two. Over three to six months, most likely six point one zero, and over twelve months, most likely six point two five.
