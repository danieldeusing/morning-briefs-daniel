Where we stand & why: the weekly review

[BR] FX in detail · Fri Oct 30 · Diff vs. Mon the shock became a trend

The dollar closed Friday at R$ 5.4460 (+1.09%), up 9.15% on the week — the biggest gain since October 2008.[1][2] On Monday it looked like a shock that would fade. Since Thursday the dollar has been rising again: the shock became a trend.

Chain 1 — without a finance minister the premium keeps growing.

Talks on the Fazenda only after COP31→ Fiscal premium rises→ Dollar +1.09%, DXY flat→ EUR/BRL fix 6.0692

According to Poder360, Lula will discuss major government changes only after COP31 in Türkiye (summit on Nov 11 and 12); Alckmin is also considered a candidate.[9] CBN reports that Lula is annoyed at Fonteles' demand for a free hand ("carta branca").[10] The DXY stayed flat at ~102.[7] So the premium comes from Brazil.

Fiscal premium: the part of the dollar rate that investors charge on top because they fear excessive government spending. Like a deposit a landlord keeps raising as long as the tenant won't say how he plans to pay his bills. Every week without a plan raises the deposit a bit.

Chain 2 — before the long weekend everyone hedges.

Mon Nov 2 Finados, B3 closed→ Companies buy dollars as protection→ Peak 5.463 around 16:00 BRT→ Part of the gain may reverse Tue

Agência Brasil cites hedging ahead of the long weekend and a tech sell-off in New York (S&P 500 −1.82%).[2] The PTAX of 5.4212 (13:04 BRT) is the October reference for company balance sheets.[3] What this means for you: part of Friday's gain is hedging, not new fear — it can flow back on Tuesday.

Chain 3 — the euro helped a little this time.

Euro-area GDP Q3 +0.4% (expected 0.2%)→ ECB hike in Dec. 86% priced in→ EUR/USD fix 1.1172 (prior day 1.1130)→ +0.4% on your rate

Euro-area GDP grew 0.4% in the third quarter instead of 0.2%; Panetta (ECB) considers further hikes possible.[12] According to BBH, a hike to 2.75% in December is 86% priced in.[13] The ECB euro rose from 1.1130 to 1.1172.[4][6] Of the +1.90% in the fix, about 0.4 points came from the euro and the rest from the real. After the fix the euro gave back some gains (~1.1140 at 14:07 GMT), which is why the evening close was 6.0611.[12][5]

My dissent: the market is pricing more rate risk than will come

Itaú vs. the yield curve · Update since Fri

According to Bloomberg Línea, the yield curve says: Selic at around 15% at end-2027; between the two rounds of voting it was still 12%.[11] Itaú says: 13.75% through end-2027, so no hike.[14] My view: I am closer to Itaú. The Copom is arguing over a pause or a cut, not over a hike.[11] If a minister with a plan is in place in November, part of the premium disappears quickly; UBS interlocutors then see the real heading toward 5.20.[36] But not this week: no name will come before COP31. Until then I consider EUR/BRL above 6.00 more likely. I am wrong if the Copom signals a hike on Wednesday — then the curve is right and the real stabilizes anyway, because rates rise.

It's Saturday, October thirty-first. The first week after the runoff is over, and for the real it was the worst since October 2008. The dollar closed Friday at five point four four six reais, up a little more than one percent and the highest close of the year. For the full week it climbed a little more than nine percent.

Your rate followed. The European Central Bank's fix jumped to six point zero six nine two, nearly two percent higher than the day before. This time the fix and the evening rate line up; in the evening the euro was at about six point zero six reais. A small part of the gain came from the euro, which rose after solid growth data from the euro area. The bulk came from the weak real.

The reason is still in Brasília. According to Poder360, Lula doesn't want to discuss the finance minister seriously until after the climate summit in Türkiye, meaning after November twelfth. He's also annoyed at Rafael Fonteles' demand for a free hand. On top of that came Friday's hedging ahead of the long weekend, since Monday is a holiday.

Itaú, by contrast, sees thirteen point seven five, so no hike. Itaú raised its dollar target to five point four zero for the end of 2026 and five point six zero for the end of 2027. That's the only fully documented new bank target since the election.

Worth noting: the dollar now sits above the highest answer in the last Focus survey. The new survey comes out Tuesday. On Wednesday the Copom decides; most expect a pause at thirteen point seven five percent. A pause would more likely support the real.

My outlook, as confidence and not a market figure: for Monday's fix, most likely six point zero six. Over the next seven days, most likely six point one zero. In thirty days, most likely six point zero eight. Three to six months out, most likely six point two zero, and twelve months out, most likely six point three five.
