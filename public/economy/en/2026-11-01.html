The Weekend: The Battle for the Fazenda

[BR] FX in detail · Sat Oct 31–Sun Nov 1 · Diff to Sat no rate, but new signals

What sets the first price on Monday and Tuesday came from politics over the weekend. Important: I know most of the reports only as a headline; I could not read the article text. I quote only what the headline says.

Chain 1 — Fonteles says "privileges," the market hears "revenue."

Fonteles: balance through "privileges," not social programs→ Saving more via tax breaks than via spending→ needs Congress, works slowly→ Fiscal premium stays

Estadão headlines on Sunday: “Lula fará equilíbrio fiscal com corte em privilégios, não em programas sociais, diz Rafael Fonteles".[8] What exactly "privileges" means is not in the headline. Boulos (General Secretariat) gets more specific on Saturday: “temos quase R$ 600 bilhões por ano em desonerações fiscais"; R$61 billion alone are parliamentary allocations (emendas).[11] My reading: both mainly mean the revenue side. That is politically tough, because every tax break has a lobby in Congress. The market wanted to hear a spending rule.

Desonerações (tax breaks): exemptions that let certain sectors pay less tax. Like discount cards the state handed out to friends. Taking them back brings in money — but every cardholder fights back, and it takes time.
Fiscal premium: the part of the dollar rate that investors demand on top out of fear of excessive government spending. Like a deposit a landlord raises as long as the tenant does not say how he will pay his bills.

Chain 2 — Alckmin would be the all-clear you don't want.

Citi: Alckmin "mais ao gosto do mercado"→ Name at the Fazenda→ Premium falls→ Real stronger, EUR/BRL lower

Valor (Oct 31): “Para o Citi, Alckmin na Fazenda estaria mais ao gosto do mercado".[9] Congresswoman Tabata Amaral also backs him (Estadão, Oct 31).[10] Kassab (PSD) offers closeness, “se medidas econômicas forem adotadas", and wants profiles like Palocci or Meirelles at the head of the economy.[12] For you this means: a name like Alckmin would be the strongest reason for a falling EUR/BRL. According to Poder360, though, the talks come only after COP31 (Nov 11–12).[13]

Chain 3 — Monday: fix without a Brazilian market.

Mon Nov 2 Finados, B3 closed→ Real trades only thinly abroad→ Weekend headlines have more impact→ ECB fix can swing randomly

The ECB publishes its fix normally on Monday; B3 stays closed, and there is no PTAX. Friday's hedging — Agência Brasil named purchases ahead of the long weekend as a driver — can only flow back on Tuesday.[3]

My Dissent: "Privileges" Are Not Enough

What the market needs vs. what Brasília offers · Update since Sat

Itaú considers a fiscal adjustment of about 4 percentage points of GDP necessary.[15] The total deficit is at 9.51% of GDP (12 months to September).[32] A plan that expressly exempts social programs and depends on Congress does not close this gap quickly. My view: the weekend was slightly positive for the real (Alckmin talk, a commitment to balance), but not enough to break the premium. Boulos' word "chantagem" shows that part of the government sees the market as an opponent.[11] I am wrong if Lula names Alckmin or a name with a free hand before COP31 — then EUR/BRL falls quickly toward 5.85.
