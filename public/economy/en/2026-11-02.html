Where we stand & why

[BR] FX in detail · Sat Oct 31–Mon Nov 2 · Monday = full rewrite

No Brazilian trading since Friday — yet the price for Tuesday is being formed right now, along three chains.

Chain 1 — An outside rate plan, two days before the Copom.

Lara Resende: Selic to ~7.5% in 12 months→ Fear of political rate-setting→ Rate advantage looks less safe, money leaves→ Real weaker, EUR/BRL higher

According to Folha, economist André Lara Resende proposes cutting the Selic gradually to 7.5% over twelve months. A joint fiscal and monetary council would set targets for four to five years.[5] BM&C reports a sharper variant: Selic to 7%, with the FX reserves used to hold up the real.[6] I could not read the Folha text itself. According to Brasil 247, fund managers react with "apreensão" and warn of inflation and depreciation.[7]

Finance Minister Durigan denies it on Sunday: "Nenhuma dessas ideias como tabelamento de juros está na mesa do presidente"; a meeting with Lula is not planned.[5] Gleisi Hoffmann, on the other hand, publicly praises Lara Resende (Valor, UOL). GZH headlines: a "canetaço no juro" — a rate cut by stroke of the pen — "tem risco de rebote".[8]

Fiscal dominance: the state is so deeply in debt that politicians push the central bank to keep rates low — so the debt stays cheap. Like parents who let the child set bedtime because arguing is too tiring. It is calm for a while, then the bill comes: here as inflation and a weak real.
Copom & Selic: the Copom is the central bank's rate committee, the Selic the benchmark rate. A high rate is like a high savings-account rate: it attracts foreign money, which has to buy reais. If the rate falls — or people believe politicians are pushing it down — the savings account becomes unattractive and the real weaker.

Chain 2 — The euro leg moves, the real does not.

Iran talks in Doha, Trump: no strikes before the election→ Brent −3%, dollar a bit weaker→ EUR/USD +0.21% to 1.1181→ USD/BRL flat → EUR/BRL cross 6.08

Iran and Qatari mediators negotiated for the third day on Sunday; Trump rules out new strikes only until the election on Tuesday.[15] Brent fell in Asia by 3.24% to $109.76.[14] The euro rose to 1.11813 (+0.21%), while the dollar against the real stayed at 5.4377.[2] That puts the calculated EUR/BRL at 6.0800, just above Friday's fix. Cheaper oil cuts both ways for the real: less inflation, but fewer export dollars.

Thin market: a trading day with few buyers and sellers. Like a flea market in the rain: whoever has to sell cuts the price sharply. Today the offshore rate is only a rough hint.

Chain 3 — Lula promises everything, the minister comes later.

Lula: "everything promised will happen", no social program cut→ Savings plan stays open→ Fazenda name only after Nov 16→ Fiscal premium stays longer

Lula on Sunday: "Tudo aquilo que foi prometido na campanha, pode ficar certo que vai acontecer"; no social program will be cut.[13] According to InfoMoney, Rafael Fonteles is the most-mentioned candidate for the Fazenda; Durigan could stay, and Alckmin is also being considered. Nobody has been formally invited. The decision is to come after Lula's trip abroad, which ends on Nov 16.[12] Update since Sun Yesterday it still said "after COP31". The 2027 budget draft provides for a primary surplus of R$ 18.6 bn.[12]

Fiscal premium: the part of the dollar rate that investors demand on top because they fear too much government spending. Like a deposit a landlord raises as long as the tenant does not say how he will pay his bills.

Copom on Wednesday: the decision comes at 18:30 BRT.[10] Itaú expects a unanimous pause at 13.75%; inflation expectations are "já desancoradas". Bradesco sees a majority in market pricing for an end to the cuts.[10] B3 options: 63.1% (as of ~Oct 28) for a pause; according to Bloomberg Línea, 10 of 18 economists expect a pause.[11][41]

My dissent: the plan is not a plan, but it is a price tag

Why the denial saves the week, but not the premium

My view: I consider it very unlikely (under 10%, my confidence) that the Selic falls to 7.5% in twelve months by council decision. The central bank is formally independent, and the minister disagrees. The signal is what is costly: part of the government would rather push down the rate than spending — Gleisi's praise shows it.[8] Former BCB chief Gustavo Loyola counters: double-digit rates "pelo menos até 2028".[9]

That is why I see the Copom on Wednesday as an independence test. A pause at 13.75% with a hard tone supports the real. A cut would now look like giving in — then EUR/BRL jumps quickly toward 6.25. The backdrop stays nervous: on Oct 26 the real had its worst day since 1999.[16] I am wrong if Lula receives Lara Resende this week and does not clearly reject the plan. Then I expect EUR/BRL above 6.30, even before a Fazenda name.

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000, the last fix (6.0692) yields a calculated R$ 6,069 — about R$ 421 more than before the runoff (5.6485). Today the ECB fixes in a thin market; the live cross is at 6.08. Wednesday evening is the turning point: a pause with a hard tone tends to push your rate down, a cut lifts it. The decision is yours.

It's Monday, November second, and it's a holiday in Brazil. The stock exchange is closed, and there's no central bank reference rate. The last European Central Bank fix still stands at six point zero six nine reais per euro, from Friday. The new fix comes out around noon Brasília time today.

The news of the weekend came from André Lara Resende. According to a report in Folha, he wants to bring the Selic benchmark rate down from thirteen point seven five to about seven point five percent over twelve months. Another outlet even reports seven percent, backed by the foreign-exchange reserves. Fund managers reacted nervously.

Finance Minister Dario Durigan quickly pushed back: a cap on interest rates is not on the president's table, and no meeting is planned. Gleisi Hoffmann praised Lara Resende anyway. My read: the plan itself is unlikely to happen. But it shows that part of the government would rather push down rates than spending. That costs credibility.

That's why the Copom decision on Wednesday at six thirty p.m. becomes a test. Itaú expects a unanimous pause, Bradesco also sees a majority for one, and options on the exchange point to roughly sixty-three percent odds of a pause. A pause with a hard tone would support the real. A cut would now look like giving in.

The rest of the world is calmer. Oil fell by a little over three percent to just under one hundred ten dollars, as Iran and Qatar keep talking. The euro rose slightly, and the dollar index held flat at just under one hundred two. That puts the euro at roughly six point zero eight reais.

At the Finance Ministry, Rafael Fonteles is the most-mentioned candidate. But Lula wants to decide only after his trip, which ends on November sixteenth. Until then, the premium for fiscal risk stays in the price.

My assessment, as confidence and not as a market figure: for today's fix, most likely six point zero eight. Over the next seven days, six point one two. In thirty days, six point one zero. Over three to six months, six point two zero, and over twelve months, six point three five. No bank has published a new target since October thirtieth.

For the exchange: a thousand euros brings roughly six thousand sixty-nine reais at the last fix. Tomorrow's brief will have the first Focus survey after the election and a look ahead to the Copom.
