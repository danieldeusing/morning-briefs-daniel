Where we stand & why

[BR] FX in detail · Mon Nov 2–Tue Nov 3 · Diff vs. Monday

Update since Mon My Monday range for the fix was 6.00–6.15, most likely 6.08. The fix came in at 6.0990: inside the range, 0.02 above the point estimate. The reason for the rest is the euro. Three chains explain the current level.

Chain 1 — The euro carries the fix, the real stands still.

Iran signals → Brent −8%→ Dollar gives way, euro +0.39% (1.1172 → 1.1216)→ Dollar/real almost flat (+0.10%)→ EUR/BRL fix +0.49% to 6.0990

Iran's president Pezeshkian said the Supreme Leader had given the government "certas atribuições" for peace talks; a draft proposal is circulating.[12] By 15:05 BRT, Brent fell 8.15% to $104.18.[12] Europe imports energy, so cheap oil helps the euro. The ECB set EUR/USD at 1.1216, and the dollar against the real (calculated) at 5.4378.[1] This morning the euro is back at 1.1192, right on ING support at 1.1180; ING sees the cap at 1.1250/70.[3][28] That is why the live cross at 6.0864 sits below the fix.[2]

Cross rate: there is hardly any direct trading for euro→real. The price is built via the dollar: euro→dollar, then dollar→real. Like a train trip with a transfer — if either of the two trains gets faster, you arrive sooner, no matter which one.

Chain 2 — The Lara Resende debate makes a rate cut more expensive.

Parts of the PT praise a rate cap, attacks on "the market"→ A cut on Wed would look like giving in→ Pause becomes the favorite (33 of 48)→ Rate advantage stays, real supported

Lara Resende's text (first published Sep 25 on Substack) wants to cut the Selic over roughly twelve months to ~7.5%.[10] Gabrielli, coordinator of Lula's government program, praises the idea; according to Poder360, part of the PT backs the rate cap (headlines only).[11] Lindbergh Farias says of the market: "estão fazendo política e sabotando a nossa economia".[10] Against that, Maílson da Nóbrega: "haverá uma fuga de capitais … O dólar vai pras alturas: 6, 7 reais".[9] Finance Minister Durigan (G1 headline): capping rates or "burning" FX reserves is "not under review".[11] According to headlines, Durigan says no meeting between Lula and Lara Resende is planned.[11]

Copom & Selic: the Copom is the central bank's rate committee, the Selic is the policy rate. A high rate is like a high savings-account rate: it attracts foreign money, which has to buy reais. If people believe politics is pushing the rate down, the savings account becomes unattractive — the real gets weaker.

Chain 3 — The Copom ahead of the pause.

Reuters polled from Oct 28 to Nov 2: 33 of 48 economists expect a pause at 13.75%, only 15 still expect a cut. The median stays at 13.75% at least into Q2 2027.[6] B3 options for a pause: 63% on Oct 29 (Estadão), ~69% on Nov 2 (VEJA, not independently confirmed) — the bet has shifted over time. Before the runoff, over 70% were on a cut.[7][8] According to VEJA, Itaú, BTG Pactual and HSBC now expect a pause (Itaú independently confirmed, BTG and HSBC not).[7] According to Reuters, interest costs the state 9% of GDP, the most since 2016; Oxford Economics expects the sell-off to keep the Copom cautious.[6] Folha reports: Lula rejects a gesture to the market and will not bring forward the Fazenda name.[11]

Fiscal premium: the part of the dollar rate that investors demand on top for fear of excessive government spending. Like a deposit a landlord raises as long as the tenant does not say how he will pay his bills. HSBC (Nov 2) sees this premium as high because Congress has moved to the right and can block reforms.[15]

My dissent: the pause is priced in, the tone is not

Why Wednesday evening matters more for your rate than today

My view: I consider a pause at 13.75% more likely than the market does — about 80% (my confidence, not the 63–69% of the options). After a weekend of rate-cap ideas and PT attacks, a cut would be a gift to the critics of central bank independence. But the market has already largely priced in the pause. For the real, the communiqué therefore matters: a hard tone on inflation expectations and the dollar supports it more than the pause itself.

The euro is the second lever that many overlook. It sits on technical support (1.118), and the ECB is already discussing hikes.[3] If it breaks through, EUR/BRL can fall even though the real does nothing. I am wrong if the Copom cuts or one voice clearly dissents in favor of a cut. Then I expect EUR/BRL above 6.25 this very week (Morgan Stanley's 5.60 × 1.12, own calculation).[19]

Practical for the exchange

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000, Monday's fix (6.0990) yields, on paper, R$6,099 — about R$451 more than before the runoff (5.6485). The peak fix on Oct 26 was 6.2523. Today the Focus matters little, because the spot is already above all the old targets. Wednesday evening remains the turning point: a pause with a hard tone tends to push your rate down, a cut lifts it. The decision is yours.

It's Tuesday, November third. Today the stock exchange in Brazil opens for the first time since Friday, and the first Focus survey after the runoff is due. The European Central Bank's fix from Monday stands at six point zero nine nine zero reais per euro, up almost half a percent from Friday.

What matters is where that comes from. Brazil had a holiday, and the real barely moved. What rose was the euro. The oil price fell about eight percent on Monday, because Iran is signaling peace talks. That helps Europe, and the euro climbed to one point one two. This morning it's a bit lower again, so the calculated rate sits at around six point zero nine.

The debate around André Lara Resende goes on. Part of the PT and Lula's program coordinator Gabrielli praise his idea of cutting interest rates by plan. Former finance minister Maílson da Nóbrega warns that this would bring capital flight, and the dollar would go to six or seven reais. Finance Minister Durigan says, according to G1, that capping interest rates is not under review.

Tomorrow at six thirty p.m., the Copom decides. In a Reuters poll, thirty-three of forty-eight economists expect a pause at thirteen point seven five percent. Exchange options put the odds of a pause between sixty-three and sixty-nine percent. My read: the pause is almost certain, and what counts is the tone. A hard tone supports the real.

In the U.S., voters go to the polls today. Prediction markets see a good three-in-four chance of a Democratic Senate. The dollar index stays flat at just under one hundred two, and a Fed hike in December is priced in at about three-quarters.

My assessment, as confidence and not as a market number: for today's fix, most likely six point zero nine. Over the next few days, six point zero eight, and also six point zero eight in thirty days. For three to six months, six point two zero, and for twelve months, six point three five. Since October thirtieth, no bank has published a new dollar target, and all Focus figures still date from before the election.

For the exchange: a thousand euros at Monday's fix bring in about six thousand and ninety-nine reais, roughly four hundred and fifty more than before the runoff. The next brief comes tomorrow with the new Focus and a look ahead to the Copom decision in the evening.
