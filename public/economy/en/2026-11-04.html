Update since Tue My range for the Tuesday fix was 6.00–6.20, most likely 6.09. The fix came in at 6.1411: within the range, 0.05 above the point estimate. I had seen the real as too stable. Three chains explain where we are.

Chain 1 — The real falls because the market is repricing politics.

Post-election Focus: dollar 5.05 → 5.34→ Lula wants to rebuild the BCB board→ Rate curve +15–17 bp, dollar +0.74% to 5.4863→ EUR/BRL fix +0.69% to 6.1411

The dollar closed at 5.4863, its highest since Dec 30, 2025; the intraday high was 5.509.[6][7][8] The euro actually slipped into the fix (1.1216 → 1.1179); the entire rise came from the real.[1] The Focus raised its dollar targets sharply.[16][19] And Valor reports: Lula is seeking more "heterodox" candidates for four seats on the central bank board by February 2027, to steer the Copom in a new direction; two names this month, with André Roncaglia (IMF) mentioned (Valor only, not independently confirmed).[11] Folha writes of six directors by end-2027 (headline only).[36] Durigan defended a "gradual" adjustment and called Lara Resende's ideas flawed.[8]

PTAX: the central bank's official dollar rate, an average of four readings in the morning. Like an official daily price at the farmers' market: contracts and taxes use it, not the 5 p.m. price.

Chain 2 — The US election lifts the euro, not the real.

Democrats win House and Senate per NPR/AP→ less US spending, US yields fall→ DXY −0.4%, euro 1.1179 → ~1.123→ Live cross ~6.16 with a flat real

According to NPR (AP count), the Democrats hold the Senate majority; in the House they have 218 of 342 counted seats per Decision Desk HQ.[10][9] CBA expects further dollar selling because a divided government will curb Trump's spending.[9] ING (Nov 3) sees EUR/USD support at 1.120, but "no radical shifts in sentiment".[27]

Cross rate: there is hardly any direct trading between euro and real. The price is built via the dollar: euro→dollar, then dollar→real. Like a train trip with a change. Today both trains are running faster: the euro is rising, the real is falling. Both lift your rate.

Chain 3 — The Copom tonight.

B3 options as of Nov 3: 72.5% hold at 13.75%, 26% cut to 13.50%. A week earlier it was 63.1% (close Oct 26).[13] Valor survey: 72 of 105 for a hold (Valor only, not independently confirmed); Reuters (Nov 2): 33 of 48.[14][15] Citi (Leonardo Porto) calculates that the dollar jump from ~5.15 to 5.40 since the last Copom costs roughly 0.4 percentage points more inflation (Valor only, not independently confirmed).[14] Dissent: SulAmérica still expects −25 bp today, then a pause in December (Valor only, not independently confirmed).[14]

Contradiction The Focus median (data Oct 30) shows 13.50% for today, i.e. a cut.[18] Options, Reuters and Valor show a majority for a hold. The Focus median moves slowly because not every participant updates every week. Options are real money — I weight them higher.
Copom & Selic: the Copom is the central bank's rate-setting committee, the Selic the benchmark rate. A high rate is like a high savings-account rate: it attracts foreign money that has to buy reais. Copom options are B3 bets on the outcome.

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000, the Tuesday fix (6.1411) yields, on paper, R$ 6,141 — about R$ 493 more than before the runoff (5.6485). Today's fix falls before the Copom and carries the midterm euro (~6.16). It's Wednesday, November fourth. Tonight Brazil's central bank rate committee, the Copom, decides on the benchmark interest rate. Tuesday's fix from the European Central Bank came in at six point one four one one reais per euro, up almost seven tenths of a percent from Monday.

This time the jump came from the real. The dollar closed in São Paulo at five point four eight six three reais, the highest since late December 2025. The first Focus survey after the runoff raised its end-of-2026 dollar forecast from five point oh five to five point three four. And according to Valor, Lula is looking for candidates for four seats on the central bank board who would cut rates faster. Only Valor reports this; it is not independently confirmed.

A second force arrived overnight. In the United States, Democrats won the House, and the Senate per NPR/AP. The dollar index fell to a five-week low, and the euro climbed to about one point one two three. For you, that means both sides are lifting your rate right now. The calculated rate this morning is around six point one six.

Now to the Copom. Options on the exchange price in a seventy-two point five percent chance of a hold at thirteen point seven five percent. Itaú expects that rate to last through the end of 2027, and per Valor so do BTG and Morgan Stanley. Only the Focus median still shows a cut, probably because many responses are older. My read: a hold at about eighty percent. What matters is the tone, and that only kicks in from Thursday, because today's fix comes before the decision.

In the bond market, fixed-rate yields rose fifteen to seventeen basis points. The inflation-linked bond due 2035 pays almost eight percent above inflation. Stocks fell for the sixth day in a row. In oil, there is a new report out of Iran about four tankers destroyed near Hormuz, so far from a single source only.

My outlook, as confidence and not as a market figure: for today's fix, most likely six point one six. By Friday, six point one four, and in thirty days, six point one two. Over three to six months, six point two two, and over twelve months, six point three five. No bank publishes a euro-real target; these numbers are my own calculation from dollar targets and euro targets.

For the exchange: a thousand euros at Tuesday's fix bring roughly six thousand one hundred forty-one reais, about four hundred ninety more than before the runoff. Tomorrow's brief will bring the Copom result and the real's first reaction.
