Where we stand & why

[BR] FX in detail · Wed Nov 4–Thu Nov 5 · Diff vs. previous day

Update since Wed My range for the Wed fix was 6.10–6.22, most likely 6.16. The fix came in at 6.1579 — almost exactly on the mark. Three chains explain where we are.

Chain 1 — The euro lifts the fix even though the real gets stronger.

Democrats win House and Senate→ DXY at five-week low→ ECB euro 1.1179 → 1.1267 (+0.79%)→ EUR/BRL fix +0.27% despite real gains

The euro rose 0.79% by the fix, while the dollar derived from the ECB fixes fell only 0.51% to 5.4654.[1] Euro-area inflation adds support: 4.0% in October, the highest in three years. An ECB hike in December is priced in at ~72%.[9]

Cross rate: there is hardly any direct trading for euro→real. The price is built via the dollar: euro→dollar, then dollar→real. Like a train trip with a transfer: yesterday the euro train ran faster than the real train slowed down.

Chain 2 — The real recovers because oil and US rates fall.

Brent −8.7% to $94.62→ less inflation fear in Brazil→ Yield curve −30 to −36 bp→ Dollar −0.65% to 5.4518

The dollar fell for the first time after three rises; the daily high was 5.498.[6][7][8] So the 5.50 level held for the second time (Tue 5.509). Morgan Stanley had expected 5.50, then 5.60, on Oct 27.[22] Brent fell on hopes that the new Congress would end the Iran war.[35] All of this happened before the Copom decision after market close.

Chain 3 — The Copom holds and closes the door only halfway.

Selic 13.75%, unanimous→ Sentence about further cuts dropped→ Rate advantage stays high for longer→ Support for the real, as long as fiscal policy holds

The central bank calls the Selic "substantially contractionary," sees an "upside asymmetry" in inflation risks, and explicitly names a lastingly weaker exchange rate as a risk.[10] The sentence that the "overall size of the cycle" depends on new data is gone.[13] The inflation forecast for Q2 2028 rose from 3.1% (per EQI) to 3.5%.[10][14] And the forecast starts from a dollar of only R$ 5.20 — about 4.6% below Wednesday's close (own calculation). If the real stays this weak, actual inflation is higher.[10]

"Substantially contractionary": the rate is already braking the economy hard. Like a driver standing firmly on the brake: they are saying "I don't need more brake right now" — but also "I'm not letting go yet."

The banks read this differently. Santander sees a pause, not an end; XP expects 13.75% through end of 2027; BV and Daycoval see cuts from mid-2027.[15][17] Genial is the only one to warn: the weak real could force a hike "in the next meetings."[15] Nomad: the rate advantage supports the real, but fiscal signals matter more.[16]

Contradiction Agência Brasil: the Copom "surprised."[11] Money Times: "bola cantada," ~70% expected.[12] Both are true: the Focus median (Oct 30) showed 13.50%, while the options (72.5%) showed a pause. For the FX market the pause was priced in.

Practical for the exchange

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000, the Wed fix (6.1579) yields arithmetically R$ 6,158 — about R$ 509 more than before the runoff (Oct 23: 5.6485). My view: until Sunday the risk is unevenly distributed. A disappointing Lula lifts your rate by perhaps 0.05–0.15; a credible plan can push it down faster, by 0.12–0.18, because of the one-sided bets. The decision is yours.

It's Thursday, November fifth. Last night the Copom held its benchmark rate unanimously at thirteen point seven five percent. After five straight cuts, that's the first pause. Wednesday's fix from the European Central Bank stands at six point one five seven nine reais per euro.

This time the move came from the euro. After the Democrats won both chambers of Congress, the dollar fell worldwide, and the euro rose by almost eight tenths of a percent. The real got stronger too, but by less. The dollar closed in São Paulo at five point four five one eight reais, its first drop after three straight gains. The five-fifty level held for the second time.

Now to the Copom. The central bank now calls the rate substantially contractionary, and it dropped the line about further cuts. It sees inflation risks tilted to the upside and explicitly names a lastingly weak real as a risk. Yet its forecast starts from a dollar of just five point two zero. XP and Itaú expect the rate to stay at thirteen point seven five through the end of 2027. Only Genial warns of a hike.

Oil also fell sharply on Wednesday, with Brent down almost nine percent to ninety-four dollars. That pushed Brazilian interest rates down by as much as thirty-six basis points, and the stock market rose for the first time after six losing days. In October, a net eighteen billion dollars flowed into the country, more than in any other month this year.

The biggest event of the week is Sunday. Lula speaks on TV and radio and plans to announce spending-cut and revenue measures. In an XP survey, seventy percent of fund managers are betting on a rising dollar. When almost everyone is on the same side, a bit of good news can lift the real fast. For you, that would mean a lower rate.

My outlook, as confidence and not a market figure: for today's fix, most likely six point one five. By next Thursday, six point one two, in thirty days six point one zero. Three to six months out, six point two zero, and twelve months out, six point three five. No bank gives a euro-real target; these numbers are my own calculation from dollar targets and euro targets, and no firm has published a new dollar target since October thirtieth.

For the exchange: a thousand euros at Wednesday's fix gets you about six thousand one hundred fifty-eight reais, roughly five hundred more than before the runoff.
