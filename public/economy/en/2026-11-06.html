Where we stand & why

[BR] FX in detail · Thu Nov 5–Fri Nov 6 · Diff from previous day

Update since Thu My range for Thursday's fix was 6.09–6.21, most likely 6.15. The fix came in at 6.1355 — inside the range, 0.015 below the point estimate. Three chains explain the current level.

Chain 1 — The euro lowers the fix, the real stays calm at first.

Hawks at the Fed and BoE→ Euro 1.1267 → 1.1224 (−0.38%)→ ECB dollar 5.4664 (+0.02%)→ EUR/BRL fix −0.36%

At the fix around 10:15 BRT, almost only the euro moved.[1] It came back from a one-month high; Fed members Barkin and Hammack spoke in favor of gradual hikes (according to FXStreet, Nov 5).[8]

Chain 2 — After the fix, the shock comes from New York.

Fear of AI regulation→ Nasdaq −5.5%→ Investors sell emerging-market currencies→ Dollar +0.67% to 5.4876

The Nasdaq lost 5.5%, the S&P 500 4.4%, the Dow 3.4% — the worst day since April 2025.[9] Trigger: the worry that the new Democratic Congress will slow AI and data centers (the corporate side is covered in the ai-usecases brief). Agência Brasil: investors "sought shelter in the dollar," including against emerging-market currencies.[7] The dollar closed at 5.4876; Folha calls it the highest close of the year.[6] That is checked only against Nov 3 (5.4863), not against Oct 26. The intraday high of 5.495 stayed below 5.50 — for the third time in a row.[18]

Risk-off: Investors sell everything that can swing and flee to the safest harbor, the dollar. Like kids on a playground who all run to the same shelter when it thunders. The real is then a jungle gym that people leave first.

Chain 3 — Rates fall anyway: the market hopes for spending plans.

Folha: permanent spending freezes under review→ less fiscal fear→ LTN Jan 2029 −10 bp to 14.82%→ Real steadier than the day suggests

According to Folha, the government is considering deducting frozen spending from the following years' limit as well, and aiming for the middle of the primary target: in 2027 that would be at least R$ 8.5 billion surplus.[14] That is only a study. After the meeting at the Alvorada, Durigan spoke of a "matching diagnosis"; he will present measures with Moretti only after Lula's trip, "on the 15th."[13] The fact that the curve fell while the real weakened shows that the pressure came from outside.[31]

Primary surplus: the state takes in more than it spends — not counting interest payments. Like a household that has something left at the end of the month before the loan payment goes out. The bigger the remainder, the less the debt has to grow.

What Sunday means now. Lula recorded the address on Thursday; it airs Sunday evening. Valor learned that he reaffirms fiscal responsibility and social pledges, but announces no concrete measures.[12] On Thursday Lula said: "Nobody invests in a disorderly economy."[15]

My view Sunday loses its punch. Without numbers there is no reason for a quick real rally — the real test moves to ~Nov 15. That favors a fix that stays on the high side until then.

It's Friday, November sixth. Thursday's fix from the European Central Bank stands at six point one three five five reais per euro, a drop of a little over a third of a percent. That came from the euro, which eased slightly. The real was almost unchanged up to the fix.

After the fix, sentiment turned. In New York the Nasdaq plunged five point five percent, its worst day since April 2025. Investors fear the new Democratic Congress will regulate artificial intelligence more tightly. Money fled into the dollar worldwide. In São Paulo the dollar closed at five point four eight seven six reais, the highest close of the year according to Folha. But the five point five zero mark held for the third time.

That's why the rate this morning sits above the fix, at about six point one six. The gap of just under half a percent is not a data error. The fix was simply measured before the sell-off.

Here's the interesting part: Brazilian interest rates fell anyway. According to Folha, the government is weighing making spending freezes permanent and aiming for a small surplus in 2027. So the pressure on the real came from outside, not from Brasília.

On to Lula. He has recorded his television address, and it airs Sunday evening. According to Valor, he commits to budget discipline but announces no concrete measures. Finance Minister Durigan doesn't plan to present those until around November fifteenth. That takes some of the punch out of Sunday; the real test comes a week later.

Among the banks, XP raises its dollar target to five point three zero for the end of the year and five point five zero for the end of 2027. Santander expects the benchmark rate of thirteen point seven five percent to hold through the whole first half of 2027. Bradesco reads the Copom statement as a quiet plea to the government for help.

Globally, the Bank of England raised its rate to four percent, the first hike since 2023. Oil climbed back to nearly one hundred dollars after attacks on tankers. At ten thirty a.m. Brasília time today, the US jobs data comes out, after today's fix.

My assessment, as confidence and not a market figure: for today's fix, most likely six point one six. By next Friday, six point one eight, in thirty days, six point one zero. Over three to six months, six point one five, and over twelve months, six point three zero. These euro-real numbers are my own calculation from dollar targets and euro targets.
