Where we stand & why

[BR] FX in detail · Fri Nov 6 · Diff vs. previous day

Update since Fri My range for the Fri fix was 6.10–6.22, most likely 6.16. The fix came in at 6.1449 — within the range, 0.015 below the point estimate.[1] Three chains explain the week.

Chain 1 — US jobs lift the dollar against everyone, not just the real.

+165,000 jobs instead of ~65,000→ Fed hike Dec.: 67–75% → 77–80%→ US 2-year yield 4.47 → 4.52%→ DXY +0.72%→ Real −0.44%, euro −0.5%→ EUR/BRL ≈ flat

The US created 165,000 jobs in October; roughly 59,000–65,000 were expected. September was revised up from 29,000 to 60,000, and unemployment fell to 4.1%.[10] Traders now put the odds of a Fed hike in December at ~80% (Reuters) or 77% (CME via Valor).[11][8] The dollar closed at 5.5115, the highest close of 2026; the PTAX of 5.5013 exceeded the Oct 26 high (5.5007).[6][5] But the euro fell too: below 1.115.[3] The fix was measured ~10:15 BRT, shortly before the data. The evening cross after the data is still at ~6.15, almost exactly on the fix: the euro fell as hard as the real.

Fed hike priced in: Traders bet money on what the US central bank will do; from the prices of those bets you can read a probability. Like betting odds before a soccer game: they are not a result, but they show what the crowd expects.

Chain 2 — The real is weaker than its neighbors.

Fiscal uncertainty after the election→ Risk premium in the real stays→ Real −0.4%, Mexican peso +0.8%

Against the Mexican peso the dollar fell 0.81% on Friday — against the real it rose.[8] Valor: "the uncertainty keeps risk premiums in the Brazilian currency high."[8]

Chain 3 — Rates fall all week.

BC does not raise the Selic+ first savings signals→ DI curve ~−40 bp since Tue→ Ibovespa +1.39%

Since Tuesday, interest-rate futures have fallen by about 40 basis points according to Valor. Reasons: the market no longer believes in a quick Selic hike, and the government is showing first signs of willingness to cut spending.[14] One fund manager warns there that this rate advantage is "fragile if the government does not deliver a relevant savings package." The stock market rose 1.39% to 168,498 points.[7] Rates down, dollar up: the pressure came from abroad.

Basis point (bp): one hundredth of a percentage point; 40 bp is 0.40 percentage points. Like cents on the euro — the smallest unit in which rates are counted.

What Lula said on Friday. At the CNPE energy council he promised "fiscal and social responsibility" and said the poorest would not pay the bill for the adjustment. On the public debt: "it is not even everything it appears to be."[12] Gross debt stands at 82.9% of GDP according to Money Times, more than 11 points higher than at the start of his third term.

My view The speech cuts both ways. "Responsibility" reassures, but whoever plays down the debt signals little pressure to save. For Sunday I expect the same pattern: commitment without numbers (Valor, Nov 5).[15] Without numbers, no quick real rally — the real test remains ~Nov 15.

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000 the Fri fix (6.1449) yields, on paper, R$ 6,145 — about R$ 496 more than before the runoff (Oct 23: 5.6485). My view: the rate is high and stable because two forces balance each other — a strong dollar pushes down the real and the euro at the same time. The risk to your rate now lies mainly on Brazil's side: a credible savings plan around Nov 15 can push it down quickly by 0.15–0.20; a weak one lifts it more slowly. The decision is yours.

It's Saturday, November seventh. There's no trading today and no new fix. Friday's fix from the European Central Bank stands at six point one four four nine reais per euro, a small gain of zero point one five percent. It's good until Monday morning.

The big moment on Friday came out of the United States. The economy added one hundred sixty-five thousand jobs in October, when economists had expected only a little over sixty thousand. Traders now see almost an eighty percent chance that the Federal Reserve raises interest rates in December. The dollar rose around the world.

What matters more for you: the euro fell almost as much as the real. So your rate stayed nearly flat, at around six point one five. Every cross-check lands close to the fix, so there's no data error.
