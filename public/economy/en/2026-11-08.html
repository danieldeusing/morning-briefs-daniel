Update since Sat No trading since Friday evening. What is new: the situation before Lula's speech, US inflation on Tuesday, and the age of the bank targets.[1]

Recap in one sentence: strong US jobs data (+165,000 instead of the expected 59,000) lifted the dollar against all currencies. The real fell 0.44%, the euro ~0.5–0.6% — which is why your rate stayed almost flat.[2][3]

Chain 1 — Lula's speech: lots of tone, few numbers.

Address Sun evening→ Pledge without spending figures→ Risk premium stays→ Mon fix ≈ flat

The sources contradict each other, but in a clear order. On Nov 4, Estadão wrote that Lula would "announce adjustment measures"; the same text also says the measures would come "in the coming weeks".[4] A day later Valor reported that Lula would name no concrete spending cuts.[5] Durigan confirmed: he and Planning Minister Moretti will present the proposals only after the trip, which ends on Nov 15.[6] I follow the newer sources. According to Estadão, the 2027 budget needs an adjustment of at least R$ 40 bn.[4]

Risk premium: the extra return investors demand for a country whose finances are uncertain. Like a higher interest rate for a friend who has already repaid you late twice. As long as the premium stays high, the real stays weak.
My view A speech without numbers solves nothing. The market is waiting for the package around Nov 15. Two headlines (only titles checked) show the dispute: the "social" wing of the government is resisting cuts (Band); Lula is said to be betting on allies rather than the market in choosing ministers (Bloomberg).[7] If Lula sounds more social than frugal today, the dollar rises on Monday. That lifts your rate.

Chain 2 — Tuesday matters more than Sunday.

US inflation Oct. (Tue 10:30 BRT)→ Fed odds Dec. 77–80% ↑ or ↓→ DXY→ Real and euro in the same direction→ EUR/BRL: net effect small

US inflation for October comes out Tuesday at 08:30 ET (10:30 BRT).[8] Traders now see a Fed hike in December at 77% (CME data per Valor) or ~80% (Reuters per InfoMoney) — both figures are secondhand; the FedWatch tool itself was not reachable.[3][9] A high reading strengthens the dollar — but as on Friday, the real and the euro then fall. For you it is more a source of volatility than a direction.

Inflation rate (CPI/IPCA): how much more expensive a fixed basket of goods has become over a year. Like the price of the same bag of groceries, measured anew each year. When it rises, the central bank is more likely to raise rates.

Chain 3 — Brazil's interest rate holds up the real. On Nov 4 the Copom left the Selic unanimously at 13.75%, ending the cuts that began in April; it gave no hint about the next steps.[10] After subtracting inflation, at 9.05% that is the highest real interest rate among 40 countries (Moneyou).[10] That attracts money. The IPCA arrives Thursday: the 12-month rate was last at 4.62%, above the 4.5% ceiling.[10] A high reading makes rate cuts in 2027 even less likely.
