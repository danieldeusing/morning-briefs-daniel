Where we stand & why

[BR] FX in detail · As of Mon 11/09 · Diff to Monday

The month in one sentence: since Lula's runoff win (10/25) the fix jumped from 5.6485 to 6.2523, fell to 5.93 and has been rising again since 10/28.[1]

Update since Mon Chain 1 — Monday: the fix rose, the real not really.

Euro 1.1165 → 1.1193 (+0.25%)→ Real weaker at fix time (+0.24%)→ EUR/BRL fix +0.49%→ by the close: dollar −0.06%

Monday's fix breaks down half and half into a firmer euro and a weaker real around ~10:15 BRT (own calculation).[1] Then the day turned. The dollar closed at R$ 5.5058 (−0.06%), the Ibovespa rose 2.22% to 172,236 points — the biggest daily gain since 10/05.[3][4] According to Agência Brasil, the drivers came from outside: US stock markets, cheaper oil and Trump's statement that new US strikes on Iran are very unlikely if the talks make progress.[3]

ECB fix: the reference rate that the European Central Bank records once a day at ~2:15 pm CET (10:15 BRT). Like a photo of the rate at a fixed time: anything that happens afterwards only shows up in the next day's photo.

Update since Mon Chain 2 — A speech without numbers does not lower the premium.

Lula: commitment, no measures→ Doubts about austerity remain→ 10Y yield 15.16% (−0.03 pp)→ Real stays near ~5.50

On Sunday Lula reaffirmed fiscal responsibility and showed concern about debt and high interest rates, but named no measures.[3] This summary comes only from Agência Brasil. According to Estadão, the 2027 budget needs an adjustment of about R$ 40 bn (R$ 39.8 bn in the text); measures are to come "in the coming weeks". Concrete measures are expected from about 11/16; one report says late November, nothing is confirmed.[14] The finance minister is Dario Durigan, the planning minister Bruno Moretti.[14] The 10-year yield eased only 0.03 points on Monday.[13]

Risk premium: the extra interest investors demand from a country whose finances are uncertain. Like a higher rate for a friend who has already paid you back late twice. A promise alone does not lower it — only on-time payment does.

Update since Mon Chain 3 — The Focus survey drops the December cut.

Focus: Selic end-2026 13.50 → 13.75%→ no cut in December→ Carry stays→ Safety net under the real

Monday's Focus (data 11/06) raises the Selic for end-2026 from 13.50 to 13.75% — the market no longer expects a cut in December.[11] The Copom held unanimously on 11/04 and ended the cuts.[15] The dollar for end-2026 rises only slightly to 5.35 (prior week 5.3428, two weeks earlier 5.0495). End-2027 stays at 5.40, end-2028 rises to 5.45. Inflation for 2027 rises from 4.76 to 4.90%.[11] Important: the Focus dollar figure is now the December average, not the rate on the last trading day.[12]

Carry: borrowing money cheaply abroad and investing it in Brazil at 13.75%. Like a savings account with a high rate that only pays off as long as the real does not fall. A high rate keeps that money in the country.

Chain 4 — Today: US inflation.

US inflation high→ Fed hike in Dec. nearly certain→ Dollar stronger against all→ Real and euro fall→ EUR/BRL little net change

The October figure arrives at 10:30 BRT.[9] Consensus: 3.9% year over year, after 3.7% (forexfundamentals, Trading Economics; TE's own forecast 3.8%).[10] TE, by contrast, writes that inflation is expected to "ease".[6] Both can be true if the monthly rate falls while the annual rate still rises (own interpretation, not verified). The market prices a Fed hike in December at 84% (TE, today); FedWatch showed 76.8% on 11/06 and only one more after that.[6][8] The Fed stands at 3.75–4.00%, minutes on 11/18, decision on 12/09.

My view Cheaper oil and friendly US stock markets moved the real on Monday more than Lula's speech. That helps in the short term, but it is not a foundation. As long as the 10-year yield stays above 15%, the market does not believe in the austerity course. Until the package (expected from about 11/16, unconfirmed), your rate tends to stay high.

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000, Monday's fix (6.1748) works out to R$ 6,174.80 — about R$ 526 more than before the runoff (10/23: 5.6485).[1] My view: the risks remain unevenly distributed. A package with numbers (expected from about 11/16) can push your rate down quickly by 0.15–0.20. Without a package it tends to rise slowly. It's Tuesday, November tenth. Your rate rose at Monday's European Central Bank fix to six point one seven four eight reais per euro. That's a gain of about half a percent, and roughly half of it came from a firmer euro, not a weaker real.

On Sunday, President Lula addressed the nation. According to Agência Brasil, he pledged fiscal discipline and spoke about debt and high interest rates. He gave no concrete measures. They are expected from about November sixteenth; one report says late November, nothing is confirmed. On Monday the dollar closed little changed at five point five oh reais, and the São Paulo stock exchange gained a little over two percent. The tailwind came from outside: cheaper oil and friendly US stock markets.

Monday's Focus survey shows an important shift. The market no longer expects an interest rate cut in December; the Selic is now expected to stay at thirteen point seven five percent through year-end. That high rate keeps money in the country and acts as the safety net under the real. For the dollar at the end of the year, the Focus sees five point three five.

Today at ten thirty Brazilian time, US inflation for October comes out. Economists expect three point nine percent, according to two sources; Trading Economics itself forecasts three point eight. Traders see a US Federal Reserve rate hike in December at roughly eighty percent. What matters for you: the number lands a quarter of an hour after the European Central Bank fix. So today's rate doesn't see it yet; Wednesday's fix will. Brazilian inflation follows on Thursday.

My outlook, as confidence and not a market figure: for today's fix, most likely six point one eight. By Friday, six point one nine; in thirty days, six point one two. Over three to six months, six point one five, and over twelve months, six point two five. All banks with an explicit year-end target see the dollar below today's level at year-end, with Commerzbank even at five point two oh, but only with credible reforms.

For your exchange: a thousand euros at Monday's fix brings roughly six thousand one hundred seventy-five reais. The next brief looks at the central bank's minutes, the reaction to US inflation, and Brazilian inflation on Thursday.
