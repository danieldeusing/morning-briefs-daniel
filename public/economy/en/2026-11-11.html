Where we stand & why

[BR] [USA] FX in detail · as of Tue 11/10 · diff vs. Monday

The month in one sentence: since Lula's runoff win (10/25) the fix jumped from 5.6485 to 6.2523, fell to 5.93 and now, at 6.2257, is almost back at the peak.[1]

Update since Mon Chain 1 — The fix came before the bang.

Real at fix time −0.63%→ Euro +0.19%→ EUR/BRL fix +0.82%→ 10:30 BRT: US inflation

Tuesday's fix breaks down into roughly three-quarters weaker real and one-quarter firmer euro (own calculation from ECB rates).[1] It was struck at ~10:15 BRT, a quarter of an hour before US inflation. What happened afterward will only show up in today's fix.

ECB fix: the reference rate the European Central Bank sets once a day at ~2:15 p.m. CET (10:15 BRT). Like a photo of the rate at a fixed time: anything that happens afterward only shows up in the next day's photo.

New Chain 2 — Hot US inflation: dollar against everyone.

US prices +0.4% (exp. 0.3%)→ Fed move Dec. nearly certain→ US 2Y yield +18.65 bp→ DXY +0.6%→ Real −1.5%, euro −0.55%→ EUR/BRL net +0.14%

US prices rose 0.4% m/m in October, the core excluding energy and food by 0.3% — both above expectations of 0.3% and 0.2%.[8][6] Correction to yesterday: there is no annual rate this time. The US statistics agency cannot calculate it because no data was collected in October 2025 due to the government shutdown.[8]

The market reacted hard. A rate move in December is now seen as all but certain: 90% for +0.25 points, 10% for +0.50 (investingLive, evening).[9] Traders see a further hike in January at around 54%.[10] TE puts December at 91%, up from 84% the day before; that is probably only the share for +0.25.[6] The ten-year US Treasury briefly rose above 5%.[9] The dollar closed at R$ 5.5860 (+1.46%).[3] But the euro fell too, by 0.55% to 1.1161.[9] Little is left for you on net: the cross is at 6.2345 this morning, only 0.14% above the fix.[2][5]

Basis point (bp): one hundredth of a percentage point; 25 bp = 0.25 points. Like cents and dollars: 18 bp are small coins, but they rarely stack up like that in a single day.

New Chain 3 — The Copom stays hawkish, the carry shrinks anyway.

Copom: "greater and longer"→ Selic 13.75% stays→ Fed hikes→ Rate gap narrower→ less of a net under the real

The minutes of the 11/3–11/4 meeting (published 11/10) say in the original: with unanchored expectations, it takes "uma restrição monetária maior e por mais tempo".[11] The economy is cooling, but inflation "voltou a acelerar" and is above the tolerance ceiling.[12] The weak real is one of four shocks driving prices.[11] There is nothing about cuts or a pause in the text.[11] TE read a hint of cuts into it — the original text does not support that.[2] Important: the Copom's forecasts assume a dollar of R$ 5.20; a lasting weaker real is explicitly an upside risk.[11] Focus sees the first cut only in March 2027.[14]

The real fell anyway. The reason is the gap: if the Fed hikes and the Selic merely holds, the advantage of parking money in Brazil shrinks. TE writes that a smaller gap "would pressure the real by reducing carry trade".[2]

Carry: borrowing money cheaply abroad and investing it in Brazil at 13.75%. Like a savings account with a high rate: if the interest on the loan in the US rises, less profit is left — and some pull out.
My view Tuesday shows your basic pattern: a global dollar surge hits the real and the euro at the same time and barely moves your rate. What holds it up is Brazilian — fiscal doubts, a 10-year yield above 15% and a Copom that sees the weak real as an inflation risk. Until a package with numbers arrives (expected from about 11/16, unconfirmed), your rate tends to stay above 6.15.

Practical for the exchange

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000 the Tue fix (6.2257) arithmetically yields R$ 6,225.70 — about R$ 577 more than before the runoff (10/23: 5.6485).[1] Only 0.4% is missing to the peak fix of 10/26. My view: all named bank targets are below today's dollar. Anyone who has to exchange anyway is near the upper edge of what analysts expect. The downside risk is a package with numbers; the upside risk is its absence. The decision is yours.

It's Wednesday, November eleventh. Your rate rose at Tuesday's European Central Bank fix to six point two two five seven reais per euro. That's a gain of just over eight tenths of a percent, and about three quarters of it came from a weaker real. That leaves your rate only slightly below the peak of October twenty-sixth.

A quarter of an hour after that fix, US inflation came out, and it was hot. Prices rose four tenths of a percent in October; economists had expected three tenths. There's no annual rate this time, because no data was collected in October of last year. Traders now see a Federal Reserve rate hike in December as all but certain.

The dollar closed in São Paulo at five point five eight six reais, its highest since July of last year. But the euro fell too. So your rate works out this morning to just six point two three, barely above the fix. That's your basic pattern: a global dollar surge hits the real and the euro at the same time.

The minutes from Brazil's central bank sound hawkish. The original says monetary policy has to be tighter and last longer than previously needed. There's no mention of cuts. The weak real is named as one of the drivers of inflation. Still, Brazil's interest-rate advantage shrinks when the Fed hikes and the Selic just holds.

The banks raised their targets after the runoff. Goldman Sachs sees the dollar at five point four five in three months, according to an Estadão report. Itaú sees five point four zero for the end of the year and five point six zero for the end of twenty twenty-seven. All of them are below today's dollar. The Focus survey now runs through twenty thirty and puts the dollar at around five point five by then.

My assessment, as confidence and not a market figure: for today's fix, most likely six point two three. By Friday, six point two four; in thirty days, six point two zero. For the month there's no bank target; that's my own derivation. Over three to six months, six point one eight, and over twelve months, six point two five.

For the exchange: a thousand euros at Tuesday's fix gets you about six thousand two hundred twenty-six reais, nearly six hundred more than before the runoff.
