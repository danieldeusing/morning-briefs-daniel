Where we stand & why

[EUR] [BR] [USA] FX in detail · As of Wed Nov 11 · Diff to Monday

Yesterday's scorecard: I estimated the Wed fix at 6.17–6.30, most likely 6.23. It came in at 6.1827, inside the range but 0.05 below my point estimate.[1]

New Chain 1 — This time the euro fell, not the real.

US inflation hot (Nov 10)→ DXY +0.67%→ Euro vs. dollar −0.78%→ Real +0.09% weaker at fix time→ EUR/BRL fix −0.69%

Break down the fix and almost only the euro is left: EUR/USD fell from 1.1214 to 1.1127, while the dollar in reais rose only from 5.5517 to 5.5565 (my own calculation from the ECB rates).[1] The euro has troubles of its own. TE points to France's politics and public finances.[5] On top of that, speculators are betting against the euro more heavily than at any time since early 2020: 78,559 contracts net, up from 28,156 in mid-September (CFTC, as of Nov 3, via MUFG).[8]

Net short: Speculators hold more bets on a falling euro than on a rising one. Like a full boat where everyone sits on one side: when the mood tips, they all switch at once — and then the euro rises fast. MUFG asks exactly that: "is the trade getting too crowded?"

New Chain 2 — The austerity package came after the fix.

Package R$ 50–60 billion (afternoon)→ Fiscal premium slightly smaller→ BR 10Y 15.18% (−0.08)→ Dollar −0.42% to 5.5628→ Cross today 6.163

Planning Minister Bruno Moretti presented the package in the budget committee in the afternoon. It is meant to save between R$ 50 and 60 billion in 2027.[9] The core is a spending cap: in 2027 the federal government may grow by only 2% in real terms instead of up to 2.5%, but only if a miss of the lower target limit looms; on top come R$ 12.8 billion of additional spending freezes.[9] Added to that are 10% fewer tax breaks and two existing triggers worth R$ 9 billion.[9] Moretti says there will "certamente" be a primary surplus in 2027.[10]

The market believes only part of it. XP counts only ~R$ 38.3 billion as permanent.[10] Nomos says: "Ainda é pouco" — over R$ 100 billion would be needed for 2027.[10] The same day came a draft to take R$ 10.5 billion of diesel subsidies out of the budget target — the opposite of saving (headline only).[11] That is why the reaction was small: −0.42% for the dollar, part of it just a correction after the previous day, according to Agência Brasil.[2]

Primary surplus: the government takes in more than it spends — not counting interest on old debt. Like a household with something left from its paycheck before it pays the loan installment. If nothing is left, debt grows on its own.

Update since Mon Chain 3 — The Fed is priced ever harder.

Dec hike > 95%→ DB: also Jan + Mar→ US yields near 5%→ Dollar stronger against all→ Euro hit harder than the real

Traders see a 0.25-point hike on Dec 8–9 at over 95% (CME FedWatch, as relayed by investingLive).[7] TE cites around 91% — a different point in time, same message: December is priced in.[6] New: Deutsche Bank expects three steps (Dec, Jan, Mar) to 4.50–4.75%.[7] Fed Governor Waller says even 0.50 points in December "should be considered", said before the CPI data and only if price pressures accelerate (TE words it more strongly: "may be necessary") — secondhand only (investingLive, TE); I have not read the speech itself.[7][6] A stronger dollar is currently pressing the euro more than the real: with a 13.75% Selic the real has an interest cushion, the euro with 2.50% does not.

My view The package is a start, not a breakthrough. The bond market understood that: 15.18% for ten years is still crisis level. The bigger force for your rate right now is the euro. As long as the euro bets do not unwind, your rate stays more likely below 6.25. If they unwind, the euro can quickly recover 1–2% — then your rate is back above 6.20, with no Brazilian news at all.

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000 the Wed fix (6.1827) yields R$ 6,182.70 on paper — about R$ 534 more than before the runoff (Oct 23: 5.6485), but R$ 70 less than at the peak on Oct 26 (6.2523).[1] My view: you are still in the upper part of what the banks expect — most targets, converted, sit at 5.99–6.16, only Morgan Stanley and Itaú 2027 above. The downside risk is a package that works, plus a euro that keeps falling. The upside risk is a euro that frees itself from the crowded bets. The decision is yours.

It's Thursday, November twelfth. Your rate fell at Wednesday's European Central Bank fix to six point one eight two seven reais per euro, a drop of just under seven tenths of a percent. This time it was almost entirely the euro. It lost nearly eight tenths of a percent against the dollar, while the real barely moved at fix time.

The euro has troubles of its own: France's politics and public finances, plus weaker growth. Speculators are betting against it more heavily than at any time since early twenty-twenty. That cuts the other way too. When everyone is on the same side of the boat, the euro can snap back fast, and then your rate rises again.

In the afternoon, the government in Brasília finally unveiled its austerity package. It aims to save fifty to sixty billion reais in twenty twenty-seven, mainly through a tighter spending cap. The market believes only part of it. According to InfoMoney, XP counts only about thirty-eight billion as permanent, and Nomos says more than a hundred billion would be needed.

The dollar closed in São Paulo at five point five six reais, down four tenths of a percent. Brazil's ten-year yield dipped to just over fifteen percent, but it's still at crisis levels. With the euro still sliding, your rate works out this morning to roughly six point one six.

In the U.S., a December rate hike is now priced as nearly certain. Deutsche Bank even expects three steps, running into March. A stronger dollar is currently hitting the euro harder than the real, because the real has a cushion from a thirteen point seven five percent policy rate.

My outlook, as confidence and not a market figure: for today's fix, six point one six is the most likely. In a week, six point one five; in thirty days, six point one two. Over three to six months, six point one zero, and over twelve months, six point one eight. No bank has changed its target since the austerity package.

For the exchange: a thousand euros at Wednesday's fix bring in about six thousand, one hundred eighty-three reais, a little over five hundred more than before the runoff.
