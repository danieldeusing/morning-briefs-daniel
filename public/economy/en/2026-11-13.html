Where we stand & why

[BR] [USA] [EUR] FX in detail · as of Thu Nov 12 · diff versus Monday

Yesterday's scorecard: I estimated the Thursday fix at 6.10–6.23, most likely 6.16. It came in at 6.2241, at the upper edge of the range, 0.06 above my point. I read the reaction to the package too kindly.[1]

New Chain 1 — This time the real fell.

Austerity package "aquém do esperado"→ Investors hedge in dollars→ BR 10Y 15.44% (+0.26)→ Dollar +1.58% to 5.6507→ EUR/BRL fix +0.67%

Break the fix down and this time it is the real: the dollar in reais rose 1.09% at fix time (5.5565 → 5.6169), while the euro against the dollar fell 0.41% (1.1127 → 1.1081). Together +0.67% (own calculation).[1] By the close the dollar had risen to 5.6507. Agência Brasil cites the "insatisfação dos investidores com o pacote fiscal".[2] Capital Economics judges that the package "fica aquém do esperado". Nomad economist Vitor Kayo says the uncertainty about what Congress will really approve "leva os investidores a buscarem cobertura em dólar".[3] The 10-year yield rose to 15.44% (high on Nov 3: 15.75%).[12]

Dollar hedge: if you hold wealth in reais and trust the state less, you buy dollars as insurance. Like an umbrella that everyone buys at the same time when the clouds get darker — the price of umbrellas (here: the dollar) rises even before it rains.

Update since Mon Chain 2 — US yields above 5%, dollar strong, euro weak.

Hot US inflation + weak 30Y auction→ US 10Y 5.07% → ~5.13%→ DXY 103.16→ Euro down to 1.1053→ slows your rate by ~0.4 points

The US 10-year yield rose 7.7 basis points on Thursday to 5.066%, the 2-year by almost 11 to 4.837%.[8] Overnight the 10-year yield climbed to 5.12–5.13%.[9][10] Reasons according to investingLive: US inflation +0.4% instead of 0.3%, a weak 30-year auction, a record deficit of $404 bn in October.[9] The DXY closed at 103.16, the euro briefly fell to 1.1053.[2][8] The strong dollar lifts your rate via the real and pushes it down via the euro. On Thursday the real part won.

Basis point: one hundredth of a percentage point. 7.7 basis points = 0.077 percentage points. Like cents and dollars: a small unit for fine movements.

New Chain 3 — The Fed fear got a little smaller.

0.50-point bet: CME Nov 10 (15%) vs. investingLive Nov 12 (~6.5%)→ December stays at 0.25 points→ slightly less dollar pressure

Right after the inflation data on Nov 10, the CME saw a 15.3% chance of a 0.50-point hike in December.[11] On the afternoon of Nov 12 it was only about 6.5%, per investingLive (single source; for Nov 11, investingLive itself gives ~14%).[8] TE still shows "~15%" — the Nov 10 level. A 0.25-point hike on Dec 8–9 remains almost certain. New York Fed president John Williams says, as paraphrased by investingLive, that the Fed will need to act over time to bring inflation back to 2% (second-hand only).[10]

My view Yesterday I wrote that the bigger force was the euro. On Thursday it was the real — and that may continue. The market demands more than R$ 50–60 bn, and until Congress delivers, the dollar hedge stays. The real already stands beyond Goldman's 3-month target (5.45) and beyond the 5.60/5.63 hurdle that Société Générale named on Oct 27.[16][35] Commerzbank and Wells Fargo consider the drop overdone.[17] Without new news from Brasília, however, I see no quick recovery.

It's Friday, November thirteenth. The market has read Brasília's austerity package, and it says it isn't enough. The dollar closed Thursday at five point six five reais, up a little more than one and a half percent. That's the most expensive the dollar has been since early June of twenty twenty-five.

Your rate rose at the European Central Bank fix to six point two two four one reais per euro, up just under seven tenths of a percent. This time the real was the cause. The weaker euro only slowed the climb a bit. Because the real kept falling after the fix, your rate works out this morning to about six point two five. That's almost the peak from October twenty-sixth.

According to Forbes Brasil, Capital Economics judges that the package falls short of expectations. Nomad's economist says uncertainty over Congress is driving investors into the dollar. Brazil's ten-year yield rose to fifteen point four four percent.

More pressure came from the United States. The yield on ten-year Treasuries sits just above five point one percent overnight, and the dollar index is at its highest since April of twenty twenty-five. There is some relief: the bet on a big Fed rate hike in December fell from fifteen percent to about six and a half percent.

Brazilian inflation for October came in at zero point one four percent, below expectations. Over twelve months, though, it stays above the ceiling. According to Agência Brasil, the number had little effect on the real.

My outlook, as confidence and not a market figure: for today's fix, most likely six point two five. In a week, six point two four, in thirty days six point two zero. Over three to six months, six point one five, and over twelve months, six point two five. No bank has changed its target since the selloff, and none gives a one-month target.

For the exchange: one thousand euros at Thursday's fix brings in about six thousand two hundred twenty-four reais. That's almost six hundred more than before the runoff. The next brief will look at Monday's Focus report and at whether speculators have abandoned their bets on the real.
