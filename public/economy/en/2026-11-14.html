Where we stand & why

[BR] [USA] [EUR] FX in detail · as of Fri Nov 13 · Diff to Monday

Yesterday's scorecard: I estimated the Friday fix at 6.18–6.31, most likely 6.25. It came in at 6.2595 — inside the range, 0.01 above my point.[1] Scorecard for the week: from the Monday fix of 6.1748 to 6.2595, so +1.37%. New Chain 1 — In the morning the real fell, in the afternoon the euro.

Dollar at fix time +0.83%→ Euro −0.26%→ Fix 6.2595 (+0.57%)→ after the fix: real calm, euro down to 1.1024→ Cross 6.236

Break the fix apart and it is once again the real: the dollar in reais rose 0.83% at fix time (5.6169 → 5.6637), the euro against the dollar fell 0.26% (1.1081 → 1.1052). Together +0.57% (own calculation from ECB data).[1] Then the picture turned. The dollar closed only 0.11% higher (R$ 5.657), the euro slid to 1.1024 — as weak as it has not been since March 2025.[2][5] For the weekend your rate is therefore 0.37% below the fix.

ECB fix vs. cross: the fix is a photo the ECB takes every business day around 10:15 am BRT. The cross recalculates the rate later from two parts (dollar in reais × euro in dollars). Like a photo of the market stall at ten o'clock and a second one in the evening: the prices keep moving in between.

Update since Mon Chain 2 — US yields +30 basis points, dollar at a 19-month high.

Producer prices 6.5% (highest since Nov. 2022)→ US 10Y 5.15% (+30 bp in the week)→ Fed hike in Dec. ~85–91% priced in→ DXY 103.42→ Euro weak, real under pressure

US producer prices rose 6.5% year over year in October (September 6.3%) — the highest since November 2022, but below the forecast of 6.6%.[8] Month over month +0.3% instead of 0.4%; according to Agência Brasil, that took away some of the rate fear.[2] The 10-year yield still holds at 5.15%.[7] TE puts the odds of a Fed hike in December at ~91%, elsewhere still 85% (probably as of Nov 10); I did not read the CME page.[6][7] Add risk aversion: the Nvidia chief resigned on Nov 12, the Nasdaq fell 0.89%; TE ties part of the rise in yields to debt worries in the AI sector.[2][7][10] All that matters here is the effect on the dollar.

Producer prices (PPI): what factories and wholesalers charge for their goods before they reach the store. When they rise, prices often reach customers later. Like flour prices at the baker: more expensive today, the bread tomorrow.

New Chain 3 — The Senate approves the package, the real catches its breath.

Senate approves base text (Thu Nov 12)→ less fear of failure (my reading)→ BR 10Y 15.34% (−0.11)→ Dollar only +0.11%

On Thursday the Senate approved the base text of the budget adjustment law. Estimated impact: up to R$ 60 bn in 2027. The Senate votes on amendments on Tuesday, Nov 17; then the law goes to the Chamber of Deputies.[2][9] Important: I know the vote only from two outlets, Agência Brasil and TE. I found neither Agência Senado nor the vote tally. According to TE, JPMorgan considers the measures "insufficient" (note not found).[9]

Amendments (destaques): after the yes on the base text, senators may still remove or change individual points. Like building a house where the floor plan stands but every room is still voted on — if a room is dropped, the house gets smaller.
My view After the Senate's yes the sell-off stopped for now. The real has not recovered, though — the market is waiting for Tuesday. For you the euro is now the bigger unknown. It sits at the low since March 2025, and many speculators are already betting against it (see right). If it bounces back, that lifts your rate, even if the real stands still.
