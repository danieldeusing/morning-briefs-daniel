Only news is new — and it is mixed.

New Chain 1 — Diesel: lower spending, but an accounting trick.

R$ 1.40/l subsidy for imported diesel ends→ State saves (amount not stated)→ but: up to R$ 10.5 bn in diesel aid outside the target→ Target looks better than it is→ Real: more headwind

The Finance Ministry said on Friday evening that the R$ 1.40 per liter subsidy for imported diesel “não será renovada”. The R$ 2.12 per liter subsidy for road diesel “continua valendo”. It was extended on Oct 27 for 30 days, so it lapses around Nov 26. More than a quarter of Brazil's diesel comes from abroad.[8] At the same time, a bill in the Chamber of Deputies takes up to R$ 10.5 bn in diesel aid out of the 2026 fiscal target. Agência Brasil itself writes that this “não elimina os gastos nem representa economia”.[8] The source gives no cost figure.

Fiscal target (meta fiscal): the number for how much more than it takes in the state may spend at most by year-end. If you remove spending from the calculation, it does not vanish. Like a diet where you don't count dinner: the scale notices anyway.

My reading: the market pays less attention to the small subsidy than to the trick. This exact pattern weakened the real in October and November. Not a new blow, but no help before Tuesday.

New Chain 2 — Only one model reacts to the sell-off.

Dollar +2.63% in one week→ TE model (vs. our Nov 14 snapshot): Q4 5.48 → 5.63; 12 mo 5.26 → 5.39→ Banks: no revision found→ Focus Mon Nov 16 = first real test

TradingEconomics now expects the dollar at 5.63 “by the end of this quarter” and at 5.39 “in 12 months time”. Our Nov 14 brief had recorded 5.48 and 5.26 there; TE shows no prior values, so this cannot be checked independently.[3] The model chases the price; it does not show an analyst opinion. All bank numbers date from Oct 26–Nov 10, so from before the sell-off. The research found no later revision.

Focus report: every week the central bank asks around 120 banks and advisers where they see the dollar, interest rates and inflation, and publishes the mean. Like a weather poll among all the country's meteorologists: one can be wrong, the middle shows the mood.
