Where we stand & why

[BR] [USA] [EUR] [Global] FX in detail · Monday = full rewrite · as of Fri Nov 13 + Mon Asia

Starting point: Friday's fix (6.2595) sits just above the Oct 26 peak (6.2523).[1] The dollar rose 2.63% over the week to R$ 5.657.[2] The euro fell to ~1.10, per TE "near its weakest level since March 2025", and absorbed part of the move: your rate rose only 1.86% (Nov 6: 6.1449).[5] Asia this morning: dollar 5.6666, euro 1.10208, DXY 103.42.[3][5][6]

New Chain 1 — Asia weighs on emerging-market sentiment.

China industry 4.8% instead of 5.3%, Japan GDP −0.1%→ Chip stocks down as much as 6%, investors cautious→ Money avoids risky currencies→ Real: headwind at the open

China's industry grew 4.8% in October (expected 5.3%), retail sales only 0.4%. Japan's economy shrank 0.1% in the third quarter. SK Hynix fell more than 6%, ahead of Nvidia's earnings on Tuesday.[15] All from brief items on a single portal.

Risk mood (risk-off): when investors get nervous, they pull money out of anything that can swing — stocks, crypto, emerging-market currencies — and park it in the dollar. Like kids who all run under the roof at the first thunder — the real stays out in the rain.

New Chain 2 — The market believes in more Fed hikes than the Fed itself.

Fed plan Sept.: policy rate ~4.1% in 2026 and 2027→ Deutsche Bank: three hikes by March → 4.50–4.75%→ US 10-yr yield 5.15%, DXY 103.42→ Real and euro weaker

In September the middle of the Fed's members showed a policy rate of 4.1% for 2026 and 2027.[28] Deutsche Bank expects 25 basis points each in December, January and March: "to 4.50%-4.75% from the current 3.75%-4.00%".[14] December is priced in at ~85–91% (TE only).[7] The minutes on Wednesday show how many members already think this way; StoneX sees a risk of a higher dollar in them.[13]

Dot plot (SEP): four times a year, each Fed member sets a dot where they see the policy rate in the coming years; the middle is the "plan". Like a family vote on allowance: no guarantee, but a hint.

For your rate: a tougher Fed pushes down the real and the euro. For EUR/BRL this partly cancels out.

Update Chain 3 — The budget: the real hinge for the real.

Import diesel subsidy R$ 1.40/l ends→ but up to R$ 10.5 bn in diesel aid outside the target→ Senate Tue Nov 17: changes to the package→ StoneX: "altista" for the dollar

The R$ 1.40/l subsidy for imported diesel ends; up to R$ 10.5 bn in diesel aid is meant to fall outside the 2026 budget target.[8] The Senate approved the basic text of the savings package on Nov 12 (up to R$ 60 bn effect in 2027); per TE, JPMorgan considers the measures "insufficient".[9] StoneX (Leonel Mattos) writes on the budget: "Impacto esperado na taxa de câmbio do real: altista." The note names no factors for a stronger real.[13] An economic team for Lula's fourth term was not named over the weekend.

Budget target (meta fiscal): the figure for how much more than it takes in the state may spend, at most, by year-end. Spending taken out of the count does not vanish. Like a diet where you do not count dinner: the scale notices anyway.

New The models turn, the banks do not. TradingEconomics sees dollar/real at 5.63 end of Q4 and 5.39 in 12 months, euro 1.11 / 1.12, DXY 103.19 / 101.69.[3][5][6] At the end of last week, per our briefs, the figures there were 5.48 / 5.26, 1.12 / 1.14 and 102.04 / 100.59 (TE shows no prior values). For your rate ≈ 6.22 end of Q4, ≈ 6.04 in 12 months (own calculation). Our research found no bank revision after Nov 12 in three search rounds.

My view The real starts with a headwind: Asia weak, strong dollar, no counterweight on the calendar. But the euro suffers from the same Fed. That keeps EUR/BRL below what the dollar alone would suggest. Touchstones: Focus today, Senate Tuesday, Fed minutes Wednesday.
