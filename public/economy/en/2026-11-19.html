ECB fix (reference rate): a photo of the market that the ECB takes every business day at ~2:15 pm CET (~10:15 BRT). What happens afterward only shows in the next day's photo. Like a class photo taken in the morning — whoever trips at noon still looks fine in it.

New Chain 1 — The Fed gets even tougher.

Fed minutes: "almost all" for more hikes→ Waller: +0.50 in December or a faster pace in 2027→ DXY ~103.9→ Money leaves emerging markets→ Dollar 5.87, euro 1.096

The minutes of the October meeting (16:17 BRT): "quase todos" see further hikes as necessary, some would be open to +0.50 points if inflation data turn out bad. Two members already voted for +0.25 in October.[10] Christopher Waller wants +0.50 points at the Dec 8–9 meeting or a clear signal of a faster pace in 2027; the report does not say which he prefers. Inflation is going "na direção errada".[11] According to TE (citing CME, the CME page itself not read), the bet for December is now 90% for +0.25 and 10% for +0.50; the day before it was 96/4.[5] Counterweight: the euro fell too, to 1.0964. That is why your rate rose less than the dollar.[4]

Fed minutes: the record of the last US rate meeting, published three weeks later. Like the class register after the teachers' conference: you read who wanted to be how strict. If it sounds strict, the dollar rises.

New Chain 2 — "Primeiro passo" and no names.

Durigan: package only a first step→ no economic team→ long rates +10 basis points→ Central bank sells US$ 1 bn→ Real falls 1.5% anyway

Finance Minister Durigan said the package is a "primeiro passo": "Vamos adotar medidas novas e adicionais imediatamente." Which ones and how big, he did not say.[9] A trader to Reuters: "E no Brasil falta a perspectiva de um ajuste fiscal mais forte."[6] In the morning the central bank sold US$ 1 bn in two auctions; the dollar kept rising anyway.[6]

Leilão de linha: the central bank sells dollars from its reserves, with a promise to buy them back later. Like a neighbor who lends you flour so you can bake today — tomorrow you give it back. It calms things briefly but changes nothing about the cause of the shortage.

New Chain 3 — The Senate delivers, but after the close.

Senate rejects all amendments→ Package not diluted, 69–2→ at 20:04, after the close→ priced in for the first time today

The Senate rejected three amendments, including the stricter spending cap of 0.6% (47–30); a fourth was withdrawn. The final round ended 69–2. The government estimates an improvement of the 2027 primary balance by "R$45.1 billion to R$56.1 billion" (Senate agency). The package now goes to the Chamber of Deputies.[8] The report came at 20:04 BRT, three hours after the FX market closed.[8][6] This is the first good fiscal news of the week, and it is not yet in the price.

Primary balance: government revenue minus spending, excluding interest. A deficit means: the state borrows money for day-to-day costs, not just for old debts.

New The first bank after the plunge: Commerzbank (Norman Liebke, Nov 18, before the afternoon): "we believe the extent of the move has been excessive and therefore expect USD/BRL to fall somewhat going forward." The focus is on the name for the finance ministry. The bank gives no number.[12]

My view Yesterday the Fed and the finance minister turned against the real at the same time; the central bank could not stop it. Today there is a headwind: the Senate delivered, Commerzbank calls the slump exaggerated. I expect a fix that catches up with Wednesday but hardly runs beyond it. The real turnaround needs a name for the economic team.

It's Thursday, November nineteenth. The European Central Bank's fix from Wednesday stands at six point three eight one eight. That's once again the highest level in our ninety-day series. Yesterday we had estimated the fix at six point three five. The range held, but the point came in a bit too low.

But here's the catch: the fix was set in the morning. In the afternoon the real dropped sharply. According to Reuters, the dollar closed at five point eight seven reais, up one and a half percent. It was the fifth gain in a row and the highest level since April twenty twenty-five. Converted into euros, the market is at around six point four three, almost one percent above the fix.

Two things pushed the real down. From the U.S. came hawkish minutes from the Federal Reserve: almost all members want to keep raising rates. Fed official Waller even wants a half-point hike in December, or a clear signal of faster hikes in 2027. In Brazil, Finance Minister Durigan said the austerity package is only a first step, with more measures coming right away. He gave no number. The central bank sold one billion dollars, and the rate kept climbing anyway.

In the evening, after the market close, came the good news. The Senate rejected all amendments and passed the package by a vote of sixty-nine to two. Now it's the Chamber of Deputies' turn. The market will see this news for the first time today.

Commerzbank calls the real's plunge overdone and expects a somewhat weaker dollar. It gives no number. No other bank has changed its forecast since the plunge. The dollar now sits above every bank base-case target.

My take, as a confidence call and not a market number: for today's fix, most likely six point four two. For the next seven days, six point four zero. For the month, six point three two. For three to six months and for twelve months, six point two two each. Over two to three years, my center of gravity stays between six and six point six.

A thousand euros brings about six thousand three hundred eighty-two reais at Wednesday's fix, and about six thousand four hundred thirty-three at the late market. Today is the last trading day before the holiday; on Friday the stock exchange and banks in Brazil are closed. Tomorrow we'll watch the fix that catches up with the plunge, and the first names for Lula's economic team. The decision is yours.
