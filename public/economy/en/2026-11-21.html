Where we stand & why

[BR] [USA] [EUR] FX in detail · Diff vs. Monday · As of Sat Nov 21, morning BRT

Since Mon The week in numbers: Monday fix 6.2838, Friday 6.5259 — +3.85%. Versus the previous Friday's fix (Nov 13: 6.2595) that is +4.26%, versus the level before the runoff (Oct 23: 5.6485) +15.53%.[1] Every day this week brought a higher fix.

New Chain 1 — The fix catches up on Thursday.

Thu fix in the morning: 6.4765→ Thu afternoon: dollar to 5.9323→ Fri: Brazil closed, offshore only 5.9491→ Fri fix 6.5259 (+0.76%)

Yesterday we warned: the Thursday fix was set before the afternoon surge and sat about 0.6% below the market. Friday's fix closed that gap. Convert the offshore dollar with the ECB euro and the market stands at 6.538 — only 0.19% above the fix.[1][2][3]

Offshore rate: when Brazil's banks are closed, banks in London and New York keep trading the real anyway, just with little volume. Like a flea market on a holiday: there are prices, but few dealers. On Monday the big market will show whether the price holds.

Since Mon Chain 2 — A week of distrust.

Mon: report on Mercadante weighs (ING)→ Package seen as too small→ DI Jan 2028 week +55 basis points→ Dollar week +4.87%, despite US$ 1 billion central bank sale

According to ING, a report on Mercadante as a possible minister hit the real on Monday.[10] Commerzbank calls the R$ 50–60 billion package for 2027 "unlikely to be enough"; debt would stand above 80% of GDP.[11] The yield curve shows it: DI January 2028 closed Thursday at 14.815%, +55 basis points on the week.[6] The central bank sold US$ 1 billion on Thursday — the dollar still closed at 5.9323.[5]

DI future: a bet on how high the interbank rate will average until a given date. When it rises, investors demand more reward for their risk with Brazil. Like a contractor who adds a surcharge for an unreliable client.

Since Mon Chain 3 — The dollar pulls worldwide, but slows.

Market prices Fed hike Dec. + Jan.→ DXY fourth weekly gain, 103.84→ Emerging markets under pressure→ but US 10Y falls five days in a row (4.94%)

TE names two drivers for the dollar: hawkish tones from the Fed and dollar demand around the Anthropic IPO (details in the ai-dev brief).[4] On Friday, though, the DXY paused (−0.05%), and the 10-year US yield fell for the fifth time in a row to 4.94%.[4][20] The global tailwind for the dollar is weakening. The real's plunge is mostly homemade.

New The silver lining — two voices against the trend. Reuters (sources in the Planalto): for the Finance Ministry, Piauí's governor Rafael Fonteles and Vice President Geraldo Alckmin are ahead; the decision "pode ocorrer já na próxima semana". Durigan "não deve ficar".[7] ING: "Were a name like Geraldo Alckmin to emerge as a front-runner, the real could see a reprieve."[10] Commerzbank (Nov 18): "we believe the extent of the move has been excessive and therefore expect USD/BRL to fall somewhat going forward."[11] Neither gives a figure.

My view The fix has now priced in all the damage, but not yet any relief. The weekend is asymmetric: a credible name can take 1–3% off on Monday, while further silence holds the rate rather than lifting it sharply. Reason: the central bank sells dollars at the peaks, and two big houses already call the move overdone. I do not see the week's peak as a sure peak for the month — but the chance of a pullback is greater now than on Monday.

Practical for the exchange

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000 the Fri fix (6.5259) yields on paper R$ 6,525.90 — R$ 242 more than at Monday's fix and R$ 877 more than before the runoff (Oct 23: 5.6485).[1] You stand above every converted bank target. My view: on the weekend there is no onshore market; provider rates refer to Friday and often carry a buffer. On Monday a name can move the rate quickly by 1–3%, in either direction. Anyone who wants to exchange part now has a historically high rate; anyone who waits is betting on further silence from Brasília. The decision is yours.

It's Saturday, November twenty-first. In Brazil the market has been shut since Thursday, first for the holiday, now for the weekend. The European Central Bank's fix from Friday stands at six point five three, up a little more than three-quarters of a percent. Yesterday we estimated six point five one. That held up.

The fix has caught up with what happened Thursday afternoon. The dollar closed then at five point nine three reais, the highest since April twenty twenty-five. On Friday only banks abroad were trading, at five point nine five. Converted into euros, fix and market line up again.

Over the week, your rate rose almost four percent, and more than fifteen percent since the runoff. Our weekly range from Monday was therefore too low. The reason was distrust: the austerity package is seen as too small, and Lula took his time with the ministers. Long-term rates rose by more than half a percentage point over the week.

There are two voices against the trend. Commerzbank calls the plunge overdone and expects a somewhat lower dollar. ING writes that a name like Geraldo Alckmin could give the real a breather. According to Reuters, the decision on the Finance Ministry could come as soon as next week, between Alckmin and Rafael Fonteles.

The funds in Chicago are still betting on a stronger real, but less than a week ago. Those who close those bets buy dollars. That can slow a recovery. Worldwide, the dollar gained for the fourth week in a row, but U.S. yields are already falling for the fifth day.

My assessment, as confidence and not a market number: for Monday's fix, most likely six point five three. For the next seven days, six point five zero. For the month, six point four zero. Over three to six months, six point two eight, and over twelve months, six point three five. All bank targets sit below today's rate.

A thousand euros at Friday's fix brings about six thousand five hundred twenty-six reais. On Monday the market reopens, with the Focus report and maybe the name for the Finance Ministry. We'll look at that in the next brief. The decision is yours.
