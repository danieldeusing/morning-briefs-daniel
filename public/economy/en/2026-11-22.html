Thu afternoon: dollar 5.9323→ Fri: offshore trading only, 5.9481→ Fri fix 6.5259→ Cross rate 6.5235 (−0.04%)

The cross rate from two independent TE values sits only 0.04% below the fix; the direct TE rate 6.5299 only 0.06% above.[3][4][5] So on Monday there is no "catch-up effect" left, as there was on Friday. Monday's fix depends on new news alone.

Offshore rate: when Brazil's banks are closed, banks in London and New York keep trading the real anyway, just with little volume. Like a flea market on a holiday: there are prices, but few traders. On Monday the big market shows whether the price holds.

New Chain 2 — The euro leg becomes a point of dispute.

US Senate without a Trump majority→ Fed harder to rebuild politically→ political risk shifts to the euro→ Commerzbank: euro end-2027 1.06 (instead of 1.18)→ 12-mo anchor for you ~11% lower

Thu Lan Nguyen (Commerzbank) writes: "We have therefore lowered our forecast sharply and now expect EUR-USD to end 2027 at 1.06", "down from our previous forecast of 1.18". Reasoning: "Without a majority in the US Senate, Trump can no longer easily reshape the Federal Reserve Board"; political risks are shifting "increasingly towards the euro".[21] ING holds the opposite view: euro 1.10–1.15 over the next 4–5 months, 1.18 at end-2027 (Foreks, 11/11).[22] TE reported the euro on 11/18 at its weakest since April 2025, just under 1.10.[5]

Why this matters for you: your rate is dollar-in-real × euro-in-dollar. Take the Focus dollar for end-2027 (5.45): with the Commerzbank euro that gives 5.78, with the ING euro 6.43 (own calculation). The spread between two euro forecasts is larger than the spread of all dollar forecasts from 5.25 to 5.60.

Since yesterday The downside edge is thicker because the euro itself is now a risk. The upside edge remains.

Structural view, 2–3 years (2028–2029): the Focus is the only source naming dollar figures: 2028 5.48, 2029 5.50; net debt 70.2% (2026) → 80.9% of GDP (2029).[15] The IMF already saw in July, before the election, gross debt at 97.8% of GDP in 2026 and 105.3% in 2031. It recommends "A binding medium-term debt anchor".[23] Santander (The Year Ahead 2027) expects the Selic "towards 12.5% by end-2027" and speaks of a "structurally higher neutral real interest rate". The key sentence: "The key issue is no longer whether Brazil will eventually stabilise public debt, but whether fiscal adjustment will come before markets demand it."[24] Santander names no dollar figure.

Neutral rate: the interest rate that neither slows nor speeds up the economy. Like the throttle level at which a car holds the same speed on a flat road. If it is "structurally higher", Brazil has to pay high interest permanently — that supports the real, but costs the state budget.
Debt ratio: government debt relative to annual economic output. If it rises year after year, it is like a loan whose payment grows faster than your salary. Investors then want more interest — and the real loses its footing.

My reading: Focus 2028 (5.48) × euro 1.06 ≈ 5.81; × 1.18 ≈ 6.47. High interest supports the real, the rising debt ratio weighs on it. Center of gravity 2028–2029 ~6.0–6.6; the uncertainty about the euro is as large as the one about the real.

It's Sunday, November twenty-second. In Brazil, the market has been at rest since Thursday, first because of the holiday, now because of the weekend. The European Central Bank's last fix, from Friday, stands at six point five three. As of Sunday morning, there was no new word out of Brasília.

The fix and the market line up. Convert the offshore dollar with the euro, and the market comes out just four hundredths of a percent below the fix. So on Monday there's nothing to catch up on. The rate depends solely on fresh news, above all the name for the Finance Ministry. According to Reuters, Rafael Fonteles and Geraldo Alckmin are the front-runners.

The most important new line concerns the euro. Commerzbank has cut its end-of-twenty-twenty-seven target from one point one eight to one point oh six dollars. Its reasoning: without a Senate majority, Trump can no longer easily rebuild the Fed, and political risk is shifting toward Europe. ING is staying at one point one eight. For your rate, that's a difference of about eleven percent, more than all the dollar forecasts combined.

Goldman Sachs sees the dollar at five point two five reais in twelve months, which Bloomberg Línea has confirmed. But that figure is still calculated against a starting rate of five point six four. Trading Economics, meanwhile, has sharply raised its model and sees your rate at six point five zero by year-end.

Looking two to three years out, the International Monetary Fund and Santander both warn about the debt load. Santander writes that the question is no longer whether Brazil will stabilize its debt, but whether that happens before the markets force it.

My assessment, as confidence and not a market figure: for Monday's fix, most likely six point five two. For the week, six point four eight. For the month, six point four zero. Over three to six months, six point two five, and over twelve months, six point three zero, within a wide range.

A thousand euros brings roughly six thousand five hundred twenty-six reais at Friday's fix. Tomorrow the market reopens, with the Focus report, the week's first fix, and perhaps the name for the Fazenda. We'll look at that in the next brief. The decision is yours.
