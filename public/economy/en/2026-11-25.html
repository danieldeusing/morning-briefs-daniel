ECB fix: the reference rate the European Central Bank measures once a day at 14:15 CET (10:15 BRT). Like a photo of traffic: it shows the jam at 10:15, not the evening.

Chain 1 — The PEC news came before the fix.

Report: Lula clears PEC→ Dollar 5.9551 (09:01) falls→ ECB measures 10:15 BRT: 5.8697→ Fix 6.4349→ Low 5.8010 (10:48)→ Close 5.9085 · evening ~6.46

At 10:30 Reuters had the dollar at 5.8317 (−1.65%), "após notícias sobre PEC do governo para limitar despesas".[9] The ECB values imply a dollar of 5.8697 at the fix — in the middle of the drop.[1][2] The 13:07 PTAX confirms the level.[7] Evening: 6.4632, +0.44% vs. the fix.[3][5] Same pattern as Monday, just reversed: Monday the ECB measured the peak, Tuesday the trough.

Chain 2 — Why the real did not hold the rally.

PEC: direction only→ no size, no limits, no date→ FX keeps 1/6 of the gain→ Rates keep ~4/5

Reuters: Lula "autorizou a equipe econômica liderada pelo ministro da Fazenda, Dario Durigan" to prepare a constitutional amendment "que tratará das despesas obrigatórias do governo".[10] According to O Globo, new limits for health, education and lawmakers' amendment funds are coming. Nobody names a figure.[10] Spiess (Empiricus): "É uma sinalização positiva, necessária, porém não suficiente."[12][13] Of −2.16%, the dollar kept only −0.35%; of −23, the DI rates kept only −19 basis points.[8][12]

PEC & mandatory spending: a PEC is a change to the constitution; it needs more votes than an ordinary law. Mandatory spending is spending fixed by law. Like rent and insurance in a household budget: you cannot just cancel them while the contract runs.

My view: the rates market believes the direction, the FX market is waiting for the price — that is, for the PEC text or a Fazenda name. Royal Rural cites "até o dia 2 de dezembro" for the name and "até 15 de dezembro" for the vote[11]; Reuters does not confirm this.

DI futures & basis point: bets on how high Brazil's policy rate will be on average; a basis point is 0.01 percentage point. Like a fever thermometer for trust in the state: the higher, the more interest investors demand.

Chain 3 — The dollar eases a bit globally.

DXY ~104.3 → 103.79→ Euro 1.0945 (month −2.04%)→ Fed hike 12/09 ~96%→ US data today after the fix

Reuters: DXY "caía 0,49%, a 103,790".[8] TE: Fed hike in December "roughly a 96% probability".[6] The US figures (GDP Q3, first estimate 3.4%; PCE October) come 15 minutes after the fix.[33] They first hit Thursday's fix — in thin Thanksgiving trading.

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000, Tuesday's fix (6.4349) yields R$ 6,434.90 on paper — R$ 126 less than Monday, R$ 151 more than on 11/16 and R$ 786 more than before the runoff (10/23: 5.6485).[1] At the evening rate (~6.4632) it would be R$ 6,463 — R$ 28 more.[3][5] The lesson from two days: peak and trough were R$ 126 apart on €1,000. It's Wednesday, November twenty-fifth. The big news came yesterday morning: President Lula authorized his finance ministry to prepare a constitutional amendment on the government's mandatory spending. According to O Globo, it would set new limits on health, education and lawmakers' amendment funds. So far, nobody has put a number, a cap or a date on it.

The market reacted right away. According to Reuters, the dollar fell as low as five point eight oh, a drop of a little over two percent. By evening the real had given back almost all of it, and the dollar closed at five point nine one. Interest rates, by contrast, held most of their decline.

The European Central Bank's fix landed squarely in that morning window. It came in at six point four three, almost two percent below Monday's record. By evening, the euro-to-real calculation was about six point four six. So on Monday the fix photographed the peak, and on Tuesday the trough.

Why didn't the real hold its rally? An analyst at Empiricus put it this way: the signal is positive and necessary, but not sufficient. The market is waiting for the text of the amendment and for a new finance minister. One agricultural source says a name could come by December second. Reuters has not confirmed that.

Globally, the dollar eased a bit. The dollar index fell to about one hundred three point eight. Markets price in roughly ninety-six percent odds of a Fed rate hike on December ninth. At ten-thirty Brazil time today, new U.S. figures on growth and inflation are due, fifteen minutes after the fix.

The São Paulo stock market didn't celebrate. The Ibovespa fell to its lowest close since mid-January, mostly because of Vale. The central bank's survey from Monday remains the anchor: the dollar at five point five five by year-end. The next survey doesn't come until November thirtieth.

My take, as a measure of confidence and not a market number: for today's fix, most likely six point four seven. For the week, six point four four. For the month, six point three eight. Three to six months out, six point two five, and twelve months out, six point three oh, within a wide range.

A thousand euros at Tuesday's fix brings in about six thousand four hundred thirty-five reais, one hundred twenty-six less than on Monday. The time of day you exchange matters more than the day. Tomorrow we'll look at today's fix, the U.S. figures, and Thursday's inflation reading in Brazil. The decision is yours.
