ECB fix: the reference rate the European Central Bank measures once a day at 14:15 CET (10:15 BRT). Like a photo of traffic: it shows the jam at 10:15, not the evening.

Chain 1 — The fix photographed the morning, not the rally.

09:01: dollar high 5.9734→ 09:30: 5.9235→ 10:15: ECB measures, dollar ≈ 5.9013→ Fix 6.4531→ 15:00: low 5.8049→ Thu morning: cross ~6.38

At 09:30 Reuters reported the dollar at 5.9235.[9] The ECB values imply 5.9013 at the fix.[1][2] Around 14:00 it stood, according to Royal Rural, in the "região de R$ 5,82".[10] This morning: TE dollar × TE euro = 6.3817, so 1.11% below the fix; TE direct 6.3886 (−1.00%).[3][4][5] The 13:02 PTAX (6.4413, −0.18%) measured in the middle of the drop.[7] The gap is real movement, not a data error.

Cross rate: work out the euro-real rate from dollar-real times euro-dollar. Like converting a price via a third currency. If it differs a lot from the fix, the market has moved since the photo.

Chain 2 — Why the real rose in the afternoon.

BCB sells US$2 billion (morning)→ Rumor: Trabuco to the Finance Ministry→ Hope for austerity measures→ Dollar −1.53% · DI −16 bp

The central bank sold US$2 billion in a "leilão de linha"; repurchase on 07/02/2027.[11] Correparti: help for corporations that at year-end "buscam dólares para remessas de lucros e dividendos".[8] In the afternoon the name Luiz Carlos Trabuco, chairman of Bradesco's board, "circulou com mais força". FB Capital: "Ainda (está) no campo especulativo, mas é melhor que o sentimento da semana passada."[8][12][13]

Leilão de linha: the central bank sells dollars from its reserves and buys them back later. Like flour you lend the neighbor for the holidays: it comes back. Help against a seasonal squeeze, not permanent.

Planning Minister Moretti dampened Tuesday's PEC hope: the government "não tem pronta uma PEC" for mandatory spending.[8] My view: the market swapped the PEC hope for a name hope. Neither is a text or an appointment yet — the rally is vulnerable.

DI futures & basis point: bets on the future policy rate; a basis point (bp) is 0.01 percentage point. Like a fever thermometer for confidence in the state.

Chain 3 — Strong US data, yet a weaker dollar.

Q3 GDP 4.3% · PCE 3.8%→ Fed hike in Dec. almost certain→ but: Nasdaq −4% (AI stocks)→ 10-yr yield 4.84% (−6 bp)→ DXY 103.68

US growth Q3: 4.3% (expectation 3.5%).[15] PCE inflation 3.8%, core 3.3%; Fed governor Waller hinted at 0.5 points.[16] Still the dollar fell: AI stocks collapsed, money fled into US bonds.[12][17] The euro held at 1.0960.[5] The decline in EUR/BRL therefore came almost entirely from the real.

PCE: the inflation measure the US central bank watches most. Like the family doctor's thermometer: it decides the medicine.

Understanding, not a recommendation

On €1,000 the Wed fix (6.4531) yields, on paper, R$6,453.10 — R$108 less than on Monday, R$805 more than before the runoff (10/23: 5.6485).[1] At this morning's rate (6.38–6.39) it would be only R$6,382–6,389 — R$64–71 less than at the fix.[3][4][5] Wednesday's fix is already history. My view: the rally rests on an unconfirmed name. If the appointment doesn't come, the rate is more likely to drift back toward 6.45; if it comes, 6.25 is possible. The decision is yours.

It's Thursday, November twenty-sixth. The real had a strong day yesterday. According to Reuters, the dollar fell one and a half percent to five point eight two, going against the trend of other emerging markets.

The European Central Bank's fix missed that move. It was set in the morning, when the dollar still stood at about five point nine zero, and it came in at six point four five. This morning, the calculation for the euro against the real shows only about six point three eight. That's roughly one percent lower. Today's fix will probably catch up.

Three things drove the real. The central bank sold two billion dollars from its reserves, with a buyback in July. Traders see that as help for companies' year-end profit remittances. Then in the afternoon a name for the Finance Ministry started making the rounds: Luiz Carlos Trabuco, the chairman of Bradesco's board. That is not confirmed.

On the budget, the news was mixed. The austerity package is now law. But on the same day, the forecast for this year's deficit rose to eighty-nine billion reais. And the planning minister says there is no finished constitutional amendment for mandatory spending.

Headwinds came from Washington. The U.S. government is proposing new tariffs on Brazil, including ten percent on aircraft. Embraer lost four percent. Brasília calls it unacceptable. The currency market ignored that yesterday.

In the U.S., the economy grew four point three percent in the third quarter, and inflation rose. A Fed rate hike in December is seen as almost certain. Still, the dollar index slipped, as AI stocks tumbled and money moved into U.S. bonds. Today is Thanksgiving, and U.S. markets are closed.

My outlook, as confidence and not a market number: for today's fix, most likely six point three eight. For the week, also six point three eight. For the month, six point three five. Over three to six months, six point two five, and over twelve months, six point three zero, within a wide range.

A thousand euros at Wednesday's fix gets you roughly six thousand four hundred fifty-three reais. At this morning's rate, that would be about seventy reais less. The rally rests on a rumor. Tomorrow we'll watch today's fix, inflation in Brazil, and whether the name gets confirmed. The decision is yours.
