ECB fix: the reference rate the European Central Bank measures once a day at 14:15 CET (10:15 BRT). Like a photo of traffic: it shows the jam at 10:15, not the evening.

PTAX: the official dollar and euro rate of the Brazilian central bank, averaged from four readings in the morning. Like the class average instead of a single grade.
Cross rate: compute the euro-real rate from dollar-real times euro-dollar. Like converting a price by going through a third currency. If it differs a lot from the fix, the market has moved since the photo.

Chain 2 — Five days, one risk premium.

Trabuco confirmed→ Fear of a loose finance minister fades→ DI Jan-30 −79 bp→ Dollar −7.03% on the week→ EUR/BRL −8.18%

EPS analyst Rostagno: “O mercado está retirando hoje os prêmios relacionados ao cenário de um ministro da Fazenda heterodoxo.”[10] The budget helped: the central government posted a primary surplus of R$ 52.268 bn in October; 47.55 bn was expected. Risk premium: the markup investors demand for uncertainty. Like a higher interest rate for a friend who has paid back late before. When a reliable treasurer arrives, the markup shrinks.
Primary surplus: the government's revenue minus spending, excluding interest. Like a household ledger where the loan payment is still missing: a plus means daily life is paid for.

Chain 3 — The euro side quietly pushes down.

France/Spain inflation weaker than expected→ ECB hike on 12/17 less certain→ Euro-dollar 1.0926 (−0.29%)→ EUR/BRL a touch lower

According to TE, inflation in France and Spain fell more than expected in November.[7] The euro-area figure comes on Tuesday; in October it was 4.0%.[35] In addition, the DXY rose to 104.04.[8] A strong dollar plus a weak euro pushes your rate down the most.

It's Saturday, November twenty-eighth. Markets are closed until Monday. Time to look back at a week that packed a punch.

On Friday the European Central Bank's fix fell almost five percent, to six point oh two. On Monday it was still six point five six. For a thousand euros, that means about five hundred thirty-six fewer reais than at the start of the week. For Friday I had expected around six point two one. That was clearly too high.

According to Reuters, the dollar in Brazil closed at five point five one, down four percent in a single day and seven percent on the week. The central bank in Frankfurt happened to take its reading right at the low of the day. After that, the dollar climbed back a little. This weekend the euro stands at around six point oh eight. That's the honest starting point for Monday.

The reason is Trabuco. The banker takes over the Finance Ministry in January, and the market has shed its fear of a loose-spending minister. Interest-rate bets for twenty thirty fell by almost a full percentage point. On top of that came a better budget in October: a surplus of fifty-two billion reais, more than expected.

From Europe there's a quiet headwind. Inflation in France and Spain came in weaker than expected. That makes the European Central Bank's rate hike in December less certain, and the euro slipped a bit. The dollar index is near its highest level since March twenty twenty-five.

My take, as a confidence call and not a market number: for Monday's fix, most likely six point oh eight. For the week, also six point oh eight. For the month, and for three to six months, six point one oh. For twelve months, six point one five, in a wide range. The market has priced in a name. For the next step it needs numbers, and those come in January at the earliest.

One more thing: no bank has published a new forecast since the plunge. Monday morning brings the central bank's Focus survey. It's the first consensus figure after this week. Tuesday brings euro-area inflation, and Wednesday Brazil's growth numbers. The decision on when to exchange stays with you.
