German-Brazilian corner

This weekend's vocabulary comes straight from the fair: arte impressa is printed art, gravura is printmaking, xilogravura is woodcut, serigrafia is screen printing. And dobra simply means "fold" — the festival's name is the hand motion that turns a printed sheet into a booklet.

Then there's the word that governs the calendar: recesso. It sounds like "vacation," but it really means a break — this time exactly two weeks. The long break here doesn't fall in summer, but around the turn of the year. For a German father, that remains the strangest calendar quirk of all: July means early fog and jackets, and the big holidays smell like Christmas.

Health: the good number for the baby — and the alarm that stays

The good news targets exactly the youngest person in the house: Paraná's health department, SESA, reports a 81.1% drop in severe respiratory cases (SARI) caused by the RS virus in infants under six months — credited to the RSV immunization program the Ministry of Health rolled out in December 2025. Published on 07/16/2026 in the context of the InfoGripe bulletin for epi-week 27/2026 (07/05–11); the source doesn't name an exact comparison window.[6]

RSV: a cold virus that gives adults the sniffles and can turn dangerous for infants. The reason is pure geometry: a newborn's smallest airways are as thin as a drinking straw — what's a bit of mucus in a wide pipe for us clogs the whole straw down there. That's why the same cold hits a baby completely differently than it hits their siblings.
SARI: Síndrome Respiratória Aguda Grave — Brazil's catch-all category for "severe respiratory infection requiring hospitalization," regardless of the pathogen. Think of it as a basket labeled "trouble breathing": RSV is one fruit inside, flu is another, Covid a third. If one basket-dweller shrinks, the basket can still stay full.
Why both can be true at once That's exactly what's happening here. In the same bulletin, Fiocruz counts Paraná among five states whose SARI incidence sits at alarm, risk, or high-risk level — with signs of a longer-term upward trend (alongside Mato Grosso do Sul, Minas Gerais, Santa Catarina, and Rio Grande do Sul). It isn't a contradiction: the 81.1% drop covers a narrow slice — RSV-caused cases in under-six-month-olds — while the alarm covers the whole basket, across every age group. For us that means: the vaccine is working visibly right where it's supposed to, but winter isn't over. This isn't the all-clear.[6]
Specifically for this weekend and Monday Two things are coming up that both mean crowds: the fair in Londrina and the start of school. For the youngest, the old pecking order still applies — outdoors over indoors, short over long, early over crowded. At the fair, the early afternoon beats the packed evening, a carrier instead of a stroller (at head height she gets sneezed on less). From Monday on, the schoolkids bring home again whatever's circulating in class: washing hands before anyone picks up the baby is the single most effective house rule. And if an infant is breathing fast or visibly labored, feeding poorly, or seeming unusually listless, don't wait until tomorrow — that's parental judgment, not something a bulletin can spell out.

Dengue: a good season, no fresh figure today — and a numbers trap

On SESA-PR's bulletin overview, Informe Epidemiológico no. 23 from 07/21/2026 remains the most recent entry; the figures aren't in the text there, only in the linked PDF. The last fully documented status remains epi-weeks 1–14/2026 (through 03/14/2026): 2,028 confirmed cases and 1 death statewide — versus 52,193 cases and 90 deaths in the same period in 2025, or down 96.1% and 98.8%. A very good season, but four months old: labeled as such, not extrapolated.[7][8]

Numbers trap: "4,549 new cases" doesn't belong to this year Search results are circulating a weekly bulletin citing 4,549 new dengue cases and one more death in Paraná. That figure belongs to the 2025 epidemiological year — the same text lists 83,665 reports, 20,050 confirmed diagnoses, and 13 deaths for that year. Anyone reading a dengue headline should first check the year: the 2025 and 2026 figures differ by roughly a factor of twenty.[13]

And because rain falls today and sun with 25 to 29 °C follows from tomorrow: walk the yard on Sunday morning and empty out anything holding water — saucers under flowerpots, buckets, tarps with dips, clogged gutters, old tires, toys. Five minutes that prevent an entire mosquito generation: the Aedes mosquito doesn't lay its eggs in streams but in small amounts of clean, standing water — a bottle cap's worth is enough, and it takes barely a week from egg to flying mosquito.

It's Saturday, July twenty-fifth, and this is the last free weekend: school starts back up on Monday. This time there's actually something on the calendar — in Londrina, the DOBRA Festival runs this Saturday and Sunday.

DOBRA means "fold" in Portuguese, and that's exactly what it's about: printed art, booklets, zines, comics, woodcuts, and screen prints. At Funcart, on Rua Senador Souza Naves, number two thousand three hundred eighty, eighty-five exhibitors from all over the country are showing their work. Admission is free until six p.m., then it costs fifteen reais per person. For the kids there's a dedicated band show, a bamboo circus, and an eco-themed play area, and between three and six p.m. there are two open printmaking workshops, explicitly free and requiring no prior experience. Music starts Saturday at five p.m. and Sunday at four p.m. To be honest about it: neither source states the fair's exact opening hours — only the music times and the three-to-six window are documented.
