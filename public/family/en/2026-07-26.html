Weather: the week's nicest day arrives as a send-off

Climatempo reports 14 to 24 °C with 0.0 mm of rain for today and describes the day as "Dia de sol, com nevoeiro ao amanhecer e à noite" — a sunny day with fog at sunrise and again in the evening. As a second portal, o tempo shows 14 °C for Ribeirão Claro in the early morning at 95% humidity and a high of around 22 °C near 2 PM, clearing up. Two degrees apart on the high, identical pattern: cold, damp morning, mild clear afternoon, not a drop. That satisfies the hard two-portal rule — planning follows this picture.[1][2]

The coming days stay on the same track and even get warmer: Monday 26/15 °C, Tuesday 27/15 °C, Wednesday 27/18 °C, each mostly clear and with no rain in the forecast. So the ground dries out and paths become walkable again — and this week's school runs stay dry.[2]

The catch with a clear winter day: UV index "extreme" Climatempo has the UV index at its highest level today. This is the classic trap you don't bring with you from Germany: at 14 °C in the morning, nobody feels like reaching for sunscreen, but the sun here still sits high enough in July to cause a sunburn within half an hour. Hat, a T-shirt with sleeves, shade between 10 AM and 4 PM — and for a four-week-old baby, the simplest rule of all applies: no sunscreen, just shade, a canopy, or a light cloth over the stroller. Infant skin at that age simply doesn't belong in direct sun.[3]

No warnings are in effect: no active geo-hydrological CEMADEN warning could be found for our region — the most recent risk forecast, from 07/24/2026, covers exclusively Rio Grande do Sul (flooding) and Pernambuco (landslides); Paraná doesn't appear in it at all. No closure on BR-153, BR-369, or PR-092 either: the widening of BR-369 between Jacarezinho and Cambará continues as a standing construction project, not an acute obstruction.[4][5]

School: the second half-year starts tomorrow

The July recesso for Paraná's state schools ran from July 13 to 24; classes resume tomorrow, Monday the 27th, for the second half-year. The date is confirmed consistently by several regional outlets and matches the official SEED 2026 school calendar, which we checked yesterday.[6]

In practical terms, that means for tonight: pack the backpack, check the uniform, sort the notebooks — and pull bedtime back to school hours. Two weeks of recesso reliably push seven- and eight-year-olds' rhythm one to two hours later; otherwise Monday morning drags. Anyone still heading to Londrina today should plan the return trip so the evening doesn't turn into a night shift.

What else the school start changes at home Starting tomorrow, two kids come home every day from a full classroom — right in the middle of respiratory season, with an infant in the house. The most effective house rule for the coming weeks is also the most boring one: wash hands before anyone picks up the baby. More on that in the health section alongside.

The word of the day is on the indoor park's sign: fundo do mar — literally "ocean floor." A climbing tower with tube slides is named that here, hundreds of kilometers from the nearest beach; the sea is a longing, not a location.

Then there are the two words that rule Monday: volta às aulas, the return to class — what Germany would call "back to school," just twice a year. And dia letivo, the official school day, the unit of measure for a Brazilian school year. For a German father, that remains the oddest calendar quirk of all: deep winter, fog over the valley — and everyone's talking about the "second half-year," which is just now starting.

Health: the alarm stays — and tomorrow the classroom mixes again

One number targets exactly the youngest person in the house, and it's good: in Paraná, the severe respiratory cases caused by the RS virus have dropped 81.1% among infants under six months — attributed to the RSV immunization Brazil's Ministry of Health added to the program in December 2025. Reporting status is epi-week 27/2026 (07/05–11).[12]

The other number sits right beside it and doesn't contradict it: in the same bulletin, Fiocruz still counts Paraná among five states whose SARI incidence sits at alarm, risk, or high-risk level — with an upward trend through epi-week 27/2026. Nationally, Fiocruz does report that RSV numbers are falling and the SARI curve is starting to tip over after five months of rising; but in many states the level stays high.[12][13]

RSV: a cold virus that barely fazes adults and can land infants in the hospital. The reason is geometry: a newborn's finest airways are as narrow as a drinking straw — what's a bit of mucus in a wide pipe for us clogs the whole straw down there.
SARI: Síndrome Respiratória Aguda Grave, Brazil's catch-all category for "severe respiratory infection, hospitalization needed" — regardless of the pathogen. Like a basket labeled "shortness of breath": RSV is one fruit in it, flu is another. If one kind of fruit drops sharply, the basket can still stay full. That's exactly what the two numbers above describe.
The first school week with a four-week-old baby Starting tomorrow, two schoolkids come back daily from a full classroom, while the state alarm keeps running. Not a reason for fear, but one for three simple house rules: wash hands before anyone picks up the littlest one — that beats every other measure. No one with a runny nose face-to-face with the baby; kiss the back of her head instead of her cheek. Air out the room briefly in the evening when everyone's sitting in one space — five minutes is enough. And if an infant is breathing fast or visibly labored, feeding poorly, or seeming unusually listless, don't wait until the next day. At the next posto de saúde appointment, it's also worth asking whether she qualifies for the RSV immunization running since December 2025 — the bulletins don't spell out the exact eligibility.

Dengue: a good season, no fresh figure — and a year trap

On SESA-PR's bulletin overview, the Informe Epidemiológico no. 23 from 07/21/2026 is the most recent entry; the case figures aren't in the text there, only in the linked PDF. The last fully documented status therefore remains unchanged at epi-weeks 1–14/2026 (through 03/14/2026, published 04/15/2026): 2,028 confirmed cases and one death statewide — versus 52,193 cases and 90 deaths in the same period in 2025, so −96.1% and −98.8%. Regionally, the 17th health region, Londrina, led with 534 cases. A very good season, but a four-month-old status: labeled as such, not extrapolated.[14][15]

Numbers trap: "22,200 cases" is from 2024 Search results prominently surface the headline "Novo boletim da dengue registra 22,2 mil casos e 11 óbitos no Paraná." That belongs to a bulletin from March 19, 2024, and has nothing to do with this season. It's the same trap as yesterday, just with a different vintage. The rule stands: with any dengue headline, check the publication year first — between 2024, 2025, and 2026, Paraná's figures differ by orders of magnitude, not nuance.[16]

That fits with Climatempo's second index: the mosquito risk stands at "high" today — after the previous days' rain and with three warm days on the way, exactly the combination that turns standing water into breeding grounds. This morning is the right moment to walk through the yard once and empty out anything holding water: saucers, buckets, tarps with dips, gutters, old tires, toys. The Aedes mosquito doesn't lay its eggs in streams but in small amounts of clean, standing water; a bottle cap's worth is enough, and it takes barely a week from egg to flying mosquito.[3]
