Health: RSV keeps rising — and today the classroom fills up [9]

RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus): a cold virus that feels like an ordinary sniffle in adults. Picture a baby's airways like a drinking straw and an adult's like a garden hose — the same amount of mucus clogs the straw instantly. That's why it's almost always the very youngest who end up in the hospital with it, while the big brother just coughs.

Fiocruz's InfoGripe bulletin for Epidemiological Week 27/2026 (07/05–11) notes that severe respiratory cases from RSV "continuam aumentando em toda a Região Sul" — meaning they keep rising in Paraná, Rio Grande do Sul, and Santa Catarina, while falling elsewhere already. Nationwide, RSV accounts for 57.2% of all positive respiratory samples over the past four weeks, with the under-two age group bearing the brunt.

For us, specifically Starting today, two schoolkids are back for several hours a day in a closed room with thirty others. That's the classic transmission route into the house. The three measures that actually help: wash hands right after coming home, no one with a runny nose holds or kisses the baby, and don't wait it out if the littlest one runs a fever or has wheezy breathing. None of this is new — today is just the day it starts to matter again.

No active severe-weather warning from INMET or Defesa Civil for the Norte Pioneiro today; the storm warning that circulated last week covered 07/24–26 and other regions. No dated closure is reported for BR-153, BR-369, or PR-092 — just the ongoing widening of BR-369 between Jacarezinho and Cambará, with no acute closure date.

Dengue: the number is in — and we live in the calmest spot [10]

Update since yesterday The SESA-PR's Informe Epidemiológico nº 23, published on 07/21/2026, can now be analyzed. It covers Epidemiological Weeks 01–28 of 2026 (SINAN data as of 07/14/2026) — and confirms the good season across the board: 5,701 confirmed cases and 4 deaths statewide. Compared with the same period in 2025, that's −93.6% in cases and −97.1% in deaths.

Autochthonous incidence: cases per 100,000 residents where the mosquito bit locally — cases brought in from a trip elsewhere don't count. It's the difference between "how many kids are sick at THIS school" and "how many across the whole country." Only this way can regions of different sizes be compared fairly.

What's interesting for us is the regional comparison, and it's a stark one. The 19ª Regional de Saúde Jacarezinho, which Ribeirão Claro belongs to (roughly 300,000 residents), reports for SE 01–28/2026 84 confirmed cases, 69 of them autochthonous, zero deaths — autochthonous incidence 22.98 per 100,000.

19ª RS Jacarezinho: 22.98 → 18ª RS Cornélio Procópio: 219.98

The neighboring region, Cornélio Procópio — right in the middle of our day-trip ring — comes in at 491 confirmed cases despite a smaller population, 474 of them autochthonous, one death, and an autochthonous incidence of 219.98 per 100,000. That's almost ten times our figure. Not a reason to cancel a day trip — but a good reason to pack repellent for the evening on a day trip there, and not ignore standing water around vacation homes or barbecue spots.

German-Brazilian corner: winter with UV "extreme"

It's the classic mistake a German makes twice here: 25 °C, a cool foggy morning, clear air — and the instinct says "no sunscreen needed." The UV index in Jacarezinho still sits at "extremo" today. In Germany, heat correlates with sunburn risk; here the two are completely decoupled in winter. A rule for the backpack: the hat has nothing to do with the thermometer.

Second calendar difference: Brazil's Dia dos Pais falls on the second Sunday of August, this year on 08/09/2026 — not on Ascension Day like the German Vatertag. Anyone sending something from Germany had better do it this week.

It's Monday, July twenty-seventh, and in Ribeirão Claro, the daily grind returns. After the recesso that started on July thirteenth, one point one million students in Paraná's state schools head back to the classroom today for the second half of the school year — with a hundred more school days still to go.

The weather is cooperating. Climatempo calls for fifteen to twenty-five degrees, fog early on, then sun and a clear night, with zero percent chance of rain — and a second portal for Ribeirão Claro shows the same picture. Tomorrow gets even warmer. But starting Wednesday, the showers come back, with roughly forty percent odds of rain in the afternoon and evening. Anyone with outdoor plans should get them done today or tomorrow.

One detail that's easy to miss as a German transplant: the UV index sits at extreme today. The cool winter air is deceptive. On the schoolyard, hat, sleeves, and shade apply just like in January, and for the littlest one in the house, it's shade, not sunscreen, all the way.

The biggest health story today comes from Fiocruz's InfoGripe bulletin for epidemiological week twenty-seven, covering July fifth through the eleventh. Severe respiratory cases from RSV keep climbing across the whole South — Paraná, Rio Grande do Sul, and Santa Catarina. Nationwide, this one virus now accounts for fifty-seven point two percent of all positive respiratory samples, and it hits children under two the hardest. For an adult, it feels like an ordinary cold. In a baby, the airway is as narrow as a drinking straw, so the same amount of mucus that's nothing for us is enough to block it.

That's not an abstract statistic today. Starting today, two schoolkids are back for hours each day, packed into a closed room with thirty others — the classic route a virus takes home. Three things actually help: wash hands the moment you walk in the door, nobody with a runny nose holds or kisses the baby, and if the littlest one runs a fever or has wheezy breathing, don't wait it out.

Dengue, on the other hand, brings genuinely good news. The Health Department of Paraná's Informe Epidemiológico number twenty-three, published on July twenty-first, is now fully analyzable. For epidemiological weeks one through twenty-eight of this year, it counts five thousand seven hundred one confirmed cases statewide and four deaths. Compared with the same period last year, that's ninety-three point six percent fewer cases and ninety-seven point one percent fewer deaths.

The regional breakdown is even more interesting.
