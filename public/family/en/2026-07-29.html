Sorted out: two tips from the wrong year [8]

Two Ourinhos events were on this morning's candidate list and got bumped back off — both looked perfect at first glance.

"2ª Festa das Nações," Ourinhos, August 13–14, Recinto da FAPI, free admission. The article says "sábado, 13 de agosto" and "domingo, 14 de agosto." But August 13, 2026, is a Thursday. August 13 last fell on a Saturday in 2022 — and that's exactly the date carried by the page's own timestamp. The article is four years old.

Tiago Iorc at SESI Ourinhos, "August 24." Here a direct fetch was enough: the page dates the concert to Saturday, August 24, 2024, published 08/08/2024. Two years old.

The weekday test: Brazilian city and association portals recycle URLs and let old stories keep surfacing in new search results. The fastest counter-check costs ten seconds: if a story names both weekday and date ("sábado, 13 de agosto") and the two don't match up in the current year, the article is from a different year — almost always. A festival that supposedly calls a Thursday "Saturday" never happened.

That's no reason to write off Ourinhos — quite the opposite: the same search round turned up the CineSolar event on 08/05, published on 07/22/2026, complete with address, time, and a bad-weather plan (the screening moves to the school gym if it rains). Just from the city hall's own announcement, not from the search result.

Health & Safety [10][11][12][13]

Dengue. The SESA-PR's Informe Epidemiológico nº 23 (published 07/21/2026, SE 01–28/2026) lists 58,105 reported, 5,701 confirmed, and 4,761 autochthonous cases for Paraná cumulatively — meaning acquired locally, not brought in. For our 19ª Regional de Saúde Jacarezinho, that's 84 confirmed cases and zero deaths; the neighboring Cornélio Procópio region, right in the middle of the day-trip ring, stands at 491. So it stays the same: routine here, repellent in the bag for the day-trip ring.

Reading the numbers correctly The same document reports a drop of −93.9% versus 2025 and −99.1% versus 2024. That comparison doesn't hold up well: it pits 28 weeks of 2026 against two full years. The seasonal peak (January through May) is already included in those 28 weeks, but it isn't a week-for-week calculation — the real improvement is large, just not quite as large as the percentage suggests. An honest look the other way: deaths rose from 2 (as of SE 26) to 4 (SE 28). Two cases in absolute terms, a doubling in percentage terms — with numbers this small, the percentage says little, but it matters a great deal to those two families.
Baby context — data gap Here we have to pass today. The last respiratory status verified specifically for Paraná is SE 26/2026 (Agência Brasil, 07/09/2026, based on InfoGripe): Paraná was, at the time, among the states showing "indícios de interrupção do avanço ou início de queda" — that is, signs that the SRAG wave in the state is stopping or already flattening out. That's the good news. The caveat: Curitiba was at the same time one of nine state capitals with SRAG activity at alert, risk, or high-risk level, with the increase there concentrated mainly in children under two to four years old. That's three weeks old. No fresher, Paraná-specific bulletin was available today. Nationally, July's reports describe a downward trend, and Paraná was already on the good side of it back then — but that's not current evidence.
SRAG: "Síndrome Respiratória Aguda Grave" — the Brazilian umbrella term for respiratory infections severe enough to land someone in the hospital. Think of it like a traffic light that doesn't count how many kids have a sniffle, but how many are hit hard. That's exactly why it's the relevant figure for a one-month-old baby — and exactly why three-week-old data isn't enough here.

When there's a data gap, the rule applies, not the number: wash hands right after coming home, no one with a sniffle near the baby, and on outings, avoid crowded halls when the baby is along.

Severe weather. According to search results, an INMET orange-level alert was in effect today for parts of Paraná, valid until midday on 07/29: rain up to 60 mm/h, gusts of 60–100 km/h, hail risk, around 131 municipalities. Whether the Norte Pioneiro is included, we couldn't confirm — the source page returned HTTP 403, and the description points toward the southern half of the state, so probably not us. The alert expires by noon today regardless, and our two weather portals report a maximum of 3.7 mm for Ribeirão Claro. We're presenting it as context, not a local warning.
