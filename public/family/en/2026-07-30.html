School: day four — and a calendar that's lying [9]

The rede estadual's recesso ran from July 13 to 24 (Monday to Friday), and classes have been back since Monday, 07/27. Today is day four. That's what the Seed-PR's resolution for the 2026 school year says, and it matches the coverage of the break's start for the roughly 1.1 million students in the state school system.

And yet a well-ranked online holiday calendar still claims today that the recesso runs through 07/31 and the second half of the year starts on 08/03. Anyone relying on it would have kept the kids home this week. It's wrong — and the kind of error is a typical one.

Why Brazilian school-holiday dates shift: In Germany, school holidays are fixed years in advance, coordinated centrally and published by the Kultusministerkonferenz. In Brazil, each state sets its own school year, by resolution of the state education authority — and that resolution sometimes gets amended mid-year too, for instance to make up missed class days. Aggregator websites pull their data once and never update it again. The only reliable chain is therefore the Seed-PR resolution → your own school's notice; everything else is a copy of a copy. Practical rule: when two sources disagree about when a break ends, the one that can cite a resolution number wins.

Health & Safety [10][11][12][13][14]

Baby context — correction from yesterday Yesterday this brief said Paraná was showing "signs that the SRAG wave is stopping or flattening" — based on the InfoGripe status for SE 26/2026. That was the freshest verified figure at the time, but it's no longer the current picture. The InfoGripe bulletin for SE 27/2026 (published 07/16/2026) states explicitly: "Os casos de SRAG por VSR continuam aumentando em toda a Região Sul (Paraná, Rio Grande do Sul e Santa Catarina)" — RSV cases keep rising across the entire southern region. And Paraná is listed there among the five states with incidence at alert, risk, or high-risk level and a growth signal (along with MS, MG, SC, RS). The follow-up bulletin for SE 28 (07/12–18/2026) confirms the direction: Paraná still at "risco ou alto risco" with "aumento das ocorrências nas últimas semanas." For a one-month-old girl, that means: caution stays high, not declining.
RSV: A respiratory virus that looks like a common cold in adults and triggers bronchiolitis in infants — Fiocruz calls it "umas principais causas de bronquiolite nas crianças pequenas." Picture a baby's airways like a drinking straw and an adult's like a drainpipe: the same amount of mucus is a throat-clear in the pipe and a total blockage in the straw. That's exactly why a sniffle the eight-year-old shakes off in two days is a doctor's-visit matter for the baby.

The national frame, so the number doesn't look bigger than it is. The same bulletin reports 115,203 SRAG cases nationwide so far in 2026, of which 60,200 (52.3%) had a positive lab result. Its own headline reads: RSV numbers are falling, but stay high in many states. Both are true at once — nationally it's trending down, in the south including Paraná it isn't yet. We're on the wrong side of the national trend.

Dengue: unchanged, and that's the good news. The SESA-PR's bulletin overview still lists today's Informe Epidemiológico nº 23 (published 07/21/2026, SE 01–28/2026) as the latest status — no nº 24 has appeared. The catch: it's comparing January–July 2025 against 2024. The report is a year old and says nothing about summer 2026. Neither number is wrong, they're just from different windows. The only figure with a current validity claim is the one from Informe nº 23 above.

Exactly the kind of thing we've been searching for in the wrong radius for weeks. The catch: the site lists no class days, no times, no age limits. So the next step is a phone call, not a sign-up.

And now the correction, which matters more than anything else in this brief. Yesterday we said Paraná was showing signs that its wave of severe respiratory illness was flattening out. That was the freshest data at the time, but it's no longer accurate. The InfoGripe bulletin for epidemiological week twenty-seven, published July sixteenth, states outright that RSV cases keep climbing across the entire southern region, Paraná named explicitly — and it places Paraná among the states with a growth signal. The follow-up bulletin for week twenty-eight confirms that direction.

For a one-month-old girl, that means caution stays high, not declining. This virus looks like a cold in adults; in infants it triggers bronchiolitis. Picture a baby's airway like a drinking straw and an adult's like a drainpipe: the same amount of mucus is a throat-clear in the pipe and a complete blockage in the straw. So the same rules still apply: wash your hands the moment you're home, keep anyone with a sniffle away from the baby, and skip crowded indoor spaces when the baby's along. Nationally, by the way, the numbers are falling — just not yet in the south.

Dengue stays calm and unchanged: Informe number twenty-three from July twenty-first is still the latest status, with eighty-four confirmed cases and no deaths for our Jacarezinho health region. One warning, though: anyone who googles dengue in Paraná will quickly land on a report touting an eighty-five percent drop. But that compares January through July of twenty twenty-five with twenty twenty-four — a full year old, and it says nothing about this summer. The same trap shows up with school: a well-ranked online holiday calendar still claims break runs through July thirty-first. It's wrong, because in Brazil each state sets its own school calendar by resolution and can amend it mid-year. When two sources disagree about when a break ends, the one that can cite a resolution number wins.
