The core ring gets a week [6][7]

For weeks, this brief's event search has ended in the same place: no dated event can be found online for Ribeirão Claro and its surroundings, so we drive. From August 12 to 16, that changes. Sesc Paraná is bringing the 45ª Semana Literária e Feira do Livro to 27 cities across the state, and two names on the city list really matter to us: Jacarezinho — about 50 km, core ring — and Cornélio Procópio in the day-trip ring. The 2026 edition's honored author is Adélia Prado; the Sesc site explicitly lists Feira do Livro, Contação de Histórias, and Oficinas as program categories — a book fair, storytelling, and workshops. Regional coverage cites over 600 activities statewide and calls the whole thing free.

What we still don't know We don't have the times or the venue for Jacarezinho. The official Sesc site only releases the city program after a dropdown selection, and that step runs through JavaScript we can't read. A local news outlet ran the headline "Semana Literária traz 20 atrações gratuitas para Jacarezinho" — 20 free attractions — but its full text is blocked for us (HTTP 403). We're treating the number 20 as exactly what it is: a headline. Ask Sesc directly for concrete times the week before August 12.

Even so, this is the best event news in a long time. A book fair with storytelling within bicycle range rather than highway range is a fundamentally different thing for a household with an infant than a cultural center 110 km away: you can go, leave, come home for lunch, and go back in the afternoon — without the whole day hanging on a sleep and nursing rhythm that isn't negotiable.

Today, and what else the week has to offer [5][11][12]

Today, nothing dated is happening in the core ring — no town festival, no holiday, no day off school afterward. Anyone who still wants to fill the Sunday has exactly one confirmed option within reach: Sesc Londrina Cadeião is open Sundays from 9 AM to 6 PM, with "Desenhando com o corpo e o som" (music, "gratuito") at 3 PM and the "Show com Souza" (price not published) at 5 PM. The exhibit Espaço Aromas e Arados runs Tuesday through Sunday from 9 AM, also free. At 1½ to 2 hours' drive, that's a morning decision, not an afternoon one.

During the week it stays quiet until Saturday arrives. Two dates are worth putting on the calendar: Tuesday 7 PM movie "Meu bolo favorito" at Sesc Londrina (free, but a weeknight evening slot) and Wednesday 08/05, CineSolar in Ourinhos — a solar-powered outdoor cinema in the parking lot of EMEF Prof. José Alves Martins, 7 PM shorts, 8 PM "A Pequena Sereia," admission and popcorn free, "livre para todas as idades." About an hour's drive. The catch is the usual one with evening cinema: for a baby, 8 PM outdoors in the Brazilian winter is late and cold.

Health: which virus is going around [3]

Baby context The Fiocruz InfoGripe national bulletin states, for the four most recent epidemiological weeks through SE 27/2026 (the week of 07/05–07/11): "VSR representou 57,2% dos casos positivos, enquanto influenza A representou 10,6% e rinovírus 24,6%" — RSV accounted for 57.2% of positive samples, influenza A only 10.6%, rhinovirus 24.6%. And Paraná is among the states with SARI incidence "em alerta, risco ou alto risco" and a growth signal in the southern region's long-term trend.
RSV: a cold virus that in adults and school-age kids usually plays out like a stubborn runny nose, but in infants clogs the finest airways — which is why it's almost exclusively the very youngest who end up hospitalized with it. Picture a garden hose and a drinking straw: a millimeter of mucus is nothing to the hose, but it completely blocks the straw. A one-month-old breathes through the straw. In practice that means: when someone in the house is "just a little congested," that's true for the grown-ups and not true for the baby — wash your hands before picking her up, and anyone with a cold skips kissing her these weeks.

How old is this number? Honestly: two weeks older than the state bulletin this brief cited on Friday. But it doesn't contradict that bulletin — it answers a different question. The state bulletin says how many severe cases there are, and that number has recently dropped noticeably. InfoGripe says which virus is behind it when someone does get sick. The answer to that second question has stayed the same for weeks: RSV, especially in children under two.

What that means for 08/08. Not that nobody goes — the Sesc Saturday is a good offering, and the three older kids have a full day there. Rather, that the baby hour of all things is the one offering that doesn't fit our baby: a closed room with several children in exactly the age group that spreads this virus. Splitting up costs us one adult this week, and nothing else.

Dengue: the best figures of the year [4]

Informe Epidemiológico nº 23 from SESA-PR (published 07/21/2026, data through SE 01–28/2026) is still the latest as of today — the bulletin index doesn't list a nº 24. This time we were able to open the PDF directly, so what's here isn't an estimate but the official figures: 58,105 reports nationwide, 5,701 confirmed cases, and 4 deaths. Year over year for the same weeks: −93.6% in confirmed cases (2025: 89,150) and −97.1% in deaths (2025: 140).

Regionally, it looks like this: our 19ª Regional de Saúde Jacarezinho stands at 84 confirmed cases and zero deaths, an incidence of 22.98 per 100,000. The neighboring 18ª Regional Cornélio Procópio, right in the middle of the day-trip ring, is at 491 cases, one death, and an incidence almost ten times higher at 219.98. The difference is big enough to change behavior: routine for us — tip out standing water once a week, empty plant saucers. Heading toward Cornélio Procópio, Bandeirantes, and Andirá, repellent goes in the bag.

The numbers trap, same every year Two incorrect comparison figures are still easy to find in circulation. First, the report "Dengue in Paraná −85% / −82% deaths" — that compares January–July 2025 with 2024, is a year old, and says nothing about this winter. Second, "−96.1%," a correct figure, but for SE 01–14/2026, meaning mid-March. Anyone reading a dengue number today should first check the epidemiological week and the year — otherwise you end up comparing two different seasons with each other.

German-Brazilian corner: one week to Dia dos Pais [9]

Sunday, 08/09, is Dia dos Pais — the second Sunday in August, explicitly not a holiday. The difference from the German Vatertag is bigger than the date: in Germany it falls on Ascension Day, a public holiday in the middle of the week, with the handcart tradition and a men's outing. In Brazil it falls on an already-free Sunday and is purely a family affair — lunch, church, a gift. In practice that means three things: school crafts projects this week are for this date; restaurants nearby will be packed at lunchtime on 08/09, so anyone wanting to eat out should book now; and anyone wanting to send something to family in Germany gets a second occasion in the year with the Brazilian date, instead of a missed one.
