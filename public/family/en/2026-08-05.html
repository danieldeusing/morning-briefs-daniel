Respiratory illness: elevated, but not rising — and what that means for the baby [3]

The most current status remains the InfoGripe bulletin for SE 29/2026 (data week 07/19–07/25/2026, published 07/30). A bulletin for SE 30 hasn't come out yet today — Fiocruz publishes on Thursdays, so tomorrow at the earliest. The bulletin sorts the states into two groups, and that distinction is the whole story: three states have SARI incidence at alert, risk, or high-risk level and a growth signal in the long-term trend — Maranhão, Rio Grande do Sul, Santa Catarina. Twelve more states are also at those levels, but without a growth signal — and Paraná is named among them. Nationally, cases are falling in both trends.

SARI and RSV: "SARI" (severe acute respiratory infection; SRAG in Portuguese) is the Brazilian reporting category for a respiratory infection severe enough to land someone in the hospital — not every cough, but the share that needs oxygen. "RSV" is respiratory syncytial virus. For adults it's a cold; for a child under two, it can swell the small airways so much that drinking and breathing at the same time stop working. Picture a garden hose swelling from the inside: with a thick hose you wouldn't notice, with a drinking straw it closes up. That's exactly why the same virus hits adults and infants so differently — and exactly why this number belongs in a family brief.
The rules for the baby stay the same Over the last four epidemiological weeks, 57.7% of all virus-positive SARI samples in Brazil were RSV — by far the largest share, ahead of rhinovirus (25.4%), influenza B (9.1%), influenza A (7.7%), and SARS-CoV-2 (1.8%). And the bulletin says explicitly where this rise sits in the three growth states: "O aumento de SRAG nesses estados se concentra especialmente nas crianças pequenas e tem sido impulsionado principalmente pelo VSR." Paraná isn't in that group — but with a one-month-old in the house, "elevated without growth" is no reason to change the routine: wash hands before anyone picks up the baby. Anyone with a cold skips kissing her these weeks. And packed indoor spaces — the Aquário on a rainy Saturday is this week's example — are the program item where splitting up makes more sense than bringing her along.

To put a contradiction in context: reporting keeps circulating the phrase that Paraná is "at risk or high-risk level" with a rising trend. That comes from the assessment for SE 28/2026 (07/12–07/18) and has been superseded by the SE 29 bulletin: the level is correct, the growth signal no longer is. We're citing the primary source here, not the secondhand reporting.

At ten-thirty there's a drum workshop, and at three-thirty in the afternoon a percussion workshop for the older kids. All free, all indoors. Add the free Churrasco do Paizão at Norte Shopping starting at eleven, and the also-free Aquário in Parque Ney Braga, and that's a complete Saturday without spending a single real — one that still works with eleven millimeters of rain.

One honest caveat: the SESC page doesn't list a minimum age for the baby session, and two hundred twenty kilometers round trip is still two hundred twenty kilometers. Worth a call ahead. Anyone who wants to stay closer to home has the artisan fair in Jacarezinho, Saturday from nine to five.

On health: the current InfoGripe bulletin, for week twenty-nine, splits the states into two groups. Three states have a high number of severe respiratory cases and a growth signal: Maranhão, Rio Grande do Sul, and Santa Catarina. Twelve more are also elevated, but without a growth signal, and Paraná is named among them. Elevated, but not rising. For the baby, that changes nothing about the routine: wash hands before anyone picks her up, and anyone with a cold skips kissing her these weeks. RSV still accounts for fifty-seven point seven percent of all positive samples, and it hits the very youngest hardest.

On dengue, there's an unusual gap. The new bulletin, number twenty-four, came out yesterday, but the file is a scanned image with no readable text — we simply can't read it. So the last solid number stays bulletin number twenty-three: five thousand seven hundred one cases and four deaths, for weeks one through twenty-eight. And a warning that's relevant again this week: the circulating figure of minus ninety-six point one percent is from mid-March, not from today.

Tomorrow, the next InfoGripe bulletin is expected, and we'll see whether the two weather portals converge on Saturday's rain. Both will be here.
