Saturday Takes the Rain, Sunday Gives It Back [1][2][3]

Today first. Climatempo puts Ribeirão Claro at 19–24°C, 3.1 mm of rain at 80% probability, and wind from the west-northwest with gusts up to 48 km/h — "Sol com algumas nuvens. Chove rápido durante o dia e à noite." For Jacarezinho, 50 km further, it's 18–24°C at 82% and 4.5 mm. This is the most uncomfortable day of the week, and it's a school day at the same time: no decision to make, just a rain jacket and laundry brought inside.

And then, for the first time in days, it becomes unambiguous. Saturday: 18–27°C, 11.3 mm at 67%. Sunday: 20–28°C and — literally — "0.0mm (no rain expected)." From Monday it turns cool and dry: 15–23°C, then just 14–21°C on Tuesday, with a note on strong temperature swings and gusts up to 53 km/h.

Update since Wednesday On August 5, this brief still said the rain curve was getting rolled fresh every morning. The comparison now shows something more precise: Saturday has held — 11.2 mm back then, 11.3 mm today — only the probability climbed from 47% to 67%, so the model has grown more confident. Sunday lost its 0.2 mm at 38% and now sits at flat zero. What moved instead was the temperature: Father's Day was supposed to bring 32°C as of Wednesday, now it's 28°C. That's the reverse of the day before yesterday's situation — the rain numbers have locked in, the warmth has pulled back. For planning purposes, that's a distinctly better order.

Where the portals disagree today For today, theweather.com puts the rain probability at 90% with a thunderstorm, Climatempo at 80% — that's close. On temperature, though, they diverge by 2–3°C: 19–24°C versus 61–71°F, or roughly 16–22°C. We're not picking a value, both stand here. On wind they agree again: 48 km/h gusts versus "strong northwest winds ranging from 14–35 mph," or up to about 56 km/h. SIMEPAR, rung 1 of our source ladder and the most regionally precise, was again unreachable with HTTP 403 — a third, tie-breaking value is therefore missing again today.
"Ciclone-bomba": That's what Brazilian headlines these days are calling a low-pressure system whose central air pressure drops especially fast within a single day. Picture a bathtub whose plug isn't pulled slowly but yanked out: the water doesn't drain gently, it rushes. In weather terms, the rushing water is air — and fast-moving air means a storm. That's exactly what has been happening over southern Brazil since August 6.
There is a wind warning today — just not confirmed to cover us First, some context: the number circulating through the media — up to 100 km/h — applies, per INMET, to Paraná's coast, and we sit about 500 km inland. But there is very much an active warning. INMET issued an aviso laranja ("Perigo," 60–100 km/h) for Vendaval on 08/06 that takes effect from Friday, 08/07 — in the words of the INMET forecast page, for "centro-leste do Rio Grande do Sul, leste de Santa Catarina, Paraná e litoral sul de São Paulo." In parallel, the INMET warning feed this morning carries a yellow Vendaval alert ("Perigo Potencial," 40–60 km/h, "baixo risco de danos") valid 08/07, 12:00 AM–11:59 PM. What couldn't be confirmed: whether the Norte Pioneiro is included. The retrieved excerpt of the municipality list names Curitiba, Paranaguá, and Cascavel, but neither Ribeirão Claro nor Jacarezinho, Cornélio Procópio, or Londrina; one media report names, for Paraná, explicitly only the Região Metropolitana de Curitiba. The older alert that named the Norte Pioneiro by name expired at 11:59 PM on 08/01. In practice that's not an all-clear, it's caution: our local forecast puts today's gusts at up to 48 km/h — right in the middle of the yellow 40–60 km/h band. So plan today as though under an active warning: secure the trampoline, patio furniture, umbrella, and clothesline, don't park under large trees.

Dengue: nº 24 Is Readable — and the Number That Matters Isn't Ours [7]

Update since Wednesday On August 5, this brief said SESA-PR's Informe Epidemiológico nº 24 had been published but couldn't be read as an image scan. That's resolved: the numbers are now available. The data stands at SE 01–30/2026, published 08/04/2026.

Paraná overall: 59,186 reported suspected cases, of which 5,775 confirmed (dengue, DSA, and DG combined), 4,810 of those autochthonous — meaning infected within the state itself. 5 deaths, one more than in bulletin nº 23. The incidence of autochthonous cases stands at 40.45 per 100,000 residents. Year over year, this remains one of the calmest seasons in a long time: −93.5% confirmed cases versus 2025 and −99.1% versus the 2024 outbreak year. Case fatality has also fallen, from 3.74 (2025) to 2.17 — a drop of 41.9%.

And now the number that matters for this weekend. Our 19th Regional de Saúde Jacarezinho (300,313 residents, covering Ribeirão Claro and Jacarezinho) reports 1,277 suspected cases, of which 94 confirmed, 78 autochthonous, zero deaths — incidence 25.97 per 100,000. That's the calm number. Right next door it looks different: the 18th RS Cornélio Procópio with 499 cases, one death, and 223.69 per 100,000 — 8.6 times the incidence. And our weekend destination, the 17th RS Londrina, with 1,616 cases, two deaths, and 126.92 per 100,000 — nearly five times home.

Incidence per 100,000: Why not just compare case counts? Because Londrina has almost a million residents and our regional has 300,000 — more people automatically means more cases, without the risk being any higher. "Incidence per 100,000" strips that out: how many cases would there be if the same number of people lived everywhere? It's like comparing two school classes — not "which class had more sick kids," but "which one had proportionally more going on." Only this number says anything about the risk in a given place. And it says: home is calm, toward Cornélio Procópio and Londrina it isn't.
Two practical consequences First, for Saturday: anyone driving to Londrina should bring repellent — not out of panic, but because dengue circulates there at roughly five times the rate it does at home, and because time outdoors (the Churrasco parking lot, the walk to the Aquário) is exactly when Aedes aegypti bites. For a one-month-old, a mosquito net over the stroller is the better tool instead — repellent generally isn't recommended under six months. Second, for Monday: after two days totaling around 15 mm of rain, Monday brings the first dry weekday — the right moment for the walk-around the property. Saucers, gutters, buckets, old tires, tarps. A week of standing water is enough for the mosquito to raise an entire generation.

Respiratory Illness: No New Bulletin — but a Detail for Our Two Little Ones [8]

The most current confirmed status remains the InfoGripe bulletin for SE 29/2026 (data week 07/19–07/25/2026, published 07/30). Fiocruz publishes on Thursdays, and yesterday was Thursday — but a bulletin for SE 30 couldn't be confirmed today, either through the Fiocruz pages or the usual PDF address. So there's no new figure here, just the last verified one, with its week attached. It'll be added as soon as it's confirmed.

For Paraná, it stays the good half of the news: the state is among the twelve units with SARI incidence at alert, risk, or high-risk level, but without a growth signal in the long-term trend. Nationwide, through SE 29, 125,914 SARI cases have been reported, of which 67,199 (53.4%) have positive lab confirmation. RSV remains the clear driver, at 57.7% of all positive samples over the last four weeks, but per the bulletin is stabilizing at a high level. Behind it: rhinovirus (25.4%), influenza B (9.1%), influenza A (7.7%), and SARS-CoV-2 (1.8%).

The detail that affects this household The bulletin notes that influenza B shows a higher incidence specifically among children under two — that's two of ours at once: the two-year-old and the one-month-old girl. Influenza B is flattening in the overall trend, but the age distribution stands. Severe influenza A cases remain elevated. In concrete terms for Saturday: the Aquário on a rainy day is exactly the program item where half of Londrina crowds together indoors. Splitting up instead of bringing everyone is the better option there — and anyone with a cold doesn't pick up the baby these weeks without washing hands first. The dry Sunday outdoors is, from this angle, the better day with an infant anyway.

School, Vaccination, Roads [9][10][11]

School. August is a full month on the SEED-PR calendar: 21 class days, no break window, no statutory holiday. Today, 08/07, is "Dia do Funcionário de Escola" and Tuesday, 08/11, is "Dia do Estudante" — both commemorative dates with completely normal classes. And Dia dos Pais on Sunday isn't a holiday: Monday starts the week as usual, at 15–23°C and jacket weather. Anyone wanting to eat out Sunday at midday should book today.

Vaccination. The Campanha Nacional de Multivacinação 2026 has been running since 08/03 and continues through 09/01, for everyone under 15, free through the SUS; the nationwide "Dia D" is Saturday, 08/22. With four kids, everything still points to a quiet weekday in the next two weeks rather than the Dia D rush — pull out the vaccination booklets beforehand, staff only administer what's documented as missing.

Roads. On the BR-369 between Jacarezinho and Cambará, the widening continues: viaduct foundation work at km 17.5, a bridge over the Rio Alambari at km 19.5, per the report no lane closures, only spot-signaled work zones, completion targeted for early 2027. Important context: the most recent status report found is from 04/10/2026 and therefore four months old — that's the last verified status, not today's situation. Build in extra time on wet pavement today and tomorrow regardless.

It's Friday, August seventh, and for three days straight this brief has complained that the rain numbers get reshuffled every morning. Today that finally stops — and in the friendliest way it could.

Saturday holds steady as the wettest day of the week, at eleven point three millimeters. Sunday now sits at zero point zero millimeters, with no percentage attached at all — the first entry in days with no chance of rain behind it. And that dry day happens to land exactly on Father's Day.

The program runs the opposite direction. Saturday is the busy event day, and it happens to be entirely indoors. So the plan writes itself: Saturday under a roof, Sunday outside.

Today itself is the storm day. Climatempo puts Ribeirão Claro at nineteen to twenty-four degrees, three point one millimeters of rain at eighty percent, with gusts up to forty-eight kilometers an hour. Jacarezinho runs even higher, at eighty-two percent. It's the most uncomfortable day of the week, and it's a school day too — nothing to decide, just a rain jacket and the laundry brought inside.

Since Wednesday, the rain curve has actually settled down. Saturday held at eleven point two millimeters back then and eleven point three today — only the probability climbed, from forty-seven to sixty-seven percent. What moved instead was the temperature: Father's Day was supposed to reach thirty-two degrees as of Wednesday, and now it's twenty-eight. The rain numbers have locked in; the warmth has pulled back. That's a much better order to plan around.

Now, more on the wind — and this time in more detail. The headlines are calling it a bomb cyclone over southern Brazil, with winds up to a hundred kilometers an hour. That figure, per INMET, belongs to Paraná's coast, and we're roughly five hundred kilometers inland. But there is an active warning. INMET issued an orange Vendaval warning on the sixth of August that takes effect today, Friday — covering, in its own wording, eastern Santa Catarina, Paraná, and the southern coast of São Paulo, with winds of sixty to a hundred kilometers an hour. At the same time, INMET's warning feed this morning carries a yellow Vendaval alert with winds of forty to sixty kilometers an hour, valid all day. What couldn't be confirmed is whether our Norte Pioneiro falls inside it: the municipality list we pulled names Curitiba, Paranaguá, and Cascavel, but no town from our corner, and one media report names, for Paraná, only the region around Curitiba. The older alert that did name the Norte Pioneiro expired back on the first of August. So this isn't an all-clear, it's caution: our local forecast puts today's gusts at forty-eight kilometers an hour, right in the middle of that yellow band. Secure the trampoline, the patio furniture, and the umbrella today.

Saturday's plan practically writes itself. SESC in Londrina has added a fourth free slot since Wednesday: baby music sessions at nine and nine-thirty, a listening workshop also at nine, a drum workshop at ten-thirty, and percussion at three-thirty in the afternoon — all free, all indoors. Only the bookbinding workshop at two is sold out, so skip that one. Add the Churrasco do Paizão in the Norte Shopping parking lot from eleven, with bounce houses, classic cars, and three live bands, plus the free Aquário in Parque Ney Braga.

Sunday, meanwhile, is nearly empty on the calendar — and for once that's the good news. Twenty-eight degrees and no rain is the one window this weekend with no catch attached. Grill, yard, a morning outside with all four kids. For a one-month-old, a dry day in your own shade beats any crowded hall. Anyone who still wants to drive out can catch the Santuário in Londrina opening Family Week with Mass and a father's blessing at eleven.

On health, there's news that was still missing on Wednesday. Dengue bulletin number twenty-four was only a scanned image back then and unreadable. Now the numbers are in, covering weeks one through thirty. Paraná counts five thousand seven hundred seventy-five confirmed cases and five deaths, down ninety-three point five percent from last year. Our regional, Jacarezinho, reports ninety-four cases and zero deaths. But the number that actually matters is somewhere else: strip out population size and compare cases per hundred thousand people, and our regional sits at just under twenty-six, Cornélio Procópio at two hundred twenty-three, and Londrina — Saturday's destination — at one hundred twenty-seven. Almost five times home. So: repellent belongs in the car for Saturday, and a mosquito net over the stroller for the baby instead. And Monday, the first dry weekday, is the right time to walk the property for standing water.

On respiratory illness, no new bulletin could be confirmed. It stays at week twenty-nine: Paraná elevated, but without a growth signal. One detail hits this household directly — influenza B shows a higher incidence specifically in children under two, and we have two of those. In practice: at a packed, rainy Saturday Aquário, better to split up than bring everyone.
