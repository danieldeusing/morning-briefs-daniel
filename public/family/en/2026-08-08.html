Respiratory illness: the state figure is a month old [7][8]

Brazilian winter is respiratory-illness season, and the household has a one-month-old — that's why this section runs every week. Today with a caveat up front: the latest state-level Paraná figure is a month old.

State-level, confirmed — SESA-PR bulletin for SE 26/2026, published 07/10/2026: 13,782 hospital admissions for SRAG in 2026 versus 16,140 in the same period last year, a drop of 14.6%. The age breakdown is the real story for us: 5,723 cases among children under 6 — more than the over-60 group (4,572). The flu vaccination rate for priority groups stood at 49.80%, still short of half.

More current nationally — Fiocruz InfoGripe for SE 29/2026: SRAG numbers keep falling across most of the country, but Paraná remains among the twelve states with an incidence at alert, risk, or high-risk level. The next bulletin is expected Thursday.

SRAG: The abbreviation stands for Síndrome Respiratória Aguda Grave — "severe acute respiratory syndrome." It isn't a disease in itself but a reporting category: anyone admitted to a hospital with cough or fever and shortness of breath or low oxygen saturation gets counted as an SRAG case, regardless of the pathogen. Picture a box where the hospital drops every slip that reads "breathing bad enough that we have to admit them." The box says nothing about the cause, but a lot about how hard the season is hitting — which is why we watch it instead of individual virus names. In infants during the Brazilian winter, it fills up mostly from RSV.
Practical for this weekend The figure "5,723 children under 6" isn't a reason to stay home — it's a reason to treat indoors and outdoors differently. Sunday in your own backyard at 29°C is by far the safest program item of the weekend; tonight's Churras is also outdoors. What we're avoiding: a crowded mall interior at peak time with the baby in your arms. And the basic rule stands: anyone with a cold doesn't pick up the little one without washing their hands first.

SESA-PR's bulletin index still lists Informe Epidemiológico nº 24 from 08/04/2026 as the latest today — no new one has appeared. The data covers SE 01–30/2026. The figures were extracted from the PDF on 08/07 and stand unchanged.

Paraná overall: 5,775 confirmed cases and 5 deaths — −93.5% versus 2025 and −99.1% versus the outbreak year 2024. That's one of the calmest seasons in a long time. At home: the 19ª Regional de Saúde Jacarezinho reports 94 confirmed cases, zero deaths, incidence 25.97 per 100,000. At the weekend destination: the 17ª RS Londrina stands at 1,616 cases and 126.92 per 100,000 — nearly the fivefold. Anyone heading to tonight's Churras will spend three hours outdoors, and that's exactly when Aedes aegypti bites: repellent belongs in the car, and for the baby, the mosquito net over the stroller instead — repellent generally isn't recommended under six months.

Monday is the day for it After today's rain, a dry week begins Monday — the right time for the walk-around the property. Saucers, gutters, buckets, old tires, tarps. A week of standing water is enough for the mosquito to raise an entire generation, and today's rain fills exactly those containers.

School, vaccination, roads [10][11]

School. Dia dos Pais isn't a legal holiday — Monday starts on schedule, at 15–23°C and jacket weather. August has 21 class days on the SEED-PR calendar and no break window; Tuesday brings the Dia do Estudante, an observance day with normal classes. Anyone planning to go out for lunch tomorrow should book today — Father's Day is one of Brazil's biggest restaurant days of the year.

Vaccination. The Campanha Nacional de Multivacinação 2026 has been running since 08/03 through 09/01, for everyone under 15, free through the SUS; the nationwide "Dia D" is Saturday, 08/22. With four kids, everything still points to a quiet weekday sometime in the next two weeks rather than the Dia D rush — dig out the vaccination booklets beforehand; only what's documented as missing gets caught up.

Roads. No active closure could be found on BR-153, BR-369, or PR-092 for this weekend, nor any current CEMADEN warning for our region. The widening project on BR-369 between Jacarezinho and Cambará is still underway — a construction project, not a closure; the latest status report found dates to 04/10/2026, so four months old. Still, build in a buffer on wet pavement today, especially for the drive back in the dark.

It's Saturday, August eighth, and tomorrow is Dia dos Pais, Brazilian Father's Day. This time the weather lined up in an unusually friendly way: rain falls today, and none falls tomorrow.

Here's the detail. For Ribeirão Claro, Climatempo calls for a high near twenty-seven degrees Celsius and a low near seventeen today, with six point two millimeters of rain at a seventy-two percent chance, and gusts up to forty kilometers an hour. More important than the amount is the wording: passing showers during the day, and the weather settles down by evening. In Jacarezinho, fifty kilometers away, the day is less friendly — there it's ninety-eight percent.

Tomorrow calls for a high of twenty-nine and a low of nineteen, zero point zero millimeters, zero percent. No rain expected. That's the warmest and the only guaranteed dry day of the whole week, and it lands right on Father's Day. After that it turns cool: Monday fifteen to twenty-three degrees, Tuesday down to just thirteen to twenty.

Since yesterday, by the way, Saturday's rain number has roughly halved while getting more likely at the same time — from eleven point three millimeters down to six point two, but the probability climbed from sixty-seven to seventy-two percent. In other words: more likely, but less of it. Sunday held its zero, for the third day running.

Now for the program, which thankfully follows the same curve. Londrina has the fullest calendar, and its Father's Day headliner runs all day today: the Churras do Paizão at the Norte Shopping, from eleven in the morning to eleven at night, free admission. The live music is clustered in the evening, with sets at five-thirty, seven-thirty, and nine — right in that dry window. On the same grounds, in the back parking lot, a classic car meetup starts at six, also free. Two free events in one place, probably the highlight of the weekend for an eight-year-old.

That resolves yesterday's timing question. Two different start times were circulating, an eleven a.m. start and evening performances, and a report dedicated specifically to this event, from August fourth, shows both were right — they just described different parts of the same day. The kids' play area is confirmed too, for this exact mall: thirty minutes costs thirty-five reais, an hour costs forty-five, and the all-day pass is sixty. In practice: drive early and you catch the daytime program from eleven, but also the passing rain. The music and the dry weather both land in the evening, so with kids, arriving late isn't just fine — it's actually the better choice.

Sunday itself is nearly empty regionally, and today that's good news for once. Twenty-nine degrees and no rain in your own backyard beats any indoor hall with four kids, one of them a month old. Anyone who still wants to drive has three free options in Londrina: the Parada do Limoeiro from eight, a Mass with a father's blessing at the Santuário at eleven, and an outdoor children's theater at four in the afternoon.

From the core ring, on the other hand, there's nothing. Neither Ribeirão Claro nor Jacarezinho has a dated Father's Day event online. The only local option is the crafts fair in Jacarezinho today, from nine to five, held every second Saturday of the month — outdoors, on a day with a ninety-eight percent chance of rain. Short and in the morning, if at all.

Two health notes, both dated. The latest state-level respiratory figure is a month old: week twenty-six, published July tenth. Thirteen thousand seven hundred eighty-two hospital admissions this year, fourteen point six percent fewer than last year — but five thousand seven hundred twenty-three of those are children under six, more than among seniors. Nationally, week twenty-nine is current, and Paraná remains among the twelve states at alert level. In practice: outdoors, yes; crowded indoor spaces with the baby, better not.

On dengue, nothing has changed — bulletin number twenty-four from August fourth remains the latest. At home it's calm, ninety-four cases and zero deaths in our region. Londrina runs at almost five times that rate per hundred thousand residents. Anyone heading there tonight and standing outdoors for three hours should bring repellent; for the baby, a mosquito net over the stroller instead.

Tomorrow isn't a holiday, Monday starts on schedule, and since it's the first dry weekday, it's also the right day to walk the property and check for standing water.
