Dengue: the search-engine figure is from 2025 [5][6]

Anyone searching "Dengue Jacarezinho" today reliably gets served a big number: 6,037 to 6,705 confirmed cases for the 19ª Regional de Saúde. The number is correct — it's just a year old. It describes the entire epidemiological year 2025 (weeks 01–53/2025), the severe outbreak year, reported in January 2026. Read as the current situation, it would be off by roughly a factor of 70.

Seasonal trap Dengue runs in annual seasons, and search results mix prior-year outbreak numbers in with current ones. Rule of thumb for home use: if a number doesn't fit the yearly total, it's almost certainly from the wrong season. If Paraná overall stands at 5,775 cases, a single region can't be at 6,705 — that's a number from a different year.

The documented current count. SESA-PR's bulletin index still lists the Informe Epidemiológico nº 24 from 08/04/2026 as the latest today; no nº 25 has appeared. Data as of: SE 01–30/2026. Paraná overall: 5,775 confirmed cases, 5 deaths, −93.5% versus 2025. At home, 19ª RS Jacarezinho: 94 confirmed cases, zero deaths, incidence 31.30 per 100,000. In Londrina, 17ª RS: 1,616 cases, 164.87 per 100,000 — a good fivefold.

Incidence per 100,000: Raw case counts can't be compared between regions, because almost a million people live in Londrina and roughly 300,000 in our region. So you convert: "How many cases would there be if both areas had exactly 100,000 residents?" Like comparing prices at the supermarket — what counts isn't the package price but the price per kilo. For us it's 26 cases per 100,000, in Londrina 127. Only this conversion shows that the mosquito is markedly more active at the weekend destination than at home.

Practical, given Monday's new rain. Monday's 1.9 mm fills exactly the containers where Aedes aegypti lays its eggs: saucers, gutters, buckets, old tires, tarps. A week of standing water is enough for an entire mosquito generation. The right day for the property walk-around is therefore Tuesday or Wednesday — dry, cool, right after the rain. And for this afternoon in Londrina: repellent in the car, and for the infant, the mosquito net over the stroller instead. Repellent generally isn't recommended under six months.

And Monday's rain has a practical consequence. One point nine millimeters fills exactly the saucers, gutters, and buckets where the mosquito lays its eggs. The right day for the property walk-around is therefore Tuesday or Wednesday — dry, cool, right after the rain.

On respiratory illness, nothing's changed, and that means: the Paraná-specific figure is a month old, week twenty-six, with five thousand seven hundred twenty-three hospital admissions among children under six. Nationally, week twenty-nine applies, and Paraná remains at alert level. In practice, for today: outdoors, yes; a crowded indoor space with the baby, better not.

Tomorrow isn't a holiday, school starts on schedule, and August has twenty-one class days with no break window. Tuesday is Dia do Estudante, an observance day with normal classes.
