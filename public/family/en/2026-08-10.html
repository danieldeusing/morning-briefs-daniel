RSV now accounts for more than half of positive samples [5]

The expected InfoGripe bulletin is out — it appeared already on Thursday, 08/06/2026, covering SE 30/2026 (07/26–08/01). The overall direction stays good: SARI cases are falling across most of the country. 130,054 reports in 2026, of which 69,895 are lab-confirmed (53.7%).

But the composition is the real story. Over the last four weeks, RSV accounts nationally for 54.1% of all positive SARI samples — and 30.3% of deaths. Not influenza, not Covid: the virus currently filling hospitals is exactly the one for which a one-month-old is the single most vulnerable group. And Paraná remains one of the 14 states with incidence at "nível de alerta, risco ou alto risco" — up from twelve the previous week.

RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus; PT: VSR): A cold virus that runs as a runny nose with a cough in adults and clogs the smallest airways in infants — bronchiolitis. Picture it this way: an adult's airways are a garden hose, a newborn's are a drinking straw. The same amount of mucus that's barely noticeable in the hose seals the straw shut. That's why the same "just a cold" in the father is a hospital case in the baby. "SARI" is the Brazilian reporting category for it — Síndrome Respiratória Aguda Grave, the cases severe enough to reach a clinic.
Translated for this week No reason to stay home — reason to sort out the indoor spaces. Outdoors is uncritical: the dry days starting Tuesday are the best program on offer this week. Wednesday's hall is small and time-limited, which is defensible. What we still avoid is a crowded indoor space at peak time — the mall on Saturday afternoon, not the plaza. And the rule that matters more than anything else: anyone with a cold washes their hands before picking up the baby. No kissing while sniffling.
