Heat plan for the weekend For three kids who run around all day, a 30-degree Saturday is a gift; for a four-week-old, it's a job. An infant doesn't yet regulate temperature through sweating the way we do — cooling happens mostly through the skin and through what we take off them. In practice that means: outdoor time before 11 AM and after 4 PM, shade in between. Shade and thin, long clothing instead of sunscreen — for a baby this small, shade is the one truly reliable measure. No parked car, not even "just for a minute": a car in the sun heats up far past the outside temperature within minutes. And a breastfeeding mother needs noticeably more to drink at 32°C too, or the baby feels it before she does.

Health: a new line for the little ones [6][7]

There's no new InfoGripe bulletin today — SE 30/2026 (07/26–08/01, published Thursday, 08/06) remains the latest. Since Fiocruz publishes on Thursdays, the next one is expected on 08/13. The overall trend stays good: SARI cases are declining across most of the country; RSV accounts nationally for 54.1% of positive samples and 30.3% of deaths in the four-week window. Paraná is named among the 14 states with incidence at "nível de alerta, risco ou alto risco."

Newly read — the flu section. In the shadow of the RSV number sits a line that matters just as much for a four-week-old: for influenza A, the bulletin says incidence has "maior impacto nas crianças menores de 2 anos," while mortality mainly hits people over 65. For influenza B, the highest incidence is also among children under two — and mortality is elevated in exactly that age group, together with the elderly. Translated: this season, our smallest one has not one virus to watch, but three.

Influenza A and B: two branches of the same flu virus. A also lives in birds and pigs, so it keeps changing its appearance and drives the big, loud waves you see in the news. B circulates almost only among humans, changes more slowly, and makes smaller waves — which is why it's often considered the milder one. In Brazil's current numbers, though, that's not true for the little ones: B hits fewer children, but hits the ones it does hit harder. Picture it: A is the big wave at the beach that soaks everyone. B is the smaller one that breaks exactly where the smallest kids are standing.
