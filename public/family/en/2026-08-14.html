Health: the RSV Wave Has Finally Turned [10]

This is the first genuinely good health line in weeks, and it arrived overnight. Fiocruz published the InfoGripe bulletin for SE 31/2026, covering 08/02–08/08 with data entered through 08/08 — the bulletin this brief was still waiting on yesterday.

SRAG and RSV: SRAG is Brazil's label for a respiratory infection bad enough to send someone to hospital — think of it as the "this one got out of hand" bucket rather than a specific illness. RSV is the virus that fills that bucket in a Brazilian winter; in adults it's an ordinary cold, in babies under one it hits the small airways and is the single most common reason infants land in a hospital bed. Which is why a bulletin about a national statistic is, in this house, a bulletin about the baby.

The headline sentence is the one to keep: every state in the country is now either falling or flat on the six-week trend. Nowhere is still growing. Nationally, 133,709 severe cases have been notified this epidemiological year, 72,061 of them (53.9%) with a positive lab result. Among positives in the last four weeks, RSV accounts for 50.4% — down from 57.7% at SE 29 — with rhinovirus at 29%, influenza B at 9.2% and influenza A at 6.5%. Fiocruz says outright that influenza A's season is over for the year.

The honest other half: Paraná is still on both lists. It is one of 14 states whose SRAG incidence sat at alert, risk or high-risk level over the last two weeks, and one of eight where RSV cases specifically remain high even while falling. So the practical rules don't change yet — no crowded indoor rooms with the baby, hands washed before anyone picks her up, and a polite no to visitors with a cold. What changes is the outlook: the curve is pointing down everywhere now, so this is the beginning of the end of the season rather than the middle of it. Sunday's Cavalgada being outdoors is, in that light, a feature.

Dengue: Still Behind the Election Curtain [11]

No progress here, and it's worth naming why rather than papering over it. The newest dengue bulletin the state publishes is still Informe Epidemiológico nº 24 of 08/04 — re-checked this morning, nothing newer on the index. The pages on saude.pr.gov.br carrying the actual case and death counts answer with "Conteúdo indisponível devido ao período eleitoral": content withheld for the electoral period, plausible with Brazil's general election in October.

So the last fully verified numbers remain those from epidemiological weeks 1–14 of 2026: 2,028 confirmed cases against 52,193 in the same stretch of 2025, a drop of 96.1%, and one death against 90. That is roughly four months old. It says the 2026 season is running an order of magnitude below the 2025 epidemic — it does not say what August looks like. Standing water in the yard still gets tipped out weekly, election or no election.

The Multivacinação runs in the background: 08/03 through 09/01, more than 1,800 vaccination rooms across all 399 municipalities in Paraná, everyone under 15, with the national "Dia D" on Saturday 08/22. If any of the four cards has a gap, that's the low-friction way to close it.

The weekend is where it gets easy. Both portals show dry for Saturday and Sunday, twenty-six to twenty-nine degrees on Saturday, twenty-nine to thirty-two on Sunday. And that upgrades Sunday's plan from a gamble to a decision. In Assaí, at nine in the morning, the tropeiro horses get blessed, and at half past nine the big Cavalgada rides through town. Viola caipira music at midday, free admission. Honest caveat, unchanged from yesterday: that's two hours each way, it's our estimate not the source's, and nobody promises a changing table.

Then comes Monday, and Monday is the real instruction of this brief. Thirty-four degrees in one series, thirty-one and a clear sky in the other. For the toddler and the baby that means outside before ten or after four, shade and a hat in between, more water than feels necessary. For a baby this young, no direct sun at all, and no sunscreen either — clothing, a hat, and shade is the whole toolkit.

Now the good news, and it's real. Fiocruz published the InfoGripe bulletin for epidemiological week thirty-one overnight, covering the second through the eighth of August. Every single state in the country is now falling or flat on the long term trend. Not one is still growing. Nationally, a hundred and thirty-three thousand seven hundred and nine severe respiratory cases have been notified this year. Among the positive tests of the last four weeks, respiratory syncytial virus accounts for fifty point four percent, down from fifty-seven point seven two weeks ago. And the influenza A season, Fiocruz says plainly, is over for the year.

The other half stays honest. Paraná is still on both watch lists — one of fourteen states in the alert band, and one of eight where RSV cases remain high even while falling. So the rules for the baby don't change yet. No crowded indoor rooms, hands washed before anyone picks her up, a polite no to visitors with a cold. What changed is the direction of travel. And it's another argument for Sunday: a procession outdoors in open air is a gentler place for a small child than any packed hall.

Dengue made no progress at all, and it's worth saying why. The newest state bulletin is still number twenty-four, from the fourth of August, and the pages carrying the actual case counts answer with a notice that content is unavailable during the electoral period. So the last fully verified reading remains weeks one through fourteen of this year: two thousand and twenty-eight confirmed cases against fifty-two thousand one hundred and ninety-three a year earlier, and one death against ninety. That's four months old. It describes the season's order of magnitude, not August. The big vaccination day, the Dia D, is Saturday the twenty-second.

Two things for the calendar. The fifty-fourth EFAPI Expo runs from Wednesday the nineteenth through Sunday the twenty-third at the exhibition park in Santo Antônio da Platina, an hour away — livestock pavilions, a rodeo, a commercial fair and a food court, with big concerts in the evenings. And registration for Ribeirão Claro's signature dish contest stays open through the twenty-fourth of August.
