Health: The Maternal Vaccine Turned the Winter Around [8][9]

This is the number of the day, and it concerns our youngest directly. In the first 26 weeks of 2026, Paraná recorded 269 severe respiratory cases in infants under six months — in the same period in 2025, it was 1,421. That's a decline of 81.1% (SE 1–26/2026 vs. SE 1–26/2025).

SARI and RSV: SARI (SRAG in Portuguese) is Brazil's statistical term for a respiratory infection severe enough that someone needs hospital care. In babies, the main trigger is RSV (VSR in Portuguese), the respiratory syncytial virus — a cold in adults, an emergency in infants. Picture a garden hose: water still flows through a thick hose even with some mucus in it; a thin hose clogs. A baby's airway is the thin hose.

The reason is a vaccine for pregnant women that's been part of the national schedule since December 2025, and Paraná has rolled it out most thoroughly: 89.84% of eligible pregnant women have received it — ahead of Rio Grande do Sul (87.58%) and Santa Catarina (86.32%).

Vaccination during pregnancy→Antibodies cross the placenta→Baby protected before it can be vaccinated itself
Still, it's not the all-clear The latest Fiocruz InfoGripe bulletin for SE 30/2026 (07/26–08/01/2026, published 08/06) still lists Paraná among the 14 states with incidence at alert, risk, or high-risk levels — even as the nationwide trend declines and Influenza B in PR is explicitly noted as „manutenção da queda." A falling trend at a high level is still a high level. For a baby just a few weeks old, the rule stays the same: avoid crowded indoor spaces, wash hands before anyone touches the baby — and anyone heading into the packed Sesc hall this afternoon is better off leaving the youngest outside or at home.

The best news of the day comes from the health statistics, and it concerns our youngest directly. In the first twenty-six weeks of this year, Paraná recorded two hundred and sixty-nine severe respiratory cases in babies under six months. In the same stretch last year, it was one thousand, four hundred twenty-one. That's a drop of eighty-one point one percent.

The reason is a vaccine for pregnant women that's been on the national schedule since December. The mother gets vaccinated, the antibodies cross the placenta, and the baby is protected in its first months of life, before it can be vaccinated itself. Paraná has rolled this out more thoroughly than any other state: eighty-nine point eight four percent of eligible pregnant women have gotten the shot.

Still, it's not the all-clear. The latest Fiocruz bulletin, covering epidemiological week thirty, still lists Paraná among fourteen states in the alert and risk band. A falling trend at a high level is still a high level. For a baby just weeks old, the rule doesn't change: avoid crowded indoor spaces. Anyone heading into the packed Sesc hall this afternoon is better off leaving the little one at home. And mark the calendar: the big vaccination push, Multivacinação's Dia D, is Saturday the twenty-second, for everyone under fifteen.
