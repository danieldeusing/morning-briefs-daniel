Paraná Is Under Respiratory Alert — and There's a Baby in the House [2][3][4]

This is the most important line of today's brief. Fiocruz's national surveillance system, InfoGripe, lists Paraná among the 14 states whose SRAG incidence sat at alert, risk, or high-risk level over the last two reporting weeks. As of: SE 30/2026 — the week of July 26 to August 1 — published 08/06. And the breakdown is clear: of all samples in which any respiratory virus was detected at all over the last four weeks, 54.1% were RSV. Across the year to date it's 42.1% — so the share has recently risen. Both percentages are reported in the bulletin explicitly as nationwide figures; it doesn't include a breakdown for Paraná alone. So it's the alert classification that applies to Paraná, not this virus share — which changes little in practical terms, since RSV is currently driving Brazil's winter wave overall.

RSV (in Portuguese, VSR) and SRAG: For adults and school kids, RSV is just an ordinary cold — a runny nose, a cough, three tired days. In infants, the same virus attacks the tiny lower airways and clogs them: a bronchiolitis. Picture it as a garden hose versus a drinking straw — the same swelling that just leaves an eight-year-old huffing nearly closes off the passage in a baby. SRAG ("síndrome respiratória aguda grave") is the reporting category behind this: any respiratory infection severe enough to land someone in the hospital. So when authorities talk about "SRAG at alert level," they don't mean more sniffles — they mean more hospital admissions.

In practice, that means one thing above all for us this week: the virus doesn't come out of thin air — it comes home via the older kids from school. What actually helps is unspectacular and cheap — wash hands before anyone touches the baby; no kisses on the face, visitors included; anyone with a cough keeps their distance from her instead of cuddling up close; and avoid packed, closed-off rooms with her over the coming weeks. That's exactly why the Weekend Pick above has her staying home instead of going to the fairground.

When Not to Wait In a four-week-old, the warning signs of bronchiolitis are: fast, labored breathing, visible retractions between the ribs or under the rib cage, flaring nostrils, blue lips, audible wheezing — and, especially treacherous, poor feeding: a baby who can no longer latch and suck properly because she keeps having to gasp for air in between. Plus any fever at all in the first month of life. That's the point where you don't wait until morning — you go straight to the Pronto-Socorro.

Prevention comes in two pieces, and neither applies to every child. Since February 2026 the SUS has offered Nirsevimab, a single-dose antibody — but per its target-group definition, only to premature infants born at up to 36 weeks + 6 days and to children up to 23 months old with pre-existing conditions, administered at the maternity ward. A healthy, full-term baby does not qualify. Second, the state health department vaccinates pregnant women against RSV; those antibodies cross over to the baby while still in the womb. Whether that happened for us is in the prenatal record.

Source Transparency Both immunization details come from search-index summaries of official pages. A direct query of gov.br/saude today returned "Conteúdo Restrito — é necessário autenticar," and saude.pr.gov.br returned HTTP 403. So we're citing no dose numbers and no publication date on these two points — please double-check the target-group rules at your local UBS health post rather than planning off this brief.

The week's second health date fits right in: Saturday, 08/22, is the national Dia D of Multivacinação; the campaign runs through September 1 and aims to update „a caderneta de crianças e adolescentes menores de 15 anos" — the vaccination record of everyone under 15. For our three older kids, that's the easy route without a weekday wait. Whether anything is due yet for the baby is a matter of checking her own caderneta.

Dengue, meanwhile, stays unremarkable, but the solid figure is old: for SE 1–14/2026, SESA Paraná reports 2,028 cases and one death, against 52,193 cases and 90 deaths in the same window in 2025 — −96.1%. Informe No. 24 from 08/04/2026 exists, but its case figures weren't accessible today either; we're not extrapolating anything. The house rule stands regardless: four dry days, then showers starting Saturday — exactly the sequence that refills saucers and watering cans.

Two portals, two locations, the same curve — that makes the Thursday-to-Friday plunge solid.

Where the Portals Disagree For Sunday, 08/23, Climatempo says 21.0 mm of rain at 40% probability — theweather.com reports 0% and "scattered clouds" for the same day. That's not a rounding difference, that's a real contradiction. Anyone planning something outdoors for Sunday (the EFAPI finale) should check again Saturday evening. A third figure is also circulating in the search index: one summary named a high of 24°C for tomorrow. Both portals we queried directly today say 31°C — we're treating the 24°C as a stale cache hit and not using it.

For the next three days, "extreme UV index" with an infant in the house means: shade isn't a nicety, it's the safety measure. From Friday on, the problem flips — then it's about jackets and closed windows at night.

EFAPI 08/19–08/23: The Weather Splits Fair Week in Two [6]

According to Tribuna do Vale, the 54th EFAPI Expo — Exposição-Feira Agropecuária e Industrial de Santo Antônio da Platina — runs "entre os dias 19 e 23 de agosto" at the Parque de Exposições. The event's official Facebook page advertises the same window ("acontece de 19 a 23 de ago"), so the dates now stand on two independent sources. During the day: exposição agropecuária, rodeio, feira comercial, praça de alimentação. One show every evening — Eduardo Costa, Zé Neto & Cristiano, Alok, Pedro Paulo & Alex, Felipe & Falcão.

The weather arc hands the fair week a very concrete recommendation: Wednesday and Thursday are the last dry, 32°C days. Anyone who wants to experience the Expo in summer weather would have to go on a weekday evening — a trade-off with school kids and the next morning to think about. Saturday afternoon brings 24°C instead of 32, but with it less dust, less heat, and a more manageable crowd in the early afternoon.

Two Open Questions First: pricing. The February source pointed to "os próximos dias" — six months later, admission and box-suite prices are still not published. A search-index summary claims free entry to the grounds; that couldn't be confirmed at any primary source and isn't treated as fact here. Second: the state page. agricultura.pr.gov.br still shows the EFAPI with the dates of last year's edition ("19/08/2025 … até 24/08/2025"). Anyone who checks there is planning for 2025.

One kids' program item is also circulating: the Palhaço Furunfo is said to perform in the rodeo arena. So far that's only in an NPDiário headline we couldn't independently open — treat it as a rumor, not a reason to go.

For: The region's biggest event, in the day-trip ring instead of 110 km away. The livestock show and food court work during the day with all three older kids, with no ticket tied to a specific time. Saturday is cool enough that heat and dust aren't an issue for the first time in weeks.
Against: Prices unknown — you'd go without knowing what the evening costs. No source documents a changing area, a nursing space, or parking. A 30–40% chance of afternoon showers. And with the baby, going during the current RSV wave isn't an option.

Londrina: A Sunday Concert, and an Exhibit About Arriving [7]

After the packed literary week, Sesc Cadeião quiets down, but doesn't go empty. The strongest family pick is Sunday, 08/23, 5 PM: the concert "Yakã: Narrativas Instrumentais" by the Tramandaí Trio, listed as "Gratuito." An hour of instrumental music in the late afternoon is easy to manage with school kids, and it's indoors — on a Sunday for which one portal forecasts 21 mm of rain, not a minor detail.

Also ongoing and free is the Contação de Histórias na Biblioteca through 08/30 — story-reading hours at the library, the format that's worked most reliably for us so far.

And through October 25, the exhibit "Entre o Sol Nascente e a Terra Vermelha: histórias da presença japonesa em Londrina" is on display there. For a German family in northern Paraná, that's more than just an art break: the story of another immigrant group that arrived in that same red earth — and a concrete hook for the question our school kids ask anyway: who was here before us? Free, and easy to combine with the Sunday concert.

There's finally something concrete happening in the core ring, too. Jacarezinho is opening enrollment for its city arts program, PROFAC: over five hundred free spots across twelve course formats, sign-up through the twenty-sixth of August. At the Parque Universitário, ceramics run Mondays and capoeira Tuesdays, both from age eight; painting and drawing on Tuesdays from age six; ballet on Wednesdays for girls seven to eleven; and contemporary dance for boys the same age. That lines up strikingly well with our two school-age kids. You can sign up at the Biblioteca Cidadã, at the CRAS offices, or at the Parque Universitário itself, with a copy of your ID and proof of address.

Starting Wednesday, the fifty-fourth EFAPI Expo takes over the fairgrounds in Santo Antônio da Platina for five days. Livestock show, rodeo, trade fair, a food court, and a concert every evening. Two independent sources confirm the dates, the nineteenth through the twenty-third of August. Ticket prices still haven't been published, six months after the announcement, and the official state page still shows last year's dates. Our suggestion for the weekend: go Saturday afternoon, with a jacket, because it'll only be twenty-four degrees by then, with a thirty to forty percent chance of showers. And go without the baby — a packed fairground in cold air, in the middle of an RSV wave, is the wrong combination.

Saturday is also the national vaccination day, the Dia D of Multivacinação, for everyone under fifteen. On dengue, Paraná stays structurally calm, but the latest solid number still comes from epidemiological weeks one through fourteen: two thousand twenty-eight cases and one death, a drop of ninety-six point one percent from last year. The August bulletin exists, but its figures still aren't accessible.

Two loose ends before we close. According to one festival portal, the FEJACAN music festival in Jacarezinho falls on November fifth through seventh — not in August. But that's not confirmed: the official Sesc page only lists the twentieth edition and the sign-up deadline, no actual date, and the city hasn't published anything either. So nothing to plan around yet. And for Sunday, the weather portals disagree sharply: one calls for twenty-one millimeters of rain, the other zero percent. Anyone planning something outdoors for Sunday should check again Saturday evening. In Londrina, a free concert runs Sunday at five at Sesc, along with an exhibit on Japanese immigration to northern Paraná running through October.
