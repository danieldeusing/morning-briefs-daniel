The Alert Is Real — It Just Applies Elsewhere [1][2]

Since 9:47 a.m. yesterday, an INMET warning has been in effect for Paraná, valid until today, 11:59 p.m.: 20 to 30 mm of rain per hour, or up to 50 mm for the day, gusts of 40 to 60 km/h, hail possible. This is no small thing, and the alert is running widely across state media. But it covers 188 municipalities in the west, southwest, and central-south of the state — Paraná is bigger by area than half of Germany, and those regions are several hours' drive from here. Jacarezinho, Ribeirão Claro, Londrina, and Cornélio Procópio are not on the list.

“Perigo Potencial”: INMET's wording doesn't say “it's coming,” it says “it could come.” Think of the cloud on the horizon that makes you bring the laundry in just in case — not rain already drumming on the roof. What matters is always whether your own town is on the municipality list.

Our numbers for today: Climatempo lists 17–31°C for Ribeirão Claro, “Sol com muitas nuvens durante o dia e períodos de céu nublado” (sun with lots of clouds, occasionally overcast), 0.0 mm rain, wind from the southeast at 9 km/h. SIMEPAR's numbers for Jacarezinho, 50 km further, confirm it independently: currently 28°C at 56% humidity and 0% chance of rain, “Tempo aberto, com poucas nuvens” (clear skies, few clouds). Both portals agree — this brief's publication rule is satisfied. Starting tomorrow, it tips cleanly into cooler weather: Fri 16–24°C, Sat and Sun 12–25°C each, zero rain everywhere.

What this means in practice A thirteen-degree daily swing this weekend. 12°C in the morning, 25°C in the afternoon — for the two-year-old that means a jacket and a T-shirt in the same bag. Compared with yesterday's forecast, today's high dropped a degree (31 instead of 32°C) and the weekend gained a degree; that's normal model drift, not a new weather pattern.

Health: The Alert Stays, the Direction Is Right [6][7][8]

SARI (SRAG in Portuguese): Brazil's catch-all category for severe acute respiratory illness — anything that puts someone in the hospital for breathing trouble, regardless of the pathogen. Like a smoke-alarm statistic: it doesn't say what's burning, but it's very precise about how often. RSV: a cold virus that lets adults off with a runny nose but swells shut the hair-thin airways in infants — which is why it gets its own age-specific tracking.

Fiocruz's national InfoGripe bulletin for epi week 31/2026 (Aug 2–8) counts 133,709 SARI cases in the current epi-year. Among lab-confirmed cases, RSV leads at 42.2%, followed by rhinovirus at 30%, influenza A at 18.6%, influenza B at 5.2%, and SARS-CoV-2 at 4%. 23 of 27 states show a falling or stable trend — but 14 states, Paraná among them, remain in the alert, risk, or high-risk band for incidence.

Paraná's own numbers tell the same two-sided story, just more concretely. For epi week 29/2026 (week ending Jul 27), the state reported weekly cases down 15.5%; year-over-year, 15,999 SARI hospital admissions in 2026 compare with 18,944 in the same period of 2025. Among pregnant women, the flu-vaccination rate stands at 76.77% (75,482 doses) — that number counts double for us, since maternal immunity carries over into a baby's first months. In short: the curve is falling, the alert still applies, and the age group it's about is ours.

Dengue: once again, no updated figures. The state's official list shows Informe Epidemiológico nº 25 from Aug 18, 2026 as its latest entry — its case numbers were once again unavailable today. The only solid data remains epi weeks 1–14/2026: 2,028 confirmed cases against 52,193 in the same window of 2025 (−96.1%), 1 death instead of 90. These numbers are four months old and describe the summer season, not August. We carry them as a structural trend and don't extrapolate.

Sunday in Londrina: Two Kids' Events at the Same Hour [4][5]

At Catuaí Shopping, every Saturday and Sunday from 2 to 5 p.m. in August, there's a Mundo Bita Meet & Greet at the mall's Parque Mundo Bita, near the Kalunga store.

Mundo Bita: needs an introduction for American ears, but not for any Brazilian toddler. Bita is a cartoon character with a hat and beard whose songs rack up billions of YouTube views — Brazil's answer to what Sesame Street is here. A “Meet & Greet” means: the character shows up in costume, and you can go, take photos, and shake hands briefly.

In parallel, downtown's Sesc Cadeião — checked directly on the program page today — has Sunday double-booked: 3:00 p.m., “Experimentação sensorial para crianças” (sensory play for kids), listed by Sesc itself as Infantil (kids') and Gratuito (free), and 5:00 p.m., the concert “Yakã: Narrativas Instrumentais” by Tramandaí Trio, also free. Sensory play is the rare format with no age floor: no reading, no sitting still, no rules to learn — it works for a two-year-old just as much as for a school-age kid, just on different levels. To be honest: Sesc doesn't publish any age ratings; this assessment is based on the format, not on an organizer's listing.

Both are in Londrina, but not at the same place, and the time windows overlap. Anyone who wants both starts with Bita at 2 p.m. and moves on to the 5 p.m. concert. If you have to choose: Bita for the two-year-old, Sesc for the two older kids.

Among the lab-confirmed cases, RSV leads at forty-two point two percent — and that's exactly the virus that swells shut an infant's tiny airways. Fourteen states remain in the alert zone, Paraná among them.

Paraná's own numbers, though, are trending the right way. In epidemiological week twenty-nine, weekly cases dropped fifteen point five percent, and year over year, hospital admissions sit at nearly sixteen thousand versus almost nineteen thousand over the same stretch last year. The flu-vaccination rate among pregnant women is nearly seventy-seven percent, which matters for the baby too, since maternal protection carries over into the first few months. On dengue, though, we're still in the dark: bulletin number twenty-five, dated August eighteenth, exists, but its numbers still aren't available.

Last, the calendar. It's a normal school week, with no day off in August. The next entry on the state calendar, September twelfth, is a Saturday and just a planning day for teachers, so nothing changes for the kids. Saturday is also national vaccination day for the Multivacinação campaign, covering everyone under fifteen. And the next real long weekend is September seventh, a Monday.
