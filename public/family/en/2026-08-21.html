Three Dry Days — and a 15-Degree Swing on Sunday [1]

The warning that was yesterday's headline is history: the INMET advisory for the western half of Paraná expired yesterday at 11:59 p.m., and no new warning can be found for today — not from Defesa Civil, not in state media. Our corner was never on it anyway.

Instead we get what a dry southern winter delivers at its best. Today 14–22°C with a few clouds and clear night skies, Saturday 12–25°C under cloudless skies, Sunday 11–26°C. Climatempo reports 0.0 mm and 0% chance of rain on all three days, and the second portal confirms the 0% day by day.

But the number that matters day to day isn't the high, it's the swing. On Sunday, a full fifteen degrees separate the 11°C at wake-up — with light early fog — from the 26°C in the afternoon. Anyone who heads out in a fleece in the morning is just hauling it around by the afternoon. And clear skies mean one thing above all this time of year: the day's warmth radiates away unchecked overnight. For a four-week-old who can't yet regulate body temperature well, these are the coolest nights since birth.

Portals disagree — starting Tuesday For Tuesday, Aug 25, the two sources diverge: Climatempo puts the chance of rain at 42% with 0.3 mm and "pancadas de chuva à tarde," while theweather.com says 0% at 15–23°C. Friday through Sunday, both agree; Tuesday, they don't — and this brief's rule applies: state both figures rather than quietly picking one. Anyone planning something outdoors for Tuesday should check again Monday.

Health: Londrina's Dengue Curve Drops Sharply [5][6][7][8]

After weeks in which the dengue situation in this brief only showed up as "numbers unavailable," there's finally something solid again — though it comes not from the state, but from Londrina. The city's bulletin from Jul 30, 2026, compares the year to date with the same period last year: 10,481 suspected cases versus 20,531 (down 49%), 1,309 lab-confirmed cases versus 4,731 (down 72%) — and 2 deaths instead of 9.

Why dengue is falling right now: The virus doesn't travel on its own — it travels inside the Aedes aegypti mosquito, and that mosquito needs standing water to breed. Think of it as a chain: no rain → no puddles, saucers, open rain barrels → fewer larvae → fewer mosquitoes → fewer infections. That's exactly why the dry southern winter we're in right now is the low point of the year — and exactly why a good July number doesn't tell us anything yet about the coming December.
Dry season→fewer breeding sites→fewer Aedes→fewer cases

For the state, the gap remains: the Informe Epidemiológico nº 25 from Aug 18, 2026, is listed on the official page, but its case numbers are once again unavailable. The last verified statewide figure therefore remains epi weeks 1–14/2026 — four months old and from the summer season, carried here explicitly as a trend only, not as the current situation.

On the second health topic, nothing has improved. Per InfoGripe's epi week 31/2026 data, Paraná remains among the 14 states whose SARI incidence sits in the alert, risk, or high-risk band; a newer bulletin (epi week 32) hadn't been published as of this morning. Among lab-confirmed cases, RSV leads at 42.2% — well ahead of rhinovirus (30%) and influenza A (18.6%).

RSV (VSR in Portuguese): a cold virus that gives adults a runny nose but swells shut infants' hair-thin airways. Think of it this way: in an adult, the bronchial tube is a garden hose; in a four-week-old, it's a drinking straw — a millimeter of swelling is nothing for the hose and almost everything for the straw. That's why the same infection means a weekend on the couch for the older kids and a hospital matter for the youngest.

Still, the direction is right: Paraná's own curve fell 15.5% in epi week 29/2026 versus the week before, and year over year, 15,999 SARI hospital admissions compare with 18,944 in the same period of 2025. Nationally, per Fiocruz, the decline among children under four is mainly driven by falling RSV hospitalizations.

In practical terms, for the baby Alert level plus RSV as the leading pathogen doesn't mean "stay home" — it means avoid crowds in enclosed spaces. An open-air fairground is far lower-risk than a packed mall on a Sunday afternoon — but neither is a must for a four-week-old. And since nights drop to 11–12°C: one extra layer, but no hat in bed.
Tomorrow: Multivacinação Day D Saturday, Aug 22, 2026, is the national Multivacinação action day — a vaccination-card check for everyone under 15, meaning all four kids. The campaign runs through September 1, but health posts have extended hours on Day D. Ten minutes before heading out to EFAPI, and it's taken care of until next year.
