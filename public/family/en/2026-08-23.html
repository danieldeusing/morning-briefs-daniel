Paraná Is Out of the Respiratory Alert — With an Asterisk

Two numbers this brief has been chasing for weeks finally landed this morning — and both are good news. In the new InfoGripe bulletin for epidemiological week 32/2026, Paraná is no longer on the list of states under respiratory alert; the count dropped from 14 to 11, and we're not one of them. And the Dengue Informe No. 25, whose case figures had been unreachable two days running, opened today: the 19th Health Region of Jacarezinho, which covers Ribeirão Claro, has 102 confirmed cases and zero deaths over 32 weeks — an eighth of the incidence next door in Cornélio Procópio. The asterisk on both: RSV stays high in Paraná despite the falling curve, and the dry season currently starving the mosquitoes of water ends Tuesday. In practical terms, today means: the last full sun day, the EFAPI finale 51 km away — and from here on, plan around September 5–7.

The Last Full Sun Day — the Streak Ends Tuesday [1][13]

Climatempo describes today's Sunday as "Dia de sol com algumas nuvens e névoa ao amanhecer" — a sun day with a few clouds and fog at dawn. 11 to 25°C, chance of rain 0%. The second portal, theweather.com, sees the same dry day and also 0% rain, but runs about 3°C lower on the high. Dry is a lock either way; whether it lands at 22 or 25 degrees only decides between a T-shirt and a light sweater.

The real story is Tuesday. Aug 25: 14–20°C, 39% chance of rain, "Sol com muitas nuvens passando a chuvoso à tarde. À noite a chuva para". Yesterday the same forecast still stood at 45% — so the signal has softened somewhat, but not vanished. Monday stays dry, though already noticeably cloudy. Anyone with outdoor plans this week has today and tomorrow for it.

Some context, with a caveat: through August 14, only 7 mm of rain had fallen in Jacarezinho for the entire month — around 16% of the monthly normal. This figure comes from a climatempo search summary, not the directly opened page. After a stretch like that, Tuesday's rain is less an inconvenience than something overdue — and the reason to go through the yard's standing water spots once more today (more on that below).

One more note on the long-range column: the 15-day tail shows 20°C for Wednesday and 35°C for Friday — a fifteen-degree jump in two days. That's a trend signal, not a planning number. For the Aug 29/30 weekend, that locks in "warm" and nothing else.

School: September 7 Is Now Backed by a Primary Source [10]

The long weekend this brief has been flagging for days as the target for a bigger trip is now in black and white in the state calendar — no longer from summaries, but from the SEED appendix to Resolução n.º 6.494/2025 itself: "2º T. – 18/05 a 04/09 – 68 d.l." and "3º T. – 08/09 a 18/12 – 70 d.l.", 201 school days total.

In practical terms: the second trimester ends Friday, Sep 4. The third begins Tuesday, Sep 8. In between sit Saturday, Sunday, and the holiday on Monday, Sep 7 (Independence Day). That's four days off in a row — enough for a trip to São Paulo with two overnight stays, without anyone missing school.

Two more points from the same document: the Prova Paraná 2026 runs "nos meses de maio e setembro" — so right at the start of the third trimester, a statewide assessment is due. And Teacher's Day is "antecipado para o dia 13 de outubro", moved up to fall right next to the Nossa Senhora Aparecida holiday on October 12. That makes for another extended weekend in October — but the exact recess days only appear in the calendar PDF's color-coded fields, which couldn't be extracted as text. Double-check before booking.

Next Weekend: the Achiropita Finale Turns 100 [7][8][9]

The São Paulo ring has a date on August 29/30 that won't repeat. The 100th Festa de Nossa Senhora Achiropita in Bixiga — an Italian street fair around Rua Treze de Maio, Rua São Vicente, and Rua Doutor Luís Barreto — runs "em todos os fins de semana de agosto" (every weekend of August), and the closing weekend of the anniversary edition is the last one. Saturdays 5–11pm, Sundays 5–10pm, "entrada totalmente franca": free admission, you only pay for what you eat.

Fitting right in, the SP Gastronomia at Ilha Musical in Parque Villa-Lobos places its second window in this exact weekend (Aug 28–30, Saturdays noon–11pm, Sundays noon–10pm; tickets from R$ 77 full / R$ 38.50 discounted in the first batch). And starting Thursday, the Festival Interlagos — Edição Automóveis (Aug 27–30) runs at the Autódromo Interlagos, where kids up to age 10 get special terms. A fourth format, the 37th Kinoforum short film festival (Aug 20–30, several cultural centers), carries animation programs — which sessions are kid-appropriate couldn't be checked at the session level.

Worth it if it turns into two overnight stays — four to six hours of driving each way isn't a day trip with an infant in the car seat. And this is exactly where it meets the school calendar above: anyone who wants to see the Achiropita in its 100th edition has to take next weekend; anyone who'd rather have four days in a row takes September 5–7 and skips the Achiropita. Both are reasonable, just not both at once.

Health: Paraná Drops Out of the Alert — and What the Asterisk Means [2]

Relevant for the baby Fiocruz/InfoGripe, epidemiological week 32/2026 (data Aug 9–15, 2026, SIVEP as of Aug 15, 2026): "11 UFs ainda estão com incidência de SRAG em nível de alerta, risco ou alto risco nas últimas duas semanas: AC, AM, BA, GO, MA, MT, MG, RS, RJ, RR e SE" — Paraná is no longer on it (in week 31 there were 14 states including PR). But: "os casos de SRAG por VSR continuam altos em Minas Gerais, Paraná, Rio de Janeiro …" — despite the falling curve, the level stays high.

The status change is real, and it's good news. But it says something narrower than it sounds: the "alert / risk / high-risk" classification compares the estimated weekly incidence of severe respiratory cases against historical thresholds — it's a statement about the burden on the system, not the risk to one specific infant in an enclosed room. That's exactly why the same bulletin still lists RSV in Paraná as high. The virus mix of the last four weeks nationwide: 44.6% RSV, 32.5% rhinovirus, 9.7% influenza B, 5.1% influenza A, 1.8% SARS-CoV-2 — the influenza A season has ended nationally. For 2026, 137,551 severe respiratory cases have been reported so far, of which 74,214 (54%) are lab-confirmed.

Metapneumovirus (hMPV): RSV's lesser-known cousin — same basic design, same target group, just without a name everyone recognizes. In adults it's a cold; in very young children it can cause the same narrowed airways. The bulletin explicitly notes a "manutenção do aumento das hospitalizações por metapneumovírus em toda a região Sul (PR, SC e RS)" — meaning hospitalizations keep climbing even as RSV falls.
Alert status gone→RSV stays high→hMPV climbing→behavior unchanged

In practice, that means the same thing as last week, just in better spirits: avoid crowded enclosed spaces, wash hands before anyone handles the baby, and anyone with a cold keeps their distance — including the older siblings. An open fairground at 25°C is far gentler than a shopping mall on a Sunday afternoon. Warning signs that shouldn't wait until Monday: noticeably rapid breathing, chest retractions between the ribs or below the breastbone, and a clear inability to feed.

Dengue: the Missing Bulletin Is Here — and We Live in the Calmest Spot [3]

For two issues running, this brief noted that the Informe Epidemiológico No. 25 existed but its figures weren't accessible. Today the PDF opened. As of 2026-08-18, cumulative for weeks 1–32/2026 (SINAN base exported 2026-08-11), statewide for Paraná:

60,551 notifications, of which 5,878 confirmed cases (dengue, dengue with warning signs, and severe dengue combined) and 5 deaths. In the same window in 2025 it was 89,635 cases and 140 deaths — a decline of 93.4% and 96.4% respectively. Against the 2024 outbreak year (617,571 cases, 730 deaths), it's down 99%. Serotypes circulating statewide: DENV-1, -2, and -3.

More interesting than the statewide number is the line that concerns us. The 19th Health Region — Jacarezinho (300,313 residents, which includes Ribeirão Claro) stands at 1,296 notifications, 102 confirmed cases, of which 84 were locally acquired, and zero deaths. Only DENV-2 has been typed.

Incidence per 100,000 & "autochthonous": Comparing raw case counts between regions of different sizes is like ranking churches by attendance without asking how big the town is. So the figures get converted to a rate per 100,000 residents. "Autochthonous" means: the mosquito that bit was local — as opposed to "imported", where someone brought the infection in from somewhere else. Only the autochthonous incidence says anything about how many infected mosquitoes are actually flying around one's own doorstep.

And by that measure, home is the calmest region for miles around: 27.97 autochthonous cases per 100,000 in the 19th Health Region Jacarezinho — versus 232.51 in the 18th Health Region Cornélio Procópio right next door (the day-trip ring!) and 127.63 in the 17th Health Region Londrina. Cornélio Procópio, with around 215,000 residents, has a good two-thirds of our population — and five times as many confirmed cases plus one death. That doesn't rule out a trip there, but it's the reason repellent matters more on a day trip west than in our own yard.

Trap defused The figure "6,655 confirmed cases" for the 19th region circulates in search results. That's from the 2025 season, not 2026. The correct value for weeks 1–32/2026 is above: 102. This same class of error — right region, wrong year — has brushed against this brief three times already this week.

The reason for the good numbers, though, isn't anything anyone did — it's the weather: dry season. No rain, no puddles, no larvae. That's why a good August figure says nothing about the coming December — and why today, the last dry Sunday before Tuesday's rain, is the cheapest moment to go through the yard once more: saucers, buckets, gutters, tarps.

It's Sunday, August twenty-third. Two numbers this brief has been chasing for weeks finally landed this morning — and both are good news. Paraná is no longer on the alert list for severe respiratory illness, and the dengue bulletin, whose case figures had been unreachable two days running, finally opened today.

Starting with respiratory illness. Fiocruz reports for epidemiological week thirty-two that eleven states still carry alert, risk, or high-risk incidence. The week before, it was fourteen, and Paraná was one of them. Not anymore. That's a real status change, and it's a good one.

But it says something narrower than it sounds. The classification compares the estimated weekly incidence against historical thresholds — it's a statement about how strained the health system is, not about the risk to one specific baby in a closed room. And the same bulletin says RSV cases in Paraná are still high, even with the curve falling. On top of that there's a virus that gets mentioned less often: metapneumovirus, RSV's lesser-known cousin, same basic design, same target group. Hospitalizations from it keep climbing across the whole South, Paraná included. So for a four-week-old, that means the same behavior as last week, just with better spirits. Avoid crowded indoor spaces, wash hands before anyone handles the baby.

Now the dengue bulletin. Informe number twenty-five, from August eighteenth, covers weeks one through thirty-two. Statewide for Paraná: five thousand eight hundred seventy-eight confirmed cases and five deaths. In the same window last year it was eighty-nine thousand six hundred thirty-five cases and one hundred forty deaths. That's ninety-three point four percent fewer cases and ninety-six point four percent fewer deaths.

More interesting than the statewide number, though, is the line that actually concerns us. The nineteenth health region, Jacarezinho, which covers Ribeirão Claro, has one hundred two confirmed cases and zero deaths. Converted to a rate per hundred thousand residents, that's twenty-eight. The neighboring region, Cornélio Procópio — our day-trip radius — sits at two hundred thirty-three, more than eight times higher. Around two hundred fifteen thousand people live there, roughly two-thirds our population, and they've had five times as many confirmed cases and one death. Londrina comes in at one hundred twenty-eight. So we live in the calmest spot around. That doesn't rule out a trip west, it's just the reason mosquito repellent matters more on a drive like that than in our own yard.

One more trap worth flagging, since it's still circulating in search results: the number six thousand six hundred fifty-five for the nineteenth region is from the twenty twenty-five season, not this year. The correct figure is one hundred two.

On the weather: eleven to twenty-five degrees today, zero percent rain, fog early on, then sun with a few clouds. A second portal also shows zero percent rain, though it runs about three degrees cooler at the high end. So dry is a sure thing. Tomorrow stays dry but turns noticeably cloudier. And Tuesday it flips: fourteen to twenty degrees and a thirty-nine percent chance of rain in the afternoon. That's the end of a dry stretch in which only seven millimeters fell through the middle of the month, around sixteen percent of normal. Which is exactly why today is the cheapest moment to go through the yard once more: saucers, buckets, gutters, tarps.

For today itself, there's one clear pick: the EFAPI finale in Santo Antônio da Platina. Fifty-one kilometers, about fifty driving minutes, last day of the fifty-fourth edition. Livestock show, trade fair, food stalls, amusement park, and Felipe and Falcão play tonight. Anyone who still wants to go this year has exactly this one day. One thing remains unconfirmed: that grounds admission is free is documented for the fifty-second and fifty-third editions, not for this one — so bring a little cash just in case. There's construction on the way in with alternating traffic, so build in extra time.

The alternative would be Londrina, which has three free events today: a sensory workshop for kids at Sesc Cadeião at three in the afternoon, a concert there at five, and the strawberry festival running all day at Norte Shopping. The catch is the usual one: the drive is one hundred eighty-five kilometers, two hours forty minutes each way. For two hours of programming, that's a five-hour driving day with an infant in the car seat. So the recommendation today is Platina.

Two dates to close on, and they're in competition. Next weekend, August twenty-ninth and thirtieth, São Paulo wraps up the hundredth edition of the Achiropita festival in Bixiga — an Italian street fair, free admission, Saturdays five to eleven at night, Sundays five to ten. Running alongside it: the second window of SP Gastronomia in Parque Villa-Lobos and, from Thursday, the car festival at Interlagos. And then there's September fifth through seventh. In between sit the weekend and Independence Day — four days off in a row. Anyone who wants to see the Achiropita in its hundredth year has to go next weekend; anyone who wants four days in a row takes September and skips the festival. Both can't happen at once.
