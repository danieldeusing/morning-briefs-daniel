Four Days Climbing — and a Sunday the Portals Can't Agree On [1][2][3]

Climatempo describes today's Thursday as "Muitas nuvens o dia todo, com aberturas de sol" — lots of clouds all day, with sunny breaks in between. 16 to 28°C, chance of rain 0%, precipitation 0.0 mm. The cross-check through a genuinely different provider comes together halfway this time, which is more than in recent issues. theweather.com shows Ribeirão Claro today at around 27/17°C, tomorrow 30/19°C, and Saturday 32/21°C — one to two degrees off climatempo, so a match. Weekend portal conflict From Saturday on, the two diverge, and noticeably so. climatempo: Sat 0%, Sun 0%, 0.0 mm each. theweather.com: Sat 40% "light rain", Sun 70% "thunderstorm". On Sunday it drags the temperature along too — theweather shows only around 31°C there instead of 35°C, which would fit a stormy afternoon. Neither figure is the obviously better one: climatempo is the more regionally precise service, but it had this same Sunday at 40% and 2.1 mm as recently as Aug 25 and has since turned it to zero — so the forecast is itself still moving. Consequence for planning: the heat is the reliable quantity, a dry Sunday isn't. Anyone who needs something weatherproof for Sunday afternoon should plan indoors.

On top of that comes an official warning that needs to be read correctly: the registry of active advisories from the national weather service INMET lists a thunderstorm warning of the "Perigo Potencial" level today for the South region — including Paraná — with "Chuva entre 20 e 30 mm/h ou até 50 mm/dia, ventos intensos (40-60 km/h), e queda de granizo", valid from midnight to 11:59pm. A look at the coverage list defuses this for us: Norte Pioneiro, Jacarezinho, Ribeirão Claro, Ourinhos, and Londrina aren't on it. The warning applies to other parts of the state.

"Perigo Potencial": INMET has three warning levels — Yellow ("Perigo Potencial"), Orange ("Perigo"), and Red ("Grande Perigo"). Yellow is the lowest: no damage expected, but the ingredients for one are present. Like a cloud on the horizon that doesn't make you cancel the picnic yet, but does make you pack a jacket. What matters is always the warning's list of municipalities, not the state name above it.

Monday is the only day climatempo additionally flags with low humidity. At 35°C and dry air, that hits the youngest first: dress them lighter or give them more to drink, stay in at midday, and for the infant at night, a damp cloth in the room is better than a fan on full blast.

Health: The Status Stays the Same, Because There's No New One [6][7]

Relevant for the baby The InfoGripe summary for week 33/2026 (data Aug 16–22) isn't accessible this morning — HTTP 404 on the same Fiocruz path where week 32 sits; week 34 as well. What still governs is Fiocruz/InfoGripe, Epidemiological Week 32/2026 (data 2026-08-09 to 2026-08-15): Paraná is not on the list of states with alert-, risk-, or high-risk incidence — but "os casos de SRAG por VSR continuam altos em … Paraná …", and hospitalizations for metapneumovirus keep climbing across the entire South.

A warning about a trap that's easy to fall into this week: search results still prominently surface a Fiocruz article on week 30/2026 (Jul 26–Aug 1) that lists Paraná as one of 14 states "em nível de alerta, risco ou alto risco". That's not the current status, but one that's two weeks older — and it says the opposite of week 32. Citing the wrong week flips the story. Week 32 applies here, until week 33 actually appears.

Why that doesn't change the household rule despite the bulletin's all-clear: the classification describes the strain on the health system — not the risk to one specific five-week-old in a crowded room. And the virus that same bulletin explicitly calls "still high in Paraná" is RSV.

RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus): The cold virus that gives adults a runny nose and narrows an infant's smallest airways. Like a straw you're sipping a milkshake through: no problem for a wide straw, but a narrow one clogs completely. That's why the same virus hits an adult and a baby in completely different ways.

Unchanged: avoid crowded enclosed spaces, wash hands before anyone handles the baby, keep siblings with colds at a distance. Don't wait until tomorrow for noticeably fast breathing, chest retractions between the ribs, or a clear inability to feed.

Dengue stands today exactly where it stood Tuesday — and that's been checked, not assumed: the official SESA/PR bulletin list still shows Informe Epidemiológico No. 25 from 2026-08-18 as the latest, and there's no No. 26. That keeps statewide figures for Paraná (weeks 01–32/2026) unchanged at 60,551 notifications, 5,878 confirmed cases, 5 deaths — and for our own region, 19th Health Region Jacarezinho: 102 confirmed cases, zero deaths, incidence 27.97 per 100,000. Still, after last Wednesday's rain and ahead of the next rain front on Tuesday, the yard sweep against standing water remains due.

All free, all under a roof, which is exactly the right call at thirty-five degrees with a contested rain forecast hanging over the afternoon. The catch is the drive: a hundred eighty-five kilometers, two hours and forty minutes each way.

The core ring, a gap in this brief for weeks, finally delivers something solid. The municipal cultural workshops in Jacarezinho are offering free ballet classes on Wednesdays for kids ages seven to eleven, at two locations, one starting at eight thirty in the morning and the other at one thirty in the afternoon. That's exactly the age range for the eight-year-old and the seven-year-old in this household. One caveat: an older report gave August twenty-sixth as the enrollment deadline, but a newer story from the same date drops the deadline entirely and just points to an open sign-up form. Better to call than to guess — the number's in the brief.

For anyone planning the long trip to São Paulo anyway, this weekend rarely lines up this well: Cirque du Soleil's "Alegría" at Parque Villa-Lobos, the twentieth Japanese Culture Festival in Liberdade running free both days from ten in the morning to seven at night, and a free kids' workshop at Japan House on Sunday for ages four to twelve. Honest take: four to six hours each way with four kids, including a five-week-old infant, in thirty-three to thirty-five degree heat — that's not an outing, that's a move. Still, it's a pattern worth filing away, because the same combination pays off far better on the long weekend at the start of September.

On health, there's nothing new because there's nothing new to report. The InfoGripe bulletin for epidemiological week thirty-three isn't accessible this morning, and week thirty-four isn't either. What still governs is week thirty-two: Paraná isn't on the alert list, but that same bulletin says RSV cases remain explicitly high there. One trap to watch for — search results keep surfacing an older article about week thirty, which lists Paraná among fourteen alert-level states. Citing the wrong week flips the story entirely.

RSV is the cold virus that gives an adult a runny nose and closes up a baby's smallest airways. Think of it like sipping a milkshake through a straw: no trouble with a wide one, but a narrow one clogs right up. For the five-week-old daughter, that still means: avoid crowded closed rooms, wash hands before anyone picks her up, keep siblings with colds away. On dengue, nothing has moved: the state's bulletin list still shows Informe number twenty-five from August eighteenth as the latest, and the local health region around Jacarezinho remains at a hundred two confirmed cases and zero deaths.

Two gaps are worth naming. The day-trip ring — Ourinhos, Cornélio Procópio, Bandeirantes, Platina, Ibaiti — turned up not a single dated family event this weekend; the usual listing sites are either empty shells or blocked outright. And a widely repeated claim about a huge year-over-year drop in dengue deaths couldn't be confirmed, because the state health department's own page returns an access error.
