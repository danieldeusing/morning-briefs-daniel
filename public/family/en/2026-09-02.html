Yesterday, this spot carried the sharpest source conflict in weeks: climatempo had Sunday at 0% and 0.0 mm, theweather.com at 80% and 19.8 mm. Those weren't two estimates of the same day, they were two different days. Today the dispute is gone — climatempo has flipped.

The most interesting figure isn't in the table, it's in the sum. Yesterday, the two portals expected a combined 14.9 mm (climatempo) against 25.1 mm (theweather.com) over the weekend — a gap of roughly 40%. Today it's 22.6 mm against 23.9 mm, a good 5%. So the models are no longer arguing over how much water falls this weekend, only over which of the two days it falls on. For planning purposes, that's the decisive shift: this weekend there's no longer a dry fallback day to pick instead.

House rule When sources disagree, both numbers get printed. On Saturday, the rain probability remains contested — 69% against 90% — and so is the amount (6.9 against 9.8 mm). This brief doesn't quietly settle that. In practice, it makes little difference: both figures mean "bring the umbrella." The conflict is noted, not dramatized.

What this means for the three days. Friday, at 28°C, becomes the warmest day of the week and therefore the last afternoon for the outdoors before the front moves through — anyone still planning a bike ride, the reservoir, or the playground this week has tomorrow and the day after for it. Saturday and Sunday belong indoors. And Monday is dry but cold: 13–17°C per climatempo, with a low of 10.6°C per theweather.com. For a morning at the roadside, that's not a small thing — it's jacket, hat, and thermos weather, not sun-hat weather.

Dengue: the Bulletin Is Here — a Sixth Death, and a Map [3]

For two weeks, this spot has said the figures were getting stale. As of yesterday, there are new ones. The Informe Epidemiológico No. 26 from Sesa Paraná covers Epidemiological Weeks 01 through 34/2026 and was published on 2026-09-01 (SINAN database exported Aug 25). Statewide: 5,962 confirmed cases, of which 4,960 are autochthonous — meaning contracted locally, not brought in — and 6 deaths. Compared with the previous bulletin, that's 84 more cases and one more death.

The year-over-year comparison remains the real story here, and it's exceptionally good: in the same period in 2025, Paraná counted 89,829 confirmed cases and 141 deaths. That's −93.4% on cases and −95.7% on deaths. Against the disaster year of 2024 (618,299 cases), it's −99.0%.

Our own region sits below the state average and still has zero deaths — 108 confirmed cases among 300,313 residents. Six more cases than the bulletin two weeks ago. That's a calm number, and it can be read as one.

Heads up The neighboring county is the outlier of the entire state. The 18th Health Region Cornélio Procópio has an autochthonous incidence of 232.98 per 100,000 — higher than any of the other 22 health regions and nearly eight times our own 29.64. That's not a distant statistic: Cornélio Procópio and its surroundings sit within day-trip range. Anyone spending an afternoon there should bring repellent for the kids and check legs in the evening — and at home, the usual routine still applies after the rainy weekend: saucers, buckets, gutters, tarps. Standing water from Saturday and Sunday is exactly what turns into mosquitoes in two weeks.
Serotype: The dengue virus comes in four variants, DENV-1 through DENV-4. Anyone who's had one is immune to that exact variant for life — but not to the other three, and a second infection with a different variant tends to run more severe than the first. Think of it like four different keys for the same lock: the one the body knows, it can fend off. The other three still fit. That's why the bulletin lists, per region, which types have been detected — in the 19th HR Jacarezinho it's DENV-2, and statewide DENV-1, -2, and -3 are circulating. That's exactly why a calm season is no all-clear for the next one.

Two Parades on Monday — and One of Them Is Finally Close to Home [4][5][6]

Londrina remains the big event, and it's well documented. The Desfile cívico-militar-cultural runs on Monday, Sep 7, on Avenida Leste-Oeste downtown: troop review 8:30am, parade start 9:00am. Around 4,900 participants in 57 comitivas are registered, plus 90 vehicles — patrol cars, motorcycles, buses, vans —, 15 animals and 13 bands and fanfares. Streets close all around. A second report from Sep 1 cites "more than five thousand registered" and over 50 entities; the order of magnitude is thus confirmed twice, though the exact figure is still moving. For an eight-year-old and a seven-year-old, that's a very good morning: horses, flashing lights, drums. Loud for a toddler, too loud for an infant.

New is the small-scale version. Until yesterday, Londrina was the only confirmed option — 185 km, a good two and a half hours. Today, alongside it, is Santo Antônio da Platina: lineup order drawn on Aug 31, formation as always in the upper half of Av. Coronel Oliveira Motta, opened by Tiro de Guerra 05-004 and the marching band of Colégio Estadual Tiradentes. After that, the APAE school, senior center, Projeto Boia Fria, and several neighborhood and rural schools. No start time for 2026 has been published — that's the gap, and it isn't filled here with last year's figure.

For Ribeirão Claro and Jacarezinho themselves, no program for Sep 7 has been published as of today. The Prefeitura Ribeirão Claro's news site loaded cleanly today; the latest entry remains the 2ª Level.Up from Aug 25, 2026, with nothing about September on it. That's a confirmed finding for this one source, not proof that nothing is happening — Prefeituras in the Norte Pioneiro tend, by experience, to post this kind of thing only a few days ahead.

Roadside vocabulary: desfile = parade. comitiva = the self-contained group marching together — a school, a club, a unit. fanfarra = the marching band, the brass and drum corps. revista às tropas = the troop review before the start, when the honored guests walk the assembled ranks. Tiro de Guerra = the local military training unit; in many small towns it traditionally opens the parade. Knowing these five words means understanding the announcements.

Respiratory Illness: Week 34 Still Returns 404 — What Week 33 Says About the Baby [7]

The InfoGripe summary for Epidemiological Week 34/2026 was queried again directly today and still returns HTTP 404 — it hasn't been published, and nothing gets extrapolated here. Week 33/2026 (data Aug 16–22, 2026) remains the valid status.

The sentence that matters for the infant sits in the age-group analysis: the decline in severe respiratory cases "nas crianças menores de dois anos" (in children under two) is attributed to falling RSV hospitalizations. So for the under-two group, the curve is heading down — and specifically because of the pathogen that's most dangerous for babies. At the same time, Paraná is among the eight states whose SRAG incidence still sits at alert, risk, or high-risk level, though explicitly "sem sinal de crescimento" — without a growth signal. On RSV itself, the report says for Paraná: falling, but "ainda um pouco elevados para essa época do ano" (still a bit elevated for this time of year).

The mix has shifted. Over the last four surveillance weeks, positive samples nationwide broke down as 38.7% RSV, 38.3% rhinovirus, 8.2% influenza B, 4.4% influenza A, and 1.6% SARS-CoV-2. The influenza A season has ended nationally. Rhinovirus has caught up and is now practically tied — and Paraná is among the states with a continuing rise in rhinovirus admissions, "porém sem impacto importante nas hospitalizações por SRAG na maioria desses estados" (though without a major impact on SRAG hospitalizations in most of these states).

Rhinovirus: the pathogen behind the ordinary common cold — runny nose, sore throat, a rough week. In school-age kids, that's usually all it is. It still shows up in this statistic because a small share of cases end up in the hospital, mostly in very young children and those with asthma. In short: if someone at home is sniffling this winter, statistically it's most likely rhinovirus — and that's the more harmless of the two figures above.

In practice, the unspectacular rule still applies, and it happens to fit the rainy weekend well: wash hands before anyone touches the baby. Avoid packed, closed rooms with the infant — which is the real constraint on Saturday and Sunday, since that's exactly where everything crowds when it rains.

It's Wednesday, September second. Outside, today is the calmest day of the week: fourteen to twenty-three degrees, fog early in the morning, no rain. That's exactly why it's worth talking about the weekend today — because something shifted there overnight.

Yesterday, this brief carried the sharpest weather disagreement in weeks. For Sunday, one portal called for zero percent rain, the other for eighty. Today that dispute is over, and not in our favor. Climatempo has flipped and now puts September sixth at sixty-one percent and fifteen point seven millimeters. Theweather dot com stands at ninety percent and fourteen point one millimeters. So both portals are now saying the same thing: Sunday is the wettest day of the week.

The most interesting number isn't in the day-by-day figures, though — it's in the total. Yesterday the two models expected a combined fifteen millimeters against twenty-five for the weekend, a gap of about forty percent. Today it's twenty-two point six against twenty-three point nine. So they're no longer arguing about how much rain falls, only about which of the two days it falls on. In practice, that means there's no dry fallback day left to pick this weekend.

Monday, on the other hand, holds. It's the third day running that climatempo calls zero percent for the holiday, and the second day the second portal backs that up independently. It'll be the coldest day, though: thirteen to seventeen degrees, with one source putting the low at just under eleven. Anyone with outdoor plans for the holiday should pack a jacket and a hat, not a sun hat.

And there are now two reasons that's worth doing. Londrina was already on the radar: the big parade starts Monday with the troop review at eight-thirty, stepping off at nine, on Avenida Leste-Oeste. About five thousand people, fifty-seven contingents, ninety vehicles, fifteen animals, and thirteen bands. For the eight-year-old and the seven-year-old, that's a very good morning. For the baby, it's simply too loud.

New is the small-scale version. In Santo Antônio da Platina, in the Norte Pioneiro and much closer than Londrina, there's a parade of its own on the same day. The lineup order was drawn on Monday, forming up in the upper half of Avenida Coronel Oliveira Motta, opened by the local Tiro de Guerra unit and the marching band of Colégio Estadual Tiradentes. A start time for this year hasn't been published, though — and last year's figure isn't being used to fill that gap. Anyone heading there should check locally on Sunday evening, and build in extra time, since that exact route runs through seven detour points from the highway-widening project.

On health, there's the news this brief has waited two weeks for. The state's dengue bulletin came out yesterday, number twenty-six, with data through the thirty-fourth week of the year. Paraná counts five thousand nine hundred sixty-two confirmed cases and six deaths — one more than last time. In the same period last year, it was nearly ninety thousand cases and a hundred forty-one deaths. The drop is over ninety-three percent.

For our own area, the picture is calm. The Jacarezinho health region, which covers Ribeirão Claro, reports a hundred eight confirmed cases, zero deaths, and an incidence below the state average. The neighboring county, though, is the outlier of the entire state: Cornélio Procópio stands at two hundred thirty-three cases per hundred thousand residents, the highest figure in Paraná and nearly eight times our own. That's day-trip distance. Anyone heading there should bring mosquito repellent for the kids. And at home, after two rainy days, the usual routine applies: saucers, buckets, gutters — whatever water sits out this weekend becomes mosquitoes in two weeks.
