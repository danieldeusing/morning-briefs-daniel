And the numbers are moving in one direction: the water this weekend brings has risen across three straight retrievals, from 14.9 mm to 22.6 mm to now 30.5 mm. What's new today is which day gets it — yesterday it was Sunday, today it's Saturday, with 21.9 mm the wettest day of the week. Saturday Takes the Rain Back — and the Total Keeps Growing [1][2]

Since Monday, climatempo has kept shifting the focus of this weekend back and forth: first everything sat on Saturday, yesterday on Sunday, today back on Saturday. Anyone looking only at the single day would call that chaos. Looking at the total shows something else — and that's the real story today.

The amount has doubled in three days. The distribution keeps shifting, but the direction is the same in every run: more water, not less. And for the first time, both days sit above 75% rain probability — until now, one of the two was always the candidate for a dry window. That candidate is gone. On top of that comes the temperature drop: from 28°C on Friday down to 23°C on Saturday and 17°C on Sunday; yesterday, Sunday still stood at 20°C. The long weekend starts in spring and ends in winter.

Source conflict On Saturday, climatempo says 78%, theweather.com 90% with thunderstorms. On Sunday it's 75% against 90%. Both figures are printed here side by side — this brief doesn't quietly settle that. In practice, though, the difference doesn't change anything — 78 and 90 both mean "pack the rain gear." What theweather.com doesn't supply today is a rainfall amount, so the millimeter column above rests on climatempo alone. On temperatures, theweather.com runs two degrees above climatempo on Saturday at 25.0°C (23°C), and 2.4°C below it on Sunday's low (10.6°C instead of 13°C).
78% or 21.9 mm — which one plans the afternoon? The two numbers answer different questions. The percentage says how likely it is to rain at all: 78% means that on roughly eight out of ten comparable days, water fell. The millimeters say how much then falls — 1 mm is one liter per square meter, so spread across the whole yard, a thin puddle. 21.9 mm in a day isn't severe weather, but it is a day when the laundry doesn't dry and the walk to school arrives wet. Rule of thumb for us: the percentage decides whether you head out. The millimeters decide what you wear. A day with 90% and 2 mm is a drizzly afternoon; a day with 60% and 20 mm is more likely to stay dry, but if it does start, you get soaked.

This isn't severe weather. 30 mm over two days is a normal cold front for the Norte Pioneiro in September, not an event for warning levels. That can't be cleanly confirmed today, though: the INMET warning map was unreachable at both addresses tried in this run (connection dropped / HTTP 404), and the CEMADEN risk forecast for Sep 3 couldn't be resolved down to text for the Norte Pioneiro. So this isn't a verified statement that "there's no warning" — it's that a warning could today neither be confirmed nor ruled out. Anyone driving the BR-153 on Saturday should check for themselves beforehand.

Statewide, 2026 remains an exceptionally calm year: 5,962 confirmed cases and 6 deaths against 89,829 cases and 141 deaths in the same period of 2025 — −93.4% and −95.7% respectively. Our own region sits well below the state average of 41.71 at 29.64, and still hasn't had a single death. That's a calm number, and it can be read as one.

Heads up Cornélio Procópio is Paraná's outlier — and it sits in the day-trip ring. With an autochthonous incidence of 232.98 per 100,000, the 18th Health Region ranks higher than any of the 22 health regions and nearly eight times our own. For a day trip there over the long weekend, that means nothing dramatic, but something concrete: repellent for the kids (for the infant, a mosquito net over the stroller and baby carrier instead — repellent doesn't belong on baby skin), long sleeves in the afternoon, and check the older kids' legs in the evening. And at home, Wednesday is the appointment: whatever's left standing in saucers, buckets, gutters, and tarps from this weekend's 30 mm of rain becomes mosquitoes in two weeks. The yard check on Wednesday after the front is the single most effective move this week.
Incidence per 100,000: Comparing the raw case counts of two counties is misleading, because different numbers of people live there. Incidence recalculates both onto the same hypothetical city of 100,000 residents. This is exactly where it matters: Cornélio Procópio reports 514 cases, Jacarezinho 108 — raw, that's nearly five times as many. But only 215,473 people live in the 18th Health Region, versus 300,313 in the 19th. Adjusted to the same population, that fivefold difference becomes nearly eightfold. It's like comparing two classrooms: six sick kids in a class of twelve is something quite different from six sick kids in a class of thirty — same number, twice as bad.

Monday Now Carries the Whole Weekend — Two Parades, One Open Start Time [4][5][6][13]

That it's three days off at all is a calendar stroke of luck: September 7, Brazil's Independence Day, falls on a Monday in 2026 and automatically extends the weekend from Saturday through Monday. Except that now exactly one of those days can happen outdoors — and for that one, there are two confirmed destinations.

Londrina is the big event. The Desfile cívico-militar-cultural runs Monday on Avenida Leste-Oeste downtown: 8:30am troop review, 9:00am parade start. Around 4,900 participants in 57 comitivas are registered, plus 90 vehicles, 15 animals, and 13 bands; a second report cites "more than five thousand" and over 50 entities, so the order of magnitude is confirmed twice over. Semana da Pátria has been running since Aug 31 and involves 185 educational institutions of the city — celebrating 204 years of independence. For an eight-year-old and a seven-year-old, that's a very good morning: horses, flashing lights, drums. Loud for a toddler, too loud for an infant. The price for it is 185 km and a good two and a half hours — each way.

The small-scale version is closer. In Santo Antônio da Platina, the lineup order was drawn on Aug 31; formation happens in the upper half of Av. For Ribeirão Claro and Jacarezinho themselves, still no program has been published for Sep 7. German-Brazilian: the Vocabulary for the Rainy Weekend

September 7 here is what October 3 is in Germany — the national holiday, only celebrated a lot louder and with school parades instead of a street fair. More useful for this week than holiday vocabulary, though, are the weather words, because those are exactly what shows up on the climatempo page when you check it yourself:

Weather in Portuguese: chuva forte = heavy rain (that's what's listed for Saturday). pancadas de chuva = showers, meaning short and intense, not continuous. garoa = drizzle — that's the day you go out anyway. nublado = overcast, sol com muitas nuvens = sun with heavy clouds. névoa ao amanhecer = fog at sunrise, exactly what's listed for early today. frente fria = cold front — the word for what happens starting Saturday. And previsão is simply the forecast. Anyone who knows these seven words no longer needs a translation to read the weather page directly — and notices right away when one portal says something different from the other.

It's Thursday, September third. Today brings thirteen to twenty-five degrees, no rain, and fog early on — and starting Saturday, three days off are locked in, because September seventh falls on a Monday this year. The only problem: the weather isn't cooperating.

The number that matters most today is a running total. Across three straight forecasts, the rain expected this weekend has climbed steadily — first about fifteen millimeters, then twenty-two point six, and now thirty point five. In three days, the total has doubled.

What keeps shifting is which day gets hit. Two days ago it was all on Saturday, yesterday it moved to Sunday, and today it's back on Saturday — now twenty-one point nine millimeters and seventy-eight percent.
