What's not settled is which single day: the model has now answered which of the two rain days is worse four different ways. In practice it no longer matters — 88 and 91% mean the same thing.

"Chove rápido" vs. "chuvoso durante o dia": These two phrases on the climatempo page are the real difference between today and tomorrow, and the percentages alone don't reveal it. Chove rápido means "it rains briefly" — bursts that pass through, with open sky in between. That's a day when a quick trip to the bakery works if you time the gap. Chuvoso durante o dia means "rainy through the day" — no gap to time. That's exactly why tomorrow carries 40% more water than today despite an almost identical percentage. Rule of thumb: the percentage tells you whether. The Portuguese phrase next to it tells you for how long at a stretch. For us, that means: today has gaps, tomorrow doesn't.

Londrina Matsuri: the first real family event in weeks — and it's running right now [7][8][9]

After several editions with an empty event radar, today has a big one. The Londrina Matsuri is in its 22nd edition and runs Friday through Monday at Parque Ney Braga — exactly across the three-day weekend. Folha de Londrina calls it "o festival que anuncia a primavera," the festival that announces spring; in the southern hemisphere, that begins Sep 22/23.

The hard facts: admission R$16.00, half-price R$8.00 ("meia entrada"), parking R$30.00 through an outside operator. For Saturday, Sunday, and Monday, the city's announcement gives 10am to 11pm. On Monday at 5:30pm, Japanese singer Nanase Aikawa performs — the festival's closing act. Two independent sources, both dated Sep 1, match exactly on date and price.

Meia entrada: Literally "half admission" — the discounted price certain groups pay in Brazil, similar to a reduced ticket at a German museum or pool. At the Matsuri that's R$8 instead of R$16. Only, neither source says who qualifies — specifically, not whether or at what age kids start paying. For a family with four kids, that's not a detail, it's the difference between R$48 and R$64 in admission. The math below assumes the less favorable case.

What this costs us: two adults at full price are R$32. If all four kids pay half-price, that adds R$32 — bringing it to R$64 admission plus R$30 parking, so R$94. If the two youngest are free, it's R$78. Add gas for 370 km round trip. This is calculated, not quoted.

German-Brazilian angle: a Japanese festival as the start of spring

A folk festival with drums, dumplings, and a singer from Tokyo, in the middle of Paraná, in its 22nd edition — surprising to German eyes, ordinary for northern Paraná. September 7 here is what October 3 is in Germany, just with school parades instead of a town fair. The Matsuri alongside it is more like a fairground — only here it's run by a Japanese immigrant community instead of the neighboring village.

Six words for the weekend: feriadão = the "big holiday weekend," when a holiday falls on a Monday or Friday — exactly what's happening now. desfile = parade. meia entrada = discounted admission. inteira = full price. estacionamento = parking, here terceirizado, meaning run by an outside company. And primavera = spring, the reason the Matsuri falls in early September. Anyone who knows these six words can read the announcement themselves — and will immediately see that the 2025 version of the same page lists different prices.

It's Saturday, September fifth, and the long weekend starts today. Three days off, because September seventh falls on a Monday this year. Only it rains on two of them.

Today runs eighteen to twenty-two degrees Celsius, with an eighty-eight percent chance of rain and just over eleven millimeters. The forecast text puts it as short bursts, not steady rain — meaning showers that pass through, with gaps in between. So today still has room to get things done if you time it right.

Tomorrow doesn't. Sunday is now the wettest day of the entire week: ninety-one percent and almost sixteen millimeters, with the forecast calling for rain through the whole day. Back on September third, that same Sunday was still showing eight point six millimeters. So the rain hasn't lightened up — it's just moved.

Monday, though, holds. Zero millimeters, twelve to seventeen degrees. That's now every single forecast pull since September first, and today, for the first time, the second weather source explicitly agrees too. A day to get outside — but a cool one, jacket and hat, not sun hats.

And for the first time in weeks, something is actually happening on this one dry day. The Londrina Matsuri, now in its twenty-second edition, runs Friday through Monday at Parque Ney Braga, all three days from ten in the morning to eleven at night.
