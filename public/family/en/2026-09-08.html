At home, the situation is as calm as it can possibly be in a dengue country: zero deaths in our own region, and an incidence sitting at one-eighth of neighboring day-trip Cornélio Procópio. That's exactly why the next paragraph appears here as plain text and not as a warning box.

Why 139mm of rain only becomes a mosquito problem three weeks from now: The Aedes mosquito doesn't lay its eggs in water — it lays them on the dry inner wall of a container, just above the waterline: a saucer, a bucket, a gutter, an old tire. There, the eggs can survive for months. Only once water floods over them do the larvae hatch, and from there it takes, depending on temperature, roughly seven to ten days to become a flying mosquito. That's why Thursday's rain won't bring mosquitoes on Friday — it brings them at the end of September. And that's why doing the round today matters far more than the same round next week: today you empty out and flip over empty containers; next week you'd be emptying out full containers with larvae in them. The effort is the same, the effect isn't.

Today's list, in the order it's worth doing: flowerpot saucers (the single most common breeding site), flip over buckets and basins, pull tarps taut or put them away, stand old tires upright or cover them, clear out gutters, cover water barrels. After that, it can rain for five days straight without anything sitting stagnant.

The other half: the bulletin also names the "manutenção do sinal de aumento dos casos de SRAG nas crianças e adolescentes de 2 a 14 anos, associado principalmente ao rinovírus" — a continuing rise among 2- to 14-year-olds, driven mainly by rhinovirus. That's exactly the age range of our two older kids.

Rhinovirus — the most harmless bug in the house, except for one child: Rhinovirus is the ordinary common cold. For an eight-year-old and a seven-year-old, that means a runny nose, a cough, maybe a day on the couch — nothing more, which is why it appears in no warning bulletin. The difference for a five-week-old infant isn't the strength of the virus, it's the pipe diameter: a newborn's smallest airways are about as thin as a pencil lead. The same amount of mucus that gives a school kid a stuffy nose can noticeably narrow her airways — and babies breathe almost exclusively through the nose in their first weeks. So the relevant job in this house isn't preventing the cold, it's breaking its path: from the school kid to the baby, and that path runs through hands and kisses, not through room air.
Heads up Five rainy days are five indoor days — the best conditions of the month for cold viruses: closed classrooms, closed living rooms, constant contact. Three rules cost nothing and change a lot: wash hands before anyone touches the baby — especially after school; no kisses on the face as long as anyone has a runny nose; and air out rooms even when it's raining — ten minutes of cross-breeze per room is enough. And the threshold for calling a doctor with a five-week-old is lower than for the older kids: fast or labored breathing, rib retractions, poor feeding, or fever should be checked right away, not waited out.

And because today is the last dry day, it's a work day in the yard. Flowerpot saucers, buckets, tarps, old tires, gutters. The reason is the life cycle of the Aedes mosquito: it doesn't lay eggs in water, but on the dry inner wall of a container, just above the waterline. The eggs can sit there for months. On dengue itself, the news is genuinely good, and it's worth just saying so. Through week thirty-four, Paraná counts sixty-two thousand two hundred fifty-two reported cases, five thousand nine hundred sixty-two confirmed, and six deaths. Over the same stretch last year, it was two hundred sixty-two thousand reports and one hundred forty-one deaths — a seventy-six percent drop in reports and a ninety-six percent drop in deaths. Tomorrow evening at seven-thirty, Concerto em Ri Maior plays at the Teatro Sesi in Santo Antônio da Platina, sixty kilometers away — a music comedy by Cia dos Palhaços that leans on sight and sound rather than dialogue, which is exactly why it works even with limited Portuguese. Indoors, seated, no crowding: about the friendliest format there is for an infant. Honestly, though, seven-thirty on a school night means getting home around ten-thirty, and the price isn't confirmed by any primary source.

Anyone who'd rather skip the school night can look ahead to September nineteenth instead: Festival Imagine in Londrina, a Saturday afternoon from two to six in the parking lot of Londrina Norte Shopping — the most family-friendly time slot in the whole month's program. For this coming weekend itself, nothing is published in the core ring, and with eighty-three percent rain on Saturday, it would have been an indoor weekend regardless.

Tomorrow's brief will check two things: whether Friday's thirty-five millimeters holds or shifts again — it more than doubled overnight this time — and whether tickets need to be reserved for the Sesi evening.
