Fifth Rain Day, Yellow Level — and the Amount Keeps Rising [1][2][3][4][5][6][7]

The severe weather system over Paraná runs out its fifth day today, and does so at its weakest level: after Red on Friday (396 municipalities, "grande perigo," over 60mm/h and gusts up to 100km/h) and Orange on Sunday, today brings a yellow warning through 11:59pm — up to 50mm a day, wind up to 60km/h, with the possibility of falling branches, flooded roads, lightning, and power outages. The regional list names the Norte Pioneiro explicitly — that's us. Yesterday's rain-accumulation window for 212 municipalities expired at midnight.

Despite the lower warning level, today's rain forecast has risen. Yesterday's brief listed 16.3mm for Monday — the same climatempo page today shows 24.6mm at 95% and still holds to the line "Sol com muitas nuvens a nublado com chuva de manhã. Tarde e noite com temporal." Update since Sun That's the second upward revision in a row. The neighboring town of Jacarezinho sits on the same page at 24.4mm / 96% and 14–16°C, plus wind from the east-southeast at 12km/h with gusts up to 32km/h — so the situation isn't local, it covers the whole corridor.

Cross-Check Two portals, two readings — this brief's two-source rule is satisfied, but not everywhere. On high temperature (16°C vs. 16°C) and rain probability (95 vs. 90%), climatempo and tempo.com agree for today — which clears the forecast for the brief. Not on the amount: 24.6mm vs. 15mm, a difference of nearly ten liters per square meter. And even less on the character of the day: climatempo writes "temporal," tempo.com writes "chuva moderada com céu parcialmente nublado" and lists no warning for the location. SIMEPAR, the state weather service and, per this brief's source ladder, the first-choice address, keeps answering with HTTP 403; INMET's live map is pure JavaScript scaffolding with nothing to extract. So the tiebreaker is missing again. Practically: plan for a rain day with thunderstorm risk, but no panic level — if you don't have to drive, don't.
What "24.6mm" actually means — and why two portals are allowed to differ this much: One millimeter of rain is one liter of water per square meter. So 24.6mm is roughly 25 liters per square meter — on a yard ten meters by ten meters, that's two and a half thousand liters in one day. Like a bucket emptied over every square meter. Probability (95%) and amount (24.6mm) are two completely different questions: the first asks "does it rain at all?", the second asks "how much comes down?" Whether it rains is easy to predict with a passing front — both portals say yes. How much falls depends on exactly where a single thunderstorm cell stalls, and that's barely calculable to the kilometer. So a gap of 15 vs. 24.6mm isn't a contradiction between the models, it's the normal margin of uncertainty. For the day's planning, the probability matters; for the state of the yard the next morning, the amount does.

Health: Nothing New — and That's Exactly Why Wednesday Counts [8]

On dengue, there's no new update today. Sesa Paraná's most recent bulletin remains No. 26 from Sep 1, 2026, for weeks EW 01–34/2026: 5,962 confirmed cases and 6 deaths statewide, against 89,829 cases and 141 deaths in the same period of 2025 — a decline of 93.4% and 95.7% respectively. The next bulletin is expected later this month; until then, this brief won't extrapolate.

Heads up Wednesday is the day against the next mosquito generation, and this week the math is especially clear-cut. Aedes eggs stick dry to the inner walls of saucers, buckets, gutters, and old tires and survive there for months. They only hatch once water floods over them — after that it takes seven to ten days to a flying mosquito. Four rain days totaling roughly a hundred millimeters have just filled every container on the property at the same time: a synchronized start for the entire next generation. Today the round would be pointless — whatever you empty is full again within the hour. Wednesday lands cleanly inside the hatching window and is dry. That Paraná's dengue situation is historically good doesn't change any of this: it's good because exactly this is being done everywhere.
