The Rain Hasn't Let Up — It's Just Landing Differently [1][2][3]

The amount hasn't changed, the timing has. On Wednesday, the two portals read like this: Saturday was the thunderstorm day at 84 to 90%, Sunday similar, and an unresolved dispute over Saturday's rainfall amount — 1.9mm against 14mm, a factor of seven. Practically irrelevant this time, since both figures are small; as a pattern, it isn't: the portals reliably agree on whether and reliably disagree on how much.
Why a forecast can still swing this much two days out: A weather model doesn't know where each individual shower will fall — it knows a warm, moist air mass is moving in, and it calculates when it tips over. Picture a glass filled right to the brim, being slowly pushed across the edge of a table: that it overflows is certain — the only question is at which fraction of an inch. Two days out, the uncertainty in that calculation still runs to a few hours, and those hours are exactly what decides, for us, whether the rain falls Saturday afternoon or Saturday night. So nothing was wrong on Wednesday, and nothing is being corrected today — it's the same air mass, just the timing is now sharper. In practice: the percentage for a given day is reliable from about 48 hours out, the time of day only from about 24. Anyone wanting to make use of Saturday should check again tonight — not because the source is shaky, but because it will know more tomorrow.

Dengue: the Current Bulletin Is Open — and It's Good [7][8]

The movement over the last two weeks is tiny. From Informe No. 26 (EW 01–34) to No. 27 (EW 01–36), 98 confirmed cases were added, +1.64% — and not a single additional death. For our region, the finding is even calmer: the 19th Health Region, Jacarezinho, stands at 109 confirmed cases and 0 deaths across 300,313 residents, incidence of autochthonous cases 29.64. Just a few kilometers away it looks different: 18th Health Region Cornélio Procópio, 233.90, 17th Health Region Londrina, 129.16 — eight and four times our incidence, respectively. Serotypes circulating in Paraná in 2026: DENV-1, -2, and -3; in our region only DENV-2 has been typed.

Why the bulletin says "EW 01 through 36" instead of "this week": Brazil counts health data in Epidemiological Weeks — numbered weeks that always start on a Sunday, so every state compares the same days. But the number in the bulletin is not the week — it's the running total since January 1. Think of it like a car's odometer: it tells you how far you've driven this year, not how far today. So the interesting figure is the difference between two bulletins — and that's even stated: 98 cases in two weeks, statewide. One caveat the bulletin names itself: its data are "preliminares, sujeitos a alteração." Reports come in with a delay, so the last two to three weeks are still undercounted and get revised upward later. Read one week and you're reading an estimate; read the year-to-date total and you're reading a hard number.
Heads up The drainage window was handed one extra day — nothing more. Aedes eggs stick dry to the inner walls of saucers, buckets, gutters, and old tires and survive there for months. They only hatch once water floods over them; from there to a flying mosquito takes seven to ten days. On Wednesday, the deadline here was "before Saturday" — since both portals now put the rain on Saturday night, it now runs through Saturday afternoon. After that come Sunday, Tuesday, and Wednesday: three floodings in four days. Any container standing empty and flipped over Saturday evening starts back at zero afterward; any full one keeps collecting for six days — and lands, by the next bulletin (No. 28, due around Sep 29 on the two-week rhythm), exactly inside the hatching window. So: empty, flip over, tighten tarps, clear gutters — today or Saturday morning, and the yard can be handed to the school kids. On the road, the rule still stands: toward Cornélio Procópio or Londrina, repellent belongs in the bag, even if it can stay in the cupboard here.
