Ninety Percent — But How Much? [1][2][3][4][5]

Chance of rain vs. millimeters: these are two completely different numbers. The percentage says whether it rains where we are — 90% means: of ten such days, nine turn out wet. The millimeters say how much comes down: 1mm is one liter of water per square meter. It's like the question "is company coming?" versus "how many are coming?" — you can agree on one and completely disagree on the other. That's exactly what's happening today.

The warning first, because it changed. Yesterday there was still an orange INMET warning over the Norte Pioneiro. For today it's a yellow one — and our region is back on the list by name, alongside nine other regions of Paraná, including the Curitiba metro region and the Norte Central with Londrina.[1] INMET itself attributes the situation to an extratropical cyclone and a cold front favoring thunderstorms and hail over southern Brazil.[2] On the agency's scale, yellow means: danger possible, not likely. That's a real easing compared with yesterday — but not a free pass.

And then it gets messy. On chance of rain, the two portals agree as rarely as ever: 90% at climatempo, 90% at tempo.com. On the amount, they're off by a factor of four — 5.6mm versus 24mm.[3][4] Even the descriptions don't line up: climatempo writes "Sol com algumas nuvens e chuva passageira", meaning sun with a passing shower and a calm night; tempo.com simply headlines "Trovoada" — thunderstorm.

A third data point helps with context. For Jacarezinho, 50km away, climatempo reports 93% and 6.9mm — essentially the same low amount range.[5] But two readings from the same provider aren't two independent sources; they only show that climatempo is internally consistent. Anyone who read yesterday's brief also knows: for today, climatempo had 10.9mm and tempo.com had 6.5mm — overnight the two swapped places and quadrupled the gap. A 24-hour-ahead rainfall-amount forecast is apparently exactly as reliable as it looks today: not very.

Source conflict, disclosed The two-portal rule is satisfied today on the whether (90% to 90%) and clearly not on the how much (5.6mm to 24mm). That's why both values are listed side by side here instead of letting one quietly win. In practice, the asymmetric rule applies: plan for the wet case — a 24mm day, when you've braced for 6mm, ruins the afternoon; the other way around, you just carry one extra jacket.

One difference from yesterday matters more than the amount: today the shower falls during the day. Yesterday both portals had explicitly placed the wet weather at night, with the day as the safe zone. Today it's reversed — climatempo even expects the night to be "com tempo firme", i.e. calm. The outdoor window isn't cancelled, but it comes with a caveat: don't plan anything you can't get under a solid roof from within ten minutes.

Twelve Degrees in 48 Hours — the Real Event of the Week [3][4]

For all the disagreement about today: starting Tuesday, the portals agree again — on the number that matters for a four-week-old baby, temperature. Monday will still be hot: 33°C at climatempo, 30°C at tempo.com. Tuesday the cold front moves through, climatempo writes "Tarde com temporal e noite chuvosa" — severe weather in the afternoon, rain at night — at 80% and 14.9mm; tempo.com comes in at 90% and 18mm. And from Wednesday on, the high sits at 21°C (climatempo) or 22°C (tempo.com), nights at 14°C, on Thursday at 13–14°C.[3][4]

That's a twelve-degree difference between Monday afternoon and Wednesday evening, and for the first time this week the two sources don't contradict each other on this. When two independent models agree on the temperature trend and only diverge on rainfall amounts, the temperature part is the reliable signal.

Practically, by Tuesday evening Nursery nighttime temperatures drop from around 23°C to 14°C. For the infant, that means: one extra layer and a sleep sack instead of just a onesie — better laid out on Monday than hunted for at midnight on Tuesday. For the two-year-old and the two school-age kids: the long-sleeved clothes that have been at the bottom of the drawer for a month belong back on top starting Tuesday. And any laundry that still needs to dry this week is best hung out Monday — two wet days in a row follow after that.

The upside: behind the front lies the first reliably dry window in days. Thursday and Friday sit at 0% and 0.0mm at climatempo, and tempo.com reports no precipitation at all for either day, describing them as "Parcialmente nublado" and "Nuvens dispersas". Friday the high climbs back to 25–26°C. Anyone who wants an afternoon outside this week: Friday.

The BR-369 Is Under Construction Through November [6]

Affects every trip to Londrina The concessionaire EPR Litoral Pioneiro is resurfacing the pavement on five routes in its network: BR-277, PR-407, and PR-508 on the Paraná coast, PR-151 in the Campos Gerais — and, the only one relevant for us, the BR-369 in the Norte Pioneiro, in the section between Cornélio Procópio and Andirá. The work is expected to run "até o mês de novembro" — through November, ahead of the summer travel season.[6]

That's not a closure, but it's the reason a trip to Londrina currently takes longer than the map app says: single-lane sections, speed limits, occasionally traffic lights. With four kids in the car, the takeaway is plain and effective — budget an extra half hour and time the return trip to fall in daylight. Especially today, with an active yellow warning and a rainfall amount the portals can't agree on, this adds up: wet pavement plus construction lanes plus darkness is the combination to avoid, not just any one of them.
