Weekend pick

Primavera dos Museus: a free guided tour through three museums in Londrina — on the sunniest Saturday in weeks

Where: starts at Museu Histórico de Londrina, R. Benjamin Constant 900 · Ring 3, ~110 km / ~1h45 via BR-369 When: Sat, Sep 26, tours at 9 a.m. and 11 a.m. Ages: the school-age kids (8 & 7) are exactly the right fit; the two-year-old in the stroller; the baby in the carrier Price: free

What it is: the Primavera dos Museus is a nationwide museum week — seven days in which participating venues open up their programming and admission is free everywhere. Londrina is taking part this year with four venues; Saturday's walking tour links three of them on foot: Museu Histórico → Museu de Arte → Museu do Café (inside the Sesc Cadeião).[6]
Why this particular Saturday: 28°C, 0% rain, sun on both services[1][2] — the first Saturday in weeks where a 110-km drive doesn't have to be planned around a storm warning. And a walking tour between three venues is a mix that works with kids: look inside, walk outside, back inside.
The honest math: take the 11 a.m. tour, not the 9 a.m. one. For 9 a.m. you'd need to leave by 6:45; for 11 a.m., 8:45 is enough, and the morning with a four-week-old baby isn't already used up before you've even left. Plan for a full day: leave at 8:45, arrive around 10:30, two hours for the tour, lunch in town, back in the early afternoon — in daylight, which is the real argument on the BR-369 construction stretch.[11]
What the announcement leaves open — check beforehand: it names neither a detailed meeting point nor a participant cap, and guided tours are almost always limited. A call to the Museu Histórico before you drive out saves a letdown on-site. Also unconfirmed: diaper-changing facilities and parking at the three venues.
The backup plan is right next door: both exhibits run the whole week, including Sunday, Sep 27 — though Sunday hours are also unconfirmed. And for anyone who'd rather not drive at all: Thursday and Friday are dry here, and the next backyard afternoon costs nothing.

Ten degrees down, then four dry days [1][2][3][4][5]

Cold front: the boundary line where a cold air mass shoves a warm one aside. Cold air is heavier, so it creeps under the warm air like water and lifts it up — and warm air pushed upward cools and rains itself out. That's why a cold front almost never arrives without rain, and why the temperature drop afterward is immediate instead of gradual. That's exactly what happens here between Tuesday midday and Wednesday evening.

Today is still the warm day. Both services list exactly 30°C as the high, with lows of 20 (climatempo) or 18°C (tempo.com). For rain, they're at 48% versus 70% and 2.6 versus 4.4 mm.[1][2] After yesterday's four-times disagreement, that's practically consensus: same day, same moderate shower. climatempo explicitly places it in the second half of the day — "Sol e aumento de nuvens de manhã. Pancadas de chuva à tarde e à noite." So the morning is the safe window. In Jacarezinho, 50 km away, it looks the same, with 46% and gusts up to 47 km/h.[3]

And then it turns. Tuesday: 87% (climatempo) and 90% (tempo.com), 12.1 and 28 mm, high of just 23 or 22°C. Wednesday, both services list 20°C as the day's high, with nights at 13 to 15°C.[1][2] From 30 to 20 degrees in forty-eight hours — and this time the two sources don't disagree on temperature on a single day of the week. When two independent models line up to the degree on the temperature curve, that's the week's reliable signal.

Practical, through Tuesday evening Nursery-room nights drop from around 20°C to 13–15°C. For the one-month-old, that means one more layer and a sleep sack instead of just a onesie; for the two-year-old and the two school-age kids, the long-sleeved clothes go back on top. Lay both out Monday evening, not while hunting for them Tuesday at midnight. And any laundry that needs to dry this week should go up today — two wet days follow back-to-back after that.

The good news sits at the end of the week. climatempo reports 0% and 0.0 mm each for Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday; tempo.com has no precipitation figure at all left for those same four days and describes them as "Parcialmente nublado" and, three times, "Nuvens dispersas."[1][2] Temperatures climb back up meanwhile: 28°C Saturday, 29 or 30°C Sunday, with climatempo adding "Sol o dia todo sem nuvens no céu" for Sunday. That's the first weekend in weeks you don't have to plan around a warning.

A warning, for context: a regional outlet reported on Sep 19 that Paraná could see gusts up to 100 km/h and hail, with a new cold front "starting Monday."[4] That broadly matches what the forecast services show — but their focus clearly sits on Tuesday and Wednesday, not today. INMET itself has nothing newer as of this morning than its Sep 18 bulletin, which names a cyclone, cold front, thunderstorms, and hail for the South without listing Paraná individually.[5] So: no named warning for us today, but the tougher stretch comes tomorrow and the day after.

Four days off starting October 12 — the next planning window [7]

Paraná moved the teachers' recess, and in our favor. Dia do Professor officially falls on October 15. The 2026 school-year resolution moves the school-free recess up to Tuesday, Oct 13 — explicitly to attach it "emendando com o fim de semana e o feriado nacional da Padroeira do Brasil" to the national holiday.[7] Oct 12 — Nossa Senhora Aparecida, also Brazil's Children's Day — falls on a Monday this year.

Sat, Oct 10→Sun, Oct 11→Mon, Oct 12 — holiday→Tue, Oct 13 — no school

That adds up to four days off in a row — enough for more than one afternoon trip, and the only such stretch before the December summer break. The school year itself stays unchanged at 201 instructional days and ends on Dec 18, 2026.[7] Anyone in the São Paulo ring planning something worth a four-to-six-hour drive should book it now: October 12 is Children's Day nationwide, and that's exactly when the big parks and kids' programming are both the most crowded and the most expensive. Looking for lodging for a holiday weekend three weeks out is already late — next week it would be too late.

Health: both curves calm, one date remains [8][9][10]

Epidemiological week (SE): health authorities don't count in calendar months but in numbered weeks — SE 36/2026 is the thirty-sixth week of this year. Like mile markers on a highway: they don't say how fast you're going, but every number maps cleanly to a point in time. That's why every health figure here carries an SE label — without it, you don't know what period it covers.

Dengue: Paraná's most current bulletin remains No. 27 from Sep 15, 2026; the state's bulletin overview still lists no newer edition as of today.[9] It covers SE 01–36/2026 and reports 6,060 confirmed cases statewide, 6 deaths, and an incidence of 42.41 per 100,000 residents.[8] For comparison with last season: over the same period in 2025, it was more than 88,000 cases. This is the quietest dengue season in years.

Respiratory illness: the InfoGripe bulletin for SE 36/2026 (Sep 6–12, 2026) keeps Paraná at the lowest tier, "Segurança," and explicitly places the state among the regions with low RSV incidence.[10] With a one-month-old at home, that's the figure that matters — and it hasn't moved in a week. So today it gets just a paragraph, not a chapter.

The date: Thursday, after the rain Aedes aegypti needs roughly seven to ten days of standing water from egg to full mosquito. This week brings four wet days — Sunday, tonight, Tuesday, Wednesday — followed by four dry ones. That exact pattern fills every saucer and tarp in the yard and then leaves them undisturbed. The Thursday walk-through breaks the cycle before it starts. Saucers, buckets, tarps, gutters, tires, flip over toys.

For Saturday, that simply means half an hour of buffer and driving back in daylight — which fits naturally with the 11 a.m. tour anyway. Midweek is the more unpleasant version of the same stretch: wet pavement plus construction is the combination to avoid, not either one alone.

It's Monday, September twenty-first. After two weeks where the two weather services couldn't agree on almost any number, today they finally line up for the whole week — and the week has a clear shape: warm today, wet and cold Tuesday and Wednesday, then four dry days in a row.

And climatempo places it squarely in the second half of the day: sun in the morning with clouds building, showers in the afternoon and evening. So the morning is the safe window.

Then it turns. On Tuesday, rain odds hit eighty-seven and ninety percent, and the high drops to twenty-two to twenty-three degrees. On Wednesday, both services agree on a high of twenty degrees, with nights at thirteen to fifteen. That's ten degrees colder than today, in forty-eight hours. A cold front is the line where cold air pushes out warm air — the cold air creeps in underneath like water, lifting the warm air up, and warm air that gets lifted cools and rains itself out. That's why it turns sharply colder right after a front instead of gradually. In practical terms: one more layer for the four-week-old, the older kids' long clothes back on top — lay both out Monday evening instead of hunting for them at midnight on Tuesday. And any laundry that still needs to dry should go up today.

Then comes the actual good news. For Thursday, Friday, Saturday, and Sunday, climatempo reports zero percent and zero millimeters each day, and tempo dot com has no precipitation listed at all for those same four days. Temperatures climb back up: twenty-eight on Saturday, twenty-nine to thirty on Sunday, and climatempo writes for Sunday, literally, sun all day with no clouds in the sky. That's the first weekend in weeks you don't have to plan around a storm warning.

Fittingly, the Primavera dos Museus — the nationwide museum week — kicks off today in Londrina. My recommendation, with the honest math: take the eleven o'clock tour, not the nine o'clock one. For nine, you'd need to leave by a quarter to seven; for eleven, a quarter to nine is enough — and the morning won't already be shot before you even leave with a four-week-old in tow.
