Why the drive back still needs to be early this time is explained next door, under "Saturday in Londrina."

One virus goes down, another comes up [4][5][6][7]

Metapneumovirus (hMPV): A respiratory virus that behaves like RSV's little brother — a cold in adults, but in infants and toddlers it can move into the lower airways and become bronchiolitis, an inflammation of the smallest bronchi. Picture the lungs as a tree: in adults, a cold clogs the trunk, which is annoying; in a baby, the thinnest branches swell shut, and those are already straw-thin to begin with. There's no vaccine for it and no specific medication — what works is simple: keep the baby away from anyone coughing, and wash your hands.

The InfoGripe bulletin for SE 36/2026 (Sep 6–12) has two findings for Paraná that point in different directions. The good one: the RSV season here is over. The bulletin explicitly names Paraná among the states where "o período de sazonalidade do VSR já se encerrou, ou os casos graves estão em um nível baixo de incidência."[4] Paraná is also not among the six states with rising SARI incidence at alarm level, nor among the four at alarm level without growth.

The other finding sits two sentences later, and it's why this section leads today: "Também há um início ou manutenção do aumento das hospitalizações por metapneumovírus em toda a região Sul (PR, SC e RS)" — hospitalizations for metapneumovirus are rising across the entire South, Paraná included.[4] This isn't an alarm, it's a seasonal handoff: one pathogen recedes, the next moves in. But for a five-week-old, this particular pathogen is the one that matters.

Concretely, because of the five-week-old None of this means staying home. It means three things: (1) anyone with a cold — even just "a bit of a sniffle" — keeps their distance from the baby and doesn't pick her up; that goes for visitors just as much as for the three older siblings, who bring everything home from school and daycare. (2) Wash hands before every contact, and have the school-age kids do it right after coming home. (3) On Saturday in Londrina, carry the baby in the sling instead of the stroller — in the sling, she's at adult height and out of coughing range of strangers' faces leaning over a stroller. Warning signs that don't wait: rapid or labored breathing, rib retractions, refusing to feed.

The background numbers remain reassuring. From January through September 2026, Paraná recorded 19,779 SARI cases against 22,823 in the same period last year — a drop of 13.3%; for influenza, 2,961 versus 3,279 cases (−9.7%).[5] And on dengue, the state's most current bulletin remains No. 27 from Sep 15, 2026 — the bulletin overview still lists no newer edition as of today.[7] It covers SE 01–36/2026 and reports 6,060 confirmed cases statewide, 6 deaths, and an incidence of 42.41 per 100,000 residents.[6] The quietest dengue season in years — which doesn't replace Thursday's walk-through, since two wet days followed by five dry ones is exactly the pattern that fills every rain barrel and then leaves it undisturbed.

Saturday in Londrina: two events, one city [8][9][10][11][12]

On Saturday, two very different things collide in Londrina. One is the reason to drive there: the Primavera dos Museus guided tours at 9 and 11 a.m., free, from the Museu Histórico via the Museu de Arte to the Museu do Café.[8] The other is the reason to drive back early: the Tropicadelia festival at the Parque de Exposições Governador Ney Braga, which the organizer's own site advertises with 30,000 visitors.[10]

The lineup is funk, trap, and rock — an evening festival, not family programming; it's listed here purely as a logistics note. And as one, it's simple: a city with 30,000 extra people looks different from the early afternoon on, when it comes to access roads, parking, and restaurants. That drives the return plan: anyone who finishes the tour at 1 p.m., has a relaxed sit-down lunch in town, and doesn't leave until around 3 p.m. will be driving against incoming festival traffic. So: a quick bite instead of a restaurant, and on the road by 2 p.m. at the latest.

And the route itself The concession company EPR Litoral Pioneiro is repaving the BR-369 in the Norte Pioneiro, on the Cornélio Procópio–Andirá stretch, "até o mês de novembro."[11] The work schedule also includes widening on BR-153 between km 1 and 52, on the BR-369 between km 4 and 22, a lane closure at km 92.75–92.89 in Cornélio Procópio, and maintenance on PR-092 at km 202.[12] No closure, but single-lane sections and speed limits: plan for half an hour of buffer, in both directions.
