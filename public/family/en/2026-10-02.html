Dengue: the quietest season in years — and now the rain arrives [4][5]

Across epidemiological weeks SE 01 through 38 of 2026, Paraná counts a total of 6,120 confirmed dengue cases and six deaths. That's according to the bulletin from the state health department, Sesa, published Tuesday, Sep 29, as reported by hojepr.com.[4] For comparison, Sesa itself put the prior years alongside: more than 619,000 confirmed cases in 2024, more than 90,000 in 2025. Two weeks earlier, the bulletin for SE 36 showed 6,060 cases — sixty more in two weeks.[5]

Regionally, the Londrina health region leads with 1,631 confirmed cases, ahead of Maringá (653), Paranavaí (583), Cascavel (548), and Cornélio Procópio (523).[5] Statewide, 65,951 suspected cases were reported, 11,105 count as probable cases, and 1,908 are still under review. Serotypes DENV-1, DENV-2, and DENV-3 are circulating.

Why the rain matters (the Aedes cycle): The dengue mosquito Aedes aegypti lays its eggs just above the waterline on the inside wall of containers — plant saucers, buckets, tires, clogged gutters. The eggs can survive dry for months. When rain comes and the water rises, the larvae hatch, and after about a week the adult mosquito takes flight. Think of the eggs like seeds waiting for watering-can day: three rainy days after a dry spell are exactly that moment.
Concretely, after Monday No cause for worry, but a clear task: on the first dry day after the rain, make a pass through the yard and garden — empty plant saucers, flip buckets over, tighten tarps, check the gutters. Doing this within a week of the rain catches the larvae before they fly. The school-age kids enjoy turning it into a scavenger hunt. For the baby: a mosquito net over the crib or stroller; insect repellent for infants this age is meant to be used only after checking with a doctor.
About the sources, honestly The Sesa website itself wasn't reachable today (HTTP 403). The total of 6,120 and the six deaths come from a hojepr.com report on the bulletin; the regional figures and the year-over-year comparison come from a search-engine summary of the same Sesa notice. Both routes cite the same figures.

Respiratory illness: Fiocruz's InfoGripe bulletin (SE 38, Sep 20–26, published Oct 1, 2026) paints a mixed picture: severe acute respiratory illness (SRAG) cases have fallen over the past six weeks but risen again over the past three.[6] Nationwide, only metapneumovirus and Sars-CoV-2 show an upward trend — and for Paraná (along with Santa Catarina), the bulletin specifically reports more metapneumovirus-related SRAG cases, with the national rise concentrated in children under 4. The rules for the baby stay the same: anyone with a cold keeps their distance.
