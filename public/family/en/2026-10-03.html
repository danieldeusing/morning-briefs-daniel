Today and tomorrow: thunderstorms, then steady rain [1][2][3]

INMET warning for today, Sat Oct 3, 12 a.m.–midnight The national weather institute INMET has issued a "Tempestade" warning at the "Perigo Potencial" level (yellow) for today; Ribeirão Claro and Jacarezinho are both on the list of municipalities.[3] The stated risks: "Chuva entre 20 e 30 mm/h ou até 50 mm/dia, ventos intensos (40-60 km/h), e queda de granizo" — heavy rain, strong gusts, hail. INMET's own advice: don't shelter under trees during gusts, and don't park the car next to power poles. In practice: tie down or bring in the trampoline and patio chairs, schedule driving for the morning, and get all the kids inside once thunder starts.
Why "1.7 mm" and "up to 50 mm" aren't a contradiction: The weather services calculate an average for the whole town. But a thunderstorm is like a bucket tipped out over just a few streets: wherever it hits, an hour brings as much rain as an entire ordinary day. A few blocks away, it stays nearly dry. INMET's yellow level is the lowest of three (yellow, orange, red) — "pay attention," not "in danger."

Both services agree: it stays wet through Tuesday. climatempo puts Ribeirão Claro's chance of rain at 85% today, 96% on Sunday and Monday, 94% on Tuesday; SIMEPAR puts Jacarezinho at 98% on all four days.[1][2] Sunday will be the roughest day: climatempo writes "Tarde com temporal e noite chuvosa" — thunderstorms in the afternoon, rain overnight — with 26.6 mm, while SIMEPAR expects 19.7 mm. Monday and Tuesday each bring another 18 to 20 mm. Altogether, that adds up to roughly 60 to 66 mm through Tuesday.

After that, the weather turns. For Wednesday and Thursday, climatempo reports dry conditions and highs up to 27 and 30°C.[1] SIMEPAR doesn't reach that far out yet — so these two days are covered by only one service so far. A good window for anything left sitting outside.

An old alert making the rounds Anyone who sees a WhatsApp message today about an INMET "laranja" (orange) alert with "ventos de até 100 km/h" and "até as 23h59 deste sábado, 4": that report is from April 2026, not today.[3] Right now, only the yellow warning above applies.

The long weekend, Oct 10–12 — plan it now [6][7][8]

Children's Day falls on a Monday this year. Oct 12 is a nationwide holiday, officially for Nossa Senhora Aparecida; Dia das Crianças falls on the same date.[6] That gives the family three days off in a row, Saturday the 10th through Monday the 12th — right after the week's first dry, warm days.

For the state schools, the time off continues after that. According to a report on the 2026 school calendar, the education department Seed-PR moved the state schools' October recess up to Oct 13 via Resolução 6.494/2025, "aproveitando o fim de semana e o feriado nacional da Padroeira do Brasil".[7] Two things remain unclear. First, how many days the recess lasts — the source names only the start date; the page was blocked today and the resolution itself wasn't reachable. Second, whether it applies to the kids' own school: elementary schools and daycare are usually municipal or private and run their own calendar. So ask in the school group chat this week whether Tue Oct 13 is off. If it is, the long weekend becomes a four-day window.

Check curitiba.pr.gov.br directly before booking anything — this brief will check back in the coming days. Closer Children's Day programs in Londrina or Ourinhos haven't been published yet.

Dengue stays low. Across epidemiological weeks SE 01–38/2026, Paraná counts 6,120 confirmed cases and six deaths, per the state health department Sesa's bulletin dated 09/29/2026, as reported by hojepr.com.[9] No newer bulletin exists yet; Sesa's own site is currently blocked for the election period, so this figure rests on that one source for now. The risk isn't that number — it's the weather: four rainy days after a dry stretch fill every plant saucer, and after about a week, the larvae take flight as mosquitoes.

Date to keep: Wednesday, Oct 7 On the first dry day, make a pass through the yard and garden — empty plant saucers, flip over buckets and toys, tighten tarps, check the gutters. The school-age kids can turn it into a scavenger hunt. For the baby: a mosquito net over the crib and stroller; insect repellent for infants only after checking with a doctor.
