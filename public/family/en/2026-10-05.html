Thunderstorm tonight, the gap on Wednesday [1][2]

The portals agree on today. climatempo reports 98% rain and 16.1 mm for Ribeirão Claro, SIMEPAR also 98% and 23.6 mm for Jacarezinho.[1][2] The amounts differ, hence the range given here. climatempo describes the day this way: "Sol com muitas nuvens e pancadas de chuva à tarde. À noite ocorre temporal" — showers in the afternoon, a thunderstorm at night.

What's a "temporal"? That's what Brazilians call a severe storm: a lot of rain in a short time, plus wind and often lightning. Normal rain is like a watering can; a temporal is like a tipped-over bucket. The water runs across streets and yards faster than the ground can soak it up.

What that means in practice: Secure the trampoline, patio chairs, and tarps before this evening. Power may go out overnight — keep a flashlight and boiled water for the baby within reach. No reliable official INMET warning for our area and today turned up. Before any longer drive, check alertas2.inmet.gov.br yourself.

The rest of the week: Tuesday stays wet, SIMEPAR sees 98% and 16.2 mm at 18–26 °C. On Wednesday SIMEPAR reports 0% — the day for laundry, the lawn, and the mosquito walkthrough. Thursday brings rain back at 98%, plus gusts up to 47 km/h.[2]

The long weekend of Oct 10–12 — plan ahead now [5][6]

Children's Day falls on a Monday this year. On 10/12, it's a national holiday for Our Lady of Aparecida, Brazil's patron saint, and Dia das Crianças falls on the same day.[5] For the family, that means Saturday the 10th through Monday the 12th off in one stretch.

School off the Tuesday after? Unconfirmed. A search summary suggests Paraná's education authority set the October state-school recess for the 13th. No document or article on it could be opened. Elementary schools and daycares are usually municipal or private and have their own calendars anyway. Worth asking in the school group chat this week — if the 13th is off, it becomes four days.

Why this rain makes November's mosquitoes: The Aedes mosquito lays its eggs at the edge of standing water — a saucer under a plant pot is enough. The eggs survive dry for months. When rain comes, larvae hatch, and new mosquitoes fly about a week later. Tipping out the water beforehand breaks the cycle.
To-do: Wednesday, 10/07 On the dry day, walk through the yard and garden once — empty plant saucers, flip over buckets and toys, check the gutters. The school-age kids can turn it into a search game. For the baby: a mosquito net over the crib and stroller; mosquito repellent for infants only after checking with a doctor.
