Two sources, two answers SIMEPAR and climatempo agree on the temperatures and on the fact that it rains from today through Wednesday. They do not agree on certainty: today 98% versus 68%, tomorrow 98% versus 77% and 17.6 versus 12.4 mm.[1][2] We show both values and calculate no average. Both are model calculations. We could not verify an official warning from INMET or Defesa Civil for the Norte Pioneiro — the search returned only old reports.
Why are the numbers so different? Each weather service calculates with its own computer model, like two cooks with the same recipe: in the end it tastes similar, but not identical. The percentage says how sure the model is that it rains at all; the millimeters say how much. 17 mm is a solid rainy day — 17 liters fall on every square meter, almost two buckets.

Yesterday's good news stays half true: SIMEPAR strongly corrected Thursday downward, from 65.2 to 23.9 mm.[1][6] In return it now expects less today (3.1 instead of 10.6 mm) and more tomorrow (17.6 instead of 12.4 mm).[1][6] Models wobble across several days — we will look again tomorrow.

Health & Safety [1][8]

Rainy week: mosquito weather Up to 28 °C and rain on almost every day through Thursday — in this alternation the dengue mosquito breeds well. We found no official dengue warning for our region, but also no current figures (see below).
Why emptying helps The dengue mosquito lays its eggs just above the waterline, for example in a plant saucer. The eggs can wait dry for a long time. When rain comes and it is warm, larvae hatch within a few days. Anyone who empties and scrubs the containers takes away the eggs' bath.
  • After every rainy day, empty, scrub and turn over saucers, buckets, kiddie pools and toys in the yard.
  • For the baby, a mosquito net over the bed and stroller. Whether a mosquito spray is allowed at this age is stated on the package or by the pediatrician.
  • Gusts up to 50 km/h on Monday: secure loose garden furniture, trampoline and toys.[1]
  • In a thunderstorm, postpone trips. Official warnings are at alertas2.inmet.gov.br.
