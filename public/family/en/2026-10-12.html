Orange tomorrow — what it means In the warning text, INMET also names power outages, falling branches and flooding. For our family that means: postpone trips to Londrina or Ourinhos tomorrow, secure the patio furniture and trampoline today, charge phones and the power bank. The warning applies all of Tuesday, 00:01 to 23:59.[1]
Two sources, two answers SIMEPAR and climatempo agree: rain every day from today through Friday, 27–28 °C today. They disagree on certainty (SIMEPAR 98% every day, climatempo 77–93%) and on Tuesday's heat (29 °C versus 25 °C).[2][3] We give both values. The same 98% on five days looks like a model default — the INMET warnings are the stronger signal here.
What do yellow and orange mean? INMET has three levels, like a traffic light with an extra color: yellow means "watch out, minor damage possible". Orange means "danger, damage likely". Red means "great danger". 100 mm of rain in one day is a lot: 100 liters fall on every square meter — ten full buckets.

Health & Safety [1][8]

Storm with a baby: prepare today For Tuesday, INMET expects gusts of 60–100 km/h and hail.[1] Power often goes out in such conditions.
  • Today: Set out flashlights, a power bank and drinking water. Plan breast milk or a bottle supply so that a few hours without power are fine.
  • Secure or bring in the trampoline, wading pool and patio furniture.
  • In thunderstorms and hail: stay inside, do not stand under trees, do not drive through flooded streets.
After the rain: mosquito weather Up to 29 °C and rain almost every day — the dengue mosquito breeds well in this alternation. We found no official dengue warning for our region, but also no current figures (see below).
Why emptying helps The dengue mosquito lays its eggs just above the waterline, for example in a flower saucer. The eggs can wait dry for a long time. When rain comes and it is warm, larvae hatch within a few days. Anyone who empties and scrubs the containers takes away the eggs' bath.

After every rainy day, empty and turn over saucers, buckets and toys in the garden. For the baby, a mosquito net over the bed and stroller; whether a mosquito spray is allowed at this age is stated on the package or by the pediatrician.

It's Monday, October twelfth. Today is a holiday: Nossa Senhora Aparecida and Children's Day. Happy Children's Day to all four kids!

The weather isn't cooperating, and now it's official. The weather service INMET lists Ribeirão Claro and Jacarezinho in its storm warnings. Today is a yellow alert: up to fifty millimeters of rain, wind up to sixty kilometers an hour, and possible hail. Tomorrow, Tuesday, it goes to orange: fifty to one hundred millimeters of rain over the day, gusts up to one hundred kilometers an hour, and hail. Wednesday drops back to yellow.

So our advice: make it a party at home today. The two older kids plan the day, with a treasure hunt, admission tickets and a movie in the evening. And set out flashlights and a power bank today and secure the yard, because with this much wind the power often goes out. Better to put off trips to Londrina or Ourinhos tomorrow.

We didn't find a confirmed Children's Day celebration near us. In Londrina, the Festa da Padroeira expects over forty thousand people today, but that's a long way in this weather.

The sure thing is the children's movies at Sesc Jacarezinho. From Wednesday through Friday, Trapalhões films play at nine-thirty and two-thirty, free of charge. The best fit for the family is the Saturday showing, the seventeenth, at seven p.m. And then it dries out: climatempo sees no rain for Saturday and Sunday. That's only one source, but a good reason to pencil in Sunday as an outing day.

It's still open whether there's school on Tuesday and on Thursday, Teachers' Day. The school group will know best.

After the rain comes mosquito weather. Empty any standing water in the yard, and use a mosquito net for the baby. We couldn't read a current dengue figure for Paraná, so we're not citing one.
