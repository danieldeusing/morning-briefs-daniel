Why does one site say 0% and another 100%? Each weather site calculates with a different computer model. If a rain front sits right on the edge of our area, one model sees it passing just by and another sees it passing just over. The further away the day, the bigger the disagreement. We'll look again on Saturday, and by then the picture for Sunday is usually clearer.

Health & safety

Dengue: The state of Paraná published "Informe Epidemiológico nº 31" on 2026-11-10.[6] We could not read the figures in it today either. The last figure we know for sure is old: in weeks SE 1 to 14/2026, Paraná counted 2,028 confirmed cases, 96.1% fewer than in the same period of 2025. That figure is from 2026-04-15 and says nothing about the situation today.[7]

Mosquito season with El Niño The Paraná health department (Sesa) warned in August of more dengue, Zika and chikungunya because of El Niño, and in September drew up plans for 2026/27 with experts.[8] According to Sesa, the epidemic years 2016, 2019, 2020 and 2024 show a direct link between El Niño and more cases. With showers and 30 °C we now have exactly the weather in which the Aedes mosquito breeds fast. After every shower: empty buckets, plant saucers, kiddie pools and toys in the yard. For the baby: a mosquito net over the crib and stroller.
What does El Niño have to do with mosquitoes? El Niño is a warming of the Pacific that changes the weather on half the world. For us it often brings more warmth and rain. The Aedes mosquito lays its eggs in small puddles, and in warm weather the eggs turn into new mosquitoes faster. More puddles plus more warmth means more mosquitoes, and more mosquitoes can bring more dengue.

A word from the weather report: pancada de chuva. Literally it's a "rain blow", meaning a short, heavy shower. Climatempo uses it for Monday: "Pancadas de chuva à tarde e à noite". Today you read the opposite for Saturday: tempo limpo, "clean weather", meaning clear sky. A good game for the kids: whoever shouts "Olha a pancada!" first when the first drops fall gets to pick dinner.

Good morning. It's Friday, November thirteenth, and the weekend is almost here. Expect showers on and off today, and all three weather sources agree. Highs at midday reach twenty-eight to thirty degrees Celsius.

Saturday looks like the best day to be outside. Two of the three sources see little or no rain, with highs near thirty degrees. Our suggestion: head out in the morning, say to the Parque Ecológico in Ribeirão Claro, and get the baby into the shade by midday.

Sunday is a toss-up, because the weather sites split. Climatempo sees no rain and thirty degrees. HG Brasil sees rain for sure and only twenty-two degrees. So keep an indoor plan ready, and check the window on Sunday morning.

Events are thin this weekend. The Festival de Primavera in Ribeirão Claro already ran in early November. A countryside cycling event, the sixth Cicloturismo Agrocultural, is said to run Saturday and Sunday. But we have it from only one source, so check with the Prefeitura first.

On health: Paraná released a new dengue bulletin on November tenth, but we couldn't read the figures. The state is warning of a heavier mosquito season because of El Niño. After every shower, empty buckets, plant saucers and toys in the yard, and put a mosquito net over the baby's crib and stroller.

Looking ahead: November fifteenth is a holiday, but it falls on a Sunday. November twentieth is a national holiday by law, and it lands on a Friday. That makes a long weekend through the twenty-second.

Tomorrow we'll take another look at Sunday's forecast and keep hunting for things to do. Have a great weekend, everyone.
