Weekend Plans

Sunday, 1st Advent: ballet in Londrina. Our favorite remains "Casa de Brinquedos" at the Ouro Verde (details in the pick above).[4] The story is made for our big ones: children break into a toymaker's house, and the dolls come to life. On stage are children barely older than they are. And the weather plays along: Climatempo sees only 0.3 mm on Sunday.[1] If you leave around 2 p.m., you have a buffer for a diaper break.

For ballet fans: "Coppélia" in the evening. At the same venue the municipal dance school shows the full ballet "Coppélia", the model for the children's piece, on Saturday and Sunday at 8 p.m. Dancing are "estudantes do 2o ano ao aperfeiçoamento", meaning the older years; the show runs until 10 p.m.[5] With a baby and a toddler that is too late; the drive home ends around midnight. For one parent with the two big ones it can be a special evening. Sympla shows no price here either.[5]

Saturday morning: dengue detectives in the yard. Dia D against dengue is on Saturday, across Brazil.[6] According to Climatempo the showers come spread over the day, so go out between two downpours.[1] One round through yard and patio: plant saucers, buckets, toys, tarps, gutters. Whoever finds the most water spots picks the movie that evening. The two-year-old gets to empty them out, which he enjoys most. Sesa does not say when and where the city runs its own actions; maybe a call will come through the school group.

Saturday afternoon indoors: the Advent wreath. When the rain comes, it is craft time. Twigs from the yard, a plate or a wire ring, four candles. On Sunday early the first candle burns, before we head to Londrina.

Health & Safety

Dengue: few cases, but Sesa warns about El Niño Paraná is quiet in 2026. In the "44 semanas do período epidemiológico" (SE 44/2026) the health authority Sesa confirmed 6,380 cases, about 93% fewer than in 2025. Six deaths in 2026 come on top of that.[6] Still, Sesa warns that El Niño may bring more rain, and more rain helps the Aedes mosquito. Hence Dia D on Saturday.[6] The report is from 2026-11-12. We found no newer bulletin and no figures for Ribeirão Claro or Jacarezinho. We leave out comparison figures for 2025: the sources give different values.
El Niño: One part of the Pacific gets warmer than usual, and that shifts the weather across half the world. Like a heater in the next room that also warms our room: in southern Brazil, El Niño often brings more rain.
Aedes cycle: The dengue mosquito lays its eggs at the edge of standing water. A forgotten saucer turns into a flying mosquito in a little over a week. Whoever empties the water stops the cycle before it flies.

What does that mean for us? No alarm, but good timing. Rain falls on Saturday, and next week Climatempo sees more wet days (see below).[1] Every container we empty and turn over on Saturday does not fill up again afterward. For the baby: mosquito net over the bed and stroller, especially at dusk.

Sun and heat: On Sunday it gets up to 28 °C.[1][2] On the way to Londrina, that means water for everyone, a hat for the big ones, and the baby out of direct sun.

Outlook: changeable week, one portal only For next week we have only the Climatempo values. Climatempo sees rain on Wed 12/2 (14.4 mm, 86%) and on Fri 12/4 (16.1 mm). For Sat 12/5 the page shows an "Aviso de mudança brusca de temperatura", a notice of a sudden temperature change, with 19 to 24 °C and 20.3 mm.[1] That is a week away, and forecasts that far ahead are uncertain. A second portal is still missing. We check every day.

German-Brazilian

Sunday is first Advent, and this year the calendar fits nicely: Christmas Eve is on a Thursday, and fourth Advent falls on 12/20. The Advent wreath comes from Germany; in Brazil it is called "coroa do Advento" and is found mostly in churches. An idea for the big ones: they explain to Grandma in Portuguese why one more candle burns each Sunday. And in a week, on the evening of 12/5, the boots go out the door: St. Nicholas Day, 12/6, is a Sunday this year.

Good morning, it's Friday, November twenty-seventh. Today the weather picture for the weekend is finally clear, because two portals for Ribeirão Claro agree.

Today stays dry, with temperatures from seventeen to twenty-four degrees Celsius, so the afternoon after school is the best time for the playground. Tomorrow, Saturday, brings short showers throughout the day, with an eighty-five to eighty-eight percent chance of rain and a high of twenty-six degrees. On Sunday, Climatempo expects hardly any rain, just three tenths of a millimeter, and it warms up to twenty-eight degrees.

That's why our pick is Sunday, the first Sunday of Advent. At the Ouro Verde in Londrina, children from the municipal dance school perform the piece Casa de Brinquedos at four p.m., rated for all ages. It's about children who sneak into a toymaker's house. The show also runs Saturday at the same time. Sympla doesn't list a price, so check before you buy.

On Saturday and Sunday at eight p.m., the dance school also performs the full ballet Coppélia at the same venue. With a baby and a toddler that's too late, but for one parent with the two big kids it could be a special evening.

Saturday is also the national day of action against dengue. Paraná is quiet this year, with six thousand three hundred eighty confirmed cases, about ninety-three percent fewer than last year. The health authority warns, though, that El Niño could bring more rain, and that helps the mosquito. So between two showers, go through the yard with the big kids, look for standing water and empty it. And when the rain comes, it's time for the Advent wreath.

For next week, Climatempo sees several wet days and, for Saturday, December fifth, a sudden change in temperature. That's only one portal and still far off, and we'll check every day.

The school year in the state network ends on December eighteenth, according to an overview, exactly three weeks from now. The authority hasn't confirmed that yet. Tomorrow we'll see whether the region announces anything more for Sunday, and what the weather looks like for next week. Have a great Friday, everyone.
