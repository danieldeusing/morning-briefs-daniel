Today & Tomorrow

Today, Saturday, will be wet, but how wet is open. Climatempo shows 18 to 27 °C, 8.8 mm and a 91% chance of rain for Jacarezinho. Agrolink shows 17 to 26 °C and only 65%.[1][2] On temperature, the two are only 1 °C apart. On rain, they agree that it is coming, but not on how certain it is. Rain jackets and rubber boots belong in the car today.

Tomorrow, Sunday, will be warmer and drier. Climatempo expects 20 to 28 °C, a 71% chance, but only 0.7 mm. Agrolink shows 18 to 27 °C and again 65%.[1][2] 0.7 mm is barely more than a few drops. Sunday is therefore the better day for the drive to Londrina.

Weekend Plans

Saturday morning: dengue detectives in the yard. Today is the “Dia D de Mobilização Nacional contra a dengue,” across all of Brazil. For it, Sesa plans “mutirões de limpeza,” that is, community cleanup drives.[5] For us, our own yard is enough. One round through the garden and yard: saucers, buckets, toys, tarps, gutters. Whoever finds the most standing-water spots picks the movie tonight. The two-year-old gets to empty them out. Best done first thing in the morning, before the rain comes.

Saturday afternoon indoors: the Advent wreath. When it rains, it is craft time. Branches from the garden, a plate or a wire ring, four candles. Tomorrow morning the first candle burns.

Saturday or Sunday, 4 p.m.: ballet in Londrina. “Casa de Brinquedos” at Ouro Verde is our tip (details in the pick above).[3] A theater hall is the best place for four kids in the rain. Anyone who wants to drive today has the checked showing. Anyone who drives tomorrow probably gets the drier road, but has to check venue and price on Sympla themselves.[4] Leaving around 2 p.m. leaves a buffer for a diaper-change stop.

Nearby: nothing found this time. In Jacarezinho, Ribeirão Claro and Ourinhos we found no family event for this weekend. The pages we check showed no readable dates, and we did not search the Prefeituras' Instagram and Facebook. That does not mean nothing is going on: a look at the school or neighborhood group is worthwhile.

Health & Safety

Dengue: few cases, but Sesa warns about El Niño Paraná is quiet in 2026. The health authority Sesa writes: “Em 2026, entre as 44 semanas do período epidemiológico, foram confirmados 6.380 casos,” and that is “93% a menos do que em 2025” (SE 44/2026). Deaths: six in 2026, versus 114 the year before.[5] Even so, Sesa warns that El Niño can increase transmission. Hence today's Dia D.[5] The report is dated 2026-11-12, with data from 2026-11-10. We found no newer bulletin; the current status is therefore unchecked. Figures for Ribeirão Claro, Jacarezinho or the Norte Pioneiro are also missing.
El Niño: A part of the Pacific gets warmer than usual, and that shifts the weather across half the world. Like a heater in the next room that also warms up our room: in southern Brazil, El Niño often brings more rain.
Aedes cycle: The dengue mosquito lays its eggs at the edge of standing water. A forgotten saucer turns into a flying mosquito in a little over a week. Anyone who empties the water stops the cycle before it flies.

What does this mean for us? No alarm, but good timing. Rain falls today, and Agrolink shows 95% and 90% for Wednesday and Thursday.[2] Every container we empty and turn over today will not fill up again afterward. For the baby: mosquito net over the bed and stroller, especially at dusk.

Sun: Tomorrow it gets up to 27–28 °C.[1][2] For the drive, that means water for everyone, a hat for the big ones, and keep the baby out of direct sun.

Outlook: wet midweek, only one portal For next week we have only the Agrolink values: Mon 11/30 50%, Tue 12/1 70%, Wed 12/2 95%, Thu 12/3 90% chance of rain, at 27 to 29 °C.[2] A second portal is missing, and forecasts this far ahead are uncertain. We check every day.

Respiratory: Nothing new today on flu, RSV or Covid.

Roads: We did not check closures on BR-153, BR-369 and PR-092. Take a quick look at the maps app before the drive to Londrina.

German-Brazilian

Tomorrow is the first Sunday of Advent. This year Christmas Eve falls on a Thursday, and the fourth Sunday of Advent falls on 12/20. The Advent wreath comes from Germany; in Brazil it is called “coroa do Advento” and is found mostly in churches. An idea for the big ones: they explain to Grandma in Portuguese why one more candle burns every Sunday. And on the evening of 12/5, the boots go out the door, because St. Nicholas Day on 12/6 is a Sunday.
