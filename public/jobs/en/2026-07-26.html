Project tenders (TED EU) — correction to yesterday

The brief from Jul 25 wrote that, of the ten ongoing procedures, "exactly two came without a hard revenue or headcount threshold": KARLAS and the Magdeburg University Hospital. That assessment came from a Jul 24 review that hadn't read the notices in full text. Today the three nearest procedures were pulled in full PDF text. The assessment doesn't hold up.

Magdeburg is tendering a SaaS solution for discharge management, CPV 72260000, participation-request deadline Aug 7, 2026, 10:00 CEST.[1] The subject matter fits — a software service, not model training. The eligibility criteria don't fit a two-person operation: at least two of the reference projects must relate to a primary, standard, or specialist-care hospital with at least 300 beds; certifications to ISO 27001, ISO 9001, and ISO 14001 are required; professional liability insurance must cover €3M per claim for personal injury, €3M for property damage, and €3M for financial loss; and a named project lead with at least four years of experience managing IT projects in healthcare is needed. That's four independent walls, not one. There is at least one easing in the Jul 15 amendment notice: the AI-powered extension modules were downgraded "from a mandatory minimum criterion (knock-out criterion) to a purely qualitative evaluation criterion" — they're now points, not a requirement.

KARLAS, the wastewater database of Saxony's State Office for the Environment, Agriculture and Geology in Dresden, is the cleanest stack match of the three: CPV 72230000, custom software development, open procedure, bid deadline Aug 24, 2026, 23:59 CEST, term Oct 16, 2026 to Sep 30, 2028.[2] But the notice literally carries the line: "This contract award is also suitable for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs): no." The field is a self-assessment by the contracting authority on the standard form, not a legal exclusion — but as a signal it's unambiguous, and it directly contradicts yesterday's assessment.

Where the actual figures sit remained open today: the notice refers to the procurement documents for the selection criteria. The revenue threshold, FTE requirement, and number of references therefore sit in a separate document on Saxony's e-procurement portal, which wasn't pulled today. So no threshold is estimated here. Anyone pursuing the case should download those documents first — not the service description.

This isn't a partner case anymore — it's a different company.

Participation request or bid — why the procedure type decides the workload: in a two-stage procedure (Magdeburg, DB), you first submit only a participation request: proof that you're even allowed to compete — revenue, references, certificates, insurance. Whoever survives that round is only then invited to submit a bid. Think of a club with a guest list: the bouncer first, then the party. In the open procedure (KARLAS), there's no bouncer — anyone can submit a complete bid right away, with eligibility and price reviewed together. In practice that means: in a two-stage procedure, a failed attempt only costs the paperwork; in an open procedure, it costs the entire bid calculation. An open procedure with unclear eligibility criteria — exactly the KARLAS case — is therefore more expensive to get wrong, not cheaper.

From 121 to 49 — what this number measures

Sunday's pull brings 49 listings: 30 freelance, 14 employee, 5 intriguing. Yesterday it was 121. The gap looks like a market collapse, and it isn't one. Of yesterday's 121 entries, 36 remain on the board today, 13 are new, and 85 no longer show up in today's sweep.

Our own archive supplies the counter-evidence in four days: 150 listings sat on the board on Jul 21, only 69 on Jul 22 — down 54% overnight — and via 98 and 107 the count was back at 121 by Jul 25. Contracts don't vanish overnight and reappear in three days; sweep coverage does exactly that. The evidence sits in the source distribution: freelancermap.de supplied 91 of yesterday's 121 entries, and 29 of today's 49. Almost the entire gap sits in a single source.

Why the board number swings like a search result, not a thermometer: a thermometer measures temperature; if it reads 12 instead of 24 degrees, it has actually gotten colder. A board pull isn't a thermometer — it's a search run: a program crawls intermediary sites and logs what it finds. If it only catches half the pages on a given day — because a search page responds more slowly, a pagination function breaks, or a provider throttles on the weekend — the list halves without a single project actually being awarded. That's why this brief never says "85 projects vanished," only "85 no longer in the sweep." What's reliable about a list like this isn't the daily count, but what stays in it consistently across many days.

What's solid today is therefore the composition, not the volume: median fit 69, eight entries at 80+, four at 85+, 40 of 49 tagged remote. And the day's most interesting number isn't 49 anyway — it's 19.

Two panels, one price: around €102

freelance.de has released its Freelancer Study 2026: the average hourly rate across all fields is €101.70, versus €100.55 the year before; €101.98 in IT, €100.49 in non-IT. It was surveyed from Jan 27 to Mar 2, 2026 among more than 3,300 respondents, supplemented with platform data; published Mar 13, 2026.[4]

That gets interesting against yesterday. This brief cited freelancermap's Freelancer Compass at €103 — a different platform, a different panel (around 5,400 respondents), a different survey period. The two figures sit 1.3% apart. Two independently surveyed panels agreeing on around €102 is a harder anchor than any single figure: this profile's €100 target sits demonstrably at the market average, not above it. Anyone charging €110+ is selling a niche, not a standard full-stacker.

On demand, the studies seem to diverge, but they're actually measuring different things. freelancermap asked retrospectively and got 49% reporting a weaker order situation than the year before. freelance.de asked prospectively: "For 2026, 68% of the surveyed freelancers expect a rather difficult (42%) or very difficult (26%) order situation." Hindsight and expectation are two different questions — and the expectation turns out more pessimistic than the experience.

But the study's hardest figure isn't a survey at all — it's platform statistics: "While around 97,400 projects were published on freelance.de in 2022, it was only about 56,000 in 2025." That's roughly 42% fewer postings in three years, counted rather than estimated. And it's the real answer to the board question from the left-hand column: a board counts postings — but the pool of postings itself is shrinking structurally, while the hourly price stands nearly still (+1.1% versus the year before). Fewer contracts at almost the same price means: competition runs on utilization, not on rate.
