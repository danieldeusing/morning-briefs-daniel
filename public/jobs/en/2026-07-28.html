Today's score is the rule-compliant one. This profile's fit formula splits the 45 possible stack points into Java/Spring 18, Angular/TypeScript 12, Cloud/AWS 8, and IoT/protocols 7. A role that matches solely on IoT protocol depth can therefore earn at most 7 of these 45 points — the 20 from July 3 was a freehand upgrade, and the note "adjusted 74 for exact-domain CV match" says so outright. The catch: applying the rule correctly leads to something uncomfortable.

The calculated ceiling for a pure niche role is 62. Stack 7 plus a full 20 for 100% remote plus a full 15 for a rate at target plus a full 10 intrigue plus a full 10 track — nothing more is possible. Today's page-1 cutoff for the board sits at Fit 71 (ranks 20 and 21 share this value). A smart-home or automotive project that comes without Java is therefore structurally excluded from the first page — no matter how well it fits. And that hits exactly the lane for which this profile sets its highest target rate: €110–140/h versus €100/h+ for the Java/Angular standard.

The finding is verifiable today. Eight entries carry an IoT, smart-home, or automotive tag, and they sit at ranks 23, 47, 59, 60, 62, 63, 66, and 67 of 70 — none on page 1. The best of them is, unsurprisingly, the one where the niche sits on Java: Amoria Bond is looking for a "Fullstack Entwickler IoT (Java/AWS)" for machine-data integration in the packaging industry, Fit 70, rank 23. The pure niche — KNX, building automation, navigation architecture in Ingolstadt — lands on pages 3 and 4.

Practical consequence: for the niche, sorting by fit is the wrong tool — anyone chasing the premium filters by tag and reads the back pages. The last sensible business day for that is Friday, Jul 31.

Telematics infrastructure, ePA, KIM, VSDM: Germany's telematics infrastructure is the country's sealed-off data network for healthcare — its own network, separate from the open internet, that doctors' offices, hospitals, pharmacies, and insurers connect to. Picture it like a corporate network spanning an entire country: to get in you need an issued card, an approved box at the door, and clearance for every piece of software allowed to speak on it. ePA is the electronic patient record, the central folder per insured person. KIM is the mail service inside it — encrypted email between doctors with guaranteed delivery. VSDM is the card check at reception: when the insurance card sits in the reader, the practice asks the insurer within seconds whether it's still valid. For the rate, what matters is that gematik specifies and approves every one of these components: there are plenty of Java developers, far fewer Java developers with gematik experience. That's exactly why this entry carries €100–130/h* instead of the standard band — though that premium isn't documented, since the listing states no figure.

Estimated value vs. ceiling value of a framework agreement: a framework agreement isn't a contract, it's a standing purchase authorization: the authority decides who it's allowed to order from over the next few years and on what terms, and then draws on it as needed. Think of a tradesman's framework contract for a property-management company — it says who comes and what the hourly rate is, not how often something breaks. The estimated value (at DB: €12.9m) is what the buyer realistically expects to draw down; the ceiling value (€18.1m) is the upper limit beyond which nothing more can happen without a new tender. For a small bidder, the estimated value matters most: minimum-revenue thresholds are usually measured against that annual volume, often at double its size.
