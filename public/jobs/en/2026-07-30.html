Halved, not shifted

Thursday's pull brings 42 listings: 30 freelance, 8 employee, 4 intriguing. Of the previous day's 87 entries, 24 are back, 18 are new, and 63 have dropped. That's the steepest daily decline since this count began, and it breaks the week's four growth days (49 → 60 → 70 → 87) down to a value below the previous low.

The obvious reading would be a demand collapse. It doesn't survive scrutiny. A market that's collapsing shifts the mix: first the expensive niches vanish, then the long-running contracts, then the senior roles — the distribution tilts. Today, nothing tilts. The freelance share sits at 71.4% against 72.4% the day before, the twiceD tag at 64.3% against 62.1%, the remote flag at 85.7% against 87.4%. And the skill rows all shrink at the same pace: Java 41× → 22× (factor 0.54), Spring Boot 32× → 14× (0.44), Angular 29× → 14× (0.48), AI integration 23× → 11× (0.48), Kubernetes 15× → 8× (0.53), twiceD 54× → 27× (0.50) — at an overall factor of 0.48. Everything halves, nothing shifts. That's the signature of a shallower sample, not a market.

One stated rate, and it's below the median

For the rate question, there's a considerably better anchor today than the directory averages of recent days: a rate study broken down by specialization, showing a €95/h median for DACH-IT and €90/h for software and web development, plus 28% of IT freelancers planning to raise their rate in 2026 against roughly one in ten planning to lower it.[1]

Median instead of average: the average adds up all the rates and divides by the count — a few high outliers (SAP specialists at €180/h) pull it up without a typical contract paying any more. The median is the rate exactly in the middle: one half of freelancers sit below it, the other half above. For a negotiation, the median is the more reliable number because it isn't skewed by extremes. That the median (€95) and the average (€97) sit this close together also says something: the distribution is tight, there's no large top cluster pulling the average up. Anyone asking for €90 to €95 in the DACH market isn't asking for the optimistic edge, but for the middle.

Against this median, the board today offers exactly one self-stated freelance figure: ProTIP ProjektService for DB Systel, max. €680/day — around €85/h, and thus below the €90 median for software development, for a 24-month contract with only 20 on-site days. The second self-stated figure in the freelance segment isn't a rate but an annual salary: „up to €80,000/year" at Experis for Techniker Krankenkasse, explicitly via temporary staffing (Arbeitnehmerüberlassung). In total, 5 of 42 entries state compensation themselves — alongside ProTIP and Experis, those are Camunda (US$126,800–190,100), Grafana Labs (€97,034–116,441), and Smartsheet (€87,500–112,500) — one states none at all, the remaining 36 bands are a researched benchmark. Yesterday it was 10 of 87.

The demand side explains why nothing is moving upward. The Freelancer Kompass 2026 (surveyed Nov 17–Dec 3, 2025) finds 43% of freelancers without secured project utilization; in IT/software, 23% report declining demand — less than automotive's 32%, but double industry/mechanical engineering and banking, each at 12%.[2] A market where 28% of providers want to raise rates and almost half have no secured utilization holds its rates — it doesn't push them up.

Exactly this stack — with no findable deadline

This brief's TED lane has so far produced tenders whose eligibility bar was too high (€5M minimum revenue at govdigital, two hospital references plus ISO 27001 in Magdeburg) or whose subject matter only roughly fit (test automation and consulting at the Oberfinanzdirektion Baden-Württemberg). Today, for the first time, there's a notice whose subject matter is the description of Daniel's core stack.

The Bavarian State Tax Office (Zentrale Vergabestelle, Munich) published a notice under TED number 86435-2026 for IT services covering Java software development and the development of web-based frontends in identity and access management. The minimum call-off volume is 640 person-days, the estimated call-off volume 2,560 person-days, the minimum term two years with two one-year renewal options (2+1+1). The procuring authority and contact (ausschreibung@lfst.bayern.de) are confirmed via the agency, submission runs through Bavaria's procurement portal.[9] Java, a web frontend, IAM, a public-sector client, a framework contract with plannable volume — on paper, this is the match this lane has been waiting weeks for.

Minimum and estimated call-off volume in person-days: a framework contract doesn't buy finished software, it buys working time on call, measured in person-days (PD) — one PD is one person's workday. The minimum call-off volume is what the agency guarantees to draw: 640 PD is roughly 2.5 person-years, spread across the term. The estimated call-off volume (2,560 PD) is the ceiling it expects, but doesn't promise. Like a phone plan with a base fee plus extra data: it pays the minimum in any case, the rest only if needed. For a one-person business, the minimum volume is the number that matters, because it caps the contract risk — 640 guaranteed PD over two years is about 27 days a quarter, so it's manageable on the side. The estimated 2,560 PD over four years, by contrast, would amount to more than a full-time position on an ongoing basis and couldn't be delivered solo.

And then the one number everything hinges on is missing. The submission deadline and estimated contract value appear in no source accessible today: the direct TED PDF pull returns only compressed document streams and, in the metadata, a creation date of Jun 27, 2026; two independent search passes found the subject matter, volume, and term, but neither the deadline nor the value. For an open procedure, the statutory minimum submission period is 30 days from when the notice was sent — with a publication on Jun 27, even that floor would have expired around Jul 27, i.e., three days ago. It remains possible that the procuring authority set a longer deadline; it isn't confirmed. Verdict, therefore: not a biddable case as long as the deadline isn't confirmed — and the only deterministic next step isn't another search, but a line to the procuring authority.
