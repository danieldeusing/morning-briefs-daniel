The listings themselves confirm it. 16 of the 71 entries state a concrete start date in the title. For 13 of those, the start is in the past — and all 13 belong to today's newcomers: Jan 1 twice, Jan 2, Feb 1, May 1, May 11, May 26, Jun 1, Jul 1 four times, and Aug 1. Only three entries start in the future (Aug 10, Sep 1, Oct 1).

That knocks out Friday's thesis. On Jul 31 this brief said AI integration, with 12 mentions, was for the first time this board's most common skill tag — ahead of Java's 11. The caveat at the time: 32 entries is a thin base, and a tag that shifts can also just be a coverage gap. That's exactly what it was. Today AI integration sits at an unchanged 11 mentions and is the eighth most common tag, while Java jumps to 30 and Angular to 28. The absolute number of AI listings has barely moved across four pulls (11 → 12 → 11 → 11); what moved was only how many Java listings stood next to it. A tag's share of this board isn't a market size, it's a function of one platform's pull depth. This correction applies retroactively to Friday's issue.

Two things are real nonetheless. First, quality: 22 entries reach fit 70 or above, up from 12 yesterday, 12 of them ≥ 75; the median climbs marginally from 62.5 to 63.0. More pages here also means more matching pages. Second, the cleanliness of the comparison: the 41 carryovers carry exactly the same fit scores today as yesterday — zero drift across 41 shared entries. So for the Aug 1/Aug 2 pair, the numbers above aren't noise artifacts.

That doesn't hold over longer stretches. Today's top entry, the iSAX mandate with Java 21, Spring Boot 3, Angular, Flutter, and AWS Cognito, already sat at rank two on Jul 31 — back then at fit 87 and under a different dedup key. Today the same listing is carried as a newcomer at fit 83. Same story with Keil Business Solutions' telematics mandate, noted here on Jul 31 as having "dropped out of the pull" and back on the board since yesterday: 88 on Jul 30, 80 today. The key is built from company and title, and when the source page serves the title slightly differently, a new entry is created. Practical consequence: "new" and "dropped" only hold up between two consecutive pulls — and a listing vanishing from the board doesn't mean it's been filled.

Four of seventy-one state a price

With 71 entries, a reliable price sample would be possible. It isn't there. Exactly four listings state compensation that isn't researched: ProTIP ProjektService at up to €680/day, roughly €85/h; Grafana Labs at € 109,000–137,000 a year; Right People Group at €70/h for an Angular frontend mandate; and Anthropic at $ 405,000–485,000 for a role in San Francisco that isn't remotely reachable from Brazil anyway. Three listings state nothing at all. The remaining 64 bands on the board are a researched benchmark and carry the asterisk — they state what the profile should charge, not what the client wants to pay.

Both German freelance rates sit below market. €85/h and €70/h stand against a median of €90/h that freelancermap's IT breakdown reports for software and web development — the lowest band on its specialization overview, while the median across all IT disciplines sits at €95/h.[2] The €70 case visibly drags its score down: fit 50 despite an otherwise decent Angular profile and 100% remote.

The anchor under the whole board is falling. The DACH freelancer survey of 5,412 respondents reports an average rate for 2026 of €103/h, down from €104/h the year before, and monthly income of €6,653, down from €8,432 — a drop of roughly 21%. Looking ahead, 62% of respondents plan no rate increase for 2026, 9% plan to lower it.[1] That's the real story behind the number: it's not the price that's collapsing, it's the willingness to defend it — plus utilization, since a one-percent rate drop doesn't explain a 21% income drop.

ANÜ (Arbeitnehmerüberlassung — employee leasing): One entry on today's board is explicitly advertised as „ANÜ" (it-excelsus, 100% remote from Essen, fit 76). That is not a freelance contract, it's agency work: you're employed by the staffing agency and loaned out to the client for the project's duration. Picture it this way: with a Werkvertrag (work contract) you sell a finished result, like a tradesman selling his installation; with staff leasing you rent yourself out by the hour, and instructions come from the client. For a GmbH managing director billing through his own company, this model doesn't fit structurally. Practical consequence: before pitching, check whether ANÜ is really meant — many agencies tack it on out of habit while meaning a service contract. If it is meant, the entry is unusable for this profile no matter how well the stack fits.

The age band is the most uncomfortable number in this research. The same breakdown lists €95/h for the 31–40 age group and €96/h for the 41–50 group.[2] A one-euro difference over ten years of professional experience — seniority is barely rewarded in this market. Anyone who wants to charge above €100/h doesn't earn the premium through years, but through the niche: on this board that means the IoT entries and mandates that require AI integration and Java together, such as Allegis's AWS Bedrock/RAG mandate (fit 79) at a researched €100–130/h.

Publication dates: 5 of the 71 entries carry an actual publication date (Jun 9, May 21, Jul 24, Jul 15, Jul 9, 2026); 66 carry "n/a." The starred rate bands come from this brief's benchmark research, not from the listings.

Project tenders (TED EU)

Three notices from the German CPV-72 window sit in the filter today with a confirmed deadline — and one of them matches this profile thematically more precisely than anything this lane has had in weeks.

Germany's Federal Office for Information Security (BSI) is tendering „Projekt 1000 – Agentic AI: Prüfanforderungen für LLM-basierte agentische künstliche Intelligenz (PRAKI)". Confirmed from the notice XML: CPV 72000000, term 18 months, submission deadline Aug 10, 2026 at 16:00 CEST, bid opening Aug 11, 2026 at 10:00 CEST, and explicitly SME-suitable.[7] The subject is identifying the key use cases for agentic AI and developing assessment requirements for them — that is, applied AI work on tools and their safeguarding, not model research. Exactly the distinction this lane looks for.

Two caveats come with it, and both are hard. First, the deadline: from today to Aug 10 there are exactly five business days left — this lane's floor, no buffer. Second, eligibility: the notice doesn't state the thresholds for revenue, headcount, or references. It points to "chapter 6.2.1.3 of the scope description" for details, which sits behind the tender documents on evergabe-online.de and wasn't opened here. So there's no number here — neither a real one nor an estimate. Anyone who wants to know whether a two-person operation can bid needs to pull the documents, and do it Monday.

Eligibility criteria and minimum revenue: Public contracting authorities can't just pick the cheapest bidder — they first have to check whether that bidder can actually carry the contract through. So they set thresholds: minimum annual revenue, a minimum number of permanent staff, professional liability coverage of a set amount, comparable reference projects. Like a landlord wanting to see three pay stubs before handing over the keys: it isn't about ability, it's about proof you can survive a setback. For a solo operation, that's the real hurdle — not the technology, but the balance sheet. That's why this lane notes, for every tender, what threshold a partner would need to cover.
