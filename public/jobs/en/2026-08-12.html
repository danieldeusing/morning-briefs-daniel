A find that slipped through yesterday. Among the documents to submit is the form "C3_Self-declaration of suitability" — followed by the sentence: "This document must also be submitted by the subcontractor/in the case of capacity borrowing." So the contracting authority anticipates bidders who borrow suitability from a third party, and has formally left that route open. For this profile, that's a pleasant irony: the partner mechanism that this track's gap analysis otherwise revolves around is provided for here, but not needed — there's only a single eligibility hurdle, and it's an insurance policy.

Capacity borrowing (Eignungsleihe): If a bidder doesn't meet a required condition on their own — minimum turnover, a reference, a certificate — they can "borrow" that qualification from another company. Procurement law explicitly allows this, but requires the lender to commit bindingly to actually participate if the contract is awarded. Like renting an apartment with a guarantor: you sign the lease, but someone with stronger credit is on the paperwork with you — and is liable if you default. That's exactly why capacity borrowing isn't a loophole but a real commitment: the partner must sign the declaration themselves and submit the same forms.

And a sentence that explains today's urgency. The notice reserves the right to "request or clarify missing or incomplete documents up to the expiry of a supplementary period set by the contracting authority" — with the decisive addition: "Applicants have no right to a supplementary request/submission or clarification of documents." Requesting supplementary documents is a discretionary rule under Section 56(2) and (3) VgV, not a bidder's right. In practice that means: proof of professional liability insurance or the bank confirmation must be on hand with the bid by 08-25. Anyone without such a policy should arrange it now — not on 08-24.

Location remains the only real gap — and it still has a day and a half. Across eight pages, the notice says nothing about home office, remote share, or on-site requirements. For a bidder residing in Brazil, that's exactly the question that decides between bidding and passing. It can only still be clarified until 2026-08-13, 23:59 CEST, and only through the portal's project area — the notice states explicitly: "Communication takes place exclusively via the project area of the portal." An email to the listed contracting-authority address is therefore not a valid channel. Second, no-longer-clarifiable point: the notice names no technology. Test automation in the KONSENS environment tends to be Java-adjacent from experience, but that's unconfirmed here; the service description and the scoring matrix "A2_Angebotsprüfung_Wertung" sit behind the portal.

Best sourced: Telefónica Deutschland. By the end of 2026, up to 1,100 full-time positions are set to go, along with the closure of roughly 60 company-owned O2 stores; affected are customer service, retail, and central functions including engineering. In Q2 2026 the company booked €265 million in restructuring provisions, with up to €155 million more in the second half. The report is from 2026-07-28 — but via an industry outlet, not the company's own statement.[4]

Restructuring provision: A company books money as an expense that it will only spend later — severance pay, termination agreements, leases on closed stores. Like entering the cost of a December move into your household budget already in July, even though nothing has actually been spent yet. For an observer, this figure is often more telling than a job-cut headline: provisions must be justified on the balance sheet, an announced cut doesn't have to be. €265 million means the separations are largely a done deal.
