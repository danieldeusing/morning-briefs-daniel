Today at 23:59 — after that, the location question is closed for good [1]

Notice 512867-2026 was pulled in full-text PDF for the third day running today, and again with the same result: "Notice identifier/version: bdecb664-f4eb-4d8d-818e-723fc5f17769 - 01." Version status 01 means there is neither an amendment notice nor a cancellation notice. Dispatch date 2026-07-23, 14:36 CEST, published in Official Journal S 141/2026 on 2026-07-24. A targeted search for a correction to this procurement number also turned up nothing today beyond mirrors of the original text. So the date that matters today stands: "Deadline for requesting additional information: 13/08/2026 23:59:59 (UTC+02:00)."[1]

What's at stake is exactly one question. The notice names two places of performance — Moltkestraße 80 in Karlsruhe and Jobstweg 7–9 in Stuttgart — and says not a word across eight pages about home office, remote share, or on-site requirements. For a bidder residing in Brazil, that single answer decides between bidding and passing. The question can only be asked through the project area of the portal ausschreibungen.landbw.de — the notice states explicitly that communication takes place exclusively there. An email to the contracting authority is not a valid channel, and after tonight, the valid channel closes.

The rest remains remarkably low-threshold. Two content-identical lots, one external person each, an estimated call-off of 160 person-days in 2026/27 and 240 each in the three following years, an eight-month term with up to three one-year extensions, up to 2030-06-30 at most. Eligibility criterion: one — a bank confirmation as a third-party declaration or proof of professional/business liability insurance. Named staff: "Not required." No estimated contract value is stated in the document.[1]

Binding period: The span during which a bid, once submitted, stays binding — the bidder can no longer withdraw or revise the price, and the contracting authority can award the contract at any point. Like a written offer to sell a car that you guarantee for two months: if the buyer takes it on the last day, your old price still applies, even if repair costs have since gone up. Here it's 59 days from 2026-08-25, so into late October. In practice that means: whoever calculates a bid on 2026-08-25 is calculating for a project start on 2026-11-01 at a rate they can't touch for two months.

And the sentence that already explained the urgency yesterday still stands unchanged. The contracting authority may request missing documents afterward, but doesn't have to — "applicants have no right to a supplementary request, submission, or clarification of documents." So the liability or bank proof belongs in the 2026-08-25 bid, not in the aftermath. Anyone who still needs to obtain such a policy now has eight working days left to do it.

Call for competition (two-stage procedure): In an open procedure, anyone may submit a bid directly; eligibility is checked together with the price. In a negotiated procedure with a call for competition, there's a preliminary round first: you apply only for permission to bid at all, and the contracting authority then invites a fixed number of applicants. Like a casting call with pre-selection — first the portfolio, then the stage, and only as many get on stage as there are advertised slots. At Dataport that's exactly nine: "Minimum number of candidates … 9 / Maximum number … 9." Anyone who doesn't clear the preliminary round never sees the pricing round. That's exactly why the revenue and reference thresholds there aren't a formality — they're the actual bottleneck.

Dataport does provide a mechanism — it just doesn't help much. The notice explicitly allows bidding consortiums and "privileged subcontractors" and adds their revenue figures to the applicant's (internal group revenue counts only 50%). Formally, twiceD could therefore be counted in. Practically, at an €8 million annual threshold, the addition means the partner supplies roughly 100% of it — twiceD's contribution would be the technical delivery, not the eligibility. On top of that, a deadline has already passed: questions on this procurement were only possible until 2026-08-10, 12:00 CEST. Anyone who still wants in is now negotiating not with Dataport but with a lead bidder — and has until 2026-08-24 to do it.[2]

At KARLAS the deadline has been extended — and it's even stated in the document. The version read today is itself an amendment notice: under section "10. Change" it names "Publisher correction" as the reason and, as the description, "extension of the tender and binding period, provision of further procurement documents as version 2." The new tender deadline is 2026-09-07, 23:59 CEST, with a 53-day binding period. In substance, it's about newly building a web-based database application for municipal wastewater disposal in Saxony, with award criteria of 60% quality and 40% price. That's by far the more accessible of the two new procedures — an open procedure with no preliminary round, with four weeks' lead time. Just with an eligibility section that the notice doesn't show.[3]
