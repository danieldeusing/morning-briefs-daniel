Nine listings a GmbH cannot sign [1]

Every listing on this board is scored on five components — stack, location, rate, intrigue, track — and not one of them asks the most basic commercial question: can this work be invoiced at all? Nine of today's 104 entries answer no. They are advertised as Arbeitnehmerüberlassung, and under German law that isn't a contracting model, it's an employment model.

Arbeitnehmerüberlassung (ANÜ / AÜG): Think of a car rental company. The car belongs to the rental firm, but for two weeks you drive it and decide where it goes. In temporary-agency work, the "car" is a person: the staffing agency (Verleiher) is the employer, the client (Entleiher) directs the day-to-day work. The law is explicit on both halves — the agency "bedürfen der Erlaubnis" (needs an official permit), and the model is permissible only "soweit zwischen dem Verleiher und dem Leiharbeitnehmer ein Arbeitsverhältnis besteht." The same paragraph caps a placement at 18 consecutive months with the same client. Practical consequence: you don't get this project as a company that writes invoices — you get it as an employee of the agency, on a German payroll with German social-security registration.[1]

Which means these projects don't arrive as company business. They arrive as an employment contract with a German staffing agency, on a German payroll, with German social-security registration.
