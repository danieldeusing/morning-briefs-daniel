Most clearly at Camunda. Yesterday it carried a band the employer itself had published — US$157,300 to 253,600 a year. Today it reads a researched estimate of €90–130k. The job page wasn't opened in between, so there's no reason to think Camunda removed the figure. It only disappeared here. Yesterday it was two employers, today it's one — without anything happening in the market.

Second, the two location fields. "100% remote, at least 3 days a month onsite" became "100% remote"; "75% remote, 1–2 days a week onsite" became "80% remote." In both cases, the onsite requirement was dissolved into a percentage — and percentages aren't bookable from Ribeirão Claro, appointments are. That exact distinction cost Page Contracting the score just yesterday. Anyone reading row 50 today no longer sees the condition that decides its feasibility.

And third, two bands moved down. The Hamburg cloud-and-IoT mandate loses €10 to 15 at both ends — in the very IoT niche this profile assigns a premium to. The Wuppertal entry slips from €90–115 to €85–110. Both bands were researched estimates yesterday and today alike, so both carry an asterisk; the change isn't a price movement, it's a different estimate of the same thing. That's the most honest sentence about this column: 100 of 109 bands are benchmarks, not offers, and a benchmark can shift by €15 overnight without a client doing anything at all.

What follows from this isn't panic, it's a reading. Touched rows are observation; untouched rows are memory — and memory gets rephrased every morning. For the top 15, where a pitch realistically comes from, that's manageable: they get re-checked often. For the middle field, the rule is: before an entry between rank 30 and 60 becomes the basis for a decline or a rate, its source page belongs open one more time.

Six Working Days — and the Same Skill Appears Twice in Today's Brief

That one, though, remains the most accessible this track has ever seen. The Oberfinanzdirektion Baden-Württemberg – Landeszentrum für Datenverarbeitung is tendering "TCK Test Automation Services" under 512867-2026, main CPV 72262000 (software development), supplemented by 72254000 (software testing) and 72266000 (software consultancy). Two content-identical lots; a bidder may bid on both but win at most one. A single eligibility criterion: a bank confirmation or proof of professional or general liability insurance. And explicitly in the eForm: "suitable for small and medium-sized enterprises: yes," plus the markers freelance, self-employed, startup.[1]

Person-day (PT): The currency of public IT contracts. A person-day is one workday for one person — not an hour, not a calendar day. Like a movie ticket: it entitles you to one visit, whether the film runs two hours or two and a half. The notice states 160 person-days for 2026/27 and 240 person-days each for the following three years. 240 person-days is roughly eleven months of one person working full time. Applying the band this board lists for senior Java in Germany — €90 to 115/h, or roughly €720 to 920 per eight-hour day — a full year's volume would come to about €173,000 to 221,000. That conversion isn't in the notice: it states neither a day rate nor an estimated contract value. It's a back-of-envelope calculation using this brief's own band figures, not a figure from the contracting authority.

The calendar is now the hard part. The tender deadline is Tuesday, 2026-08-25 at 11:00 CEST. Six full working days remain from today (Mon 17 through Mon 24), and the deadline expires in the morning of the seventh. The question deadline already passed on 08-13 at 23:59 — the one open question, namely whether the two places of performance, Karlsruhe and Stuttgart, imply an onsite requirement, can no longer be clarified through the official channel. The notice stays silent on it across every page. Anyone bidding, bids with that uncertainty; anyone who chooses not to bid at least has a documented reason.

How unusually low this bar sits shows in the comparison with the normal case. The Bayerisches Landesamt für Steuern tendered "IT services for Java software development" under 75482-2026 earlier this year and required a minimum average annual net revenue of €3.2 million over the past three years, plus a reference project of the same scale and at least four staffed specialist roles. That notice has been closed since 02-23 and only serves as a benchmark here — but as a benchmark it's stark: the normal case demands a company balance sheet, the OFD demands an insurance policy.[2]

And a coincidence that isn't one. The best addition on today's board is a quality/test engineer mandate at mund consulting: Kubernetes, Spring Boot, Angular, plus Cypress/Playwright and, explicitly, AI tools and agents for test automation — Frankfurt, 100% remote, term through 2029-12-31 plus option. The same skill the OFD is buying over eight months with up to three annual extensions through 2030-06-30. Test automation twice, both running to the end of the decade, one public and one private — the public route costs a bid with a deadline, the private one an email. Both pay in the same band.

SAP's Hiring Freeze Lets Exactly One Role Through — and It's on the Board

In early July, SAP tightened its rules for new hires and business travel to free up funds for its AI restructuring; Handelsblatt dates the report to 2026-07-02.[4] What falls through the net and what doesn't is described concretely by the follow-up reports: new hiring is now essentially limited to AI-focused positions, and internal travel without an AI angle is on hold.[5]

Exactly one SAP position appears on this board — and it's the exception that defines the freeze. "(Senior) SAP AI Engineer (f/m/d)," Garching near Munich, with alternative locations Walldorf, Berlin, Ratingen, and Hamburg. According to the board field, it was posted on 2026-07-15, just under two weeks after the tightening. The stack: Python with LangChain and LlamaIndex, SAP CAP on Java or Node, REST, SAP BTP, agentic AI frameworks, and RAG. Fit 63, rank 74 of 109. The deductions are quickly told — stack 22, because the Java share only enters via SAP CAP and Angular doesn't appear at all; location 12 for a hybrid model with 0–50% travel readiness. Intrigue 9 and track 10 keep the score up: enterprise AI in the product is exactly the subject area the employee track explicitly seeks.

Compensation is the sore point — and systematically so. This position carries a researched band of 85–120 k€/year* in the board; the lower bound sits below the employee track's 100 k€ threshold, the upper bound above it. SAP itself states no figure. After today's Camunda change, that's true for eight of the ten employee entries: only Grafana Labs publishes a band at all, and it does so in both of its entries. This track's mandatory criterion is therefore, for eight of ten entries, not verifiable but only plausible — a weakness no formula fixes, only a salary question in the first conversation.

The framework behind it is the same as with the rates. The caveat remains the same as always: the Kompass is a platform survey, not official statistics, and 100 of the 109 bands here are researched benchmarks, not client figures. Both numbers describe what a market price would be — not what actually gets paid.
