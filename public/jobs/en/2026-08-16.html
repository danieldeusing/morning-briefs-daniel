The highest confirmed hourly rate on this board is €95. At the same time, eight bands start at €100 or above — and every single one of them is an estimate. Put differently: the zone above €100 that the target profile is aimed at is populated on this board exclusively by its own benchmark table. No client has confirmed it. That doesn't devalue the estimates — they come from the same market observation the surveys reflect — but it does place them correctly: they are expectation, not observation.

Median vs. Average: Two different ways to find "the middle." The average adds everything up and divides by the count — a single top earner pulls it upward. The median lines everyone up by size and takes whoever stands in the middle; outliers don't matter to it. Like a school trip: if one kid brings €500 in pocket money and nine others bring €10 each, the average comes to €59, the median to €10. The median describes the typical case better. In the Freelancer-Kompass, the two sit close together (€95 median, €97 average) — so the distribution isn't skewed by a handful of high-price profiles.

Which brings us to the figure that ran in yesterday's brief. The 08-15 edition cited the Freelancer-Kompass 2026 average as €103, down from €104 the year before — quoted from a March write-up.[3] Today's look at freelancermap's own evaluation, dated 2026-07-02, gives for the IT disciplines: median €95, average €97, and for software and web development specifically €90.[1] Both figures come from the same Kompass, so they don't contradict each other — they measure at different scopes. The €103 is the average across all disciplines in the survey, SAP and management consulting included, and those profiles traditionally bill above developer level. The closer you zoom in on Daniel's actual discipline, the lower the figure gets: €103 across everything, €95 across IT, €90 across software and web development.

The second, independent anchor says the same thing over a different range. freelance.de's Freelancer-Studie 2026, published 2026-04-09 and based on roughly 3,300 responses, puts the IT average at €101.98/h.[2] Two separate platforms, two separate surveys, the same order of magnitude — the range of €90 to €102 is well supported. What neither survey measures is the deal itself. They ask freelancers what they charge; they don't ask clients what they paid. The four figures in the table above are the other end of that question — and they sit at €70, €75, €85, and €95.

What follows from this isn't a target correction, it's conversation prep. €100/h is defensible for a senior with thirteen years of Java/Spring and Angular — but not with the line "that's market price." Market middle for the discipline is €90. The €100 has to be carried by something else: the niche, bilingual client contact, architecture responsibility, or a term long enough to save the client search costs. Anyone arguing from the survey figure today is arguing against themselves.
