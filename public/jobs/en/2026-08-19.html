Average vs. median: the average adds up all the values and divides by the count — a single high outlier pulls it up. The median is the value exactly in the middle: half fall below, half above. Picture ten people in a bar, each earning €2,000 a month; a millionaire walks in and the average jumps past €90,000 — the median stays at €2,000. For hourly rates that means: €102 is the average, €90 is the median for software and web development. The twelve-euro gap isn't measurement noise — it's the fingerprint of a few very high-billing niches: SAP/ERP consulting sits at €120 in the same dataset.

And then there's a third number that isn't a survey at all. Every figure above is self-reported by providers — what freelancers ask for, not what ends up in a contract. This board contains a small but fundamentally different sample — eight entries where the client itself published a rate. Six of them are hourly: €70, €75, roughly €85 (posted as a €680 daily-rate cap), €87, €95, and €100. Average €85.33, median €86 — about €16 below the survey average and still below the €90 median for software and web development.

Why that's not a contradiction, but the negotiation itself. The surveys measure the supply side, these eight entries measure the demand side — and between them sits exactly the range that gets negotiated. The evidence base is small (six hourly rates), but it's the only non-self-reported figure this brief has. In practice that means: anyone walking into a negotiation with "the market average is €102" is arguing from a supplier survey. In half the cases, the other side of the table is working from a number between €70 and €100.

The Swiss threshold sits above the Swiss market

The employee track of this profile has a hard mandatory threshold for Switzerland: at least CHF 150,000 gross a year or CHF 140 per hour, and an offer below that gets discarded. Today, for the first time in weeks, a dated external source exists for what the Swiss market actually pays — and it calls that threshold into question.

The IT-Lohnstudie Schweiz 2026 from itjobs.ch, published on 2026-01-21, cites a range of CHF 115,000 to 145,000 a year for experienced developers focused on Java, C#, or frontend, with a comment that sums up the situation well: "Experienced developers are the foundation. Wages are stable."[4] Zurich serves as index 100; all other regions sit at 90 to 98 percent of it. In other words: the ceiling of the published senior band sits five thousand francs below the floor of the profile's own filter — and outside Zurich, up to ten percent lower still.

Wage band and regional index: a salary study rarely gives one figure but a range — typically the middle band where most salaries for a role fall; outliers above and below aren't captured. The regional index says how the same job scales by location: Zurich = 100 means an offer in Basel or Bern for the same work typically sits at 90 to 98 percent of the Zurich value. Like rent per square meter — the same apartment, a different price depending on the city. Setting a threshold above the band doesn't filter out the bad offers, it filters out the entire role.

What that means for this track. A Senior Software Engineer title won't regularly clear the CHF 150k mark in Switzerland — that takes lead, staff, or specialist roles, or a bank/pharma premium. Today's board looks exactly like that: ELCA informatique in Zurich (fit 81, Senior Java Engineer focused on agentic AI) shows CHF 140–190k/year* on the board — researched, not published by the employer, and the lower half of that band sits below the threshold itself. The entry was revalidated live today: the posting is still online, the apply button is active, the posting date remains 2026-06-09.
