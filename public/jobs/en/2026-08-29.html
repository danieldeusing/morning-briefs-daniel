A stock, not a snapshot

The board grows today from 163 to 168 entries — five additions, not a single removal. On its own, that's not an event: days with no removal are the rule on this board, not the exception. What's telling is the curve behind it. On Aug 1, there were 42 entries here; today, 168. In between lie 212 additions and 86 removals — and 43 of those 86, half of the month's removals, fell on a single day (Aug 11, when the stock collapsed from 128 to 92). Strip out that one cleanup day, and the board loses about one and a half entries per day against just under eight additions. Over the last seven days: 30 additions, 3 removals.

Stock list vs. snapshot — why it matters for how you use this: An entry doesn't disappear from this board when the project gets awarded. It disappears when the source stops delivering it on the next run — and project marketplaces often leave postings up for weeks. The board is therefore closer to a bulletin board where nobody takes down the old flyers than to a live overview. In practice that means three things. First: sorting by fit is still the right way to read the board — it tells you what would be a match. Second: it says nothing about whether the mandate is still open. 154 of 168 entries state no hiring date, and the same 154 are explicitly marked as carried forward. Third: the detail page itself belongs open before any outreach — that's not distrust of the data, it's the one piece of information the board can't structurally provide.

This is exactly where today's dataset beats yesterday's. Of the 154 carry-forwards, 153 anchor their latest verification to yesterday, Aug 28. On Aug 28 that was different: there, 135 of 143 carry-forwards pointed back to Aug 25 — three days and one intervening dataset away. The carry-forward chain has shrunk from three days to one — the same data quality this brief flagged on Aug 28 is fixed one run later. The one entry still pointing to Aug 25 is, of all things, JetBrains: the entry with the fourth-highest fit score on the whole board.

And the twelve real verifications sit exactly where decisions get made. Five went to the top of the freelance track (EnCo, iSAX, teclift, Keil, Allegis), five to the five additions, one each to ELCA and to a Personio role. That's the right distribution for a small sample — but it doesn't add a single point to the reliability of the remaining 156 entries.

Skills shortage and record unemployment at the same time — how does that work? A market overview opened today reports that unemployment in IT and engineering in Germany stands at its highest level since records began in 2011, and that the number of advertised computer-science positions fell 33% within a single year — the article itself cites no source, but the figures trace through a linked DW article to the VDI/IW Ingenieurmonitor (Aug 3, 2026) and are confirmed there.[2] The industry association Bitkom, meanwhile, counts 109,000 unfilled IT positions (down from 149,000 two years earlier) and states explicitly that the decline is due to the economic climate, not a larger supply of skilled workers.[3] Both can be true at once, because "the IT labor market" isn't one market, it's many. Companies are cutting generalist and legacy roles while simultaneously advertising narrowly scoped specialist roles that nobody applies for. The result looks like a supermarket where one shelf is empty and another stays full: in aggregate, "shortage" and "surplus" are both true — just never in the same aisle. For this board, that means: the average rate is falling while the niches (automotive safety, telematics, RAG integration on a Java stack) hold their bands. Those exact niches sit in today's top five spots. Evidence honesty: the Bitkom survey dates from Aug 7, 2025, making it a year old; the 2026 study report, based on 2025 data, describes an „IT-Arbeitsmarkt im Umbruch, geprägt von wirtschaftlicher Unsicherheit".[4] The two figures from the market overview are now primary-source confirmed via the VDI/IW Ingenieurmonitor (see [2]) — only the market overview's own citation chain stopped one hop too early.
