The cut at 72

The board drops today from 168 to 45 entries. 129 entries vanish under their old ID in one day — the previous record was Aug 11 with 43. For the rest of August, a removal was the exception: on 14 of 28 days, nothing left at all.

But the decisive number isn't 129 — it's 71, and then one more correction. 71 is the highest fit score among the removed entries. Lay both datasets side by side and the cut is clean: all 38 existing entries at fit 72 or above are preserved, and 129 of the 130 below it vanish under their old ID. The one survivor below the line is the OC Recruitment entry — freshly found live today and rescored from 62 to 58 in the process. But two of those 129 vanished IDs aren't genuine removals: the Angular/data-visualization role in Frankfurt (formerly fit 54, now 56) and the QUANTUM Python/FastAPI contract in Cologne (formerly fit 66, now 58) are still running unchanged, just under a new ID — an aggregation naming artifact, not a market event. Genuine removals are therefore 127, genuine new additions 4: the EBICS banking role in Frankfurt (72, right at the cutoff), plus 59, 57, and 44 below the line.

A relevance cut, not an expiration date — and why that's a difference that matters: An entry doesn't disappear from this board when the mandate is filled. It disappears when the next run stops carrying it forward. Until now, that made the board closer to a bulletin board that nobody ever clears the old flyers off than a live overview — it grew across August from 42 to 168, almost entirely by accumulation. Today someone cleared it out, but sorted by fit, not by age. Two things follow from that. First: the 127 genuinely removed mandates aren't "over," they're "too poor a fit" — they're still live on freelancermap, just not here. Second, and more important: the board still doesn't say whether an entry is still open. 43 of the 45 entries state no posting date at all. The detail page still has to be opened before every outreach message — a clean board doesn't change that.

Verification depth is improving proportionally, not in absolute terms. 11 entries were opened live today: EnCo and iSAX as re-checks, plus JetBrains and ELCA, plus seven newly found. Against 12 of 168 yesterday, that's two fewer in absolute terms — but 24% instead of 7% of the board. The flip side shows up in the data: of the 34 carried-forward entries, 29 carry the note "open since 2026-08-28," and only 3 point to yesterday. The carry-forward chain, which had shrunk to a single day yesterday, now reaches back two days again. Two entries carry no anchor at all: EFK (with its known anomalous "start 9/2024" date) and the JetBrains Kotlin role, which was only cross-checked via the board overview.

Track 3: TED is back — and the best tender can't be bid alone

After several runs with an empty filter, querying the TED search API directly today returns five tenders that pass the stack and geography filter. One of them opened in full text — and it happens to be the direct hit.

StMELF Bavaria is tendering "framework agreements for programming and consulting services for application development." Lot 1: estimated value €9,375,000 excluding VAT, framework ceiling €14,062,500, 75,000 work hours over four years (Jan 4, 2027 through Dec 31, 2030). The stack is spelled out verbatim in the notice: "Racy10, based on the Apache Wicket, Spring, and JPA frameworks, and Racy25, based on Angular, Spring, JPA."[3] That's Daniel's core stack, once in its old form and once in its new one. Dividing the estimated value by the hours works out to a budgeted rate of €125/h — a calculation this brief made from two figures in the notice, not a stated figure from the contracting authority, and well above Daniel's target of roughly €100/h.

Eligibility criterion "reference ≥ €1 million" — why skill alone isn't enough here: On a public tender, the authority checks before the bid whether a bidder is even allowed to play. That runs through hard, pre-published evidence: revenue, staff, insurance — and reference projects. A reference criterion works like a bouncer who doesn't ask whether you can dance, but whether you've already been in this exact club before. StMELF requires one to five comparable projects, each "with a contract value of at least €1 million," carried out "as prime contractor" and explicitly in the field "digitalization of state subsidy procedures," in the same Java stack.[3] twiceD has the stack but no million-euro reference in state subsidy procedures — and unlike a missing certificate, that can't be purchased after the fact, only brought to the table. That's why this reads ⚠️ partner required rather than "directly biddable." On top of that comes professional liability insurance as an explicit exclusion criterion, plus separate self-declarations of general and activity-specific annual revenue; their thresholds aren't in the notice itself but sit behind a link into the Bavarian bidder portal, and are therefore undocumented today.

What the cut did to the tracks

The employee track falls from 20 to 2, the intriguing track from 14 to 2. What's left: ELCA Zurich and the JetBrains Kotlin role as employee, JetBrains' forward-deployed engineer and Anthropic as intriguing. Two fit scores dropped without the listing itself changing ELCA now sits at 72 instead of 81, JetBrains' forward-deployed role at 72 instead of 82. That's not a downgrade of the offers, it's the end of a bookkeeping practice: both previously carried an "adjusted" correction on top of their own component total; today both are cleanly recalculated. ELCA comes out to stack 30 + loc 12 + rate 10 + intrigue 10 + track 10, which is simply 72. In practical terms: the gap between the best employee role and the mid-field was never as large as it looked for nine days. At JetBrains, the title changed too — same URL, same role, but "Staff" instead of "Principal" as of yesterday.

The most striking new addition is also the lowest-scored. Anthropic is looking for a Staff Software Engineer, Claude Code — exactly the frontier-AI-harness example that Daniel's Track-1 filter names by name as "very welcome." Compensation: $320,000 to $625,000 a year, printed, well above any target threshold for this profile. Location points 2, track points 2. The context doesn't make it easier: Claude Code hit $1 billion in annualized revenue within six months of launch and was above $2.5 billion in run-rate by February 2026.[8] The team is building the fastest-growing developer tool in the industry right now — and the one condition Daniel can't meet is the zip code.

The two remaining employee roles are worth a closer look at the companies behind them. ELCA's "Agentic Java Engineer" comes out of a purpose-built agentic-engineering practice that's co-hosting an industry event in Zurich with Swiss Fintech Innovations on Sep 8[7] — not an AI label slapped on a Java role, but an actual business line. The counterpoint remains the posting date: Jun 9, 2026, now 82 days old, still the oldest dated entry on the board. At JetBrains, meanwhile, a hiring wave is under way for "Forward Deployed Engineers" around the agentic development platform JetBrains Central[6] — which is why the same company appears twice on a 45-entry board.

ANÜ / temporary staffing — the contract type behind two entries: Two mandates on today's board (fit 76 and 73) are posted as "ANÜ." That's not a freelance engagement, it's Arbeitnehmerüberlassung (temporary employee leasing) under German law: for the project's duration you're employed by the staffing agency and "leased out" to the end client — like a rental car where the customer drives and the owner stays the rental company. The reason it exists is to prevent disguised self-employment: anyone embedded in a team and chain of command like an employee quickly stops counting as self-employed under German law, and the back-tax exposure hits both sides. For twiceD, that's decisive: ANÜ runs on an employment contract between the individual and the staffing agency, not a services contract with the GmbH. Anyone inquiring as managing director of their own company while resident in Brazil is better off clarifying this before the conversation — it determines whether the mandate even fits twiceD's legal structure at all.

The rate picture after the cut

The cut wiped out most of the documented basis for the rate analysis. Yesterday this board carried eight employer-printed hourly rates (€70, 75, 80, 85, 85, 87, 95, 100/h; mean €84.63) — a sample that hadn't moved in eight days. Today two remain: €75/h and €80/h. The six removed were the higher ones, not because employers pay less, but because the well-paying entries sat below the fit line. A sampling effect, not a price signal — reading "the mean fell from €84.63 to €77.50" as a market trend is reading the cut, not the market.

What's left is still uncomfortable, though. Both printed rates sit below the €90/h the Freelancer-Kompass 2026 names as the floor for software and web development, and well below the DACH median of €95/h across all IT freelancers.[1] Above that sits the researched layer: 26 of the 45 entries carry the €90–115/h* band, midpoint €102.50. Between what two employers actually printed here and the benchmark floor for this segment lies a gap of roughly 12 to 17% — the distance a negotiation has to close.
