For the first time, something falls off the board — at the series' lowest verification depth

For six observation windows this board grew without a single entry ever disappearing: 61, 71, 78, 87, 95, 106. Today it stands at 103. Three additions, six departures — the first net decline since this series has been tracked, and the first departures of any kind.

Before that turns into a market claim, the counter-check belongs right next to it. Tracing the six vanished entries back into yesterday's dataset, all six carry the identical sentence in the fit_reason field: "carried forward from 2026-09-05 dataset, not individually re-verified 2026-09-06." So they were already unverified carryovers yesterday — and today none of the six listings was opened to confirm it had actually ended. The dataset states no reason for any of the six departures. An expired listing and a listing simply not found again today look identical in this data.

The verification depth backs the cautious reading. 6 of the 103 entries were opened live today — 5.8%, down from 14.2% yesterday and the lowest figure of the series. 97 entries are carried over from the day before — 96 of them with that exact note, "carried forward … not individually re-verified 2026-09-07," one with the variant that omits the "not individually re-verified" tail. Of the six entries ≥ 80, three were revalidated (Frank Recruitment 85, iSAX 83, IBS-C 81); EnCo (83), teclift (82), and Keil (80) are stuck on verification dates from earlier days. On a day when 94% of the board went untouched, "six listings are gone" is mostly a statement about coverage.

"Carried forward" — why a disappearance here proves nothing: The board gets re-assembled every morning. Whatever today's collection run finds again stays in; everything else gets carried over from yesterday's dataset without anyone actually opening the listing — that's the "carried forward" note. Like copying a shopping list instead of opening the fridge: if the milk is still on it, that only means it was on there yesterday. If an item drops off the list today, the milk might be empty — or the person copying it just didn't look. In practice that means: a departure without a live check isn't news about the market, it's news about the collection run. The reverse holds too, for the 97 remaining carryovers — their existence today is equally unverified.

The median climbs — mechanically. From 68 to 71, while the average barely moves, 66.4 → 66.5. The reason is arithmetic, not quality: all six departures sat in the 62–63 band, below the midpoint. Cutting the weak entries from a list raises the median without a single remaining entry getting any better. Top score unchanged at 85, six entries ≥ 80, 31 ≥ 75.

Track 3: a line in the fine print that only one source knows [1]

The tender from the Free State of Bavaria, via the State Ministry of Food, Agriculture, Forestry and Tourism (StMELF), stands unchanged: five identical lots of application development in the in-house reference architectures "Racy10" (Java, Apache Wicket, Spring, JPA) and "Racy25" (Java/TypeScript, Angular, Spring, JPA). Today's fetch shows the notice active, with no corrigendum and no withdrawal; the deadline stays 09/24/2026, 10:00.[1]

One aggregator surfaces a requirement line that wasn't captured yesterday — and it could not be confirmed today. tendigo.de places between ISO 27001 and professional liability insurance, word for word: "Bereitschaft zur Sicherheitsüberprüfung (Ü1) nach BaySÜG bei sicherheitsrelevanten Aufgaben" — willingness to undergo a Ü1 security clearance under BaySÜG for security-relevant tasks. The counter-check comes back negative: three other mirrors of the same notice — tenderzen.de, vergabepilot.ai, and beschaffungsmarkt-office.de — list the eligibility criteria at comparable depth (ISO 27001, liability insurance, turnover, references) and contain no Ü1, BaySÜG, or security-clearance line at all. The primary documents on eVergabe Bayern are only retrievable behind a bidder login, and TED blocks direct access.[1][6] So the point stands here as unconfirmed — a question for the contracting authority, not a fact. And even if it is confirmed, the qualifier is important enough not to skim past: the requirement would be task-specific, not blanket — applying to security-relevant tasks, not to every line of code under the framework contract.

Ü1 under BaySÜG — why the question matters even though the line is unconfirmed: Bavaria's Security Clearance Act has tiers; Ü1 is the simplest — a basic background check of the person by the authority before deployment to security-sensitive positions. What would be asked here isn't even a completed check, just the willingness to undergo one. Like the clause in a lease that says "willing to provide a credit report": you don't need to have it on hand, but you need to be able to produce it if asked. Why the question belongs here anyway: every other gap in this tender — certificate, revenue proof, references, extra headcount — a partner can fill. A background check on a person, a partner cannot supply: it would be tied to the exact individual being deployed. And clearances of this kind by German authorities effectively presuppose a traceable domestic history, which from Ribeirão Claro would be at minimum an open question. That is exactly why the point is worth raising — but as a query to the contracting authority, not as a settled hurdle: today it appears on a single aggregator page and on no other.
