Project Tenders (TED EU) — Seven Hits, Zero Readable Thresholds

After several days on which this track reported nothing but failures, today's pull from the TED search API finally delivered substance: 226 notices carrying an IT classification (CPV 72*) with place of performance Germany, published since 09/01/2026.[1] Seven of them are stack-relevant and carry a deadline that clears this brief's five-business-day rule. They're listed below.

The most important find needs no interpretation. DVZ Mecklenburg-Vorpommern — the state's central IT service provider — is tendering a framework agreement whose title literally reads "framework agreement for Java development." That's not "IT services that might involve Java somewhere" — Java is the subject matter itself. In this track's history to date, it's the most precise stack match yet.

Framework agreement: The authority isn't awarding a single project — it's awarding the right to be called on. It's looking for one or more suppliers it can draw individual orders from over the years, without re-tendering each time. Like a tradesperson who's on a property manager's list: the list guarantees not a single job, but without it, you never get called at all. For a two-person setup that cuts both ways — access lasts a long time, but the entry bar is sized against the whole possible draw-down volume, not against the first order.

Minimum annual revenue (the 2× rule): Public contracting authorities want to see that a bidder won't choke on the contract — the usual proof is an annual revenue of roughly double the contract value. Like a bank that wants to see income for a loan clearly above the installment. That's exactly where a solo setup fails first: not on the technology, but on a number in the balance sheet. The partner in a bidding consortium mainly supplies that number.
