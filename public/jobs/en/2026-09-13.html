What's required (verbatim from the notice): at least two suitable reference contracts of ≥ 12 months each within the last three years · a self-declaration on average headcount and number of managers over the last three years · professional liability insurance with a "minimum coverage sum of €2,000,000.00 per claim," or a self-declaration to obtain it before award · company profile (max. 3 pages) · bank reference (≤ 3 months old) · most recently published annual financial statement · a statement of total and segment revenue · commercial register extract (≤ 6 months old). Bid language: German.[2] Partner provides: the second twelve-month reference in Java development, if the GmbH can't document two of them on its own — as a bidding consortium with a Java systems house that supplies the reference track record. No partner needed for: revenue (no threshold required), headcount (no floor), certificates (none required), language (DE), insurance (the €2 million sits below this profile's partner threshold and can be deferred via self-declaration).

Why the absence of numbers is the news here. The standard killers for a two-person setup are a minimum annual revenue (often double the contract value), a required FTE count, and ISO 27001 or BSI C5 certification. None of those three requirements appears in this notice. The contracting authority wants to see revenue and headcount only as a declaration, with no threshold attached. A self-declaration of an average of two employees is therefore admissible — it's simply visible, and can be weighted qualitatively in the eligibility review. A risk, but not a disqualifying criterion.

EVB-IT service contract: the standard template contract German public authorities use for IT services — comparable to the pre-printed terms of a lease, except the federal government negotiates it once, centrally, rather than every authority doing its own. For a bidder, that means two things: the contract terms are known in advance and non-negotiable, and they're not tailored to a two-person setup — liability, cooperation duties, and contractual penalties are worded identically for contractors of any size. That's why the liability-insurance line in the eligibility criteria isn't a formality — it's the price of admission.

The open question isn't capacity, it's paperwork. "Two comparable reference contracts of at least twelve months each within the last three years" is the one requirement a solo setup could genuinely miss — not because the work doesn't exist, but because it has to be documentable under the GmbH and in the required shape. This brief can't answer that; it's the one point Daniel has to settle himself before Oct 5. If the answer comes back positive, the verdict is ✅ direct and twiceD is biddable on its own. There's a second caveat alongside it: the entire criteria text comes from a single source, the TED search API — the detail page wouldn't open today (HTTP 405), and none of the alternative sources checked (Vergabemarktplatz M-V, the usual tender portals) showed the criteria text openly. Before bidding or lining up a partner on this basis, Daniel should request the official tender documents directly from DVZ. A second unknown remains alongside it: the notice states no estimated contract value, so the call-off volume over 24 months can't be assessed — a framework contract that in practice demands three full-time developers in parallel wouldn't be deliverable even with eligibility met.

The structural problem sits one level deeper. The IT Salary Study 2026 gives experienced software engineers in Switzerland a "salary range: CHF 115,000 – 145,000."[6] This profile's employee threshold sits at CHF 150k/year — above the entire market band. As long as that holds, an average-paid Swiss senior role never clears the track's mandatory criterion; only the upside outliers make it through. The only printed CH figure on the board confirms exactly that: Bank Julius Bär spans CHF 121,500 to 206,500 — the floor sits below the threshold, the ceiling well above it.

Salary range vs. threshold: a published range describes what the market typically pays — from a junior-ish senior up to an in-demand specialist. A threshold like "from CHF 150k" isn't a market observation at all, it's a decision rule: below it, the move isn't worth making. When the rule sits above the range, that's not a math error, it's a deliberate narrowing to the top end. In practice that means: in Switzerland, it's not the roles that are scarce, it's the offer that actually clears the threshold.

In Germany it's the other way around. The top of the employee track remains Camunda and Personio, both at Fit 73 — and Personio is the only employer on the entire board printing an annual figure without a research asterisk: €178,750 for a Staff AI Engineer in Munich, albeit hybrid with two office days a week.[1] The two Camunda roles are the only high-scoring employee entries with full remote clearance for Germany.
