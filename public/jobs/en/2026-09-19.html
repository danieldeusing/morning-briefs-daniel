The context behind it is confirmed: Anthropic opened its sixth European office in Milan in May 2026 and plans to triple its international headcount; the Zurich research office has been running since late 2024.[3] A German-speaking customer-facing role in Munich is exactly what comes next from a plan like that — not the research, but the people who get the model running at the customer's site.

The shortage is halving, the rates aren't moving [4][5][6]

Perhaps the most important number this week isn't on the board. Bitkom counts around 79,000 unfilled IT positions in Germany for 2026 — down from 109,000 in 2025 and 149,000 in 2023.[4] That's nearly a halving in three years, and it's the economic foundation under every rate negotiation of recent years: we're scarce, so we cost more.

"Unfilled positions" — what's actually being counted: This number doesn't measure how many developers are missing, but how many positions companies report as open in a survey. Like the count of empty chairs in a restaurant: it falls when more guests arrive — but just as easily when the restaurant clears tables away. Both movements look identical in the statistic. A drop from 149,000 to 79,000 could mean more people got hired — or that companies are posting fewer openings because budgets are cut and teams are planned smaller. For the market price, the result is the same either way: fewer open positions mean less competition for any single candidate.

That the second reading applies is confirmed today. SAP — the largest software employer in the German market — suspended most non-AI hiring and most business travel in early July 2026; per the reporting, exceptions exist only for AI-related hiring and travel, and an employee confirmed in August 2026 that the rule still applies. The reasoning in the memo: spending discipline amid rising AI costs.[6] A company cutting travel to pay for tokens isn't posting extra Java roles.

And that's why the rate bands are where they are. The 2026 Freelancer Compass puts the median for software and web development at €90/h, €95/h across all IT disciplines, €120/h for SAP/ERP.[5] The five postings that actually print a figure on the board today come in at 95 · 85 · 80 · 80 · 75 €/h — median €80/h, ten euros below the discipline median.[1] Yesterday's conclusion doesn't soften with the Bitkom number, it hardens: the premium no longer comes from scarcity, only from differentiation. On the board, the four automotive and eight IoT rows carry bands above €100 — while 57 interchangeable Java/Spring/Angular mandates all carry the same €90–115/h* band.
