What a fit score is — and isn't: it's not a measurement, it's a point award under fixed rules: stack overlap up to 45, remote suitability up to 20, rate up to 15, intrigue up to 10, track fit up to 10. Like an exam graded by two different graders — the work stays the same, the points don't. A score is therefore valid for "which line do I read first," not for "is the market worse today than yesterday."

The direction of the correction isn't random, though — it's stated verbatim in the reasoning fields. Today's run withholds partial credit that yesterday's run still gave: "Python/FastAPI has no dedicated bucket in the locked algorithm" (QUANTUM: stack 12 → 0) and "React not Angular, no frontend-bucket credit." The Frank Recruitment automotive mandate, too — yesterday's top line of the board at Fit 85 — now sits at 66 via the mirror domain freelancermap.com: stack 38 → 26, because the React-credited-toward-Angular allowance and the partial automotive credit both drop out, rate 12 → 8, because a researched band gives exactly 8 points under the rulebook.

My take: the stricter numbers are the correct ones. Today's run scores the way the point scheme actually describes it; yesterday's run was generous in its interpretation. In practice, that means: the values above 80 printed in recent issues were too high, not today's too low — and that's the more comfortable direction of error, because an inflated score tempts a pitch the stack can't actually back up.

Exception Callista is the only outlier that moved up — and that's exactly the case where I trust the new number less. The St. Gallen mandate jumps from 58 to 72 because the location points rise from 5 to 15. Yesterday's entry carried the more specific reasoning: remote work is allowed only within Switzerland, plus 20 % on-site — not workable from Brazil. The same entry noted a start date of 2024-07-01, suggesting a rolling or stale listing. Before any contact, ask about both: the remote geography and whether the mandate is even still open.

Rates hold. Revenue doesn't. [2][3][4][5]

The Freelancer-Kompass 2026 — freelancermap's annual survey of its own users — delivers two numbers this year that seem to contradict each other. First: the median requested hourly rate in the DACH region sits at €95, and pricing sentiment is firm — 28 % plan to raise it in 2026, only around one in ten plan to lower it. Second: "17 percent less revenue in one year," per the survey the steepest income decline since the survey began.

Rate and utilization are two different levers: the hourly rate is the price tag, annual revenue is the price tag times the hours sold. Like a taxi whose per-kilometer fare stays unchanged while fewer people get in — the fare board says nothing about the shift. If revenue falls 17 % while rates hold, idle time is the problem, not the price list. The practical consequence is uncomfortable but clear: a discount doesn't fix this — it further damages the one lever that's still working. What helps is longer contract terms and renewals. That's exactly why the teclift line in the board (12 months) is worth more than its 68 fit points suggest — provided the mandate is even still running: the listing itself dates the start to 10-01-2024, not 2026.

The day-rate site doesn't contradict this, it refines it. For September 2026 it lists Spring Boot mid-level at 620–820 €/day, senior microservices at 880–1,150 €, and enterprise architect at 1,100–1,450 € — over an eight-hour day, that's roughly €110–181/h for the upper two tiers. The upper band exists, it's just not the band that shows up in broker listings: this board's 33 lines, with their researched median of €102.50/h, sit cleanly at the floor of the senior-microservices tier. And with a 5–12 % premium for Frankfurt, Munich, and Stuttgart, it becomes clear why the three top lines sit exactly there.

The automotive niche's market sentiment includes the number of the month: Volkswagen's supervisory board approved roughly 50,000 additional jobs for cuts on 2026-09-04; total planned cuts now stand at about 100,000 out of more than 650,000 employees worldwide. For this profile, that's a sentiment signal, not a demand signal — the cuts hit manufacturing and administration, while software headcount at OEMs typically runs on a separate track. It doesn't show up in the board either: two automotive lines, one of them 100 % on-site in Berlin. The honest read is: the niche is small today, not demonstrably shrinking.

The real market story today, though, isn't in the board at all — it's in the Freelancer-Kompass twenty twenty-six. Rates are holding: the median rate freelancers request across the German-speaking region sits at ninety-five euros an hour, and twenty-eight percent plan to raise it this year, with only around one in ten planning to lower it. And yet, at the same time, project revenue is down seventeen percent from last year — the steepest drop since this survey began.

Those two numbers don't actually contradict each other — they're measuring two different things. The hourly rate is the price tag. Annual revenue is the price tag multiplied by the hours actually sold. Think of a taxi whose per-mile fare never changes, even as fewer people climb in — the fare card tells you nothing about how slow the shift was. If revenue drops while rates hold steady, the problem is idle time, not the price list. And a discount doesn't fix that — it just breaks the one lever that's still working. What actually helps is longer contracts and renewals. Which is exactly why the twelve-month listing sitting in today's board is worth more than its sixty-eight fit points suggest.
