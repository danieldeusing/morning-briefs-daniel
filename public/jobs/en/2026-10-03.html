Cursor Now Belongs to SpaceX [4][5]

SpaceX closed its acquisition of Anysphere, the company behind the AI code editor Cursor, on 2026-08-14. The contract was signed on June 16, the price was $60 billion, paid entirely in SpaceX stock. Cursor is now part of the SpaceXAI unit.[4][5] The news is seven weeks old. It's here because Cursor sits on the employee track's AI-harness watchlist. The product side of the story belongs in the ai-dev brief.

All-stock deal: The buyer doesn't pay with cash but with its own shares. Like buying a house by paying with shares in your own company instead of a wire transfer. The Cursor shareholders become SpaceX shareholders — per the stock-exchange filing, around 389 million SpaceX Class A shares.[4]

What that means for the watchlist: Cursor was an independent scale-up that rose from a $29.3 billion valuation in late 2025 to $50 billion in July 2026, with annual recurring revenue doubling to around $2 billion over the same stretch.[11] Now it's a unit inside a US aerospace-and-AI conglomerate. Whether hiring practices, remote rules, or pay bands have changed, none of the sources say. My read: Cursor stays on the list, but applying first needs a fresh look at the current careers page. No Cursor role sits in the board today.

Switzerland: Record AI Jobs, Pressure on Entry-Level Positions [6][7][8]

According to PwC's AI Jobs Barometer, the number of AI-related job postings in Switzerland rose by around 9,000 in 2025 to a record of roughly 25,000. That's still only 1.8 % of all advertised positions.[6] The figure is from June 2026, so it's context, not weekly news. A separate study by jobs.ch, jobup.ch, and JobScout24 covering 7.3 million listings shows the other side: the share of entry-level positions in 2025 was 32 % below the 2019–2022 average.[7] The two figures come from different datasets and can't be combined.

Together they add up to a clear picture: in Switzerland, demand for AI experience is growing while entry-level jobs shrink. That favors senior profiles with applied AI practice — exactly the profile the employee track is looking for. The Adecco Swiss Job Index fits that: in the second quarter of 2026, open positions fell 2.4 % versus the prior quarter, IT professions rose against the trend.[8] The third-quarter index hasn't been published yet.

The caveat sits in the board: today there isn't a single Swiss employee line, and the two Swiss freelance mandates pay below band and require on-site presence. The record is real, but it hasn't yet reached remote-capable senior roles paying over CHF 150 k.
