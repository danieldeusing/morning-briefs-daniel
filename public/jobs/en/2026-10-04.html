Stack match: unclear. Subject per the portal: software development, IT security, software testing, data protection — no stack named, details behind a registration.[4] I'm not carrying over the contract value the portal displays: it sits in the double-digit billions and is obviously a misreading for an HR framework contract.[9] A partner would likely need to supply: presumably staffing breadth across four fields — a solo shop can't commit to staffing across multiple profiles alone. A realistic role for twiceD: subcontractor for Java development under a large staffing provider. Only worth it if the documents show a Java/Angular focus.
ISO 27001 based on IT-Grundschutz: ISO 27001 is a certificate confirming that a company manages its information security in a planned way — with rules, responsible people, and annual audits. The "IT-Grundschutz" variant follows the BSI's catalog of building blocks. Like a safety-inspection seal for how a company handles data. For a two-person shop, getting there usually takes many months and an auditing body — not achievable by October 20.

An important caveat on the gap analysis: the quoted sentence doesn't clearly say who must be certified. It's also possible the contracting authority runs its own environment under ISO 27001 and contractors just work within it. In that case twiceD could bid alone. Only a bidder question or a look at the tender documents can clarify that.

My take: this is the best tender in weeks, and it has two paths. Path B (more likely): the tender cites EU Regulation No. 885/2006, which requires ISO 27001 certification for the paying agency's own information systems — that points to the contracting authority's own operations, not the bidder. Then bid directly. Path A: if the bidder itself must be certified after all, twiceD counts as a subcontractor or in a bidding consortium with an ISO-27001-certified IT provider that already works for government clients; twiceD supplies the Racy25 expertise, the partner supplies the seal. Request the documents Monday and ask the bidder question — question deadlines usually fall well before the submission deadline. A TED notice number (632025-2026) shows up in the TED PDF export, but the match to this tender is only supported by metadata.[3]

Bidding consortium vs. subcontractor: In a bidding consortium, two companies bid together and are jointly liable — like two tradespeople who sign a contract together. As a subcontractor, you work for the bidder, who signs alone — like the electrician the general contractor brings in. For twiceD, the subcontractor path is simpler: less liability, fewer proofs required, but also no seat of its own in the framework agreement.

It's Sunday, October fourth. Two things matter today.

The first is a public tender out of Bavaria. The State Leadership Academy for Food, Agriculture, and Forestry in Landshut is hiring developers through a framework agreement. One of the two reference architectures is Java with Spring and JPA, plus Angular and TypeScript — almost exactly the twiceD stack. The submission deadline is Tuesday, October twentieth.

There's a hurdle in front of it, though. The tender calls for certification under ISO twenty-seven thousand one, based on the BSI's IT-Grundschutz framework. twiceD doesn't have that certification, and there's no way to get it before the deadline. But the wording doesn't clearly say who needs to be certified — it might only cover the contracting authority's own environment. So Monday's job is to request the documents and ask a bidder question. If the bidder itself has to be certified, this only works with a partner who already holds that seal, with twiceD coming in as a subcontractor for the Java and Angular work.

The second story is about the jobs board. Yesterday it had shrunk to twenty-two entries. Today it's back up to thirty-four. All twenty-two from yesterday are still there with the same score, plus twelve new ones. So the count mostly tracks the search, not the market.

At the top, One Day Ahead is still in front — Java and Angular, fully remote, fit score seventy-seven. New in second place is iSAX Consulting, Java and Angular with cloud work, ninety percent remote, fit score seventy-five. My suggestion: reach out to both of those first on Monday.

One warning. The DOS Software-Systeme mandate runs through employee leasing, which means formal employment with the broker, not invoicing through the GmbH. Worth asking upfront whether a different contract is possible.
