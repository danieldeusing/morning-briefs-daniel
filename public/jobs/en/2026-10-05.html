The Price Holds, the Volume Doesn't [1][3][4]

Two studies, one picture. The Freelancer-Kompass survey polled 5,412 freelancers between November 2025 and February 2026. Result: the hourly rate is stuck at 103 €, down from 104 € a year earlier — no increase for the first time since the survey began. Monthly income fell on average from 8,432 to 6,653 €, about 21 % less.[3] Respondents don't name price as the main reason, but rather a lack of incoming work (61 %) and strong competition (31 %).

The Bitkom study shows the other side. The IT-specialist gap has shrunk from 149,000 (2023) through 109,000 (2025) to 79,000 — almost cut in half.[4] Even so, 97 % of companies with IT openings say filling them is hard, and 76 % expect the shortage to worsen again.[4]

Skills gap: The number of IT jobs companies want to fill but can't. Like empty seats on a bus with no passenger to take them. The smaller the gap, the less a client has to bend to meet a freelancer's price. A gap of 79,000 is still large — but no longer large enough to pull prices up.

What the board says about it: the 28 researched euro bands average exactly 100 €/h — almost identical to the Kompass average.[1][3] Every rate a listing prints itself sits below that: 65, 75, 60–90 €/h. Important: the Kompass average covers all respondents, not just senior Java profiles. A senior with Java plus Angular usually lands closer to the upper end.

My take: today isn't a market where you open at 120 €/h and wait for a counteroffer. Quote 100–110 €/h for the top mandates and argue with remote share, the BR time zone as edge-hours coverage, and contract length — not with scarcity. Save 120 €/h for the niche lines (automotive, KNX) or for when the client names a number first.
