Fewer Contracts, Same Price [1][3]

The numbers from the Freelancer Compass 2026: IT freelancers earn a median of 95 €/h and around 6,000 € a month. SAP and ERP specialists sit at the top with 120 €/h, software and web developers at the bottom with 90 €/h.[3] More than half, 56 %, rate the order situation as worse than last year.[3] Yesterday's edition cited 103 €/h from the same study — but that's the Compass's average across all freelance professions, not IT-specific; IT freelancers' median simply sits below that cross-industry average.

Median: Line up every hourly rate from lowest to highest and take the one in the middle. Half earn more, half earn less. For a price negotiation, the median is more honest than the average, because a few SAP consultants at 150 €/h can't shift it.

What the board says about it: the 27 researched euro bands have a median of the midpoints of 97.5 €/h — between the software median (90) and the overall median (95), just above both.[1][3] Every rate a listing prints itself sits lower: 65, 75, 60–90 €/h. A senior profile with Java plus Angular plus cloud should sit above the software median; but the Compass doesn't break out seniority levels, so that's an estimate, not a measurement.

My take, unchanged: quote 100–110 €/h for the top mandates and argue with remote share, the BR time zone as edge-hours coverage, and contract length. When more than half of freelancers rate the order situation as worse, a fast, clear contact wins more than a high opening price. Save 120 €/h for niche lines (automotive, KNX) or for when the client names a number first.
