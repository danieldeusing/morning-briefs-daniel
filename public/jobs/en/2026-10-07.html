Cooler Market, Split Prices [1][2]

The new number: across the entire freelance market, the average hourly rate in 2026 fell for the first time, from 104 to 103 €. That's a small move, but it's the first one downward.[2] IT sits at a median of 95 €/h, which is even below non-IT freelancers (100 €). Per month, IT freelancers still earn 6,000 €, 17 % more — so they're better utilized, not more expensive.[2]

The mood: 56 % of IT freelancers see a worse order situation than last year, 38 % call their situation bad or very bad. On price, they're staying stubborn: 28 % want to raise rates, 62 % plan no change, only 10 % expect a cut.[2] The survey ran from November 2025 to February 2026 with 5,412 respondents — so it reflects the winter, not the fall.[2]

The split: SAP/ERP rises from 115 to 120 €. Software and web development sits at the bottom with 90 €.[2] That's Daniel's own lane — and 10 € below twiceD's target of 100 €/h.

Specialization premium: a general practitioner and a heart surgeon are both doctors, but only one of them is on every corner. Say "web developer" and you're in the long line. Have a specialty few others can do, and you set the price yourself. That's exactly what the Compass measures: a 30 € gap between the broad and the narrow IT lane.

My take: 100 €/h isn't a moonshot price, but it needs an argument. The 31 researched board bands average 97.5 €/h — the market for senior Java/Angular remote supports the 100 €.[1] The Compass doesn't break out seniority levels or stacks within "software/web," so the 90 € is a blended value of junior PHP and senior Spring. Concretely:

  • Opening at 100–110 €/h for the top mandates (the 77, 75, and 69 scores), argued with Java/Spring enterprise, cloud, and 13 years — not with "fullstack web."
  • Floor at 90 €/h only in exchange for a term ≥ 6 months and 100 % remote.
  • 110 €+ only in the niche (KNX, automotive) or when the client names a number first.

For context, with a caveat: the press release cites Bitkom on roughly 109,000 missing IT specialists — but that's the 2025 figure; Bitkom's own September 2026 study puts the current number at roughly 79,000.[2] Either way, it fits the picture: demand hasn't disappeared, budgets are just more cautious.
