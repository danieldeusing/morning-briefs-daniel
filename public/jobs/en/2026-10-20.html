It's Tuesday, October twentieth, and the most interesting news today doesn't come from the freelance board but from public tenders. On TED, nine German IT tenders are open, four of them a fit for the profile.

The best one is small: the Free State of Bavaria is looking for software development amounting to one hundred person-days per year, with three extension years. The award goes solely to the lowest price, and the notice sets no turnover threshold. Bids are due by Monday, November sixteenth, at eleven a.m. German time. What's still open is which technology is wanted and whether you have to be on site in Munich. My advice: download the documents today and check exactly that.

The second candidate is a framework agreement from FIZ Karlsruhe for scientific-technical software development, deadline November eighteenth. For the tenders from Baden-Württemberg and Thuringia, twiceD probably needs a partner.

On the board, only one entry is new: Montash is looking for a full-stack developer for Java and React, remote, through the end of the year. That's worth a short pitch at one hundred euros an hour. According to freelancermap, the median for software development is ninety euros an hour.

Tomorrow we'll see whether the Bavarian documents require an on-site presence and whether the board brings new Java projects.
