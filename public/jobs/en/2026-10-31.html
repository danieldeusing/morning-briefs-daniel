New: e-mobility with OCPP — good niche, questionable budget [1][5]

Computer Futures (SThree) is looking for a Senior Java Backend Developer for an e-mobility product. The core: Java/Spring Boot microservices in an AWS cloud, plus RabbitMQ, MariaDB, Redis and GitLab CI. Kubernetes, OCPP and experience with charging infrastructure are "an advantage".[5] The work is remote, with one to four on-site appointments per year within the EU. The project language is English.[5]

OCPP: The language a charging station uses to talk to its server. The station reports "car plugged in, 22 kW, card X"; the server replies "start charging" or "cancel". If you know OCPP, you already know the same way of thinking as with Z-Wave or MQTT: devices send messages, a backend decides.

Why it fits: This is protocol integration between devices and the cloud — close to the IoT niche of this profile. But only Java and Spring Boot are required; OCPP is a plus. That is why the listing sits at fit 66 on the board, not higher.[1]

Two catches:

  • Budget: The page speaks of a "market-standard nearshore range" and hides the day rate.[5] Nearshore usually means: developers from countries with lower rates. The board band of €90–115/h* is an estimate and probably too high for this project.
  • Location: "Remote (EU / Nearshore)" — Brazil is not in the EU.[5] Before the pitch, clarify whether working from Brazil through a German GmbH is allowed.

My verdict: pitch it, leading with protocol experience close to OCPP (Z-Wave, MQTT, M-Bus). Leave the rate open until the day rate is known. If it is clearly under €90/h, decline — you don't sell a niche at the nearshore price.
