The Two Verbs of Advancing

2 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked against verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br, and conjugator.reverso.net

haben or sein — in football vocabulary too: German perfect-tense forms need an auxiliary verb, and which one a verb takes isn't a choice — it's a fixed property of the verb. A club that qualifies for the next round is actively doing something to an object (itself) — that's why it takes haben: er hat sich qualifiziert. A club that climbs the table, on the other hand, undergoes a pure change of state, with no object — that's why it takes sein: er ist aufgestiegen. Portuguese and Spanish don't have this problem: there, ter/haber build every compound tense without exception, no matter what the verb means.

DE: strong verb of the ablaut series ei–ie–ie (like bleiben, schreiben); as a verb of movement/change of state, it takes sein in the perfect. PT subir: the stressed forms raise the stem vowel u to o (sobe, but unstressed subimos) — this affects the present tense and the entire subjunctive. From Matchday to the Round of 16

Two words for one matchday Brazil and Spanish happen not to meet here — Spanish, like European Portuguese, adopts jornada, while Brazilian Portuguese keeps its own word rodada (literally "round"). Anyone reading Brazilian sports sites sees only rodada — „pela 21ª rodada do Campeonato Brasileiro", as today's preview report puts it. ncnews.com.br

One word, two sports' worth of meaning The verb behind "a escalação" — escalar — looks identical in Spanish and means something completely different there. Priberam lists Portuguese escalar under point 6 as „indicar tarefas, funções ou horários para determinadas pessoas" — exactly what a coach does when naming the starting lineup: escalar o time. Spanish escalar, on the other hand, means "to climb" — SpanishDict translates „La canción no ha dejado de escalar puestos en la lista de éxitos" as "the song has kept climbing up the charts." So in Spanish a team could "escalar in the table," but a lineup can never "escalar" — for that you need alinear. Same letters, two sports' worth of meaning. priberam.org · spanishdict.com

the round of 16

Vasco da Gama qualified for the round of 16 of the Copa Sul-Americana on Wednesday.

Red Bull Bragantino also advanced to the round of 16 with a 1-0 win over Sporting Cristal.

as oitavas de final

O Vasco da Gama se classificou para as oitavas de final da Sul-Americana na quarta-feira.

O Red Bull Bragantino também avançou às oitavas com um 1 a 0 sobre o Sporting Cristal.

los octavos de final

Vasco da Gama se clasificó a los octavos de final de la Copa Sudamericana el miércoles.

Red Bull Bragantino también avanzó a los octavos con un 1-0 sobre Sporting Cristal.

An eighth stays an eighth Unlike the matchday word, DE, PT, and ES agree here: all three count literally "in eighths" — 16 teams, 8 duels. Only the grammatical gender switches: das Achtelfinale is neuter singular, as oitavas and los octavos are pure plural forms that never appear in the singular. English breaks with the eighths logic entirely and instead counts the remaining teams: round of 16.

It's Thursday, July thirtieth. Today's rotation theme is soccer and sports, and the hook is quite literally tonight's game: at seven thirty this evening local time, Corinthians hosts Athletico-PR at the Neo Química Arena in São Paulo — matchday twenty-one of Brazil's Série A.

Going into the match, the table looks like this: Palmeiras lead with forty-seven points, eight ahead of second-place Flamengo on thirty-nine. Athletico-PR sit third with thirty-six points, while Corinthians are all the way down in ninth with twenty-eight — four points behind fifth-place Bahia, which marks the so-called G5 zone, the range that earns a spot in next season's Copa Libertadores.

Wednesday night also brought good news for two Brazilian clubs in the continental Copa Sul-Americana. Vasco da Gama won one-nil at home in São Januário against Independiente Medellín, making it three-two on aggregate after a two-two first leg — their next round-of-16 opponent is Olimpia of Paraguay. And Red Bull Bragantino also won one-nil, in Bragança Paulista against Sporting Cristal, with Pedro Henrique scoring in the fifty-eighth minute. Both clubs advanced with the same narrow scoreline.

That gives us today's vocabulary, built around two verbs. To qualify — classificar-se in Portuguese, clasificarse in Spanish — both swap a c for a qu before the vowel e, which is why it's eu me classifiquei and yo me clasifiqué. And aufsteigen, "to climb" or "to be promoted," a strong German verb that takes the auxiliary sein because it describes a pure change of state, not an action done to an object.

But today's most interesting trap isn't a new word at all — it's a familiar one: escalar. In Portuguese, escalar means to name the starting lineup — the coach escala the team. In Spanish, that very same word means to climb, and figuratively, to climb up a league table too. Two neighboring languages, one identical word, two completely different sporting meanings.

Then there's a bit of Brazilian football slang: mata-mata, literally "kill-kill," the word for the knockout format in a cup competition — win or go home, there's no second leg to save you. And a small difference between the Portuguese-speaking countries: Brazil says rodada for a matchday, Portugal says jornada, just like Spanish does.

Three words from earlier issues get refreshed today: table leader from July twenty-third, now with new numbers; and the chase and razor-thin from July sixteenth, both fitting for tonight's narrow one-nil results.
