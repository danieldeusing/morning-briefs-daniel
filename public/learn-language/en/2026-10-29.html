Vocabulary for the Title Race: virar o jogo, empatar, a rodada

Thursday, October 29 — rotating theme Soccer & Sports, part three. The occasion is Wednesday night in the Brasileirão: Fluminense turn the game around against Internacional with a stoppage-time goal, and Santos and Palmeiras draw 0-0. In the ESPN table (retrieved on October twenty-ninth; the page shows no date), Flamengo and Palmeiras now sit level on 70 points each at the top. So we learn the words for a tight title race: to turn a game around, to equalize, to overtake in the table, plus matchday, winning goal and a goalless draw. A false friend waits between Portuguese and Spanish: a jornada does not mean matchday in Brazil. 10 entries, including 3 verbs with full conjugation, among them 3 reviews.

  • FALSE FRIEND la jornada ≠ a jornada — in Mexico the matchday is la jornada (the league counted "Jornada 1" to "Jornada 17"). In Brazil a jornada is the working day or a day's journey; the matchday there is a rodada. dicio.com.br · dicio.com.br · es.wikipedia.org
  • BR-PT virar o jogo — to turn the game around. Last night's ESPN headline: "Fluminense marca no fim, vira sobre Internacional". The noun a virada, which you learned on Jul 16, comes from this verb. espn.com.br
  • CONJUGATION empatar = two German verbs — PT and ES use empatar for "to equalize" and for "to draw". German splits the two: one gleicht aus (equalizes) at 1-1, but one spielt (plays) 0-0. conjugacao.com.br · reverso.net
  • BR-PT aos 51 do segundo tempo — Brazil counts the minutes per half. Lucho Acosta's winning goal came "aos 51 minutos do 2º tempo", that is, in the 96th minute of the match. espn.com.br
  • PHRASE rebasar — in Mexico you overtake with rebasar, on the road and in the table. The DEM explicitly says "particularmente un automóvil a otro". Spain says adelantar. dem.colmex.mx
  • CONJUGATION glich aus, ausgeglichen — German ausgleichen is a strong verb (ei–i–i) like gleichen, and separable: Fluminense gleicht aus. verbformen.de
  • IDIOM o jogo só acaba quando termina — "the final reckoning comes at the end" (German: "Abgerechnet wird zum Schluss"). With two teams level on points, the saying fits well right now.

Same points, but not equally good: Imagine two kids collecting marbles. Both have 70 marbles. One kid went collecting 33 times, the other only 32 times. Who is better off? The kid with one try fewer — that kid still has one more chance. This is exactly how to read the ESPN table (retrieved on Oct 29; the page shows no date): Flamengo have 70 points from 32 games, Palmeiras 70 from 33. A season has 38 matchdays. So Palmeiras have five games left, Flamengo six. Today you need three verbs for that: to turn around (turn a defeat into a win), to equalize (turn a deficit into a draw) and to overtake (pass someone in the table).

Title Race, Matchday, Winning Goal — and a False Friend

The words for the final matchdays in the Brasileirão

Why "minute 51" is not a mistake: In Germany the soccer clock runs straight through: from 0 to 90, then comes stoppage time. In Brazil the clock starts again at zero in the second half. There it is o primeiro tempo and o segundo tempo, and people say "aos 51 do segundo tempo". That is the 51st minute of the second half, so 45 plus 6, so the 96th minute. In Mexico stoppage time is usually written as an add-on: "al 90+6". A goal this late has its own feel in all four languages — and last night exactly such a goal was scored.

"briga" — a quarrel on the schoolyard, a fight in the table In daily life a briga is a quarrel or a scuffle: os meninos brigaram no recreio. In sports language it becomes any fight for a ranking: a briga pelo título, a briga contra o rebaixamento, a briga por vaga na Libertadores. The second example sentence is almost word for word the ESPN line about Bahia's win over São Paulo: "segue na briga por vaga na Libertadores". Mexico uses the same image with la pelea. Jornada, rodada — two words swap places Anyone who knows Spanish can easily say *a próxima jornada in Brazil and mean the next matchday. But the Dicio dictionary lists as main senses for jornada "Caminhada que se faz no período de um dia" and "Trabalho desempenhado no decurso de um dia" (no separate football entry) — that is, a day's march and a working day. A jornada de trabalho is working time. The matchday is a rodada, according to Dicio "No futebol, série, ciclo de partidas de um campeonato". At the bar the same word also means a round of drinks: uma rodada de chope. Redundant on purpose The Brazilian saying literally means "The game only ends when it ends" — a tautology, like the English original. The joke is intended. German turns it into merchant language: the bill is settled only at the end. On the imperative in the second example: in daily speech Brazil says não comemora, although the grammar calls for não comemore. You will hear both.

Ten entries today, including three verbs with full conjugation and three reviews.

The first verb is to turn a game around. In Portuguese that's virar o jogo, and that's exactly how the headline put it: Fluminense vira sobre Internacional. In Mexican Spanish you say remontar el partido.

The second verb is to equalize. Portuguese and Spanish both use empatar, and they use the same verb for playing to a draw. German splits the two. And German ausgleichen is a strong verb: Fluminense glich aus, Fluminense hat ausgeglichen.

The third verb is a review from July: to overtake. In Brazil that's ultrapassar, in Mexico rebasar, on the road and in the table alike. In Spain it would be adelantar.

Today's false friend is jornada. In Mexico, la jornada is the matchday. In Brazil, though, a jornada is the working day or a day's journey, and the matchday is a rodada.

Three more words for the table. The title race is a briga pelo título, or la pelea por el título. The winning goal is o gol da vitória, or el gol del triunfo. Lucho Acosta scored for Fluminense in the fifty-first minute of the second half, because in Brazil the minutes restart in each half. And goalless simply means without goals in both languages: sem gols, sin goles.

The two other reviews are the comeback, a virada and la remontada, and razor-thin, apertado and muy apretado. Finally, a saying for the end of the season: it ain't over till it's over is o jogo só acaba quando termina in Brazilian Portuguese. Tomorrow the theme switches to travel and traffic.
