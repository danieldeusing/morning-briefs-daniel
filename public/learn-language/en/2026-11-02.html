Economy Vocabulary: to cap, to deny, to wait and see

Monday, November 2 — Finados, a public holiday in Brazil; the B3 stays closed. Rotation topic: Economy & Finance, episode thirteen. The occasion: according to Brasil 247, the economist André Lara Resende wants to cut the Selic from 13.75% to 7.5% in twelve months and to create a new "Conselho Fiscal e Monetário Nacional" for it. Finance Minister Dario Durigan rejected this on Sunday evening: a tabelamento de juros is not "na mesa do presidente". On Wednesday, November 4, the Copom decides. Today we learn the words for these three days: deckeln (to cap), dementieren (to deny), abwarten (to wait and see), plus the denial, the rate pause and the Fazenda. 11 entries, 3 of them verbs with full conjugation, including 3 refreshers from July and August.

  • FALSE FRIEND a Fazenda ≠ just "the farm" — fazenda is a large estate, but a Fazenda is also the finance ministry. Dicio gives both senses. Spanish does the same: hacienda is both an estate and the tax authority. dicio.com.br
  • BR-PT tabelar os juros — "to fix interest rates by table", that is, to cap them. This is how Brasil 247 headlined on Nov 1: the government "não vai tabelar" interest rates. brasil247.com

  • FALSE FRIEND o feriado ≠ as férias — feriado is a single public holiday like today. Férias are vacation or time off.

Three verbs for rate week: to cap, to deny, to wait and see

3 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked on verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

What is happening in Brazil this week: Imagine borrowing money costs a fee. The central bank decides how high that fee is: that is the policy rate, in Brazil a Selic. Right now it is at 13.75%. An economist says: "That is far too expensive. We should set a ceiling and get down to 7.5% in a year." That is deckeln (to cap). The finance minister replies: "No, we are not planning that." That is dementieren (to deny). And the central bank? It meets on Tuesday and Wednesday and may simply leave the rate where it is. That is abwarten (to wait and see). Three verbs, three roles in one story.

"tabelar" — Brazil's word for the price cap The German deckeln comes from the pot lid: it is closed on top, nothing goes higher. English thinks of a hat, to cap. Brazil has its own word from the era of price controls: tabelar, to "write a price into a table" and thereby fix it. The noun is o tabelamento. Brasil 247 headlined on Nov 1: "Durigan reage a Lara Resende e diz que governo Lula não vai tabelar taxa de juros". In the text, the minister rejects any "mecanismo de tabelamento da taxa básica de juros". The neutral word is limitar; according to Brasil 247, Lara Resende's plan wants to "estabelecer limites para a Selic". In Mexico, people also say poner un tope. Careful: on Mexican roads, el tope is the speed bump. brasil247.com · brasil247.com

dementieren, desmentir — and "demente" The German dementieren comes from the French démentir. PT and ES have the same image: des-mentir, "to take the lie away", that is, to say: "That is not true." The DEM defines desmentir as "Negar la veracidad o la afirmación de algo", with the example "desmentir los rumores". The trap: PT and ES demente sounds similar but means "demented, mentally confused". A minister who is demente has a very different problem. And one more subtlety: Brasil 247 does not write desmentiu but descarta and reage: "Ministro da Fazenda descarta proposta de limitar a Selic". Descartar means "to rule out". dem.colmex.mx · brasil247.com

Denial, rate pause, Fazenda — the words that go with them

4 nouns and 1 phrase · around the Copom meeting of November 3/4 · ES-MX nouns not individually checked against the DEM

Why a "no" is news: Imagine a rumor going around school: "Tomorrow the test is canceled." Everyone talks about it. Then the teacher says: "Not true, the test is on." That clear "not true" is a Dementi (denial). That is exactly how Sunday went in Brazil. First, the word was that Lula would hear out Lara Resende's plan. That made many investors nervous; Brasil 247 writes of "apreensão". In the evening the denial came from the finance ministry, the Fazenda. Now everyone is waiting for Wednesday. If the rate stays the same, that is a Zinspause (rate pause).

A participle becomes a noun PT and ES turn the participle into a noun: desmentido means "denied" and also "the denial". Dicio defines desmentido as "Palavras ou expressões com que se desmente". German takes the word directly from French, le démenti; that is why the plural is die Dementis. The genders almost match: DE das, PT and ES masculine. On the time in the first sentence: Brasil 247 published Durigan's reaction on Nov 1 at 8:09 p.m. The second sentence holds an old press truth: whoever denies loudly often confirms that there was something to deny. dicio.com.br · brasil247.com

A pause is not an end — and "juros" is plural A rate pause is not a turning point but a stop along the way. Brasil 247 writes: "A Selic está atualmente em 13,75% ao ano e permanece em dois dígitos desde 2022." For Wednesday, according to today's Economy brief, a pause at 13.75% is the favorite, for example at Itaú and Bradesco. Important for the language: PT almost always says os juros, in the plural. Os juros estão altos means "interest rates are high". Mexico says las tasas instead of los intereses. And the central bank "holds" the rate: PT manter, ES mantener, EN to hold. brasil247.com · jb.com.br

Farm or ministry? PT and ES are twins here Whoever hears fazenda in Paraná thinks of cattle and coffee. Dicio gives as the first sense: "Grande propriedade destinada à lavoura ou à criação de gado". The second sense is the state treasury: "Conjunto dos órgãos que, em um governo, são responsáveis pela administração do dinheiro de um país." That is why Dario Durigan is o ministro da Fazenda, as Brasil 247 calls him. Spanish has exactly the same doubling: la hacienda is an estate and also the tax authority. Mexico's finance ministry is called Secretaría de Hacienda y Crédito Público, Hacienda for short. So the false friend affects only German and English: Fazenda is not just a farm. Watch the capital letter: a Fazenda = ministry, uma fazenda = farm. dicio.com.br · brasil247.com

feriado ≠ férias — and the long weekend is called "emendar" in Brazil PT o feriado is a single public holiday. As férias are vacation or time off, always plural. Whoever says vou tirar férias is going on vacation. For the bridge day, Brazil has its own verb: emendar o feriado, literally "to join the holiday on".
