Three verbs: durchfallen, ausnutzen, beheben

3 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked at verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

What happened to C++26: A programming language like C++ is an ISO standard, like the format of an envelope. Before a new version applies, the countries vote on the finished draft, almost like a final exam. This time the vote ended with "Disapproved", in other words: failed. The biggest point of contention was contracts, a new language feature for preconditions and postconditions in code. According to Andreas Fertig at heise, this has happened for the first time in the language's history. What happens next is up to the committee, which meets from November 16 in Búzios — in Brazil. heise.de
Verb DE strong, separable (durch-), a→ä in du/er (fällst, fällt) → participle durchgefallen, perfect with sein · PT-BR reprovar, regular -ar, transitive · ES-MX reprobar, stem change o→ue under stress (repruebo, but reprobamos)
durchfallen

C++26 ist bei der ISO-Abstimmung durchgefallen.

Mein Bruder ist zweimal in der Führerscheinprüfung durchgefallen.

Wenn der Entwurf noch einmal durchfällt, verschiebt sich alles.

to fail

C++26 failed the ISO ballot.

My brother failed his driving test twice.

If the draft fails again, everything gets delayed.

reprovar (ser reprovado)

O C++26 foi reprovado na votação da ISO.

Meu irmão foi reprovado duas vezes na prova de direção.

Se o rascunho for reprovado de novo, tudo atrasa.

reprobar

C++26 reprobó la votación de la ISO.

Mi hermano reprobó dos veces el examen de manejo.

Si el borrador vuelve a reprobar, todo se retrasa.

Who is the subject? In German, the examinee fails by themselves. In Portuguese, according to Priberam, reprovar is transitive: the examiner "reprova" someone — "Não dar (em exame ou acto) por suficientemente instruído". The examinee is therefore in the passive: ele foi reprovado. Priberam also lists "Votar contra" and "Desaprovar, rejeitar" — so it fits the vote as well. In Mexican Spanish the student is often the subject: reprobé el examen. This usage is common, but we could not check it in a dictionary today (RAE site blocked). priberam.org

Los atacantes explotaron una cadena de tres fallas.

One German verb, two meanings Ausnutzen means two things: to use a flaw maliciously (exploit) and to use an opportunity well (make the most of). PT-BR and ES-MX separate these cleanly. For the attack you use explorar / explotar, for the good opportunity aproveitar / aprovechar (example 3). Caution: in everyday speech explorar also means "to explore" (explorar a cidade), and explotar also means "to explode". The object clarifies the meaning: explorar uma falha is always the attack.

Vulnerability, attacker, library — and a false friend

The words from every security advisory and every code review

Why a green check at Ivanti proves nothing: Ivanti has a scanning tool that checks whether foreign files sit on the VPN appliance. Picture a night guard who counts whether all the doors are shut. According to the security firm Volexity, the attackers exploited three flaws in a row, left no malware behind and manipulated the logs. It is as if the burglar locks the door behind them and forges the guard's logbook. The guard reports "all quiet". That is why covering one's tracks is quite literal here: there is no trace left for a tool to find. bleepingcomputer.com

Technical and human verbformen.de defines Schwachstelle as "Der Punkt, an dem etwas oder jemand verletzlich oder angreifbar ist". So the word fits both software and people. PT-BR and ES-MX separate the two: in code it is vulnerabilidade / vulnerabilidad, for a person rather ponto fraco / punto débil (example 2). In advisories you will also read the shorter falha (PT) or falla (ES). verbformen.de

library ≠ livraria ≠ librería The English library tempts you toward livraria and librería. But both primarily mean bookstores: Priberam defines livraria as "Loja ou comércio de livros", and the Spanish Wiktionary defines librería as "Establecimiento donde se venden libros". Priberam does also list "Grande colecção de livros" for livraria, but in code you say biblioteca. Priberam has its own computing sense for it: "Conjunto de código já compilado que pode ser usado para desenvolver outros programas ou rotinas." Bonus for Mexico: according to Wiktionary, the bookshelf is also called el librero there. priberam.org · priberam.org · es.wiktionary.org

One apple, four languages The image comes from Greek myth: Eris, goddess of discord, throws a golden apple among the goddesses, and the quarrel begins. The German Wiktionary calls the Zankapfel a "Lehnübertragung von lateinisch pomum Eridos" from the 16th century. English has apple of discord alongside bone of contention. Portuguese has preserved the old word pomo for apple; Spanish simply says manzana. de.wiktionary.org

Smudge or delete? The German image is soft: you verwischen (smudge) tracks until they can no longer be recognized. PT-BR and ES-MX are more direct: apagar and borrar both mean "to delete" — the same verb as deleting a file. That fits Ivanti well, since logs were manipulated there. The three renderings are common equivalents; we have no dictionary entry for them today.

It's Tuesday, November third. Today's rotation topic is software and technology, installment fourteen. We're covering how to fail, how to exploit, and how to fix. Eleven entries, three of them verbs with full conjugation, plus two refreshers.

The first hook comes from today's software brief. C plus plus twenty-six failed the ISO ballot, and according to heise, that's a first in the language's history. From November sixteenth, the committee meets in Búzios, Brazil. In German, durchfallen takes sein in the perfect: ich bin durchgefallen. Portuguese says reprovar, but in the passive: ele foi reprovado. Spanish says reprobar, with a stem change: yo repruebo, but nosotros reprobamos.

The second hook is Ivanti. The attackers exploited three flaws and then covered their tracks. For ausnutzen in the sense of an attack, Brazil says explorar and Mexico says explotar. For making the most of an opportunity, both use something else: aproveitar and aprovechar. And to cover your tracks is apagar os rastros in Portuguese and borrar las huellas in Spanish.

The third verb is beheben, to fix. Portuguese corrigir and Spanish corregir share the same trap: before o and a, the g turns into a j. So in both languages it's eu corrijo, yo corrijo. Spanish also changes the e to an i: corrigió, but corregimos.

Today's false friend is library. Livraria and librería primarily mean bookstores. A code library is a biblioteca, la biblioteca. Distinctly Brazilian is o aplicativo, the app. In Portugal, it's a aplicação.

Finally, an idiom. The sticking point in C plus plus was contracts, in other words the bone of contention. In Portuguese that's o pomo da discórdia, in Spanish la manzana de la discordia. We also refreshed maintenance and backward-compatible. Tomorrow the topic switches to family and daily life.
