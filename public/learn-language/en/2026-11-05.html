ausgleichen

Kurz vor Schluss gleicht die Heimmannschaft doch noch aus.

Wir haben in der Nachspielzeit ausgeglichen.

to equalize

Just before the end, the home team equalizes after all.

We equalized in stoppage time.

If they equalize now, the game ends in a draw.

empatar

No finzinho, o time da casa consegue empatar.

A gente empatou nos acréscimos.

empatar

Casi al final, el equipo local logra empatar.

Empatamos en el tiempo de compensación.

Si empatan ahora, el partido termina en empate.

Verb DE weak, regular → participle gedreht, perfect with haben · PT-BR virar (o jogo), regular -ar · ES-MX remontar, regular -ar · phrase: “ein Spiel drehen”
drehen (ein Spiel)

Nach dem 0:1 zur Pause dreht die Mannschaft das Spiel noch.

Letzte Saison haben wir drei Spiele gedreht.

Glaubst du, dass sie das Spiel noch drehen?

to turn (a game) around

After trailing 0–1 at halftime, the team turns the game around.

Last season we turned three games around.

Do you think they'll still turn the game around?

virar (o jogo)

Depois do 0 a 1 no intervalo, o time vira o jogo.

Na temporada passada, a gente virou três jogos.

Você acha que eles ainda viram o jogo?

remontar

Tras el 0-1 al medio tiempo, el equipo remonta el partido.

La temporada pasada remontamos tres partidos.

¿Crees que todavía remonten?

The comeback has a name The verb becomes a noun: PT-BR a virada, ES-MX la remontada. In German you say “die Aufholjagd” or simply “das Spiel gedreht”. After a question with creer, Spanish also allows the subjuntivo: ¿Crees que remonten? It stresses that the answer is uncertain.

to score (a goal)

The striker scores the first goal in the tenth minute.

Gol or golo? In Brazil the goal as a scoring act is o gol, plural os gols. Priberam gives golo as the equivalent in Portugal, and baliza for the goal frame. In ES-MX it stays el gol, plural los goles. priberam.org

League phase, away game, goalkeeper — and the goal into your own net

Five nouns for a European cup night

What is the league phase? The Champions League used to be played in small groups of four. Today there is a single big table with 36 clubs. Each club plays eight games against eight different opponents, half at home and half away. Places 1 to 8 go straight to the round of 16. Places 9 to 24 first play play-offs with a home and an away leg. From place 25 on, it is over. This week was matchday 4, so half of the league phase has been played. uefa.com · zdf.de

Why a zebra? Priberam explains dar zebra as “[Brasil, Informal] Ter resultado inesperado ou dar mau resultado”. The phrase is almost always in the past: Deu zebra! Heads up: as an insult for a person, zebra means “fool” according to Priberam — so talk only about results, not about people. priberam.org

Verb and noun as a pair Today you learn both together: empatar is the verb, o empate / el empate the noun. German lacks such a pair: you say gleicht aus, and in the end there is a Unentschieden. Note how the score is written: PT-BR says “1 a 1”, ES-MX writes “1-1”.

It's Thursday, November fifth. Today's rotation topic is football and sport. The hook is the European cups: on Tuesday and Wednesday it was matchday four of the Champions League league phase, and today the Europa League is on, including Leverkusen against Marseille. Eleven entries, three of them verbs with full conjugation, and two refreshers.

The first verb is to equalize. In Portuguese and Spanish it is the same word: empatar, completely regular. In German it is strong and separable: Leverkusen gleicht aus, Leverkusen glich aus, Leverkusen hat ausgeglichen. The result afterward is a draw, o empate in Portuguese and el empate in Spanish.

The second verb is to turn a game around. In Brazil you say virar o jogo, and the comeback is called a virada. In Mexico you say remontar, and the noun is la remontada.

The third verb is to score a goal. In German the participle has no ge: ich habe ein Tor erzielt. In Portuguese it is marcar um gol, with a small trap: eu marquei, spelled q u. In Mexican Spanish you say anotar un gol. In Brazil the goal is o gol, in Portugal o golo.

Today's false friend is in goal. In Spanish, el portero is the goalkeeper. In Portuguese, o porteiro is now the doorman. The goalkeeper is o goleiro in Brazil and o guarda-redes in Portugal. And the team is o time in Brazil, from the English word team, and el equipo in Mexico.

What exactly is the league phase? According to UEFA, thirty-six clubs play in one big table. Each club plays eight games, four at home and four away. The top eight go straight to the round of sixteen, and places nine through twenty-four play the play-offs. After this week's fourth matchday, we're at the halfway point.

Finally, a Brazilian animal. When the underdog wins, Brazilians say: deu zebra, there was a zebra. In Mexico you say: dio la sorpresa. Tomorrow the topic changes to travel and transport.
