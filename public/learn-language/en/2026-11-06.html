Bus Trip Vocabulary: virar, fazer baldeação, a rodoviária

Friday, November 6 — rotation topic Travel & Transport, installment eleven. Today there is no Feriadão and no traffic forecast. The next holiday, the Proclamação da República on November 15, falls on a Sunday. So today is about an ordinary bus trip: leaving the Rodoviária, turning at the roundabout, changing buses in Ourinhos. Plus a false friend that can leave you embarrassed at a ticket counter in Brazil. 10 entries, including 2 verbs with full conjugation, among them 3 refreshers from last Friday.

  • FALSE FRIEND o boleto ≠ el boleto — in Mexico, el boleto is the ticket. In Brazil, o boleto is the payment slip you pay at the bank. The bus ticket here is called a passagem. dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx
  • BR-PT a rotatória — the roundabout. Priberam marks the word as Brazilian; Portugal says a rotunda. Mexico says la glorieta. priberam.org · dem.colmex.mx
  • CONJUGATION ich bin abgebogen, ich bin umgestiegen — both new verbs form the perfect with sein and are separable: ich biege ab, ich steige um. verbformen.de · verbformen.de
  • PHRASE fazer baldeação — that is how Brazilians say “to change (buses)”. The Folha wrote, for example, that the city expects commuters to “façam baldeação em estações como Belém e Barra Funda”. dicio.com.br
  • PHRASE tomar un camión — in Mexico, el camión is not just the truck. The DEM calls it a vehicle “para transportar pasajeros o carga”. Anyone commuting to work takes the camión. dem.colmex.mx
  • IDIOM todos os caminhos levam a Roma — an expression that is almost word for word the same in all four languages: “All roads lead to Rome.”

Two Verbs for Getting Around: abbiegen, umsteigen

2 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked at verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

Why “ich biege ab” splits into two parts: Picture a German verb like abbiegen as a train with a locomotive and a car. The locomotive is biegen, the car is ab. In a main clause the locomotive moves forward to second position and the car stays at the end: Ich biege an der Ampel links ab. In a subordinate clause both stay together: …, weil ich links abbiege. In the perfect, -ge- slips in between: abgebogen, umgestiegen. Portuguese and Spanish have no such train. There the verb is a single piece: virar, doblar, transbordar. For you that is second nature, but for your kids in German it is a real hurdle.
Verb DE strong (ie–o–o), separable → bog ab, abgebogen, perfect with sein · EN to turn, regular · PT-BR virar, regular · ES-MX doblar, regular (no o→ue)
abbiegen

Am Kreisverkehr biegst du rechts nach Ourinhos ab.

Wir sind zu früh abgebogen und standen plötzlich mitten im Zuckerrohr.

Bieg nach der Tankstelle links ab.

to turn

At the roundabout, you turn right toward Ourinhos.

We turned too early and suddenly found ourselves in the middle of the sugarcane.

Turn left after the gas station.

virar

Na rotatória, você vira à direita para Ourinhos.

A gente virou cedo demais e de repente estava no meio do canavial.

Vira à esquerda depois do posto.

doblar

En la glorieta, doblas a la derecha hacia Ourinhos.

Doblamos demasiado pronto y de repente estábamos en medio del cañaveral.

Dobla a la izquierda después de la gasolinera.

“virar” — to turn and to become The Portuguese virar leads two lives. In traffic it means “to turn”: the Dicio dictionary gives the sentence “o carro virou a esquina”. In everyday speech, though, it just as often means “to become”: “virou famosa da noite para o dia” — “she became famous overnight”. The sentence decides: virou à direita is a direction, virou médico is a profession. The Mexican doblar is double too: it also means “to fold”. For turning, the DEM cites examples like “doblar a la izquierda” and “doblar en la esquina”. And the word for gas station: o posto in Brazil, la gasolinera in Mexico. dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx

Verb DE strong (ei–ie–ie), separable → stieg um, umgestiegen, perfect with sein · EN to change (US also to transfer), regular · PT-BR baldear, in everyday speech usually fazer baldeação, -ear verb · ES-MX transbordar, regular
umsteigen

In Ourinhos steigen wir in den Bus nach São Paulo um.

Bist du in Londrina umgestiegen oder direkt gefahren?

Mit dem Direktbus musst du nicht umsteigen.

to change (buses)

In Ourinhos, we change to the bus to São Paulo.

Did you change in Londrina or go direct?

With the direct bus, you don't have to change.

fazer baldeação

Em Ourinhos, a gente faz baldeação para o ônibus de São Paulo.

Você fez baldeação em Londrina ou foi direto?

Com o ônibus direto, você não precisa fazer baldeação.

transbordar

En Ourinhos transbordamos al autobús a São Paulo.

¿Transbordaste en Londrina o te fuiste directo?

Con el autobús directo, no tienes que transbordar.

“fazer baldeação” — to change buses, Brazilian style The verb baldear is in the dictionary, but in everyday speech you almost always hear the phrase fazer baldeação. The examples in Dicio all come from the Folha de S.Paulo, for instance “esses usuários devem fazer baldeação em pontos predefinidos”. Priberam in Portugal explains baldear as “de um comboio para outro”. Comboio is the Portuguese word for train; Brazil says o trem. Mexico uses transbordar. The DEM explains it as “pasar de un transporte a otro de la misma clase” and quotes: “me tocaba transbordar a otro tren”. German has a whole family: you already know einsteigen, plus aussteigen and today umsteigen. dicio.com.br · priberam.org · dem.colmex.mx

At the Rodoviária: Ticket, Bus Station, Connection

Three new words for the ticket counter — and a trap when paying

FALSE FRIEND Noun DE f, pl. -n · EN the ticket · PT-BR a passagem, f · ES-MX el boleto, m — PT-BR o boleto is the payment slip
die Fahrkarte

Die Fahrkarte nach São Paulo kaufe ich am Schalter.

Hast du die Fahrkarte schon auf dem Handy?

the ticket

I'll buy the ticket to São Paulo at the counter.

Do you already have the ticket on your phone?

a passagem

Vou comprar a passagem para São Paulo no guichê.

Você já tem a passagem no celular?

el boleto

Voy a comprar el boleto a São Paulo en la taquilla.

¿Ya tienes el boleto en el celular?

Say “boleto” at the counter and you get a bill In Mexico, according to the DEM, el boleto is a slip that proves the right to a service — with examples like “boleto de camión” and “boleto de avión”. In Brazil, o boleto is something quite different. Dicio calls it the “Nome popular de Boleto Bancário”, a document for a debt, used to pay for goods and services. If you ask for a boleto at the bus counter, people think of the payment slip. The ticket is called a passagem, per Dicio a “Bilhete ou comprovante desse pagamento”. Bonus from the DEM: in Mexico, boleto is also colloquially “a matter” or “business”: “no es tu boleto” — “that's none of your business”. dem.colmex.mx · dicio.com.br · dicio.com.br
BR-PT Noun DE m, pl. Busbahnhöfe · EN the bus station · PT-BR a rodoviária, f · ES-MX la terminal (de autobuses), f
der Busbahnhof

Der Bus kommt um 22 Uhr am Busbahnhof von Ourinhos an.

Der Busbahnhof liegt am anderen Ende der Stadt.

the bus station

The bus arrives at the Ourinhos bus station at 10 p.m.

The bus station is on the other side of town.

a rodoviária

O ônibus chega às 22h na rodoviária de Ourinhos.

A rodoviária fica do outro lado da cidade.

la terminal

El autobús llega a las 10 de la noche a la terminal de Ourinhos.

La terminal está del otro lado de la ciudad.

“rodoviária” — the train station of the highway The word comes from a rodovia, the highway. Dicio explains it as “Estação ou terminal de ônibus destinado à compra e venda de passagens”. So that is also where the passagem from above is sold. It is the short form of estação rodoviária. And the bus itself? O ônibus in Brazil, o autocarro in Portugal. Note the time of day in the ES sentence: Brazil says às 22h, Mexico in everyday speech a las 10 de la noche. dicio.com.br · motaword.com
Noun DE m, pl. Anschlüsse · EN the connection · PT-BR a conexão, f · ES-MX la conexión, f
der Anschluss

Wenn der Bus Verspätung hat, verpassen wir den Anschluss.

Der Anschluss nach Curitiba fährt zehn Minuten später.

the connection

If the bus is late, we'll miss the connection.

The connection to Curitiba leaves ten minutes later.

a conexão

Se o ônibus atrasar, a gente perde a conexão.

A conexão para Curitiba sai dez minutos depois.

la conexión

Si el autobús se retrasa, perdemos la conexión.

La conexión a Curitiba sale diez minutos después.

One word for bus and internet In German, the Anschluss is the next bus, but also the internet or phone connection. PT a conexão and ES la conexión cover both just the same. The first sentence is interesting: after se, Portuguese uses the future subjunctive atrasar, because the lateness has not happened yet. Spanish simply uses the present after si: si se retrasa. German and English do too: “wenn er Verspätung hat”, “if it is late”.

On the Road: Roundabout and Three Old Friends

One new word and three refreshers from Oct. 30

Why Brazil and Portugal have different words: Imagine two siblings who moved to different cities long ago. They still speak the same language, but each invented their own words for new things. The bus did not exist yet when they parted. So Brazil says o ônibus, Portugal o autocarro. For the roundabout it is a rotatória versus a rotunda, for the shoulder o acostamento versus a berma. The same goes for giving someone a ride: dar carona in Brazil, dar boleia in Portugal. Mexico and Spain have the same pattern: el acotamiento versus el arcén. Only one rule matters for you: when you hear a word in a Portuguese source, check whether it is also used that way in Brazil.
BR-PT Noun DE m, pl. -e · EN the roundabout (US also traffic circle) · PT-BR a rotatória, f (Portugal: a rotunda) · ES-MX la glorieta, f
der Kreisverkehr

Nimm im Kreisverkehr die zweite Ausfahrt.

Am neuen Kreisverkehr staut es sich jeden Morgen.

the roundabout

At the roundabout, take the second exit.

Traffic backs up at the new roundabout every morning.

a rotatória

Na rotatória, pega a segunda saída.

Todo dia de manhã forma fila na rotatória nova.

la glorieta

En la glorieta, toma la segunda salida.

Todas las mañanas se hace fila en la glorieta nueva.

rotatória, rotunda, glorieta Priberam marks rotatória with “[Brasil]” and explains it as “Praça ou largo circular onde desembocam várias ruas e o trânsito se processa em sentido giratório. = ROTUNDA”. The word comes from rotatório, “rotating”. The DEM describes the Mexican la glorieta as “Plaza circular situada en la confluencia de dos o más calles o avenidas”. Originally, a glorieta is a round clearing in a park where several paths meet. You know saída and salida from the first sentence from last Friday. priberam.org · dem.colmex.mx
REVIEW Noun DE m, pl. unchanged · EN the shoulder · PT-BR o acostamento, m (Portugal: a berma) · ES-MX el acotamiento, m · refreshed from Oct. 30
der Seitenstreifen

Auf dem Seitenstreifen darf man nur im Notfall halten.

Der Seitenstreifen ist hier so schmal, dass kaum ein Auto draufpasst.

the shoulder

You may only stop on the shoulder in an emergency.

The shoulder here is so narrow that a car barely fits.

o acostamento

No acostamento, só pode parar em caso de emergência.

Aqui o acostamento é tão estreito que mal cabe um carro.

el acotamiento

En el acotamiento solo puedes pararte en caso de emergencia.

Aquí el acotamiento es tan angosto que apenas cabe un carro.

Quick refresher — with the Mexican legal text On Oct. 30, the shoulder came up with the Finados travel wave. Today, the official view from Mexico. The traffic regulations for federal highways define the acotamiento as the area between the edge of the roadway and the crown of the road, “destinada para la parada o el estacionamiento eventual de los vehículos”. Note a second Mexican word in the second sentence: angosto for “narrow”. Brazil says estreito, and Spanish knows estrecho too. In Mexico, though, in everyday speech you are more likely to hear angosto. dof.gob.mx
REVIEW Noun DE f, pl. -en · EN rush hour · PT-BR o horário de pico, m · ES-MX la hora pico, f · refreshed from Oct. 30
die Stoßzeit

In der Stoßzeit ist der Stadtbus immer voll.

Wir fahren lieber nach der Stoßzeit los.

rush hour

At rush hour, the city bus is always packed.

We'd rather leave after rush hour.

o horário de pico

No horário de pico, o ônibus vive lotado.

É melhor a gente sair depois do horário de pico.

la hora pico

En hora pico, el camión siempre va lleno.

Mejor salimos después de la hora pico.

“vive lotado” and “el camión” Two everyday words are hiding in the first sentence. In Brazil, viver plus an adjective means “to be that way all the time”: o ônibus vive lotado — “the bus is constantly jam-packed”. In Mexico, you ride the camión in the city. To Germans that sounds like a truck, but the DEM calls the camión a vehicle “que se utiliza para transportar pasajeros o carga” and gives examples like “camión de pasajeros” and “tomar un camión”. For the long trip to São Paulo, the sentence above therefore uses el autobús. dem.colmex.mx
REVIEW Noun DE m, no pl. · EN return traffic · PT-BR o retorno, m · ES-MX el regreso, m · refreshed from Oct. 30
der Rückreiseverkehr

Den Rückreiseverkehr am Sonntagabend meiden wir immer.

Im Radio warnen sie schon vor dem Rückreiseverkehr.

return traffic

We always avoid the Sunday-evening return traffic.

The radio is already warning about the return traffic.

o retorno

A gente sempre evita o retorno de domingo à noite.

No rádio já estão avisando sobre o retorno.

el regreso

Siempre evitamos el regreso del domingo en la noche.

En la radio ya están avisando del regreso.

Quick refresher — “domingo à noite”, “domingo en la noche” On Oct. 30, the topic was the return trip from the Finados weekend. The next holiday, November 15, falls on a Sunday this year. So no Feriadão, and the return traffic stays an ordinary Sunday evening. Note the prepositions for “in the evening”: Brazil says à noite, Mexico en la noche. In Spain you more often hear por la noche. And remember: on a road sign, Retorno is the turnaround point, not the trip home.

To Finish: All Roads Lead to Rome

An expression that is almost the same in all four languages

IDIOM Idiom four languages, one image: the Romans' old road network
Alle Wege führen nach Rom

Über Ourinhos oder über Londrina? Egal, alle Wege führen nach Rom.

Ob mit Umsteigen oder direkt — alle Wege führen nach Rom.

All roads lead to Rome

Via Ourinhos or via Londrina? Doesn't matter, all roads lead to Rome.

With a change or direct — all roads lead to Rome.

Todos os caminhos levam a Roma

Por Ourinhos ou por Londrina? Tanto faz, todos os caminhos levam a Roma.

Com baldeação ou direto — todos os caminhos levam a Roma.

Todos los caminos llevan a Roma

¿Por Ourinhos o por Londrina? Da igual, todos los caminos llevan a Roma.

Con transbordo o directo — todos los caminos llevan a Roma.

Same image, small differences Rarely is an expression so alike in all four languages. Watch the verb: German führen, English lead, Portuguese and Spanish levar and llevar — literally “to take” or “to carry”. New in the second sentence is the Spanish noun el transbordo, the change of vehicles, matching transbordar. And tanto faz is Brazilian for “it makes no difference”; Mexico says da igual or da lo mismo.

The two verbs are abbiegen and umsteigen, German for "to turn" and "to change buses." Both are separable and form the perfect with sein: ich bin abgebogen, ich bin umgestiegen. To turn is virar in Portuguese and doblar in Mexican Spanish. Heads up: in Brazil, virar also means "to become." And doblar doesn't diphthongize. It's yo doblo. To change buses is usually fazer baldeação in Brazil and transbordar in Mexico.

Now the false friend of the day: boleto. In Mexico, el boleto is the ticket. In Brazil, o boleto is the payment slip from the bank. The bus ticket in Brazil is a passagem. You buy it at the rodoviária, the bus station. In Mexico that's la terminal. The connection is a conexão in Portuguese and la conexión in Spanish.

On the road, the roundabout joins the list: a rotatória in Brazil, a rotunda in Portugal, la glorieta in Mexico. As a refresher, three words come back from October thirtieth: the shoulder, o acostamento and el acotamiento; rush hour, o horário de pico and la hora pico; and return traffic, o retorno and el regreso. And by the way, in Mexican cities you ride the camión, which there means the bus too.

To finish, an expression that's nearly identical in all four languages: all roads lead to Rome. In German, alle Wege führen nach Rom. In Portuguese, todos os caminhos levam a Roma, and in Spanish, todos los caminos llevan a Roma. Safe travels. Tomorrow the topic moves to food and cooking.
