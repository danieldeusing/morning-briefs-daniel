Economy Vocabulary: ansteigen, abwerten, kürzen

Monday, November 9. Rotation topic Economy & Finance, installment fourteen. The occasion: Friday's (November 6) ECB reference rate was 6.1449 reais per euro. Before the runoff, on October 23, it was 5.6485 — a gain of almost 9%. So the real has weakened sharply. Lula addressed the nation on Sunday evening; today's Economy brief did not verify the content. What is clear, according to Estadão: the 2027 budget needs at least R$ 40 billion in adjustments, and concrete measures are due from November 16, according to Jornal do Comércio. Three Verbs for the Exchange Rate: ansteigen, abwerten, kürzen

3 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked at verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

One story, three verbs: Imagine you are trading collector cards. Suddenly everyone wants your euro cards, and nobody wants the real cards. Now you get more real cards for one euro card than last week. The euro rate has risen (angestiegen), the real has devalued (abgewertet). Why do people want the real less? They fear that the government is spending more than it takes in. So the market is waiting for a sign: where does the government want to cut (kürzen)? Since the runoff it is almost 9% more reais per euro. Good for your euro bills, bad for everything Brazil imports.
CONJUGATION Verb DE strong, separable → stieg an, angestiegen, perfect with sein · EN to rise, irregular (rose, risen) · PT-BR subir, irregular (u→o) · ES-MX subir, regular
ansteigen

Der Euro-Kurs ist seit der Stichwahl um fast 9 % angestiegen.

Wenn die Preise weiter ansteigen, bleibt der Leitzins hoch.

Am 29. Oktober stieg der Dollar wieder an, auf 5,38 Reais.

to rise

The euro rate has risen almost 9% since the runoff.

If prices keep rising, the key rate will stay high.

On October 29, the dollar rose again, to 5.38 reais.

subir

A cotação do euro subiu quase 9% desde o segundo turno.

Se os preços continuarem subindo, a Selic vai continuar alta.

Em 29 de outubro, o dólar voltou a subir, para R$ 5,38.

subir

El tipo de cambio del euro subió casi 9% desde la segunda vuelta.

Si los precios siguen subiendo, la tasa de referencia va a seguir alta.

El 29 de octubre, el dólar volvió a subir, a 5.38 reales.

“volta a subir” — the headline, word for word On Oct. 29, DGABC headlined: “Dólar volta a subir e vai a R$ 5,38 com dúvidas sobre ajuste fiscal no Lula 4”. Two patterns are in there. First, voltar a + infinitive means “to do again”: volta a subir = “rises again.” Mexico says vuelve a subir in the same way. Second, vai a R$ 5,38: the rate “goes to” 5.38. On the German side: ansteigen and steigen are almost the same. Ansteigen stresses the rise from a given level. Both form the perfect with sein: Der Kurs ist angestiegen, never “hat.” You have known steigen since June 22. dgabc.com.br · verbformen.de

CONJUGATION Verb DE weak, separable → wertete ab, abgewertet, perfect with haben · EN to devalue, regular · PT-BR desvalorizar, regular · ES-MX devaluar, accent trap (devalúo)
abwerten

Der Real hat seit der Stichwahl gegenüber dem Euro abgewertet.

Ein Land wertet seine Währung ab, damit seine Exporte billiger werden.

Wertet der Real weiter ab, bekommst du mehr Reais für deine Euros.

to devalue

The real has lost value against the euro since the runoff.

A country devalues its currency so that its exports become cheaper.

If the real weakens further, you get more reais for your euros.

desvalorizar

O real se desvalorizou frente ao euro desde o segundo turno.

Um país desvaloriza a sua moeda para que as exportações fiquem mais baratas.

Se o real se desvalorizar mais, você recebe mais reais pelos seus euros.

devaluar

El real se devaluó frente al euro desde la segunda vuelta.

Un país devalúa su moneda para que sus exportaciones sean más baratas.

Si el real se devalúa más, recibes más reales por tus euros.

Who devalues — the state or the market? In German, both work: Die Regierung wertet die Währung ab (it does so deliberately) and der Real wertet ab (it loses value on its own). PT and ES make the second case reflexive: o real se desvalorizou, el real se devaluó. English separates the cases more sharply. To devalue is usually a government decision. When the market pushes the rate down, people tend to say the real weakened or lost value. That is why EN sentences 1 and 3 do not use devalue. In Brazil the case is clear right now: no decree, but the market has pushed the real from 5.6485 to 6.1449 per euro since Oct. 23. verbformen.de · reverso.net

BR-PT Verb DE weak → kürzte, gekürzt, perfect with haben · EN to cut, irregular (cut, cut) · PT-BR cortar, regular · ES-MX recortar, regular
kürzen

Wo soll die Regierung kürzen?

Die Firma hat das Reisebudget um 20 % gekürzt.

Wenn wir nicht sparen, werden wir die Ausgaben bald kürzen müssen.

to cut

Where should the government cut?

The company has cut its travel budget by 20%.

If we don't save, we will soon have to cut spending.

cortar

Onde o governo deve cortar?

A empresa cortou 20% do orçamento de viagens.

Se não economizarmos, vamos ter que cortar os gastos em breve.

recortar

¿Dónde debe recortar el gobierno?

La empresa recortó 20% el presupuesto de viajes.

Si no ahorramos, pronto vamos a tener que recortar los gastos.

“o tesouraço” — when the scissors become a fighting word PT cortar means both to cut (with a blade) and to cut back; the tool is a tesoura, the scissors. The ending -aço makes something big and heavy. So a tesouraço is a “giant pair of scissors,” a slash-and-burn on spending. The word is political. Guilherme Boulos wrote on Oct. 29, according to DGABC: “O ‘tesouraço’ foi derrotado nas urnas e pela mobilização popular.” The same text has the neutral version: corte de gastos sociais, the cutting of social spending. For budgets, Spanish likes recortar and the noun el recorte. English is the shortest: cut stays the same in all three principal parts. dgabc.com.br

Austerity, Forecast, Economy — the Related Words

3 nouns and 1 idiom · meanings checked at Dicio, DEM (COLMEX) and DWDS

Why everyone is watching November 16: Imagine a family that spends more every month than it earns. It lives beyond its means. At some point the bank says: “Show me a plan, or it gets more expensive.” The plan is an austerity course (Sparkurs). Brazil is that family right now, only bigger. According to Estadão, the 2027 budget has to be adjusted by at least 40 billion reais. The central bank has raised its inflation forecast (Prognose). And the economy (Konjunktur), meaning how much work and buying goes on in the country, is expected to grow more slowly in 2027. According to Jornal do Comércio, the government wants to show the plan from November 16.
BR-PT Noun DE m, pl. -e (mostly sg.) · EN austerity · PT-BR o ajuste fiscal, m (colloquial: o arrocho) · ES-MX la austeridad, f
der Sparkurs

Der Markt wartet auf einen klaren Sparkurs.

Ein harter Sparkurs ist im Wahlkampf unbeliebt.

austerity

The market is waiting for a clear austerity plan.

Tough austerity is unpopular during a campaign.

o ajuste fiscal

O mercado espera um ajuste fiscal claro.

Um ajuste fiscal duro é impopular na campanha.

la austeridad

El mercado espera una política de austeridad clara.

Una austeridad dura es impopular en campaña.

“ajuste” sounds gentle, “arrocho” sounds like pain The Brazilian press almost always says ajuste fiscal, literally “fiscal adjustment.” On Nov. 4, Seu Dinheiro ran, with Estadão material: “Lula fará pronunciamento neste domingo para anunciar medidas de ajuste fiscal”. Those who oppose the cuts say o arrocho. The word comes from a piece of wood used to tighten ropes; in the figurative sense Dicio gives “Aperto, dificuldade”. A well-known phrase is arrocho salarial, the squeeze on wages. In Mexico la austeridad is the usual word; the DEM offers the example “Pidió buscar fórmulas de austeridad, productividad y ahorro”. seudinheiro.com · dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx

Three images for looking ahead German takes the Greek Prognose, “foreknowledge.” PT-BR thinks of a projector: a projeção throws the numbers forward. Dicio gives “Cálculo feito por antecipação” for it, and the example “projeção revela crescimento de 3,5% para próximo trimestre”. Mexico says el pronóstico, also for the weather: pronóstico del tiempo. Nice for Thursday's soccer topic is the DEM example “Contra todos los pronósticos ganó el equipo visitante”. Watch the gender: DE and PT feminine, ES masculine. On the number in the first sentence: on Nov. 4 the Copom left the Selic at 13.75% and raised its 2026 inflation forecast from 5.2 to 5.6%. dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx · moneytimes.com.br

Konjunktur, conjuntura, coyuntura — same root, different focus The DWDS describes the Konjunktur as the cyclical course “der gesamtwirtschaftlichen Aktivität”. The word looks like PT a conjuntura and ES la coyuntura. On their own, though, they mean something more general. Dicio defines conjuntura as “circunstância, situação”, that is, the situation at a given moment. The DEM says of coyuntura: “Situación que se presenta casualmente”. The first sense in the DEM is even a body part: “Articulación movible de dos o más huesos”, the joint. The compounds conjuntura econômica and coyuntura económica do exist, though: they mean the economic situation. For Konjunktur as economic activity (“the economy is doing well”), a atividade econômica or la actividad económica is the closest everyday rendering. On the number in the first sentence: the Focus survey (data from Oct. 30) expects 1.76% growth for 2026 and 1.20% for 2027. dwds.de · dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx · bcb.gov.br

“Verhältnisse” only in the plural — and Brazil also says “além” In the idiom, Verhältnisse is always plural and means the resources one has. English says means, also plural. For Brazil, O Antagonista writes: “Viver acima das possibilidades é um erro financeiro frequentemente citado por especialistas”. The same text has the variant viver além das possibilidades, with the same meaning. For Mexico we found no dictionary source today for a fixed expression. That is why the ES column has the safe paraphrase: “to spend more than one earns.” The opposite is the advice from the same article: with an orçamento, a budget, you plan below your means. oantagonista.com.br · en.wiktionary.org

The three verbs tell this story: ansteigen, abwerten, kürzen. In Portuguese they are subir, desvalorizar and cortar, and in Mexican Spanish subir, devaluar and recortar. The newspaper DGABC headlined at the end of October: O dólar volta a subir, the dollar rises again.

Watch out for two traps in the tables. In Portuguese it's ela sobe and eles sobem, but nós subimos. And in Spanish, devaluar takes an accent: yo devalúo, but nosotros devaluamos.

When it comes to cuts, Brazil has a fighting word: o tesouraço, the giant scissors. Guilherme Boulos wrote that the tesouraço had been defeated at the ballot box. The press neutrally says o ajuste fiscal. That's austerity, and in Spanish, la austeridad.

Now the false friend of the day: die Konjunktur is not simply a conjuntura. Conjuntura on its own means the situation. Conjuntura econômica does exist, and it means the economic situation. For Konjunktur as economic activity, the everyday word is usually a atividade econômica, and in Spanish la actividad económica. The word for forecast is a projeção in Portuguese and el pronóstico in Spanish.

The idiom of the day: über seine Verhältnisse leben. In English, to live beyond one's means, and in Portuguese, viver acima das possibilidades.

Finally, three refreshers: der Wechselkurs, o câmbio. Der Haushalt, o orçamento, which also means a cost estimate. And the rate pause, which really did arrive on November fourth: the Selic stays at thirteen point seven five percent. Tomorrow the topic switches to software and technology.
