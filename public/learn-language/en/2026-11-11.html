Plus a false friend from the kids' room: the German Ventilator is not an English ventilator. 10 entries, 3 of them verbs with full conjugation, including 3 reviews.

  • FALSE FRIEND der Ventilator ≠ the ventilator — the Ventilator in the bedroom is called a fan in English. An English ventilator is usually the breathing machine in a hospital. PT o ventilador and ES el ventilador match the German. en.wiktionary.org · dwds.de
  • BR-PT o feriadão — the long weekend. Priberam marks the word as [Brasil, Informal]. The next feriadão is coming soon: Fri Nov 20 to Sun Nov 22, because of Dia da Consciência Negra. priberam.org · serasaexperian.com.br
  • CONJUGATION eu prevejo, ele prevê — PT prever conjugates like ver: eu vejo → eu prevejo, eu vi → eu previ. conjugacao.com.br
  • CONJUGATION pronostiqué · vacío — ES pronosticar spells qu before e. ES vaciar carries an accent in the present: yo vacío, but nosotros vaciamos. reverso.net · reverso.net
  • PHRASE estou com calor · tengo calor — when you feel hot, you say "estou com calor" in PT and "tengo calor" in ES. In German that is "mir ist heiß", never "ich bin heiß".
  • GENDER die Hitze → o calor, el calor — feminine in German, masculine in PT and ES. Same with the fan: der, o, el.

Three Verbs: predict, air out, empty

3 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked against verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

Why three weather apps disagree A weather forecast is an estimate, not a clock time. Each app calculates with its own model. That is why today's family brief shows a 91 to 95% chance of rain for Thursday from all three sources, but different amounts: Climatempo 6.3 mm, HG Brasil 7.4 mm. In Portuguese the forecast is called a previsão do tempo, in Spanish el pronóstico del tiempo. They contain today's two verbs: prever and pronosticar. climatempo.com.br · hgbrasil.com
Verb DE weak, separable (er sagt Regen vorher) → participle vorhergesagt, perfect with haben · PT-BR prever, irregular like ver · ES-MX pronosticar, regular -ar, but c→qu before e
vorhersagen

Die App sagt für Donnerstag viel Regen vorher.

Gestern hat niemand die Hitze am Wochenende vorhergesagt.

Kann man das Wetter für den 20. November schon vorhersagen?

to predict

The app predicts a lot of rain for Thursday.

Yesterday nobody predicted the heat this weekend.

Can anyone predict the weather for November 20 yet?

prever

O aplicativo prevê muita chuva para quinta-feira.

Ontem ninguém previu o calor do fim de semana.

Dá para prever o tempo para o dia 20 de novembro?

pronosticar

La app pronostica mucha lluvia para el jueves.

Ayer nadie pronosticó el calor del fin de semana.

¿Ya se puede pronosticar el clima para el 20 de noviembre?

Show conjugation

DE: In a main clause vorher moves to the end of the sentence (ich sage vorher), and in the participle ge sits in the middle: vorhergesagt. Subjunctive II uses the würde form, because sagte vorher looks like the simple past. PT: prever follows ver: prevejo, prevê, preveem (no circumflex since the spelling reform), preterite previ, previu, subjunctive preveja. ES: pronostiqué and the whole subjunctive (pronostique) spell qu to keep the k sound. verbformen.de · conjugacao.com.br · reverso.net

Verb DE weak, regular, with e-insertion (du lüftest, er lüftet) → participle gelüftet, perfect with haben · PT-BR arejar, regular -ar · ES-MX ventilar, regular -ar
lüften

Nach dem Regentag lüften wir das ganze Haus.

Hast du das Kinderzimmer heute Morgen gelüftet?

Lüfte lieber früh, bevor die Hitze kommt.

to air out

After the rainy day, we air out the whole house.

Did you air out the kids' room this morning?

Better air it out early, before the heat comes.

arejar

Depois do dia de chuva, a gente areja a casa toda.

Você arejou o quarto das crianças hoje de manhã?

É melhor arejar cedo, antes de o calor chegar.

ventilar

Después del día de lluvia, ventilamos toda la casa.

¿Ventilaste el cuarto de los niños hoy en la mañana?

Mejor ventila temprano, antes de que llegue el calor.

Show conjugation

DE: The stem ends in -t, so an e goes before the ending: du lüftest, er lüftet, ihr lüftet. Subjunctive II uses the würde form, because lüftete looks like the simple past. PT: arejar is regular; the present and preterite of nós are the same (arejamos). In everyday speech people often just say abrir as janelas. ES: ventilar is completely regular. Heads up, mix-up: ES ventilar also means, figuratively, "to make a topic public". verbformen.de · conjugacao.com.br · reverso.net

REVIEW Verb DE weak, separable (wir leeren den Eimer aus) → participle ausgeleert, perfect with haben · PT-BR esvaziar, regular -ar · ES-MX vaciar, with accent í in the present · refreshed from 10/28
ausleeren

Nach dem Regen leeren wir die Untersetzer der Blumentöpfe aus.

Die Kinder haben den Eimer im Garten ausgeleert.

Ich leere die Wasserschale vom Hund jeden Tag aus.

to empty

After the rain, we empty the saucers under the flowerpots.

The kids emptied the bucket in the garden.

I empty the dog's water bowl every day.

esvaziar

Depois da chuva, a gente esvazia os pratinhos dos vasos.

As crianças esvaziaram o balde no quintal.

Eu esvazio a vasilha de água do cachorro todo dia.

vaciar

Después de la lluvia, vaciamos los platos de las macetas.

Los niños vaciaron la cubeta en el jardín.

Yo vacío el plato de agua del perro todos los días.

Show conjugation

DE: The prefix aus separates in a main clause (ich leere aus), and in the participle ge sits in the middle: ausgeleert. PT: esvaziar is regular, subjunctive que eu esvazie. ES: vaciar stresses the i in the present and writes it with an accent: vacío, vacías, vacía, vacían, but vaciamos; likewise in the subjunctive (vacíe, but vaciemos). Mexican: the bucket is called la cubeta. verbformen.de · conjugacao.com.br · reverso.net

Five new words for a rainy day and a hot weekend

Noun DE f, no pl. · EN heat · PT-BR o calor, m · ES-MX el calor, m — gender switch DE ↔ PT/ES
die Hitze

Ab Freitag kommt die Hitze zurück, bis 30 Grad.

Bei dieser Hitze ist mir den ganzen Tag heiß.

the heat

From Friday the heat comes back, up to 30 degrees.

In this heat, I feel hot all day.

o calor

A partir de sexta, o calor volta, até 30 graus.

Com esse calor, estou com calor o dia inteiro.

el calor

A partir del viernes regresa el calor, hasta 30 grados.

Con este calor, tengo calor todo el día.

Mir ist heiß, not "ich bin heiß" PT and ES express the feeling with possession: PT estou com calor ("I am with heat"), ES tengo calor ("I have heat"). For the weather you say PT está calor or faz calor, ES hace calor. In German the person is in the dative: "mir ist heiß". An "ich bin heiß" sounds like something quite different in German.

Stifled and embarrassed PT abafado comes from abafar, "to stifle, to smother". For weather, Dicio explains it as "Sufocante, abafadiço". The DEM describes ES bochorno as "Calor intenso, pesado y sofocante, que no aligera la más mínima brisa". Careful: in Spanish bochorno also means "shame, embarrassment". dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx

A blow of rain A pancada literally means "the blow". For rain, Dicio gives the meaning "Chuva forte e passageira" — heavy, but over quickly. The DEM describes the Mexican chubasco in just that way: "Lluvia fuerte y abundante, que pasa rápido". In Brazilian weather reports you often read the plural: pancadas de chuva. dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx

False friend DWDS describes the Ventilator as a "Gerät besonders zur Lüftung von Räumen und Kühlung von Motoren" (a device used especially for ventilating rooms and cooling engines). In English this device is called a fan. Wiktionary does list a ventilating apparatus for ventilator too, but in everyday use people think of medicine first: "A machine that moves breathable air into and out of the lungs of a patient". Anyone who asks "Can I have a ventilator?" in a hotel gets an alarmed look. Also watch the verbs: PT-BR ligar, ES-MX prender — to switch on. dwds.de · en.wiktionary.org

Air out or not? Two of today's words meet here: if you air out in the evening, you also let the pernilongos in. So it is better to air out in the morning and close the windows before dusk — or to have a window screen, PT a tela, ES-MX el mosquitero.

Another feriadão Priberam defines feriadão as [Brasil, Informal] "Fim-de-semana prolongado por um feriado na sexta-feira ou na segunda-feira" and names ponte as a synonym. That is exactly what happens on November 20: Dia da Consciência Negra has been a national holiday since Law 14.759/2023 and falls on a Friday in 2026. Note the mood in the second sentence: PT se chover (future subjunctive), ES si llueve (indicative). priberam.org · serasaexperian.com.br

It's Wednesday, November eleventh. Today's rotation topic is family and daily life, episode sixteen. The theme is the weather: predicting, airing out and emptying. Ten entries, three of them verbs with full conjugation, plus three reviews.

According to the family brief, Thursday brings a rainy day, with a ninety-one to ninety-five percent chance of rain. To predict is prever in Portuguese, and it works like ver: eu prevejo, ele prevê, eu previ. In Spanish it's pronosticar. Watch the spelling: yo pronostiqué, with q u. The forecast itself is a previsão do tempo and el pronóstico del tiempo.

After the rain, we air out the house. In Portuguese you say arejar, in Spanish ventilar. Both are regular. And we empty out standing water, so the dengue mosquito can't lay eggs. In Portuguese that's esvaziar, in Spanish vaciar, with an accent: yo vacío. In Brazil, the everyday word for mosquito is o pernilongo.

From Friday through Sunday it turns hot, twenty-nine to thirty-one degrees. Heat is masculine in Portuguese and Spanish: o calor, el calor. When you feel hot, you say estou com calor or tengo calor. Before the rain it's often muggy: abafado in Portuguese, bochornoso in Spanish. A short rain shower is a pancada de chuva and el chubasco.

Today's false friend: the German Ventilator is called a fan in English. An English ventilator is usually the breathing machine in a hospital. Portuguese and Spanish say o ventilador and el ventilador, just like German. For sunscreen, Brazilians say o protetor solar and Mexicans say el bloqueador.

One last thing: from the twentieth to the twenty-second of November there's a long weekend, thanks to Dia da Consciência Negra. In Brazilian Portuguese that's o feriadão, in Spanish el fin de semana largo. Tomorrow the topic switches to soccer and sports.
