Three Verbs: billigen, sich enthalten, bekämpfen

3 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked against verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

What "consent not required" means for the Bundesrat: Imagine the class votes on a field trip, and afterward the parents' council still gets to look at it. For some laws, the parents' council must say "yes," or the trip is off. For others, it can only object. The emergency reform belongs to the second kind. The Ärzteblatt writes: „Eine Zustimmung ist zu dem Gesetz nicht notwendig, die Bundesländer könnten aber den Vermittlungsausschuss anrufen." This committee is like a mediation table: the Bundestag and the states look for a compromise there. The word anrufen has nothing to do with the telephone here. It means "to officially bring in."
Verb DE weak, regular → billigte, gebilligt, perfect with haben · EN to approve, regular · PT-BR aprovar, regular · ES-MX aprobar, stem change o→ue
billigen

Der Bundestag hat die Reform im Oktober gebilligt.

Ich billige diese Entscheidung nicht.

Wenn die Länder das Gesetz billigen, kann es kommen.

to approve

The Bundestag approved the reform in October.

I don't approve of this decision.

If the states approve the law, it can go ahead.

aprovar

O Bundestag aprovou a reforma em outubro.

Eu não aprovo essa decisão.

Se os estados aprovarem a lei, ela pode sair.

aprobar

El Bundestag aprobó la reforma en octubre.

No apruebo esta decisión.

Si los estados aprueban la ley, puede salir adelante.

Two meanings in PT and ES aprovar and aprobar also mean "to pass someone on an exam": O professor me aprovou — the teacher passed me. The German billigen cannot do that. There you say "bestehen." The third PT sentence contains aprovarem: that is the future subjunctive after se, a form Spanish no longer has in everyday use. This form is not in the table below and is not cross-checked.

Verb DE strong, reflexive → enthält, enthielt, enthalten, perfect with haben · EN to abstain, regular · PT-BR abster-se, irregular (like ter) · ES-MX abstenerse, irregular (like tener)

Hast du dich bei der Wahl im Verein enthalten?

to abstain

AfD e Linke se abstiveram na votação.

Prefiro me abster, não conheço os detalhes.

abstenerse

AfD y Linke se abstuvieron en la votación.

Prefiero abstenerme, no conozco los detalles.

¿Te abstuviste en la elección del club?

A verb that behaves like "to have" abster-se is ter with a prefix, and abstenerse is tener with a prefix. If you know tive, teve, tiveram, you also know abstive, absteve, abstiveram. Spanish works the same way: tuve → me abstuve. In PT, watch the accent: ele se abstém (singular, acute), eles se abstêm (plural, circumflex). In everyday speech, Brazil likes to put the pronoun before the verb: eu me abstenho. The Ärzteblatt names the abstention of the AfD and the Left literally: „die sich bei der Abstimmung über das Gesetz enthielten". aerzteblatt.de

Three words versus one German needs a light verb: ein Veto einlegen. PT, ES and EN turn the noun directly into a verb. Conjugacao lists vetei, vetou, vetaram — fully regular. In Brazil, you often hear veto parcial in the news: the president strikes only individual parts of a law. This is general knowledge; we have not documented a current veto case for today. conjugacao.com.br

Why you never say "bicha" in Brazil Priberam lists bicha in the sense "Fileira de pessoas, umas atrás das outras" with the label "[Portugal, Informal]". In Brazil, however, bicha is a derogatory word for gay men. This second meaning comes from our own language knowledge. So always say a fila, and for "to stand in line" fazer fila or ficar na fila. Mexico also says hacer fila. Spain often says hacer cola instead. priberam.org

Outbreak, bud, fit El brote in Spanish is also the bud of a plant: the disease "sprouts." O surto has a second meaning in Brazilian everyday speech: ter um surto means "to freak out, to have a fit." A volcanic eruption, by contrast, is a erupção and la erupción. These meanings come from our own language knowledge and were not looked up in a dictionary today.

It's Sunday, November fifteenth. Today's rotation topic is politics, law and health. The occasion: in October, the German Bundestag passed the reform of emergency care. The AfD and the Left abstained, and next up is the Bundesrat, expected on November twentieth. There are eleven entries today, three of them verbs with full conjugation, plus three reviews.

The first verb is billigen, "to approve." In Portuguese it's aprovar, in Mexican Spanish aprobar. In Spanish, the o turns into ue under stress: yo apruebo, but nosotros aprobamos. In Portuguese the o stays: eu aprovo.

The second verb is sich enthalten, "to abstain." In Portuguese you say abster-se, in Spanish abstenerse. Both behave like the verb "to have": ter and tener. So: eles se abstiveram, ellos se abstuvieron. And in Portuguese, the accent separates the singular, se abstém, from the plural, se abstêm.

The third verb is bekämpfen, "to fight," combater in Portuguese and combatir in Spanish. Fittingly, according to our family brief, the Paraná health authority announces a Dia D, an action day against the mosquito. The date is open.

From parliament, we add the vote: die Abstimmung, a votação and la votación. "To veto" is simply vetar in both languages: o presidente vetou. The committee is a comissão and la comisión. And as a review, the law: a lei and la ley, both feminine.

At the doctor's, you learn the emergency room, o pronto-socorro in Brazil, literally "quick help," and the outbreak, o surto and el brote. As a review, the waiting line: in Brazil, always say a fila, never bicha. The word bicha means "line" only in Portugal.

The false friend of the day is Rezept. At the doctor's, it's a prescription in English, not a recipe. Portuguese and Spanish do it like German: a receita and la receta cover both the kitchen and the medicine. Tomorrow the topic switches to economy and finance.
