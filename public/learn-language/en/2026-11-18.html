So today we learn how to plan a rainy weekend, why kids in Brazil brincam and in Mexico brincan (it is not the same thing), and why you never want to be constipado in Brazil. 10 entries, 3 of them verbs with full conjugation, including 2 reviews.

  • FALSE FRIEND brincar (PT) ≠ brincar (ES) — in Brazil it means "to play, to have fun", Priberam: "Divertir-se". In Spanish it means "to hop, to jump", SpanishDict: to jump, to skip, to hop. priberam.org · spanishdict.com

Why a rainy weekend needs a plan: Imagine you are packing for a trip and you do not know if the sun will shine. So you bring a plan A for outside and a plan B for inside. That is what this long weekend looks like. For Sunday, Climatempo gives an 81% chance of rain, HG Brasil only 47%. The two weather services do not agree. So it pays to plan for rain: rubber boots in the car, a game for indoors in the bag. climatempo.com.br
BR-PT Verb DE weak, separable (ein-) → participle eingeplant, perfect with haben · PT-BR planejar, regular -ar (in Portugal planear) · ES-MX planear, regular -ar
einplanen

Für Sonntag sollten wir Regen einplanen.

Hast du das lange Wochenende schon eingeplant?

to plan (for)

We should plan for rain on Sunday.

I'm planning an extra hour for the drive to Londrina.

planejar

A gente devia planejar o domingo contando com chuva.

Eu planejo uma hora a mais para a viagem até Londrina.

Você já planejou o feriadão?

planear

Deberíamos planear el domingo contando con lluvia.

Planeo una hora más para el viaje a Londrina.

¿Ya planeaste el fin de semana largo?

One letter separates two countries Priberam marks all three meanings of planejar with [Brasil], for example "Fazer um plano de". The dictionary lists planear as the general synonym. That is also the Spanish form, so the trap is small: if you come from Spanish, you easily say planear in Brazil. People will understand you, but it sounds like Portugal. The German ein- has no word of its own in PT and ES. The sense of "counting on something" comes from contando com / contando con. priberam.org

Constipado? Better not in Brazil In Portugal, the common cold is a constipação. Priberam marks this meaning with [Portugal] and writes: "Equivalente no português do Brasil: resfriado." The second meaning is "Dificuldade em defecar (ex.: constipação intestinal severa)" — constipation. So anyone who says "Estou constipado" in Brazil is more likely reporting digestive problems. Say Estou resfriado or Peguei um resfriado instead. For sich erkälten, Linguee gives resfriar-se as the PT translation. priberam.org · linguee.de

FALSE FRIEND Verb DE weak, regular → participle gehüpft, perfect with sein · PT-BR pular, regular -ar · ES-MX brincar, spelling c→qu before e (brinqué)
hüpfen

Die Kinder hüpfen in Gummistiefeln von Pfütze zu Pfütze.

Gestern ist sie den ganzen Nachmittag auf dem Sofa gehüpft.

Hüpf nicht so wild, du machst dich nass!

to hop / to jump

The kids hop from puddle to puddle in their rubber boots.

Yesterday she jumped on the sofa all afternoon.

Don't jump around so wildly, you'll get wet!

pular

As crianças pulam de poça em poça com as botas de borracha.

Ontem ela pulou no sofá a tarde inteira.

brincar

Los niños brincan de charco en charco con sus botas de hule.

Ayer ella brincó en el sillón toda la tarde.

¡No brinques así, te vas a mojar!

Brincar is not brincar Same word, two meanings. In Brazil, brincar means "to play", especially for children. Priberam gives "Divertir-se" and "Entreter-se com alguma coisa infantil". In Spanish, brincar means "to hop": SpanishDict gives to jump, to skip and to hop. An example sentence from there: "The children skipped and played." If you say "As crianças brincam" in Brazil, they play. If you say "Los niños brincan" in Mexico, they hop. "To play" in Spanish is jugar. By the way: the children's theater "Casa de Brinquedos" in Londrina means "toy house" — o brinquedo comes from brincar. priberam.org · spanishdict.com

The words for a long weekend when nobody knows whether it will rain

Why two weather services do not say the same thing: An 81% chance of rain does not mean it rains for 81% of the day. It means: on eight to nine out of ten days with a weather pattern like this, it rains at some point at the location. Climatempo and HG Brasil give different forecasts, so for Sunday they give 81% and 47%. For Saturday the gap is even bigger: Climatempo gives 1.5 mm, HG Brasil about 37 mm. The honest answer is: open. climatempo.com.br

It's Wednesday, November eighteenth. Today's rotation topic is family and everyday life, and the theme is a long weekend when nobody knows if it's going to rain. Ten entries, including three verbs with full conjugation and two reviews.

Here's the backdrop. The family brief calls for showers this afternoon and evening. Friday is Consciência Negra, a holiday, so the weekend runs Friday through Sunday. For Sunday, Climatempo puts the chance of rain at eighty-one percent, while HG Brasil says just forty-seven. So we start with "to plan for." In Brazil that's planejar, in Mexico planear. Planear is also what they say in Portugal, so in Brazil it sounds foreign.

The first false friend of the day is brincar. In Brazil, brincar means to play. In Mexico, brincar means to hop. To hop in Portuguese is pular. And in German, remember: ich bin gehüpft, with sein, because it's a verb of motion.

The second false friend is constipação. In Portugal, that's a cold. In Brazil, a cold is a resfriado, and constipação means, well, constipation. So "to catch a cold" is resfriar-se in Portuguese and resfriarse in Spanish, with an accent: yo me resfrío.

We also have drizzle, a garoa in Brazilian Portuguese and la llovizna in Spanish. The weather forecast is a previsão do tempo and el pronóstico del tiempo. Rubber boots are botas de borracha, and in Mexico, botas de hule. We reviewed the rain shower and the umbrella. Tomorrow, the topic switches to soccer and sports.
