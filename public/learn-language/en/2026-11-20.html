  • GENDER a carona ≠ o carona — feminine, it is the ride („Viagem gratuita em qualquer veículo"); masculine, it is the person who rides along („Pessoa que viaja sem pagar passagem"). dicio.com.br
  • FALSE FRIEND levar carona ≠ getting a ride — according to Dicio, levar ou tomar carona means „Ser rejeitado, ser caloteado": to be turned away or cheated. If you want a ride, say pegar uma carona. dicio.com.br
  • FALSE FRIEND el tope (ES-MX) — in Mexico this is the speed bump on the road. In the rest of the Spanish-speaking world you mostly know tope as an upper limit. dem.colmex.mx
  • CONJUGATION Sie ist verreist. — the perfect tense of verreisen with sein often describes a state: she is away right now. mitfahren also takes sein: ich bin mitgefahren. verbformen.de · verbformen.de
  • CONJUGATION que eles viajem ≠ a viagem — the verb is spelled with j, the noun with g. Both sound the same. conjugacao.com.br
  • BR-PT o porta-malas ↔ la cajuela — the trunk is literally "bag carrier" in Brazil and "little box" in Mexico. Spain says el maletero. dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx

Two verbs for the long weekend: mitfahren, verreisen

2 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked against verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

Why „mit" moves to the end of the sentence: Think of mitfahren as a verb in two parts held together by Velcro. In the infinitive they stick together: Ich will mitfahren. But as soon as the verb is conjugated and sits in position two, the Velcro rips open. The part fahre stays up front, and the part mit slides to the end: Ich fahre am Freitag mit. In the perfect tense, the ge slips into the middle: mitgefahren. verreisen has no Velcro. The prefix ver- is sewn on tight: Ich verreise, ich bin verreist — without ge. Portuguese and Spanish do not know this trick. There, a whole expression carries the meaning: pegar carona, irse de aventón.
Verb DE strong (a–u–a), separable → fuhr mit, mitgefahren, perfect with sein · EN to ride along, irregular (ride–rode–ridden) · PT-BR pegar carona, regular with spelling change g→gu (peguei) · ES-MX irse de aventón, irregular (ir)
mitfahren

Kann ich am Freitag bei dir nach Ourinhos mitfahren?

Meine Schwägerin ist mit den Nachbarn an die Küste mitgefahren.

Ich würde gern mitfahren, aber das Auto ist schon voll.

to ride along

Can I ride along with you to Ourinhos on Friday?

My sister-in-law rode along with the neighbors to the coast.

I'd love to ride along, but the car is already full.

pegar carona

Posso pegar uma carona com você até Ourinhos na sexta?

Minha cunhada pegou carona com os vizinhos até o litoral.

Eu pegaria carona com prazer, mas o carro já está cheio.

irse de aventón

¿Me puedo ir contigo de aventón a Ourinhos el viernes?

Mi cuñada se fue de aventón con los vecinos a la costa.

Con gusto me iría de aventón, pero el carro ya va lleno.

„carona" and „aventón" — who gives, who takes Dicio explains a carona as „Viagem gratuita em qualquer veículo; boleia: pegar uma carona". Boleia is the word from Portugal; in Brazil you hear carona. The direction decides the verb: according to Dicio, dar carona means „Conduzir gratuitamente alguém em um veículo qualquer" — that is, to give someone a ride. Pegar carona means to ride along. Be careful with levar carona: it means „Ser rejeitado, ser caloteado". Mexico works the same way. The DEM explains aventón as „Transporte gratuito que ofrece alguna persona a otra" and lists dar aventón, pedir un aventón and irse de aventón. The shared core: a free ride among acquaintances. Anyone who pays for it uses a Mitfahrgelegenheit (carpool ride) in German — you know that one from Jul 24. dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx

Verb DE weak, inseparable → verreiste, verreist (without ge-), perfect with sein · EN to travel, regular (US traveled) · PT-BR viajar, regular · ES-MX viajar, regular
verreisen

Über das lange Wochenende verreisen viele Familien aus Ribeirão Claro.

Die Nachbarn sind verreist, deshalb gießen wir ihre Pflanzen.

Wenn wir mehr Zeit hätten, würden wir bis an die Küste verreisen.

to travel

Many families from Ribeirão Claro travel over the long weekend.

The neighbors are away, so we're watering their plants.

If we had more time, we would travel all the way to the coast.

viajar

Muitas famílias de Ribeirão Claro viajam no feriadão.

Os vizinhos estão viajando, por isso a gente está regando as plantas deles.

Se a gente tivesse mais tempo, viajaria até o litoral.

viajar

Muchas familias de Ribeirão Claro viajan durante el puente.

Los vecinos andan de viaje, por eso les estamos regando las plantas.

Si tuviéramos más tiempo, viajaríamos hasta la costa.

„verreist sein" — away, not en route Verbformen lists sein as the auxiliary verb: ich bin verreist. But sentence two is not a report about a trip; it describes a state: the neighbors are simply not there right now. English says to be away here, not have traveled. Brazil uses the progressive form: estão viajando. Mexico likes andan de viaje — andar instead of estar sounds perfectly everyday there. Watch the spelling in PT: the subjunctive verb is que eles viajem with j, the noun a viagem with g. Conjugacao.com.br lists viajem for eles in the Presente do Subjuntivo. Sentence three shows the conditional: viajaria, viajaríamos. verreisen last appeared in the brief on Jul 15. verbformen.de · conjugacao.com.br

The participle has no ge- because ver- is inseparable. EN: travel – traveled – traveled (UK travelled). PT and ES: regular in -ar; in PT, viajamos is the same in the present and the preterite. verbformen.de · conjugacao.com.br · reverso.net

In the car: passenger, trunk, speed bump

Three new words for the road — with two Brazilian-Mexican pairs

GENDER Noun DE m, pl. - (f die Beifahrerin) · EN the passenger (up front: front-seat passenger) · PT-BR o carona, m — a carona, f, is the ride · ES-MX el copiloto, m
der Beifahrer

Der Beifahrer liest die Karte, und ich fahre.

Als Beifahrerin schläft sie immer nach zehn Minuten ein.

the passenger

The passenger reads the map, and I drive.

As a passenger, she always falls asleep after ten minutes.

o carona

O carona lê o mapa, e eu dirijo.

De carona, ela sempre dorme depois de dez minutos.

el copiloto

El copiloto lee el mapa y yo manejo.

De copiloto, siempre se queda dormida a los diez minutos.

One word, two genders In Dicio, carona appears twice. As a feminine noun it is the free ride; as a masculine noun it is the „Pessoa que viaja sem pagar passagem". So o carona is literally the one who rides along for free. In everyday speech, many Brazilians use it for anyone sitting next to the driver. Mexico borrows a word from aviation: el copiloto. Note the driver verbs in sentence one: Brazil says dirigir, Mexico manejar. And in sentence two, Mexico prefers se queda dormida ("stays asleep") over se duerme. dicio.com.br
BR-PT Noun DE m, pl. Kofferräume · EN the trunk (US; UK boot) · PT-BR o porta-malas, m, plural unchanged · ES-MX la cajuela, f (Spain: el maletero)
der Kofferraum

Passt die Kühlbox noch in den Kofferraum?

Die Koffer liegen schon im Kofferraum, wir können los.

the trunk

Does the cooler still fit in the trunk?

The suitcases are already in the trunk, we can go.

o porta-malas

A caixa térmica ainda cabe no porta-malas?

As malas já estão no porta-malas, dá para ir.

la cajuela

¿Todavía cabe la hielera en la cajuela?

Las maletas ya están en la cajuela, ya nos podemos ir.

„porta-malas" — the bag carrier The word is built like its German counterpart: a mala is the suitcase, porta comes from portar, to carry. Dicio explains it as „Parte do veículo […] em que se acondiciona a bagagem" and lists it as „substantivo masculino de dois números" — that is, o porta-malas and os porta-malas. It also notes that for a car one says „simplesmente mala". In Brazil, though, you almost always hear porta-malas. Mexico says la cajuela, which the DEM defines as „Compartimento de un automóvil en donde se guardan maletas, herramientas y otros objetos". The cooler is a caixa térmica in Brazil and la hielera in Mexico. dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx
FALSE FRIEND Noun DE f, pl. -n · EN the speed bump · PT-BR a lombada, f (also o quebra-molas, m) · ES-MX el tope, m — elsewhere mostly "upper limit"
die Bremsschwelle

Vor der Schule gibt es drei Bremsschwellen hintereinander.

Fahr langsam, da vorne kommt eine Bremsschwelle!

the speed bump

There are three speed bumps in a row in front of the school.

Slow down, there's a speed bump up ahead!

a lombada

Na frente da escola tem três lombadas seguidas.

Vai devagar, tem uma lombada ali na frente!

el tope

Frente a la escuela hay tres topes seguidos.

¡Ve despacio, ahí adelante hay un tope!

„tope" — limit or bump The DEM describes the Mexican tope as „Obstáculo en forma de bordo o de boyas metálicas que se pone sobre el asfalto", built „para evitar que los automóviles excedan el límite de velocidad". Its example sentence fits ours exactly: „Pusieron topes a la salida de la escuela para proteger a los niños". The DEM also lists the meaning "upper limit" — that is how you know the word from the rest of the Spanish-speaking world, for example la fecha tope. Brazil says a lombada, literally a "long ridge in the ground". In Dicio you will find a 2010 Folha report on it: „uma lombada em um quarteirão é o suficiente para o carro duplicar o gasto de combustível". O quebra-molas, the "spring breaker", is just as common. English in Britain has a nice image for it: sleeping policeman. dem.colmex.mx · dicio.com.br

Back on Sunday: drive back, toll plaza, semi-truck, bus station

One new word and three refreshers from Oct 30 and Nov 6

Noun DE f, pl. -en · EN the return trip / the drive back · PT-BR a volta, f · ES-MX el regreso, m
die Rückfahrt

Für die Rückfahrt am Sonntag rechnen wir mit viel Verkehr.

Auf der Rückfahrt halten wir in Ourinhos zum Mittagessen.

the drive back

For the drive back on Sunday, we're expecting a lot of traffic.

On the way back, we'll stop in Ourinhos for lunch.

a volta

Na volta, no domingo, a gente espera muito trânsito.

Na volta, a gente para em Ourinhos para almoçar.

el regreso

Para el regreso del domingo esperamos mucho tráfico.

De regreso, paramos en Ourinhos a comer.

„a volta" and „el regreso" — two verbs behind them Behind both nouns stands a verb for coming back. Dicio explains a volta as „Ato ou efeito de voltar, de retornar ao local de onde partiu; regresso: viagem de volta ao Brasil". The DEM explains el regreso as „Acto de regresar" and lists el viaje de regreso. In everyday speech, Mexico likes regresar where Spain often says volver. Careful in PT: dar uma volta is a walk or a spin around the block, not a drive back. You know the round trip from May 29: ida e volta, ida y vuelta. In PT sentence two, you see a gente para: in Brazil, a gente often replaces nós in conversation and takes the third-person singular verb form. dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx
REVIEW Noun DE f, pl. -n · EN the toll booth / toll plaza · PT-BR a praça de pedágio, f · ES-MX la caseta (de cobro), f · refreshed from Oct 30
die Mautstelle

Am Feiertag wird die Schlange an der Mautstelle riesig.

Kann man an der Mautstelle mit Karte zahlen?

the toll booth

On the holiday, the line at the toll booth gets huge.

Can you pay by card at the toll booth?

a praça de pedágio

No feriado, a fila na praça de pedágio fica enorme.

Dá para pagar com cartão no pedágio?

la caseta

En el puente, la fila en la caseta se pone enorme.

¿Se puede pagar con tarjeta en la caseta?

Quick refresher — with „fica" and „se pone" On Oct 30, the toll booth was about small change. Today it is about becoming: German says "wird riesig", Brazil fica enorme, Mexico se pone enorme. Both verbs otherwise mean "stay" and "put", but here they mean "become" for a state that changes. And the question about the card: Brazil casually asks Dá para …? ("Is it possible to …?"), Mexico impersonally with ¿Se puede …?. Remember: o pedágio in Brazil is the toll and often the toll booth itself, too.
REVIEW Noun DE m, pl. Sattelzüge · EN the semi-truck / tractor-trailer · PT-BR a carreta, f · ES-MX el tráiler, m · refreshed from Oct 30
der Sattelzug

Der Sattelzug hat für die Kurve beide Spuren gebraucht.

Der Fahrer des Sattelzugs hat uns zum Überholen ein Zeichen gegeben.

the semi-truck

The semi needed both lanes to take the curve.

The semi driver signaled for us to pass.

A carreta precisou das duas faixas para fazer a curva.

O motorista da carreta fez sinal para a gente ultrapassar.

Quick refresher — lane and signal On Oct 30, the focus was the trap carreta ≠ carretera. Today two words for the traffic lane join in: a faixa in Brazil, el carril in Mexico. You give a signal with fazer sinal in Brazil and hacer señas in Mexico. To pass is ultrapassar and rebasar. The driver in Mexico is often el chofer, stressed on the e. In Spain you mostly hear chófer, stressed on the o.
REVIEW Noun DE m, pl. Busbahnhöfe · EN the bus station · PT-BR a rodoviária, f · ES-MX la central de autobuses / la terminal, f · refreshed from Nov 6

the bus station

The bus station is packed; everyone wants to go to the beach.

I'll pick you up at the bus station at eight.

a rodoviária

A rodoviária está lotada, todo mundo quer ir para a praia.

Eu te pego na rodoviária às oito.

Quick refresher — „pegar" and „camión" On Nov 6, rodoviária came from a rodovia, the highway. Today a verb from above returns in PT sentence two: Eu te pego means "I'll pick you up" — so pegar can also mean to pick up. Mexico says pasar por alguien for that. And the bus? In Mexico it is often el camión. The DEM explains the word as „Vehículo automotor de cuatro o más ruedas que se utiliza para transportar pasajeros o carga" and lists camión de pasajeros and tomar un camión. For Brazilians, by contrast, o caminhão is only the truck. dem.colmex.mx · dicio.com.br

To finish: sich auf die Socken machen

An idiom for the moment when you set off

Idiom DE colloquial · EN to hit the road · PT-BR pegar a estrada, colloquial · ES-MX salir a carretera
sich auf die Socken machen

Es ist fünf Uhr — machen wir uns auf die Socken!

Nach dem Mittagessen machen wir uns auf die Socken nach Hause.

to hit the road

It's five o'clock — let's hit the road!

After lunch, we'll hit the road home.

pegar a estrada

São cinco horas — bora pegar a estrada!

Depois do almoço, a gente pega a estrada de volta.

salir a carretera

Son las cinco — ¡a salir a carretera!

Después de comer, salimos a carretera de regreso.

Socks, road, highway Duden lists the phrase as „sich auf die Socken machen (umgangssprachlich: aufbrechen [um irgendwohin zu gehen])". English hits the road, Brazil "takes" it — again with pegar, the verb of the day. Pegar a estrada is colloquial; Priberam from Portugal does not list it. There you only find tomar a estrada a, and that means something quite different: to get ahead of someone. Bora in PT sentence one is the spoken short form of vamos embora, "let's go". Mexico says salir a carretera neutrally; you already know la carretera from the semi-truck. Have a good trip — and a calm drive back on Sunday. duden.de · priberam.org

It's Friday, November twentieth. Today is a holiday in Brazil, the Day of Zumbi and Black Consciousness. It has been a nationwide day off since a law passed in December of twenty twenty-three. Because the day falls on a Friday this year, there's a long weekend running through Sunday. The rotating topic is travel and traffic, installment thirteen. Today there are ten entries, including two verbs with full conjugation and three refreshers.

The first verb is mitfahren, to ride along. The "mit" moves to the end of the sentence, and the perfect tense uses sein: ich bin mitgefahren. In Brazil you say pegar carona, in Mexico irse de aventón. Direction matters. Dar carona means to give someone a ride, pegar carona means to catch one. But be careful: according to the Dicio dictionary, levar carona means being turned away or cheated.

The second verb is verreisen, to travel. Here, too, the auxiliary is sein, but it's often a state: the neighbors are verreist, meaning they're simply away. In Portuguese and Spanish, the word is viajar. Watch the spelling in Portuguese. The verb form que eles viajem is spelled with a j, while the noun a viagem is spelled with a g.

In the car, there's a trick with gender. A carona is the ride, o carona is the person riding along. In Mexico, the person next to the driver is el copiloto. The trunk is o porta-malas in Brazil, literally the bag carrier, and la cajuela in Mexico.

The false friend of the day is el tope. In Mexico, it's the speed bump in front of the school. In the rest of the Spanish-speaking world, you mostly know tope as an upper limit. In Brazil, the speed bump is a lombada or o quebra-molas, the spring breaker.

For Sunday, you learn the drive back: a volta in Brazil, el regreso in Mexico. Plus three refreshers. The toll booth is a praça de pedágio and la caseta. The semi-truck is a carreta and el tráiler. And the bus station is a rodoviária and la central de autobuses. By the way, in Mexico the bus is often called el camión.

To finish, an idiom: sich auf die Socken machen. In English, to hit the road. In Portuguese, pegar a estrada, and in Mexican Spanish, salir a carretera. Have a great trip into the long weekend. Tomorrow the topic switches to food and cooking.
