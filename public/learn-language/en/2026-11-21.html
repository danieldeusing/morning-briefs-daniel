Three Pot Verbs: to Bring to a Boil, to Cover, to Thicken

3 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked against verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

Why "auf" jumps to the end of the sentence: Picture the verb aufkochen as a train with two cars: auf in front, kochen behind. In the infinitive the cars are coupled together. In a normal sentence German uncouples them: the car kochen moves to slot two, and the little car auf rolls all the way to the end. That gives "Ich koche die Bohnen kurz auf" (I briefly bring the beans to a boil). In the perfect tense the ge sits in the middle, between the cars: auf-ge-kocht, ab-ge-deckt, ein-ge-dickt. Portuguese and Spanish don't have these trains. There, each of the three verbs is a single word that never splits: ferver, tampar, engrossar.
Verb DE separable, weak → kochte auf, aufgekocht, perfect with haben (according to verbformen.de, some forms also with sein) · EN to boil / to bring to a boil, regular · PT-BR ferver, regular · ES-MX hervir, irregular: e→ie / e→i
aufkochen

Koch die Brühe kurz auf und stell dann die Hitze klein.

Ich habe das Wasser schon aufgekocht.

Sobald der Eintopf aufkocht, kommt der Deckel drauf.

to boil

Bring the broth to a boil, then turn the heat down.

I've already boiled the water.

As soon as the stew boils, the lid goes on.

ferver

Deixa o caldo ferver e depois abaixa o fogo.

Eu já fervi a água.

Assim que o ensopado ferver, você coloca a tampa.

hervir

Deja que hierva el caldo y luego bájale al fuego.

Ya herví el agua.

En cuanto hierva el guisado, le pones la tapa.

One verb, two stems Hervir is one of the verbs whose stem changes with the stress. Under stress, the e becomes ie: hiervo, hierves, hierven. In some unstressed forms, the e becomes i: hirvió, hirvieron, in the subjunctive hirvamos, in the gerund hirviendo. For nosotros in the present, the stem stays plain: hervimos. The Brazilian ferver, by contrast, is regular. In the third sentence both languages use the subjunctive after "as soon as": assim que ferver is the future subjunctive, en cuanto hierva the present subjunctive. reverso.net

One letter separates a lid from a slap The verb and the noun look alike: Brazil says tampar and a tampa, Mexico tapar and la tapa. If you ask for a tapa in Brazil with good Spanish, you're asking for something else. The Dicio explains tapa as "Golpe aplicado com a mão; bofetão, bofetada" — a blow with the hand. For the lid you need the m: a tampa da panela. One consolation: the verb form ele tapa of tapar also exists in Portuguese, in the sense of "to cover up, to close". dicio.com.br · dicio.com.br

Verb DE separable, weak → dickte ein, eingedickt, perfect with haben (according to verbformen.de, some forms also with sein) · EN to thicken, regular · PT-BR engrossar, regular · ES-MX espesar, regular (no stem change)
eindicken

Lass die Soße ohne Deckel noch zehn Minuten eindicken.

Ich dicke den Eintopf mit einer zerdrückten Kartoffel ein.

Hast du die Bohnenbrühe mit Mehl eingedickt?

to thicken

Let the sauce thicken for another ten minutes without the lid.

I thicken the stew with a mashed potato.

Did you thicken the bean broth with flour?

engrossar

Deixa o molho engrossar mais dez minutos sem a tampa.

Eu engrosso o ensopado com uma batata amassada.

Você engrossou o caldo do feijão com farinha?

espesar

Deja que la salsa espese otros diez minutos sin la tapa.

Yo espeso el guisado con una papa machacada.

¿Espesaste el caldo de los frijoles con harina?

Dick, grosso, espeso — three images German takes the adjective dick ("thick") and builds ein-dicken from it. Portuguese takes grosso ("thick") and makes en-gross-ar. Spanish takes espeso and makes espesar. If you know the adjective, you can guess the verb. Watch out with espesar: it looks like a stem-changing verb but is regular — yo espeso, not *espieso. Mexican usage in the second sentence too: la papa instead of la patata. reverso.net

The Stew for the Rainy Weekend

Five new words around the pot — from the lid to the leftovers the next day

Why a word doesn't fit exactly: Picture three kitchens where the same fire burns, but each cooks its own favorite dish. In the German pot simmers an Eintopf: everything together in broth, often with beans or lentils. In the Brazilian pot braises an ensopado: small pieces of meat slowly cooked with vegetables. In the Mexican pot sits a guisado: the main dish, usually meat in a sauce. The three words overlap a lot, but they don't match completely. That's why you learn the word people in each kitchen actually say here, not a word-for-word translation.
Noun DE m, pl. Eintöpfe · EN the stew · PT-BR o ensopado, m · ES-MX el guisado, m
der Eintopf

Bei Regen gibt es bei uns einen Eintopf.

Der Eintopf schmeckt am zweiten Tag noch besser.

the stew

When it rains, we make a stew.

The stew tastes even better on the second day.

o ensopado

Quando chove, a gente faz um ensopado.

O ensopado fica ainda melhor no segundo dia.

el guisado

Cuando llueve, hacemos un guisado.

El guisado sabe todavía mejor al segundo día.

What the dictionaries say The DWDS describes the Eintopf as "in einem Topf zubereitete, meist dickflüssige Suppe" (a mostly thick soup prepared in one pot) — and, of all things, gives Brazil as its example: "Die Feijoada, das brasilianische Nationalgericht – ein Eintopf aus schwarzen Bohnen mit Pökelfleisch". The Dicio explains ensopado as "Carne bem temperada, cortada em pequenos pedaços, para cozer a fogo lento". The Mexican dictionary DEM calls the guisado "Platillo principal o fuerte de la comida, generalmente preparado con carne acompañada de alguna salsa". And before everything goes into the pot: sweating onion and garlic until translucent is called refogar in Brazil (learned on July 4), acitronar in Mexico. dwds.de · dicio.com.br · dem.colmex.mx · dem.colmex.mx

Cadê? — the Brazilian search question The Dicio describes a tampa as "Peça móvel com que se tapa ou cobre a abertura" of a container, with the example tampa da panela. The first PT sentence has cadê: that's how people in Brazil casually ask "where is …?". It's shorter than onde está. Mexico sticks with ¿dónde está?. For the pot, Brazil says a panela, Mexico la olla. dicio.com.br
Noun DE m, pl. Schnellkochtöpfe · EN the pressure cooker · PT-BR a panela de pressão, f · ES-MX la olla express / la olla de presión, f
der Schnellkochtopf

Im Schnellkochtopf sind die Bohnen in einer halben Stunde weich.

Öffne den Schnellkochtopf erst, wenn der Dampf raus ist.

the pressure cooker

In the pressure cooker, the beans are soft in half an hour.

Only open the pressure cooker once the steam is out.

a panela de pressão

Na panela de pressão, o feijão fica macio em meia hora.

Só abre a panela de pressão depois que o vapor sair.

la olla express

En la olla express, los frijoles quedan suaves en media hora.

Abre la olla express solo cuando ya haya salido el vapor.

A pot with a valve The Mexican DEM lists the entry "olla express u olla de presión" and explains it as a pot with a "tapa que cierra herméticamente y una válvula por la que sale gradualmente el vapor". Food cooks faster in it because the steam pressure means "se alcanzan temperaturas muy elevadas". The DEM spells express with two s. The PT sentence has macio — the same word that came up on Nov. 14 for tender meat. dem.colmex.mx

a pinch

Add a pinch of salt to the stew.

A pinch of sugar takes the acidity out of the tomato sauce.

a pitada

Coloca uma pitada de sal no ensopado.

Between two fingers A pinch is as much as fits between thumb and index finger. All three languages also use the word figuratively, for "a tiny bit": eine Prise Humor, uma pitada de humor, una pizca de humor ("a pinch of humor"). Memory aid for Spanish: pizca sounds like "a pinch" as in a little pinprick. Mexican usage in the first sentence: ponle — the le points to the guisado.
Noun DE m, usually pl. die Reste · EN the leftovers · PT-BR as sobras, f pl. · ES-MX las sobras, f pl. (from the feast: el recalentado, m)
die Reste

the leftovers

Ainda tem muita sobra da festa.

Las sobras del guisado nos las comemos mañana.

Todavía hay muchas sobras de la fiesta.

Mexico has a word for the day after For the meal the day after a celebration, Mexico has its own word. The DEM explains el recalentado as "Comida que se hace para aprovechar los sobrantes de la reunión del día anterior" and gives the example: "¿Nos vemos mañana para el recalentado?" Literally it means "the reheated". In Brazil the sobras usually end up in the marmita for the next day's lunch. The second PT sentence has tem instead of há: that's how people in Brazil casually say "there is/are". dem.colmex.mx

At the Table: One Idiom and Two Reviews

For the guest who always finds something — and two words from Nov. 14

BR-PT Idiom DE colloquial · EN to nitpick · PT-BR procurar pelo em ovo (Brazil) · ES buscarle tres pies al gato
das Haar in der Suppe suchen

Das Essen war super — hör auf, das Haar in der Suppe zu suchen.

Mein Schwager sucht bei jedem Rezept das Haar in der Suppe.

to nitpick

The food was great — stop nitpicking.

My brother-in-law nitpicks every recipe.

A comida estava ótima — para de procurar pelo em ovo.

Meu cunhado procura pelo em ovo em toda receita.

La comida estuvo buenísima, ya no le busques tres pies al gato.

Mi cuñado le busca tres pies al gato a cada receta.

Hair, egg, cat — three images for the same grumbler The DWDS explains the German image as "in etwas insgesamt Gutem, Gelungenem nach einem Anlass für Kritik suchen" (to look for a reason to criticize in something that is good and successful overall). Wiktionary marks procurar pelo em ovo as "(Brazil, idiomatic)": "to search for problems or faults where there isn't any real or relevant one". An egg has no hair — so the search is pointless. The Spanish image works the same way: Wiktionary translates buscarle tres pies al gato as "to look for something that doesn't exist, split hairs". In the PT sentence, note para without an accent: that's the imperative of parar, "stop". dwds.de · wiktionary.org · wiktionary.org

Quick refresher — and the opposite of "thicken" On Nov. 14 the sauce came with the false friend salsa: in Brazil that's parsley, and the sauce is called o molho. Today it needs the verb from above: o molho engrossa, la salsa espesa. If the sauce is too thin, Brazil says ralo, Mexico aguado — literally "watered down". You know köcheln ("to simmer") in the first DE sentence from Nov. 7.

It's Saturday, November twenty-first. Today's rotation topic is food, cooking and restaurant, installment thirteen. It's thirty degrees Celsius today, with rain coming in the afternoon. That's stew weather. There are eleven entries, three of them verbs with full conjugation, plus two reviews.

The first verb is aufkochen, to bring to a boil. It's separable: ich koche die Brühe auf, ich habe sie aufgekocht. In Portuguese you say ferver, completely regular. In Mexican Spanish it's hervir, and that one is irregular: yo hiervo, él hirvió, que nosotros hirvamos.

The second verb is abdecken, to cover, tampar in Portuguese and tapar in Spanish. Here's the false friend of the day. In Mexico, la tapa is the lid. In Brazil, a tapa is a slap with the hand. The lid there is a tampa, with an m. The third verb is eindicken, to thicken, engrossar in Portuguese and espesar in Spanish. Espesar looks irregular, but it isn't: yo espeso.

Then the stew. In Brazil they say o ensopado, in Mexico el guisado. Along with it, the pressure cooker, a panela de pressão and la olla express. A pinch is uma pitada and una pizca. The leftovers are las sobras in both languages. And Mexico has its own word for the meal the day after the feast: el recalentado.

The idiom of the day: das Haar in der Suppe suchen, to look for a hair in the soup. In Brazil you look for a hair in the egg, procurar pelo em ovo. In Spanish you look for three feet on the cat, buscarle tres pies al gato. Finally, two reviews: the sauce, o molho and la salsa, and tender, macio and suave.
