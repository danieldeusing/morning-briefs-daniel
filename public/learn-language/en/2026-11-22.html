Political vocabulary: rejeitar, sancionar, o projeto de lei

Sunday, November 22 — rotation topic Politics, Law & Health, installment twelve. The occasion is in Brasília: the Senate has pushed through the savings and tax package PLP 299/2026, first by 45 to 31 and, according to Diário de Pernambuco, by 69 to 2 in an extra round on 11/18. The Chamber of Deputies is due to vote this week. If it changes the text, it goes back to the Senate. After that, the president has to sign. You learn exactly this path today: reject, amend, sign into law. Plus a vaccine word and a very Brazilian idiom. 12 entries, including 3 verbs with full conjugation, among them 2 reviews.

  • FALSE FRIEND sancionar (PT) ≠ sanktionieren (DE) — when Lula sanciona a law, he signs it and puts it into force. The German “sanktionieren” now mostly means “to punish”. dicio.com.br · dwds.de
  • CONJUGATION rechazar → yo rechacé, que rechace — before e, z becomes c. The Brazilian rejeitar stays regular: eu rejeitei. reverso.net · conjugacao.com.br
  • CONJUGATION modifiqué, promulgué — the same trap twice: c→qu and g→gu before e, so the sound stays the same. reverso.net · reverso.net
  • BR-PT acabar em pizza = “to fizzle out” (German: „im Sande verlaufen“), literally “to end in pizza”. The phrase comes from football: a crisis meeting at Palmeiras ended without a solution, but with pizza. wiktionary.org
  • BR-PT a pauta = the agenda. Plus the verb pautar: “to put on the agenda”. dicio.com.br
  • GENDER el orden del día (m) ≠ la orden (f) — masculine means “order, sequence”, feminine means “command”. The agenda is therefore always el orden del día.

Three law verbs: reject, amend, sign

3 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked against verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

How a law travels through Brasília: Imagine a letter that two people have to sign one after the other, and at the end the boss puts his stamp on it. The Senate has already signed the letter. Now the Chamber of Deputies reads it. It can reject it (rejeitar): then it lands in the wastebasket. It can amend it (alterar): then it goes back to the Senate, which has to review the changes. Or it accepts it as it is. In the end the letter lies on the president’s desk. He signs it (sanciona), or he vetoes it (veta, learned on 11/15). With these three verbs you can read almost any politics headline of the week.

ablehnen

to reject

The Senate rejected the amendment.

The opposition rejects the austerity package.

If the chamber rejects the text, the bill is shelved.

rejeitar

O Senado rejeitou a emenda.

A oposição rejeita o pacote de corte de gastos.

rechazar

El Senado rechazó la enmienda.

La oposición rechaza el paquete de recortes.

A letter that jumps In Spanish, z never stands before e or i. That is why you write rechacé and, in the subjunctive, rechace, rechacemos, even though the sound stays the same. The Brazilian rejeitar does not have this trap. In the third PT sentence, notice se a Câmara rejeitar: that is the Futuro do subjuntivo, which no longer exists in everyday Spanish. Mexico simply uses the present there: si la Cámara rechaza. You learned the opposite of reject on 11/15: to approve, aprovar, aprobar. reverso.net · conjugacao.com.br

Nachbessern is more than changing In German, nachbessern carries a reproach: the first version was not good enough. Alterar and modificar are neutral, they simply mean “to change”. In the press you often read the noun instead: a Câmara fez emendas ao texto. In Spanish the rule from above applies again, this time with c: before e it becomes qu, so modifiqué and que modifique. Without this change you would read “modifizé”. verbformen.de · reverso.net

sancionar ≠ sanktionieren The Brazilian dictionary Dicio explains sancionar as “Aprovar uma lei; dar sanção a; validar”. It lists no meaning “to punish”. The DWDS does give sanktionieren the sense “etw. gutheißen, anerkennen” (to approve), but in law mainly “Strafmaßnahmen verhängen” and “Verstöße … ahnden” (to impose penalties, to punish violations). A German speaker can easily misread “Lula sanktioniert das Gesetz”. The counterpart is a sanção: the president’s signature. For Mexico we use promulgar, “to proclaim”. That keeps the sentence unambiguous. Watch promulgué: g becomes gu before e, otherwise it would sound like “promulché”. dicio.com.br · dwds.de · reverso.net

Substance and procedure German separates two things: der Impfstoff is what is in the syringe, die Impfung is the procedure. Portuguese and Spanish usually use a vacina and la vacuna for both. For the procedure there are a vacinação and la vacunación. In everyday speech you say tomar a vacina in Brazil, and ponerse la vacuna or vacunarse in Mexico. According to Brasil de Fato, Anvisa approved the dengue vaccine Butantan-DV in November 2025 for people aged 12 to 59, distributed only through the SUS. brasildefato.com.br

The path of a law: bill, amendment, majority

Five new words for the voting week in the Câmara

Why 45 votes are enough and 31 are not: Imagine a school class with 81 kids. For a normal question, it is enough if more kids say yes than no. For an important question a stricter rule applies: more than half of the whole class must say yes, no matter how many are absent. That is the absolute majority, a maioria absoluta. A complementary law, lei complementar, is one of the important questions. The Senate has 81 seats, so you need 41 yes votes. With 45 it worked. In the Câmara, with 513 seats, the number is 257.
Noun DE m, pl. Gesetzentwürfe · EN the bill · PT-BR o projeto de lei, m · ES-MX la iniciativa de ley, f
der Gesetzentwurf

Der Gesetzentwurf liegt jetzt bei der Abgeordnetenkammer.

Die Regierung hat einen neuen Gesetzentwurf vorgelegt.

the bill

o projeto de lei

O projeto de lei agora está na Câmara dos Deputados.

O governo apresentou um novo projeto de lei.

la iniciativa de ley

La iniciativa de ley ahora está en la Cámara de Diputados.

El gobierno presentó una nueva iniciativa de ley.

PL, PLP, PEC Brazilian headlines rarely spell out the whole word. PL is a normal projeto de lei. PLP is a projeto de lei complementar, like the savings package PLP 299/2026. PEC is a proposta de emenda à Constituição, a constitutional amendment. In Mexican Spanish the bill is usually called iniciativa; the DEM lists iniciativa de ley as a fixed expression. The mistake for German speakers: o projeto is not a “Projekt” in the sense of a construction project, but here a bill. dem.colmex.mx · diariodepernambuco.com.br

Three lives of emenda In Brazil, a emenda has two further meanings. Emendas parlamentares are budget funds that deputies earmark for their region — a perennial topic of every budget week. And the verb emendar means “to connect” in everyday speech: emendar o feriado is the long weekend (bridge day). Watch the n in Spanish: enmienda, not “emienda”. And note the image in derrubar: an amendment is not “rejected”, it is “knocked over”.

The rule from the constitution Article 69 of the Brazilian constitution says: “As leis complementares serão aprovadas por maioria absoluta.” That is why, for the savings package, it is not only yes against no that counts, but yes against the total number of seats. Watch the spelling: PT maioria with i, ES mayoría with y and an accent. The opposite is a minoria and la minoría. planalto.gov.br

The fast-lane word Regime de urgência is a procedure in Brasília: deadlines get shorter, and the text can go to the floor without a long committee round. That is why “Câmara aprova urgência” is not yet a vote on the law itself, only on the speed. Note direto (PT) and directo (ES): Spanish keeps the c. diariodepernambuco.com.br

Pauta, pautar, em pauta Dicio explains pauta as “Relação dos assuntos a serem discutidos numa reunião”. Brazilian newspapers turn it into a verb: Motta pautou o projeto means “Motta put the bill on the agenda”. Em pauta means “currently under discussion”. Fourteen hours, eighteen pizzas Wiktionary marks acabar em pizza as “(Brazil, informal)” and explains it in politics like this: “to be closed without being fully resolved”. The phrase comes from a headline of the 1960s: “Crise do Palmeiras termina em pizza”. The club leadership sat together for fourteen hours, found no solution and in the end ordered eighteen pizzas. The German “im Sande verlaufen” fits the course of events, but not the mockery: in Brazil there is an undertone that the guilty get away unpunished. For a dropped case file, Spanish also has dar carpetazo, “to shelve”. wiktionary.org · wiktionary.org

It’s Sunday, November twenty-second. Today’s rotation topic is politics, law and health, installment twelve. The occasion comes from Brasília: the Senate has pushed the savings package through, and the Chamber of Deputies is due to vote this week. There are twelve entries, including three verbs with full conjugation and two reviews.

The first verb is to reject. In Portuguese you say rejeitar, completely regular. In Mexican Spanish it’s rechazar, and there a letter jumps: yo rechacé, with a c instead of a z. The second verb is to amend, in Portuguese alterar, in Spanish modificar. Again a letter jumps: yo modifiqué.

The third verb brings the false friend of the day. When Lula sanciona a law, he signs it. The German word sanktionieren, by contrast, mostly means to punish. In German you therefore say unterzeichnen. In Mexico they say promulgar, with the form yo promulgué.

Now the path of a law. The bill is called o projeto de lei in Brazil and la iniciativa de ley in Mexico. The amendment is a emenda and la enmienda. And emendar o feriado means taking a long weekend in Brazil. A complementary law needs an absolute majority, a maioria absoluta. In the Senate, with eighty-one seats, that’s forty-one votes. According to Diário de Pernambuco, the text passed forty-five to thirty-one, and in the extra round sixty-nine to two.

Urgency is a urgência. The regime de urgência is the fast lane to the floor. The agenda is a pauta in Brazil. In Spanish it’s el orden del día, masculine, because la orden is a command.

For health, you learn the vaccine, a vacina and la vacuna. According to Brasil de Fato, the Butantan dengue vaccine is available to people aged twelve to fifty-nine, only through the SUS.

The idiom of the day is very Brazilian: acabar em pizza, to end in pizza. It’s what you say about a scandal that fizzles out with no consequences. It goes back to a crisis meeting at Palmeiras that ended with pizza instead of a solution. Finally, two reviews: the vote, a votação, and the fiscal target, a meta fiscal.
