Friday, November 27. The family brief has a drive to Londrina on Sunday. It did not check today whether the BR-153, BR-369 or PR-092 are closed anywhere; it advises looking at the map app beforehand.   • FALSE FRIEND überholen ≠ to overhaul — on the road, English says to pass. Overhaul only fits the workshop meaning, which the DWDS explains as "to check for faults and restore, renew". dwds.de

Two Verbs for the Country Road: to Pass, to Swerve

2 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked against verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

Why "überholen" is two verbs: Last Friday it was about the Velcro strip in mitfahren: the part mit rips off and moves to the end of the sentence. With überholen you get both. Stress the Ü and it is Velcro: Der Fährmann holt uns über, perfect übergeholt. Per the DWDS, that means "to carry across to the other shore". Stress the hol and the prefix is sewn on tight: Das Auto überholt uns, perfect überholt, without ge-. In road traffic you only need the sewn-on variant. Ausweichen, on the other hand, is always Velcro: Ich weiche aus, ich bin ausgewichen. Portuguese and Spanish don't know the trick. There, ultrapassar and desviar are simply one verb. dwds.de
FALSE FRIEND Verb DE weak, inseparable → überholte, hat überholt (without ge-), perfect with haben · EN to pass (also to overtake), regular · PT-BR ultrapassar, regular · ES-MX rebasar, regular (pan-Spanish also adelantar)
überholen

Überhol den Lkw erst nach der Kurve, vorher siehst du nichts.

Auf der BR-369 hat uns ein Motorrad rechts überholt.

Wenn die Linie nicht durchgezogen wäre, würde ich jetzt überholen.

to pass

Don't pass the truck until after the curve — you can't see anything before that.

On the BR-369, a motorcycle passed us on the right.

If the line weren't solid, I would pass now.

ultrapassar

Só ultrapassa o caminhão depois da curva, antes não dá para ver nada.

Na BR-369, uma moto ultrapassou a gente pela direita.

Se a faixa não fosse contínua, eu ultrapassaria agora.

rebasar

No rebases el tráiler hasta después de la curva; antes no se ve nada.

En la BR-369, una moto nos rebasó por la derecha.

Si la línea no fuera continua, rebasaría ahora.

"überholen" and "overhaul" — the same only in the workshop The DWDS lists three meanings. First, vehicles: "to run or drive past someone or something and leave them behind", with the example "im Straßenverkehr wird links überholt". Second, figuratively: to surpass someone "in performance". Third, technical: to check a vehicle "for faults and restore, renew" it. Only this third meaning is the English to overhaul. On the road, you say to pass. The rule from the DWDS example also holds in Brazil: the Código de Trânsito says the ultrapassagem "deverá ser feita pela esquerda, obedecida a sinalização regulamentar". So sentence two describes a violation. In everyday speech Mexico says rebasar; Motorpasión headlines "Rebasar por la derecha en carretera está prohibido en México". The solid line in sentence three is a faixa contínua in Brazil. dwds.de · planalto.gov.br · motorpasion.com.mx

Verb DE strong (ei–i–i), separable → wich aus, ausgewichen, perfect with sein, object in the dative · EN to swerve (around), regular · PT-BR desviar (de), regular · ES-MX desviarse, regular with accent (me desvío)
ausweichen

Auf der PR-092 weichen alle den Schlaglöchern aus.

Weich dem Hund aus, aber brems nicht zu hart!

Der Lkw-Fahrer ist dem Motorrad im letzten Moment ausgewichen.

to swerve around

On the PR-092, everyone swerves around the potholes.

Swerve around the dog, but don't brake too hard!

The truck driver swerved around the motorcycle at the last moment.

desviar

Na PR-092, todo mundo desvia dos buracos.

Desvia do cachorro, mas não freia muito forte!

O caminhoneiro desviou da moto no último momento.

desviarse

En la PR-092, todos se desvían para no caer en los baches.

¡Desvíate para no pegarle al perro, pero no frenes tan fuerte!

El chofer del tráiler se desvió en el último momento para no pegarle a la moto.

Dative, "de" and "se" — three ways to swerve In German you swerve to a thing, in the dative: dem Hund, den Schlaglöchern. Brazil attaches the preposition de: desviar do cachorro, desviar dos buracos. Mexico makes the verb reflexive: me desvío, se desvió. In the PT sentence two, note the everyday form: for the command to você, many Brazilians simply use the present form desvia, freia. The textbook imperative would be desvie, freie. In sentence two, Mexico says pegarle for "to hit, to strike". And look closely at the present tense: eu desvio and yo me desvío are spelled like the noun for the detour, which comes further down. verbformen.de · conjugacao.com.br

When the Road Is Shut: Closure, Detour

Two new words for the traffic report — and one word with a false bottom

Noun DE f, pl. -en (fully closed: die Vollsperrung) · EN the closure / road closure · PT-BR a interdição, f · ES-MX el cierre, m (fully closed: el cierre total)
die Sperrung

Wegen einer Sperrung auf der BR-153 stehen wir seit einer Stunde.

Die Sperrung gilt bis Sonntagabend.

the closure

Because of a closure on the BR-153, we've been stuck for an hour.

The closure is in effect until Sunday evening.

a interdição

Por causa de uma interdição na BR-153, a gente está parado há uma hora.

A interdição vale até domingo à noite.

el cierre

Por un cierre en la BR-153, llevamos una hora parados.

El cierre es hasta el domingo en la noche.

"interditar a pista" — how the report sounds Dicio explains the verb interditar tersely as "Tornar ou declarar interdito. Proibir." A Folha example from 2012 shows the road meaning: the traffic authority CET "vai interditar a rua até a remoção da árvore na pista". That holds a second word for Sunday: a pista in Brazil is the roadway, not the ski slope. In the report you mostly hear the participle: a pista está interditada. Mexico uses the noun el cierre, from cerrar. Milenio headlined: "Accidente múltiple provoca el cierre total de la carretera" — that is the full closure. ES sentence one has llevamos una hora: with llevar plus a time expression you say "for … already". dicio.com.br · milenio.com
FALSE FRIEND Noun DE f, pl. -en · EN the detour · PT-BR o desvio, m — in the news often "embezzlement" · ES-MX el desvío, m (also la desviación) · ES-MX road sense not cross-checked in a dictionary (DEM and RAE blocked)
die Umleitung

Die Umleitung führt durch das Dorf und kostet uns zwanzig Minuten.

Folg einfach den Schildern für die Umleitung.

the detour

The detour goes through the village and costs us twenty minutes.

Just follow the detour signs.

o desvio

O desvio passa pelo vilarejo e faz a gente perder vinte minutos.

É só seguir as placas do desvio.

el desvío

El desvío pasa por el pueblo y nos hace perder veinte minutos.

Nada más sigue los letreros del desvío.

"desvio" — detour or embezzlement The noun comes from the verb above: desviar → o desvio. Dicio gives as its first meaning "Mudança de direção ou de posição" — a change of direction, so also the detour. But it also lists "Subtração fraudulenta: desvio de material". Its example sentence is about the "suposto esquema de desvio de recursos em contratos de empréstimos consignados". When the news talks about desvio de verbas, it means embezzled money, not a road. In Mexico, traffic reports also write rutas alternas, for example EjeCentral with "cierres viales y rutas alternas según CAPUFE". Watch the form: alterna, not alternativa. ES sentence two has nada más for "just, only". dicio.com.br · ejecentral.com.mx

On the Route: Construction Zone, Pothole

Two obstacles that wait on every country road in the Norte Pioneiro

Noun DE f, pl. -n · EN the construction zone (building: construction site) · PT-BR a obra, f (on signs often plural: obras) · ES-MX la obra, f (official: zona de obras)
die Baustelle

Vor der Brücke ist eine Baustelle, da ist nur eine Spur frei.

Auf der Baustelle wird sogar am Samstag gearbeitet.

the construction zone

There's a construction zone before the bridge; only one lane is open.

They're working in the construction zone even on Saturday.

a obra

Antes da ponte tem obra, só uma faixa está liberada.

Na obra tem gente trabalhando até no sábado.

la obra

Antes del puente hay obras; nada más hay un carril abierto.

En la obra trabajan hasta los sábados.

"obra" — from the artwork to the bridge repair Obra first means "work", as in obra de arte. But Dicio also lists two construction meanings: "Edifício em construção" and "Reparos de certo vulto, em prédio, pontes, viadutos, estradas etc." The second one is the construction zone on the country road. In Mexico, a standard of its own regulates the signs for it. The NOM-086-SCT2-2023 is titled "Señalamiento y dispositivos para protección en zonas de obras viales". For the traffic lane, Brazil says a faixa, Mexico el carril — you know both from 11/20. New is liberada: a lane that is liberada is "cleared for use". dicio.com.br · economia.gob.mx
Noun DE n, pl. Schlaglöcher · EN the pothole · PT-BR o buraco, m (repair: o tapa-buraco) · ES-MX el bache, m
das Schlagloch

Nach dem Regen ist die Straße voller Schlaglöcher.

Ich bin in ein Schlagloch gefahren, und jetzt ist der Reifen platt.

the pothole

After the rain, the road is full of potholes.

I hit a pothole, and now the tire is flat.

o buraco

Depois da chuva, a estrada está cheia de buracos.

Caí num buraco e agora o pneu furou.

el bache

Después de la lluvia, la carretera está llena de baches.

Caí en un bache y ahora se me ponchó la llanta.

"buraco" is any hole, "bache" only the one in the road Portuguese has no word of its own for the pothole. Dicio explains buraco quite generally as "Qualquer abertura num corpo; furo, orifício". The road is hidden in the word for the repair: o tapa-buraco, which Dicio calls colloquially a "Conserto no asfalto que consiste em, literalmente, tapar os buracos que nele aparecem". Spanish is more precise. The RAE explains bache as "Hoyo o desigualdad en el pavimento de calles, carreteras o caminos" (wording from the search result; the RAE page itself was blocked). ES sentence two holds two Mexican tire words: la llanta for the tire and ponchar for "to flatten" — both not cross-checked in a dictionary today. dicio.com.br · dicio.com.br · rae.es

Quick Refresher: Shoulder, Roundabout, Return Traffic

Three words from 10/30 and 11/06 — now with today's verbs

REVIEW BR-PT Noun DE m, pl. unchanged · EN the shoulder · PT-BR o acostamento, m (Portugal: a berma) · ES-MX el acotamiento, m · refreshed from 10/30 and 11/06
der Seitenstreifen

Bei einer Panne hältst du auf dem Seitenstreifen und stellst das Warndreieck auf.

Auf dem Seitenstreifen wird nicht überholt!

the shoulder

If you break down, pull over onto the shoulder and set up the warning triangle.

No passing on the shoulder!

o acostamento

Se o carro quebrar, pare no acostamento e coloque o triângulo.

Não se ultrapassa pelo acostamento!

el acotamiento

Si se te descompone el carro, oríllate en el acotamiento y pon el triángulo.

¡No se rebasa por el acotamiento!

Quick refresher — with "orillarse" On 11/06 came the official definition from Mexico. Today the Brazilian one: Priberam marks acostamento with "[Brasil]" and explains it as "Superfície da via pública, à direita da faixa de rodagem, destinada à paragem ou à passagem de veículos". Sentence two links the word to the verb of the day: ultrapassar and rebasar. New in ES sentence one is orillarse, "to pull over to the edge", from la orilla. Motorpasión adds that "te puedes detener en el acotamiento sin riesgo a ser multado". For a breakdown, Brazil says o carro quebrou, Mexico se descompuso el carro. priberam.org · motorpasion.com.mx
REVIEW Noun DE m, pl. -e · EN the roundabout (US also traffic circle) · PT-BR a rotatória, f (Portugal: a rotunda) · ES-MX la glorieta, f · refreshed from 11/06
der Kreisverkehr

Nimm im Kreisverkehr die zweite Ausfahrt Richtung Londrina.

Am Kreisverkehr vor der Stadt staut es sich jeden Sonntag.

the roundabout

At the roundabout, take the second exit toward Londrina.

Every Sunday, traffic backs up at the roundabout outside town.

a rotatória

Na rotatória, pegue a segunda saída sentido Londrina.

Todo domingo forma fila na rotatória da entrada da cidade.

la glorieta

En la glorieta, toma la segunda salida hacia Londrina.

Todos los domingos se hace fila en la glorieta a la entrada del pueblo.

Quick refresher — "sentido" instead of "direção" On 11/06 it was about where the words come from: rotatória from "turning", glorieta from the round square in the park. Today, direction. On Brazilian signs and in conversation you often see sentido: sentido Londrina, "toward Londrina". Mexico says hacia. PT sentence one also holds pegue, the polite command form of pegar — with the gu from 11/20. And the traffic jam? Brazil and Mexico both simply say "a line forms": forma fila, se hace fila.
REVIEW Noun DE m, no pl. · EN return traffic · PT-BR o retorno, m · ES-MX el regreso, m · refreshed from 10/30 and 11/06
der Rückreiseverkehr

Wenn wir vor fünf losfahren, entgehen wir dem Rückreiseverkehr.

Im Rückreiseverkehr überholen viele viel zu riskant.

return traffic

If we leave before five, we'll miss the return traffic.

In the return traffic, a lot of people pass far too recklessly.

o retorno

Se a gente sair antes das cinco, escapa do retorno.

No retorno, muita gente ultrapassa de um jeito muito arriscado.

el regreso

Si salimos antes de las cinco, nos libramos del tráfico de regreso.

En el regreso, mucha gente rebasa de forma muy arriesgada.

Quick refresher — the drive back or return traffic On 11/20 came die Rückfahrt: your own trip home, a volta and el regreso. The return traffic is everyone else who wants to get home at the same time. Mexico uses el regreso for both; in sentence one, el tráfico de regreso makes the difference clear. PT sentence one has the futuro do subjuntivo: se a gente sair, not se a gente sai. And once more the trap from 11/06: on a road sign, Retorno is the turnaround point, not return traffic.

It's Friday, November twenty-seventh. The family brief has a drive to Londrina on Sunday. It didn't check today whether any road along the way is closed. So today we cover the words for the country road. The topic is travel and transport, episode fourteen, with nine entries, two of them verbs and three refreshers.

The first verb is überholen, to pass. In Portuguese it's ultrapassar, in Mexican Spanish rebasar. Careful with English: to overhaul only fits the workshop, when an engine is rebuilt. On the road, you say to pass. Brazilian traffic law says you pass on the left. And in Mexico, too, passing on the right on a highway is prohibited.

Überholen has a twin. Stress the first syllable and it's separable, and it means ferrying someone across to the other shore. For the car, you only need the fixed form: the car passed us, without the ge.

The second verb is ausweichen, to swerve. It's strong and separable, and it forms the perfect with sein: ich bin ausgewichen. In the past tense it's du wichst aus and ihr wicht aus. Brazil says desviar de, as in desviar dos buracos. Mexico makes the verb reflexive: me desvío.

When the road is shut, Brazilians talk about the interdição, and the roadway is called a pista. The question for Sunday morning is: A pista está interditada? In Mexico, a closure is el cierre, and a full closure is el cierre total.

The false friend of the day is o desvio. On a sign, that's the detour. In the news, though, desvio often means someone embezzled money or materials. In Mexico, the detour is el desvío, and traffic reports talk about rutas alternas.

Along the way, the construction zone and the pothole are waiting. The construction zone is obra in both languages. The pothole is simply o buraco in Brazil, the hole. In Mexico, it's el bache. To finish, three refreshers: the shoulder, that's o acostamento and el acotamiento, the roundabout, that's a rotatória and la glorieta, and return traffic. Safe travels on Sunday.
