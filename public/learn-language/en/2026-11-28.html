Two verbs, two levels of heat: sweat, sear

2 verbs with full conjugation · DE · EN · PT-BR · ES-MX · cross-checked at verbformen.de, conjugacao.com.br and conjugator.reverso.net

Sweat or sear? The color decides. Picture the onion in the pan. Over medium heat with little fat, it turns soft and translucent but not brown. That is andünsten. Over high heat, meat gets a brown crust and smells roasted. That is anbraten. For the brown stage, Portuguese and Spanish say "gild": dourar comes from ouro, dorar from oro, and both mean gold. For the pale stage, Brazil says refogar. Dicio explains it as "Passar os temperos (cebola, alho, tomate etc.) por gordura fervente". That is exactly how almost every rice dish in Brazil begins: first refogar the garlic, then add the rice. dicio.com.br
Verb DE weak, separable → dünstete an, hat angedünstet, perfect with haben · EN to sauté (gently), regular · PT-BR refogar, regular with g → gu before e · ES-MX sofreír, irregular (like reír)
andünsten

Dünste zuerst Knoblauch und Zwiebel in etwas Öl an.

Für den Reis habe ich nur den Knoblauch angedünstet.

Wenn du die Zwiebel zu heiß andünstest, wird sie braun statt glasig.

to sauté

First, gently sauté the garlic and onion in a little oil.

For the rice, I only sautéed the garlic.

If you sauté the onion over too high a heat, it turns brown instead of translucent.

refogar

Primeiro refoga o alho e a cebola num pouco de óleo.

Para o arroz, eu só refoguei o alho.

Se você refogar a cebola em fogo muito alto, ela fica marrom em vez de transparente.

sofreír

Primero sofríe el ajo y la cebolla en un poco de aceite.

Para el arroz, solo sofreí el ajo.

Si sofríes la cebolla a fuego muy alto, se dora de más en vez de quedar transparente.

Three words, not quite the same heat Refogar and andünsten go well together: spices and onions briefly in hot fat, without color. Sofreír is allowed a bit more. Wiktionary explains it as "Cocinar un alimento calentándolo brevemente con poco aceite o grasa", in such a way "que la comida se dore sin pegarse a la sartén". If you explicitly want "translucent" in Mexico, say so: hasta que esté transparente. In PT sentence one, you see again the everyday command to você using the present-tense form: refoga instead of the textbook refogue. PT sentence three contains the future subjunctive from Friday: se você refogar, not se você refoga. wiktionary.org · dicio.com.br

DE: weak and separable. Because the stem ends in -t, an e is inserted: du dünstest an, er dünstet an, ihr dünstetet an. Subjunctive II and preterite are identical; in everyday speech people say ich würde andünsten. EN: sauté – sautéed – sautéed, the accent stays. PT: regular, but before e, g becomes gu: refoguei, refogue, refoguemos. Refogamos is the same in the present and the preterite. ES: like reír and freír. The stressed-stem forms carry the accent on the í (sofrío, sofríe, sofríen). In the preterite, the e drops out in the 3rd person: sofrió, sofrieron. Subjunctive nosotros: sofriamos, without an accent. Two participles: sofreído and sofrito; according to Wiktionary, sofrito is used more often as an adjective. verbformen.de · conjugacao.com.br · reverso.net

Verb DE strong (a–ie–a), separable → briet an, hat angebraten, umlaut in the present (du brätst an), perfect with haben · EN to brown, regular · PT-BR dourar, regular · ES-MX dorar, regular (no o → ue)
anbraten

Brat das Fleisch bei hoher Hitze von allen Seiten an.

Gestern habe ich die Zwiebeln zu lange angebraten.

Er brät den Speck an, bevor er die Bohnen in den Topf gibt.

to brown

Brown the meat on all sides over high heat.

Yesterday I browned the onions for too long.

He browns the bacon before he adds the beans to the pot.

dourar

Doura a carne de todos os lados em fogo alto.

Ontem eu dourei a cebola por tempo demais.

Ele doura o bacon antes de colocar o feijão na panela.

dorar

Dora la carne por todos lados a fuego alto.

Ayer doré la cebolla demasiado tiempo.

Él dora el tocino antes de echar los frijoles a la olla.

The umlaut moves, the "o" stays In German, the vowel jumps: ich brate an, but du brätst an and er brät an. In the preterite it becomes briet, and in the participle angebraten again. In Spanish it is the other way around, and quiet. Dorar looks like a stem-changing verb following the pattern dormir → duermo, but it is not. Reverso lists it under the regular pattern amar: yo doro, not *yo duero. The same applies to dourar: eu douro, ele doura. In PT sentence one, you see again the everyday imperative doura. In ES sentence three: in Mexico, bacon is el tocino and beans are los frijoles. verbformen.de · reverso.net · conjugacao.com.br

What goes into the pot: broth, savory

Two new words — and two words with a false bottom

Noun DE f, pl. -n · EN the broth · PT-BR o caldo, m · ES-MX el caldo, m
die Brühe

Gieß die Linsen mit einem Liter Brühe auf.

Bei einer Erkältung hilft eine heiße Hühnerbrühe.

the broth

Pour a liter of broth over the lentils.

When you have a cold, a hot chicken broth helps.

o caldo

Cubra as lentilhas com um litro de caldo.

Quando a gente está resfriado, um caldo de galinha quente ajuda.

el caldo

Cubre las lentejas con un litro de caldo.

Cuando estás resfriado, un caldo de pollo bien caliente ayuda.

"caldo de cana" — not a broth Dicio lists caldo with two meanings. The first is our broth: "Alimento líquido resultante do cozimento de carne e/ou legumes em água". The second is a surprise: "Sumo, suco extraído de certas frutas: caldo de cana". So anyone who orders um caldo de cana at the Feira gets fresh sugarcane juice and not soup. Also watch the animal: chicken broth is caldo de galinha in Brazil and caldo de pollo in Mexico. dicio.com.br
FALSE FRIEND BR-PT Adjective DE adjective · EN savory (not hearty) · PT-BR salgado · ES-MX salado
herzhaft

Zum Frühstück esse ich lieber etwas Herzhaftes als etwas Süßes.

Willst du die Füllung süß oder herzhaft?

savory

For breakfast, I'd rather have something savory than something sweet.

Do you want the filling sweet or savory?

salgado

No café da manhã, eu prefiro algo salgado a algo doce.

Você quer o recheio doce ou salgado?

salado

En el desayuno prefiero algo salado que algo dulce.

¿Quieres el relleno dulce o salado?

"herzhaft" is not "hearty" — and "salado" has bad luck DWDS knows two sides of herzhaft. For laughing, it means "tüchtig, kräftig, gehörig" (hearty, vigorous, proper), as in "ein herzhaftes Gelächter". There, a hearty laugh fits. For food, it means "von kräftigem Geschmack, würzig" (of strong flavor, spicy/seasoned), as in "ein herzhaftes Essen". In English that is savory. A hearty meal, by contrast, is a generous, filling meal. Portuguese and Spanish simply use salt for "herzhaft". Dicio also lists the noun o salgado for Brazil, "O mesmo que salgadinho": coxinha and the like at a party. Colloquially it means "Exagerado: preço um tanto salgado" — like our English "steep" prices. Wiktionary has one more meaning for Latin America: a person is salada if they "tiene mala suerte". dwds.de · dicio.com.br · wiktionary.org

Advent and table culture: mulled wine, mealtime, oversalted soup

A word for tomorrow, a greeting for lunchtime and an idiom with three ingredients

Noun DE m, pl. -e · EN mulled wine · PT-BR o vinho quente, m · ES-MX el ponche, m — in Mexico a hot fruit punch, not wine
der Glühwein

Morgen ist der erste Advent, dann gibt es bei uns Glühwein.

Bei dreißig Grad im Schatten trinkt hier keiner Glühwein.

mulled wine

Tomorrow is the first Sunday of Advent, so we'll have mulled wine.

Nobody here drinks mulled wine when it's thirty degrees in the shade.

o vinho quente

Amanhã é o primeiro domingo do Advento, então a gente vai tomar vinho quente.

Com trinta graus na sombra, ninguém aqui toma vinho quente.

el ponche

Mañana es el primer domingo de Adviento, así que vamos a tomar ponche.

Con treinta grados a la sombra, aquí nadie toma ponche.

Glühwein in June, Ponche for the Posada Mulled wine exists in Brazil too, just in a different season. Portuguese Wikipedia describes vinho quente as "bebida quente de origem europeia feita com vinho tinto e especiarias". In Southern Brazil it is a "mistura aquecida de vinho, gengibre, açúcar e especiarias como cravo e canela", and it is "tradicionalmente servida durante as quermesses e festas juninas". June is winter there. In December, Mexico drinks something else hot: el ponche. According to Spanish Wikipedia, it contains "trozos de caña de azúcar, manzana, guayaba, tejocotes, jamaica, tamarindo, canela y ciruelas pasas", and it is made "para las posadas, que son las fiestas de los nueve días previos a la Navidad". So the wine is missing. pt.wikipedia.org · es.wikipedia.org

"Mahlzeit!" — a greeting from the 18th century DWDS explains the office greeting as "Gruß zur Mittagszeit und vor oder nach dem Essen" (a greeting at midday and before or after eating). It comes from "gesegnete Mahlzeit!", which arose in the 18th century from "Gott segne die Mahlzeit". That is why people say it in the hallway too, without a plate in hand. Brazil and Mexico, by contrast, wish something only to the person who is eating right then: Bom apetite! and ¡Buen provecho!. Wiktionary explains buen provecho as a wish that something agrees with you, and adds: "Dícese frecuentemente de la comida o bebida." And the casual, ironic "prost Mahlzeit!" means roughly "well, isn't this a fine mess", according to DWDS. dwds.de · wiktionary.org
IDIOM Idiom DE colloquial · EN to rain on someone's parade · PT-BR melar (os planos, a festa), informal · ES-MX aguarle la fiesta a alguien

to rain on someone's parade

The neighbor wants to rain on our parade with his complaint.

Don't let the weather rain on your parade, we're grilling anyway.

melar a festa de alguém

El vecino nos quiere aguar la fiesta con su queja.

No dejes que la lluvia te agüe la fiesta; vamos a hacer carne asada de todos modos.

Salt, honey, water — three ways to spoil something Literally, versalzen means "zu stark salzen; durch Zufügen von zu viel Salz verderben" (to salt too heavily; to ruin by adding too much salt), according to DWDS. DWDS explains the idiom as "etw., das jmdm. wichtig ist, hintertreiben, zu vereiteln suchen" (to undermine or try to thwart something that matters to someone). The participle has two forms: versalzt and versalzen. In the kitchen, people mostly say die Suppe ist versalzen. Brazil spoils with honey: melar means "Adoçar, untar ou cobrir com mel" according to Dicio. In everyday use it means "to sabotage"; Folha wrote that a group "queria melar a convenção". Mexico spoils with water: aguar means to dilute. Wiktionary gives the figurative meaning as "raro fuera de la expresión fija aguar la fiesta". ES sentence two contains the subjunctive agüe: the diaeresis dots on the ü make sure you pronounce the u. dwds.de · dicio.com.br · wiktionary.org

"propina" — thanks in Mexico, caution in Brazil Spanish Wiktionary explains propina as "Pequeña cantidad de dinero que se da a cambio de un servicio además e independientemente del precio o salario convenido". It's Saturday, November twenty-eighth. Tomorrow is the first Sunday of Advent. Today's topic is food, cooking and restaurants, installment fourteen, with ten entries, two of them verbs and three of them refreshers.

The two verbs are two levels of heat in the pan. In German, andünsten means softening the onion over medium heat until it's translucent. In Brazil that's refogar, and it's how almost every pot of rice begins. In Mexico you say sofreír. The verb works like reír: yo sofrío, él sofrió.

Anbraten is the hot stage, with the brown crust. Portuguese says dourar, Spanish says dorar. Both come from the word for gold. And dorar stays regular: yo doro, not yo duero. In German, by contrast, the vowel jumps: ich brate an, du brätst an, ich briet an.

For the pot, there's broth, which in Portuguese and Spanish is el caldo. Careful at the Brazilian street market: a caldo de cana is fresh sugarcane juice, not soup.

Herzhaft is a half false friend. For laughing, hearty works; for food, you say savory. Portuguese and Spanish go with salt: salgado and salado. In Brazil, o salgado is also the little party snack, and a preço salgado is a steep price.

For Advent, there's Glühwein, or mulled wine. In Southern Brazil they drink vinho quente too, but in June, at the Festas Juninas. In Mexico, December means hot ponche at the Posadas, made with sugarcane, guava and cinnamon, but no wine.

At lunchtime in a German office, people say Mahlzeit. The greeting dates back to the eighteenth century, from gesegnete Mahlzeit, or blessed mealtime. In Brazil people wish you Bom apetite, in Mexico Buen provecho. And if someone oversalts your soup, they're sabotaging your plans. Brazil spoils things with honey, melar. Mexico spoils them with water, aguar la fiesta.

To finish, three refreshers: the stew, the leftovers and the tip. The tip is today's false friend. In Mexico, la propina is the tip. In Brazil, propina can also mean a bribe. Say a gorjeta there instead. Tomorrow, on the first Sunday of Advent, the topic switches to politics, law and health.
