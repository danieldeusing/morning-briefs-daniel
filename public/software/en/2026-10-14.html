Ivanti Connect Secure: Exploited, No Login, No Traces

Ivanti confirms a "limited exploitation in the wild" for CVE-2026-104935 in Connect Secure.[1] According to BleepingComputer, the flaw lets attackers bypass authentication and then run commands on the device. Volexity discovered it. A group Volexity calls UTA0576 chains three flaws up to root privileges, on devices that were fully patched until then.[1] Defused dates the first attacks to 10/01, Volexity to 10/03.[1]

SSL VPN gateway: Connect Secure is the door employees use to get into the company network from outside. Picture the guard at the factory gate: anyone who gets past him is already on the premises. That is exactly why devices like this have been a favorite target of state-backed attackers for years.

The details are what make this unpleasant. According to BleepingComputer, the attackers drop no malware and no web shell, and they tamper with the logs so that Ivanti's own Integrity Checker Tool finds nothing.[1] Shadowserver counts more than 5,500 reachable Connect Secure instances. And for a privilege escalation in the chain, Ivanti is still working on a fix, per BleepingComputer.[1]

According to heise, 25.1.1.1, 25.1.2.2, 22.8R2.4 and older are affected. The fixes are 25.1.1.2, 25.1.3.0, 25.1.3.1 and 22.8R2.5. heise gives a CVSS 3 score of 8.6.[2] BleepingComputer gives no score, but confirms the affected versions, and we have not read the Ivanti advisory itself. CISA added the flaw to the KEV catalog on 10/13; US federal agencies must act by 10/16.[3]

To check If you run Connect Secure, patching alone is not enough. If the device was reachable from the internet since 10/01 and the Integrity Checker is blind, treat it like a possibly compromised system: rotate credentials and certificates, and review connections from the gateway into the network.

My take: this is not the first Ivanti round of its kind, and the pattern stays the same. If you are choosing a VPN gateway today, read the word "appliance" as a risk. A lean WireGuard setup or an overlay like Tailscale has a much smaller attack surface than a web portal with a login sitting in front of the network. The Ivanti October update also contains fixes for Neurons Patch for Intune (CVE-2026-109412) and Xtraction.[4]

Microsoft Patch Tuesday: Record Count, But Which One?

BleepingComputer counts 1,411 closed flaws, 70 of them critical, and calls it a record.[1] The aggregator Senserva arrives at 1,759, but on the same page also lists 1,255. These numbers are not reliable. We did not check Microsoft's own Update Guide. All we know for sure: it was a very large Patch Tuesday.

Three zero-days: CVE-2026-96951, a security feature bypass in the Windows Shell, is already under attack and is in the KEV catalog.[1][2] Publicly known but not exploited were CVE-2026-108398 in the .NET Framework and CVE-2026-92707 in MessagePack-CSharp, a denial-of-service flaw.[1]

Security feature bypass: Windows normally warns you before you open a file from the internet. A bypass makes sure that warning never appears. Like a smoke detector with its battery pulled: it doesn't start the fire, but nobody hears the alarm.

MessagePack-CSharp is the interesting point for .NET developers. The library ships as a NuGet package in many services, often indirectly. If you build .NET, check the lockfile, not Windows Update.

According to BleepingComputer, Home and Pro editions of 24H2 received their last update on 10/13. The path leads to 26H2. Enterprise and Education run until 2027-10-12. Windows 10 Enterprise LTSB 2016 has also been out of support since yesterday.[3]

The deadlines are 2027-05-17 (Server 2016/2019, Windows 10 Enterprise 2019 LTSC) and 2027-06-19 (newer Windows 10 versions, older Windows 11 versions, Server 2022). The July 2026 updates are a prerequisite. According to heise, WSUS is not affected.[4] If you have forgotten Windows VMs in your homelab, run their updates now.

Docker Hub: Other People's Tokens via Account Conversion

The trick was almost elegant. An attacker created an account with a personal access token, converted the account into an organization, and added the victim as owner. Docker Hub attached the token to the victim without changing the secret and without asking. According to BleepingComputer, the token then acted at the registry level as the target user.[1]

Personal access token (PAT): A long password meant only for machines, for example for docker push from CI. Think of a spare key: here you could have your own spare key rewritten to fit someone else's front door, and still keep it.

The bug existed since 2024-08-26. The report came in on 09/30, and per Docker the fix was live in less than four hours. Affected users were notified on 10/02, and transferred tokens were shut off on 10/08. Docker reviewed 304,000 pushes made with transferred tokens and found no exploitation. The flaw is tracked as CVE-2026-105445; Docker rates it "critical," and the report gives no CVSS score.[1]

My take: Docker handled this well, quickly and transparently. Still, the case shows why you don't pull images by name alone. If you pin image@sha256:… in production or verify signatures, you never depended on a bug like this. If you did not get an email from Docker, per Docker you need to do nothing.[1]

SAP Patch Day: CVSS 10 in the Python SDK [1][2]

For its October Patch Day, SAP published 16 new security notes and four GitHub advisories. The most severe flaw is CVE-2026-89111 in the SAP Cloud SDK for Python, CVSS 10.0 per heise, fixed in version 0.45.2. BleepingComputer describes it as an SSRF flaw around OAuth2. According to heise, flaws in NetWeaver Visual Composer (CVE-2026-89110), S/4HANA (CVE-2026-89107) and Manufacturing Integration and Intelligence (CVE-2026-89108) are also critical. No attacks are known.

SSRF (Server-Side Request Forgery): The attacker gets a server to call addresses on their behalf that are not reachable from outside. Like a letter you hand to the building superintendent so he delivers it internally, somewhere you could never reach yourself.

For developers, the SDK is the relevant piece, not the ERP: it is an ordinary PyPI package. A pip list | grep -i sap in BTP projects is enough to check.

SonicWall SMA1000: Attacks Reported [1]

Update since Mon For CVE-2026-102255, the pre-login SSRF flaw in WorkPlace (SMA1000 6210, 7210, 8200v), Defused and Previdian report exploitation attempts against honeypots, per heise. heise gives CVSS 10.0. SonicWall's own advisory SNWLID-2026-0017 still knows of no exploitation, and the flaw is not in the KEV catalog. The hotfixes are 12.4.3-03670 and 12.5.0-03082, and they also close a Zip Slip flaw (CVE-2026-102257) that requires an admin login. If two independent honeypot operators are against you, don't wait for the vendor advisory.

Ghostscript: Server Takeover via PDF Upload

CVE-2026-39919 is a heap overflow in Ghostscript's JPEG2000 adapter before 10.08.0. The flaw has been known since 09/15; BleepingComputer's headline yesterday: "Ghostscript flaw lets hackers hijack servers via PDF uploads."[1] On the score, sources disagree: Wiz says 9.8 (CVSS 3.1), Vulert 7.8. No attacks are known.[2]

Why this affects more people than you might think: Ghostscript often runs invisibly, behind ImageMagick, in thumbnail services, in PDF previews of ticketing and document management systems. Check inside the Docker image, not just on the host. Debian lists the flaw in its own security tracker.[2]

CISA KEV: Old Flaws, New Attacks [1][2]

On 10/08, CISA added five flaws to the KEV catalog, some of them eleven years old: CVE-2015-3306 in ProFTPD, CVE-2015-5477 in ISC BIND, CVE-2016-3081 in Apache Struts, CVE-2021-3199 in ONLYOFFICE Docs and CVE-2023-22894 in Strapi. The deadline ran out on 10/11. heise reported on it Monday.

KEV catalog: The list from the US cybersecurity agency CISA of flaws that are demonstrably being attacked. Like a list of burglary methods the police have already seen at real crime scenes: not theoretical, but documented.

The lesson is old but true: attackers don't scan for the newest CVE, they scan for the forgotten server. A Strapi from 2023 or a BIND from 2015 in the basement is more dangerous than any of today's headlines.

Let's Encrypt: 64 Days in Staging Today [1]

From today, Let's Encrypt's staging environment issues certificates with a 64-day lifetime. In production this becomes the default from 2027-02-10, optionally also 45 or 6 days. The last 90-day certificate expires on 2027-05-11, and the step to 45 days follows from 2028-02-16. Domain validation reuse drops from 30 to 10 days. Caddy renews based on remaining lifetime anyway; Certbot cron jobs with fixed intervals should be tested against staging now.

[1] heise.de (10/12)

EU Cyber Resilience Act: The Ivanti Test [1]

Since 2026-09-11, manufacturers must report exploited flaws to ENISA: early warning within 24 hours, notification within 72 hours, final report 14 days after the fix. ENISA's reporting platform has been running since the same day. Obligations for open-source stewards apply only from 2027-12-11. Ivanti and Microsoft's Shell zero-day are thus the first big cases under the new regime. The reports are not publicly visible, but it will be interesting to see whether the EU comments on a vendor that informs people only after two weeks of attacks.
