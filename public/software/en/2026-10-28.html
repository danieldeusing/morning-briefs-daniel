PHP2Shell: From Noise to Break-In

New since Tuesday On Oct 27, heise summarizes what happened over the weekend. After the emergency patch of Oct 21, there were millions of attack attempts. Amazon and Cloudflare saw them, but for a long time without success. Then came the proof: Adel Karimishiraz, head of detection at Perplexity, runs PHP honeypots and documents 35 launched shells on three honeypots running PHP 8.4.26. The attacks came from two IP addresses.[1]

The situation in Germany remains bad. On Friday, Censys found about 4.2 million vulnerable PHP instances in Germany, and on Tuesday morning still over 4 million. Censys itself warns that some Linux distributions backport fixes into old version numbers. The figure is therefore an indicator, not an exact value.[1]

For context: Recorded Future rates both flaws (CVE-2026-109618, CVE-2026-109619) at 9.2 on CVSS v4.0; we did not check an NVD score. They are fixed in 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27 and 8.5.12, released on Oct 21 at 13:52 UTC. According to Recorded Future, older, unsupported branches should not be considered safe.[2] The CISA deadline for US federal agencies ends tomorrow, Oct 29.[3]

Honeypot: A decoy server deliberately left vulnerable on the internet. It holds nothing valuable, but logs every attack. Picture a car with the door open and a camera inside: when someone climbs in, you know the trick works.
Our View A honeypot proof is not proof of break-ins at real companies. But it shows the attackers found the path to running code, and that is the real threshold. The version is the interesting part: 8.4.26 is the last version before the fix. Anyone saying "we're almost up to date" is exactly the target.

Brotli: Now Officially Exploited

New since Tuesday CISA added CVE-2026-111497 to the KEV catalog on Oct 27, under "Google Brotli Unchecked Return Value Vulnerability." The deadline for US agencies is Nov 10.[1] That changes the status: until now it was described as a dangerous flaw. Now CISA lists it as exploited.

As a reminder: the bug sits in the Brotli decoder. Specially crafted compressed data can write past the end of an internal buffer. Node.js fixed it on Oct 21 in 22.23.4, 24.22.1 and 26.12.1 and rates it high.[2] According to heise, it also affects Bun, Python and other software. On Oct 26, PHP released updated Windows builds because of Brotli.[3][4]

The flaw was reported by the "OpenAI Codex Security" team. What that says about AI as a bug finder is covered in the ai-dev brief.[2]

Our View Brotli is the flaw you miss because it appears in no package.json. It lives in the runtime, in a Python wheel, or in a PHP build for Windows. The inventory matters more here than the patch itself: where in your stack is Brotli used to decompress external data?

Node.js: More Than Just Brotli [1]

The October Node.js release fixes further flaws that get drowned out by the Brotli noise. Two of them (CVE-2026-105483, CVE-2026-106069) are rated high and concern hostname checking in TLS. That means that under certain conditions a client accepts a certificate that does not match the server. On top of that comes CVE-2026-105482 in dns.resolve (high), plus three medium and three low flaws. The fix versions are the same as for Brotli.

Hostname check: A TLS certificate says "I am api.example.com." The client must verify that this matches the address it called. Without that check, it is like an ID card where nobody looks at the name.
[1] nodejs.org (Oct 21)

Adobe Commerce: The Third KEV Entry of the Week [1]

On the same day as Brotli, CVE-2026-48356 in Adobe Commerce and Magento entered the catalog. CISA describes it as unrestricted upload of files of dangerous type. The deadline is likewise Nov 10. Yesterday we reported on 953 Magento shops with a hidden script. Whether the two are connected is not established. Anyone running a shop should check both, though: patch level and foreign files in the upload directory.

[1] cisa.gov KEV (Oct 28)

Apple: Over 150 Fixes, Also for Old Devices [1][2]

Apple released a big round on Oct 26. iOS, iPadOS and macOS 27.2 each fix over 150 issues, according to heise (counted from Apple's release notes). tvOS, watchOS and visionOS 27.2 and Safari 27.2 come along with them. For older systems there are iOS/iPadOS 26.7.2 and 18.7.11, macOS Tahoe 26.7.2 and Sequoia 15.8.2, with fewer fixes, but serious ones according to Apple.

Apple names no flaw as actively exploited. heise considers that incomplete: the company Calif.io has published a jailbreak said to work up to iOS 27.0.1 and 27.1, and plans to follow up with details. We did not check individual CVE ratings.

Our View For developers with test devices sitting in a drawer, this is the relevant news: even iOS 18 still gets fixes. Don't leave old devices unpatched on the Wi-Fi "for testing."
[1] support.apple.com (Oct 26) · [2] heise.de (Oct 27)

Nextcloud and Collabora: Deadline Passed, Web Shells Remain

CVE-2026-110447 sits in the built-in CODE server of Collabora Online (the Nextcloud app richdocumentscode). The score is 9.2 on CVSS v4. The flaw has been fixed since version 26.4.303 of Sep 11. But web shells on Nextcloud servers have existed since Sep 10.[1] CISA made the entry on Oct 23, and the deadline expired on Oct 26. On the same day, CVE-2026-114625 in Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator also entered the catalog.[2] heise reports observed attacks for both.[3]

Heads up For this flaw, an update is not enough. If you ran the built-in CODE server before Sep 11, look for foreign files and unknown processes. A patch closes the door, but doesn't throw out anyone who is already inside.

DuckDB 2.0.0: The First Big LTS [1]

DuckDB 2.0.0 ("Cinnamon Teal") has been out since Oct 21, and heise presented it in detail on Oct 27. It is the biggest feature release so far, with almost 20,000 commits since 1.5. As an LTS it gets support until at least October 2027. The key points:

  • Server mode: with CONNECT you attach remote databases, including PostgreSQL and MySQL. A new optimizer sends queries to the remote server instead of transferring whole tables.
  • Asynchronous I/O for Parquet, CSV and the native format, meant for faster reads from S3 and the like.
  • SQL triggers and a new parser with precise error messages.
  • An agent mode for the CLI with compact output. heise calculates that the savings amount to only about 0.5% of total input.
Heads up Older DuckDB versions cannot read files written by 2.0. The developers advise testing on a shadow system first. The first patch release is planned for Nov 18.
Our View Remote pushdown to PostgreSQL is the interesting piece. For ad-hoc analysis on a production Postgres replica, that makes DuckDB a serious tool. I would still wait for 2.0.1 before letting a pipeline write files in the new format.
[1] heise.de (Oct 27)

CRA: Two Case Studies in Two Days

Since Sep 11, the reporting obligations under Article 14 of the Cyber Resilience Act have applied. For actively exploited flaws, according to law-firm summaries, that means: early warning within 24 hours, notification within 72 hours, final report 14 days after the fix. Reports go through ENISA's Single Reporting Platform.[1] With PHP2Shell and Brotli, two widely used building blocks are now in the KEV. Vendors selling a product with built-in PHP or Brotli in the EU should check their reporting obligation. We did not re-read the legal text itself today.

DuckDB 2.0 and PostgreSQL 19 can wait until November.

Good morning, it's Wednesday, October twenty-eighth. Until the weekend, the PHP flaw known as PHP2Shell was mostly noise, with millions of attack attempts and no proven success. Now there's proof.

According to heise, a security researcher at Perplexity has documented thirty-five launched shells on three decoy servers. All of them ran PHP eight point four point twenty-six, the last version before the fix. The scanner Censys still sees roughly four million vulnerable PHP versions in Germany. The deadline set by the US agency CISA runs out tomorrow.

The second big story is Brotli. On Tuesday, CISA added the flaw in the Brotli decoder to its catalog of exploited vulnerabilities. Node.js already fixed it on October twenty-first, but it also affects Bun, Python and PHP on Windows. An AI security team at OpenAI found it. There's more in the ai-dev brief.

The same day, a flaw in Adobe Commerce and Magento went into the catalog. And for Nextcloud with the built-in Collabora server, an update isn't enough, because attackers have been planting web shells there since September.

Apple released a big round of updates on Monday. iOS, iPadOS and macOS twenty-seven point two each fix more than one hundred fifty issues. Even iOS eighteen and older Macs get fixes. Apple doesn't name any flaw as exploited, but a new jailbreak shows that's not the whole picture.

Now for the good news. DuckDB two point zero is a release with long-term support through October twenty twenty-seven. It can pass queries straight through to PostgreSQL. One caution: older versions can't read the new files. PostgreSQL nineteen arrives on November nineteenth, and version fourteen gets its last update on November twelfth.

My advice for today: check your PHP servers for old versions, do the Brotli inventory, and on Nextcloud, look for foreign files. Tomorrow we'll see how things look after the PHP deadline and whether CISA adds more entries.
