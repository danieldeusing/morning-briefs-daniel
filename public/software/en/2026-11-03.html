Ivanti Connect Secure: The Check Was Green, the Device Wasn't

Integrity Checker Tool: A tool from Ivanti that checks whether foreign files sit on the VPN appliance. Like a night guard who counts whether all the doors are locked. If the burglar locks the door behind him and forges the log, the guard reports "all quiet."

Ivanti has patched two more zero-days in Connect Secure: CVE-2026-111357 (privilege escalation) and CVE-2026-118527 (file upload after login). According to BleepingComputer on Nov. 2, both have been used in attacks "since at least October 1."[1] Volexity observed the group UTA0576, which chains them with the authentication bypass CVE-2026-104935. The result is code execution as root without logging in.[1]

The important sentence comes further down: according to Volexity, the attackers installed neither malware nor a web shell and tampered with the logs. That is how they evaded Ivanti's Integrity Checker Tool.[1] Shadowserver counts over 5,000 instances reachable from the internet; how many of them are patched is not known. Both new flaws have been in the KEV since Oct. 30, with deadlines of Nov. 2 and Nov. 13.[2] The report does not name affected and fixed versions; they are in the Ivanti advisory, which we did not retrieve. We also did not verify CVSS scores for the new flaws.

Our take A clean integrity check proves nothing with this chain. If you had an unpatched device on the network since October, treat it as compromised: rotate credentials, end sessions, compare VPN logs against external sources (firewall, SIEM). And yes, this is the third Ivanti topic in three weeks. Replacement planning belongs on the agenda.

GitLab: Over 1,000 Servers Still Open

CVE-2026-97229 is an integer overflow in the regex compiler of the CI/CD component. According to BleepingComputer, it could let "an unauthenticated user" execute code on the GitLab server.[1] Patches have been available since Oct. 12 in 19.4.2, 19.3.5 and 19.2.8. Even so, Shadowserver, as reported by BleepingComputer, counts over 1,000 vulnerable instances. For the separate flaw CVE-2026-110264 it is even over 11,300.[1] The KEV entry came on Oct. 29, and the deadline ended on Nov. 1.[2]

Our take Three weeks after the patch and with a KEV entry, 1,000 open servers are no coincidence but forgotten self-hosted instances. If you have a GitLab "somewhere in the basement": today is the day to find it. As reported yesterday, Ruby needs updating too for this flaw.

C++26 Has Failed

DIS ballot: DIS stands for "Draft International Standard." Before ISO publishes a standard, the national standards bodies vote on the nearly finished draft. Like the final inspection before moving in: normally a formality. This time the inspector did not sign off on the house.

The ISO ballot on the C++26 draft ended after twelve weeks with "Disapproved," result of Oct. 23.[1] A two-thirds majority and at most one quarter no votes are required; the draft did not manage that. Andreas Fertig writes at heise that, to his knowledge, a DIS ballot went negative for the first time in the history of C++. ISO does not publish official vote counts.[1]

The point of contention is Contracts, meaning preconditions and postconditions directly in code. Some national bodies consider the feature "not yet in a release-ready state." The WG21 committee has four options: a revised draft back to a DIS ballot, back to the committee draft phase, publication as a Technical Specification, or ending the project. In the last case, according to Fertig, C++23 would remain the valid standard and the features would slip to C++29.[1]

The decision comes at the next WG21 meeting starting Nov. 16 in Búzios in the state of Rio de Janeiro. That makes Brazil, of all places, the venue of the most important C++ meeting in years.

Our take For users, little changes in the short term: GCC, Clang and MSVC implement features before ISO publication anyway. The damage is of another kind. A standard that gets stuck on a single feature shows that consensus in the committee is breaking. Our bet: Contracts get dropped or become a Technical Specification, and C++26 arrives late. Fertig himself finds the reasons for the no votes weak: "A new paper with old arguments is not new information to me."
[1] heise.de, Andreas Fertig (Nov. 2)

Infrastructure and Industry

OpenVDDK: VMware's Hidden Library, Rebuilt

VDDK: A VMware library that backup and migration tools use to read virtual disks. Like the key to the storage room: without it, you cannot get to your own boxes.

In August, VMware restricted the VDDK to partners; customers could no longer reach the download, and competitors' migration tools got weaker. Cloud architect Andrija Panić therefore wrote OpenVDDK, a replacement for libvixDiskLib.so.8.0.3 in Go under the Apache license, with no VMware code.[1] It reads disks but does not write, and in tests it ran with vSphere 6.7 through 9.1 as well as with Forklift, Proxmox VE and nbdkit. Panić names a German cloud provider as a user but stresses that these are not independently verified references.[1]

SUSE Cuts About Ten Percent

SUSE CEO Dirk-Peter van Leeuwen announced by email that the global workforce will shrink by "approximately 10 percent," and names "automation and AI" as the driver.[2] Affected employees will find out by Nov. 4, and the fiscal 2027 strategy follows on Nov. 16. SUSE is owned by the investment firm EQT.

Canada Without Daylight Saving, Microsoft in Chaos

Parts of British Columbia, Alberta and Manitoba abolished daylight saving time on Nov. 1. In Outlook, appointments afterward are sometimes an hour off, and only organizers can correct shared appointments.[3] Microsoft's workaround for Power Automate and Fabric: pick a different time zone, for Manitoba for example "Bogota." SharePoint On-Prem gets the correct zones on Nov. 10, Power Pages not until around Dec. 12.[3] Our take: anyone who stores time zones themselves instead of using the IANA database learns here why you don't.

Windows: Smart App Control Can Be Turned On Again

After the October update KB5127648, Smart App Control in Windows 11 24H2 and later was sometimes grayed out. Microsoft fixes this with KB5127936 and updates from Oct. 28.[4]

robots.txt: One Entry for All AI Crawlers

Google plans to honor a vendor-neutral token in robots.txt from 2027 that excludes AI training, according to heise on Nov. 2.[1] The token comes from IETF work. For website operators this would be progress: one line instead of a growing list of bot names. We did not retrieve the syntax details.

Stack Overflow Under 1,000 Questions per Month

In September 2026 the Stack Exchange API counted "only 969 questions"; at the peak in March 2014 it was about 207,000.[2] The community mainly blames "moderation perceived as excessively strict"; AI was "only the last blow." Our take: both are true, and the problem goes deeper. If nobody asks in public anymore, the answers that search engines and models learn from will be missing in the future too.

What Tuesday Is Worth

Three things. First: patch Ivanti devices and, if in doubt, treat them as compromised; a green integrity check is not enough. Second: inventory self-hosted GitLab, WS_FTP and Synology instances, because those are exactly the ones people forget. Third: C++ teams wait for Nov. 16 before putting Contracts on roadmaps.

It's Tuesday, November third. Two stories define the day: the ISO vote on C plus plus twenty-six has failed, and at Ivanti Connect Secure, attackers fooled the vendor's own checking tool.

First, Ivanti. According to BleepingComputer, two new flaws have been used in attacks since at least October first. The group Volexity calls UTA zero five seven six chains them with a known authentication bypass and gets root access without logging in. It installed no malware and forged the logs, so Ivanti's integrity check found nothing. Our take: if you had an unpatched device since October, treat it as compromised.

At GitLab, BleepingComputer says more than one thousand servers are still open to the flaw numbered ninety-seven twenty-two twenty-nine, even though patches have been out since October twelfth. The deadline set by the US agency CISA has passed.

The surprise of the day comes from standards. The draft of C plus plus twenty-six failed the ISO vote, which Andreas Fertig at heise says is a first in the language's history. The sticking point is Contracts. Starting November sixteenth, the committee meets in Búzios, Brazil, to decide whether there will be a new draft or whether the features move to C plus plus twenty-nine.

More security news: Progress is fixing fifteen flaws in its WS FTP file transfer server, one of them with the top score of ten. Veeam is patching its Management Pack, and attacks are under way on Drupal Commerce and on old Synology NAS devices.

On the tools side, there's the first alpha of Node twenty-seven, a toolchain manager called dotnetup for dot NET, and Grafana thirteen point three. A developer has rebuilt VMware's hidden disk library as open source, and SUSE is cutting about ten percent of its jobs.

Two side notes. Parts of Canada have ended daylight saving time, and Microsoft calendars there run an hour off. And Stack Overflow got only nine hundred sixty-nine questions in September.
