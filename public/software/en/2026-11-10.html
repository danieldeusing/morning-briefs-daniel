SSHole: a niche flaw becomes a target

The flaw in OpenSSH before 10.6p2 is now being exploited. According to BleepingComputer, GreyNoise counted the first successful exploitation on Nov 7 at 00:46 UTC. Since then, "16 successful exploitation chains" from six IP addresses have followed. Previdian saw 20 attempts from 13 IP addresses in nine countries. Exploit code appeared about four hours after the fix.[1]

On the mechanics: "An oversized password causes musl's password-hashing function to return * on error, which OpenSSH mistakenly treats as a match."[1] So only systems with musl libc, without PAM and with password login are affected, in practice mostly Alpine. The fix is 10.6p2 from Nov 5.[2][3] The CVSS score is 8.1; it comes from MITRE as CNA, and the NVD merely adopted it.[4]

What attackers do afterward is the usual routine: probe the system and containers, query cloud metadata, read password hashes and root SSH keys, and modify authorized_keys so they can return. One attacker got root and installed a Python C2 agent called "TAC Agent".[1]

PAM: A toolkit in Linux that lets programs verify logins instead of doing it themselves. Without PAM, sshd checks the password directly through the C library. Like a doorman who normally gets the guest list from the office and here has to decide on his own.
Heads up CISA has listed the flaw in the KEV catalog since Nov 9; the deadline for US agencies is Nov 12.[5] Check: sshd -T | grep passwordauthentication. If it says yes, upgrade to 10.6p2 or set PasswordAuthentication no. Either way, restart sshd afterwards: the update does not restart the running service.[3] If you were affected, look for password logins on accounts with "*" in the shadow field, for foreign SSH keys, and for files or services belonging to TAC Agent.[1]
Our take Yesterday we called the flaw "narrow", and that still holds. But narrow does not mean harmless: the affected systems are the ones nobody maintains, such as an old Alpine image used as a bastion. Four hours to exploit is by now the norm, not the exception.

N-able takes N-central offline

On Nov 8 a repo called "Able2Shell" appeared with proof-of-concept code for more than 30 alleged zero-days in N-central, N-able's RMM platform. According to BleepingComputer, N-able then took the hosted environment offline. There are no CVE numbers yet. The researcher, Sean Bales, says he tested against the pre-release version 2026.4.0.31, and the search was AI-assisted. N-able says there is "no evidence of active exploitation". N-able also asks on-prem customers, per BleepingComputer, to shut down their N-central servers until a hotfix ships. BleepingComputer did not verify all of the findings itself.[1]

RMM (Remote Monitoring and Management): Software that IT service providers use to manage many customers' computers remotely and keep them updated. Whoever takes over the RMM gets all the customers at once. Like a master key for every apartment in the building.
Our take Taking it offline is the right call, even without confirmed attacks: a public PoC repo for an RMM platform is an invitation. If a service provider runs N-central for you, ask today whether your instance is hosted or on-prem. On-prem servers are also supposed to stay off for now.
[1] bleepingcomputer.com (Nov 9)

Edge devices and NAS

SonicWall SSL-VPN

CVE-2026-19868 bypasses the SAML checks in the SSL-VPN, including MFA enforced via SAML. According to BleepingComputer: CVSS 7.1; affected are Gen7 up to 7.3.3-7015 and Gen8 up to 8.2.2-8015; fixes in 7.3.4-7013 and 8.2.3-8015; no workaround. The same update closes two management-interface flaws rated 8.8.[1] heise reports renewed attacks on Nov 9.[2]

Plex2Shell on QNAP

According to heise, QNAP is closing the Plex flaws that attackers use to turn NAS devices into crypto miners and proxies; heise speaks of "active and far-reaching exploitation". The most severe is CVE-2026-96656 (high), plus three medium ones. Plex Media Server is vulnerable up to 1.43.2; the fix is 1.43.3.10861.[3]

SAML: A standard that lets a central login service confirm to an application: "This person is genuine." The application then checks no password of its own. Like a visitor badge from reception that every door in the building accepts.

Agent configs as a leak: 2,539 secrets

According to heise, Apiiro examined more than 100,000 copies of configuration files such as .claude/settings.local.json. Result: 2,539 real secrets in 1,994 public repos. In 539 of 1,105 repos examined more closely, the agent appeared as co-author in the commit.[1] The file is meant to be local, but without a .gitignore entry it ends up in the commit, along with the tokens the agent was granted for commands.

Our take This is not an AI problem but an old .env problem in a new file. Two quick steps: add local agent configs to your global .gitignore and run a secret scanner as a pre-commit hook. The tools themselves are a topic for the ai-dev brief.
[1] heise.de (Nov 9)

Outlook.com rejects small senders

According to heise, Outlook.com has been rejecting mail from small senders without DMARC since about Nov 5, with the error 550 5.7.1 and the text "Message lacks SPF/DKIM/DMARC Auth Records". A moderator on the Microsoft support forum classifies it as an outage. The known rule applies only to senders with more than 5,000 mails a day: since May 5, 2025 they need SPF, DKIM and DMARC with at least p=none.[1]

DMARC: A DNS record in which a domain says how recipients should treat mail that fails SPF or DKIM checks. Like a notice on the mailbox: "Please don't accept mail without my stamp."
Our take Whether bug or quiet tightening: a DMARC record with p=none takes five minutes. If you look after customer domains or your own invoice mail, set it now.
[1] heise.de (Nov 9)

Caddy 2.11.8 and 2.11.9

Caddy 2.11.8 (Nov 4) brings a big hardening round, a few breaking changes and the log_level option. One day later came 2.11.9: it fixes a regression in the PHP directory index and brings FastCGI security fixes, including header overwriting and a path bypass on Windows. The release notes name no CVE numbers. The recommendation: "If you're on 2.11.8 and serve PHP, we recommend upgrading."[1]

Our take If you use Caddy only as a reverse proxy in front of containers, you can read the 2.11.8 breaking changes at your leisure and go straight to 2.11.9. If you serve PHP via FastCGI, do it first.

DockTail: containers into the tailnet via label

DockTail reads Docker labels such as docktail.service.enable and publishes the containers as Tailscale Services. You need only one Tailscale device per host, not one per container. Tailscale Services have been generally available since February 2026.[1]

Our take For homelabs and small customer setups this is exactly the right level: exposure as a label next to the container, instead of a sidecar per service.

CRA: the reporting obligation has run for two months

Since Sep 11, 2026, manufacturers under the Cyber Resilience Act must report actively exploited vulnerabilities: an early warning within 24 hours, the report within 72 hours. ENISA's Single Reporting Platform went live the same day. Most other obligations apply from Dec 11, 2027.[1][2]

Heads up These details come from a law firm's summary; we did not open the Commission's page ourselves today.
Our take Cases like SSHole show why the 24-hour deadline is tough for manufacturers: between fix and exploit lay four hours. Anyone selling software with digital elements in the EU needs a reporting path that also works on weekends.

What Tuesday is worth

First: check Alpine hosts with sshd on password login; the flaw is being attacked now. Second: work through the Patch Tuesday list in the afternoon. Third: if you look after customers with N-central, ask the provider. Everything else can wait until Thursday.

It's Tuesday, November tenth. Yesterday we told you the OpenSSH flaw wasn't in the catalog of actively exploited vulnerabilities. Since Monday evening, it is.

The flaw now has a name: SSHole. According to BleepingComputer, GreyNoise counted sixteen successful attack chains from six addresses, the first on November seventh. Exploit code showed up about four hours after the fix. Only Linux systems using the musl library, without PAM and with password login are affected, mostly Alpine in practice. Attackers then read out keys and set themselves up with their own SSH keys. The fix is OpenSSH ten point six p two, and the deadline for US agencies is Thursday.

At N-able, more than thirty alleged zero-day flaws in the N-central remote management platform have gone public, complete with sample code. N-able has taken the hosted platform offline and says there's no evidence of attacks. There are no CVE numbers yet.

CISA's catalog also gained three more flaws: in GPUStack, in the telephony software FreeSwitch, and in IBM Aspera Faspex. The deadline here is Thursday too.

Today is Microsoft's Patch Tuesday. At press time there were no numbers yet. In October, according to BleepingComputer, there were fourteen hundred eleven fixes. This evening, two new Xen advisories also go public.

According to heise, the firm Apiiro found two thousand five hundred thirty-nine real credentials in local configuration files of AI coding agents that ended up in public repos. It's an old problem in a new file: add it to your gitignore and use a secret scanner.

Outlook dot com has been rejecting mail from small senders without a DMARC record for about a week. A moderator on the Microsoft forum calls it an outage. A DMARC record takes five minutes, so it's best to set one now.

On releases: Caddy two point eleven point nine brings security fixes for FastCGI. PostgreSQL fourteen gets its final release on Thursday. And long-term support for Angular twenty ends on November twenty-eighth.
