Patch Tuesday: 1,235 Fixes, a Zero-Day in Outlook

On Nov 10, according to BleepingComputer, Microsoft fixed 1,235 flaws. That count includes 142 Edge/Chromium CVEs. Microsoft's own count is lower: 1,093 CVEs, 63 of them critical (Tenable). Not counted are mostly Azure Linux/Mariner (82), plus 25 in Edge and 1 in Entra. BleepingComputer rates 66 flaws as critical: 48 remote code execution, 15 privilege escalation and 3 use-after-free bugs in Edge.[1]

CVE-2026-124194, a spoofing flaw in Outlook, is already under attack. It lets attackers capture NTLM hashes. "Exploitation requires network connectivity and user interaction," writes BleepingComputer, so the attacker needs a click or an opened email.[1] CISA added the flaw to the KEV catalog on Nov 10; the deadline for US agencies is Nov 24.[2] Tenable gives CVSS 6.5 (Microsoft rates the flaw "Moderate").

NTLM hash: An encrypted form of the Windows password that is sent along when you sign in over the network. Whoever has the hash can often crack it or pass it on directly, without knowing the password. Like an impression of your front-door key: not the original, but often enough for a copy.
Our view Over 1,200 fixes sound like an emergency. For most teams, though, only one line counts: the Outlook flaw. Blocking outbound SMB (port 445) at the perimeter takes much of the sting out of NTLM theft like this. We did not check the Microsoft and Tenable numbers against Microsoft's own guide.
[1] bleepingcomputer.com (Nov 10) · [2] cisa.gov KEV feed (version 2026.11.10)

Chrome: the Year's Eighth Zero-Day

With Chrome 157.0.8103.50/.51 (Windows/macOS) and .50 (Linux), Google closes CVE-2026-125350, a use-after-free bug in the XML component. Google "is aware that an exploit for CVE-2026-125350 exists in the wild". According to BleepingComputer, it is the eighth attacked Chrome zero-day this year.[1] CISA has listed the flaw in the KEV since Nov 10, deadline Nov 24.[2] Chromium derivatives such as Edge, Brave or Vivaldi need their own update.

Use-after-free: A program frees memory and then keeps using it anyway. In the meantime, an attacker puts their own data there. Like a hotel room where the old guest still has a key after the next one has moved in.
[1] bleepingcomputer.com (Nov 10) · [2] cisa.gov KEV feed (version 2026.11.10)

Barracuda: Two Flaws, One Root Access

Two flaws in Barracuda devices are under attack: CVE-2026-116606 bypasses authentication in the Email Security Gateway (ESG), and CVE-2026-111560 is an OS command injection. "If exploited in combination, these vulnerabilities could allow an attacker to bypass authorization controls and obtain root access to the device." According to BleepingComputer, so far it affects "a small number of organizations". The patches are about a week old: BNSF-40425 for ESG from 9.4.017, BNSF-40430 for older versions.[1]

Heads up CISA's deadline is short: both flaws have been in the KEV since Nov 10, and the deadline is already Friday, Nov 13.[2] According to BleepingComputer, besides ESG it also affects other products such as WAF and ADC. The report gives no CVSS score; we did not read the vendor document ourselves.[1]
[1] bleepingcomputer.com (Nov 10) · [2] cisa.gov KEV feed (version 2026.11.10)

Xen Orchestra: An Empty Cookie Is Enough

In Xen Orchestra, the web interface for XCP-ng and Xen servers, a login bypass is being exploited (advisory VSA-2026-062). According to BleepingComputer, the trick is a cookie with the value authenticationToken=j:%7B%7D, an empty JSON object. After that, an attacker can "perform actions with 'Admin' privilege", which includes taking over VMs. TDC NET Security found it. It is fixed in 6.9.1 (stable) and 6.10.0, with xo-server 5.211.2 and 5.213.1 respectively. "A CVE identifier and CVSS score have not been assigned yet."[1]

Our view A hypervisor console does not belong on the open internet. If you make XO reachable only through VPN or Tailscale, you do not have this problem. To hunt for traces: grep the proxy logs for exactly this cookie value.
[1] bleepingcomputer.com (Nov 10)

Xen Advisories: The Four from Last Night

Yesterday we announced the Xen advisories for the evening. There turned out to be four. XSA-529 ("XAPI: data-dependent compare function used for Pool Secret", CVE-2026-102705) and XSA-530 ("XAPI: Multiple RBAC issues", five CVEs) came out at 18:00 UTC. XSA-531 and XSA-532 followed at 18:45 UTC: in Linux xen-netback, a guest can trigger a use-after-free or a double unmap of memory (CVE-2026-99286, CVE-2026-99287).[1] We have not checked the severity. If you run Xen hosts, wait for your distribution's kernel and XAPI packages.

SAP Patch Day: Four Times CVSS 10

In November SAP closes 35 flaws, four of them with the top score CVSS 10. Two together form "ICM2SHELL" (CVE-2026-76964, CVE-2026-89126). "PROXYGATE" (CVE-2026-89103) is an unauthenticated remote code execution in the gateway, and CVE-2026-120017 is missing authentication in NetWeaver's SAML2 Service Provider. Onapsis says of PROXYGATE: "no SAP user, no password, and no user interaction, yet full control of the SAP system". There is no mention of attacks yet.[1]

Our view SAP flaws often stay open for months because there is no maintenance window. That backfires: according to BleepingComputer, CISA has marked 14 SAP flaws as attacked since November 2021.[1] If you look after SAP customers, put the gateway fix in the next window, not the one after. The figures come from the press; we did not read SAP's own page.
[1] bleepingcomputer.com (Nov 10)

Qubole: The Trusted Access as an Entry Point

Between Oct 14 and 26, according to Wiz, attackers took over two IAM users of Qubole. Qubole is a data platform that reaches into its customers' AWS accounts through IAM roles. Through these roles (AssumeRole), the attackers got into the accounts of dozens of companies. Sentra confirms dozens of victims, and Mandiant took part in the investigation. BleepingComputer found no statement from Qubole. AWS says: "AWS services were not compromised by this issue and operated as designed."[1]

AssumeRole: An AWS mechanism that lets a foreign account take on a role in your account for a while. Like a master key for the cleaning company: handy, until someone steals the key from the cleaning company.
Our view AWS is right, and that is the problem: everything ran "as designed". Secure trust policies for third parties with an External ID and narrow permissions, and watch CloudTrail for AssumeRole calls from foreign accounts. A third party you no longer use loses its role right away.
[1] bleepingcomputer.com (Nov 10, AWS statement updated Nov 11)

KEV: Four Deadlines in Two Weeks

CISA's KEV catalog now has 1,779 entries (version 2026.11.10). On Nov 10, besides Outlook, Chromium and Barracuda, CVE-2026-111793 in Openwave Webtop Media Transcoder and CVE-2026-20320, an XXE flaw in Cisco BroadWorks, were added; deadline Nov 24.[1] Tomorrow, Nov 12, the deadline runs out for SSHole (CVE-2026-123158), GPUStack, FreeSwitch and IBM Aspera Faspex. For FreeSwitch, 1.11.3 helps; for Faspex, 5.0.19 PL1.[1][2] Older and still open: Apple CVE-2024-27834 until Nov 18 and SonicWall SSLVPN CVE-2026-19868 until Nov 20.[1]

XXE: An XML file asks the parser to load an "external entity", for example a local file. A badly configured parser obediently does so. Like a letter that says: "Please also read out the note in the drawer."

npm: Command Server in the Blockchain

On Nov 3, according to Sonatype, attackers published "runtime loaders in 12 releases across 10 hijacked npm packages". Sonatype calls the campaign PolinRider. The loader reads the last transaction of an Ethereum wallet and finds its server's address there. It runs when the module loads, not through an install script. Sonatype attributes the campaign to the Lazarus group. The page does not name the packages.[1]

C2 via the blockchain: Malware needs a command server (C2). Instead of hard-coding the address, it reads it from a public blockchain transaction. Nobody can delete that. Like a message on a bulletin board that no authority is allowed to take down.
Our view npm install --ignore-scripts does not protect you here, because the code only runs on require. What helps: lockfiles, a waiting period for new versions, and monitoring outbound connections to Ethereum RPC endpoints in the build.
[1] sonatype.com (Nov 3)

PostgreSQL 14: The End Is Tomorrow

"Final release is November 12, 2026," says the PostgreSQL versions page. After that, version 14 gets no more fixes. The current ones are 18.6, 17.11 and 16.15.[1] If you still run 14, apply the last minor release and plan the upgrade, ideally straight to 17 or 18 with pg_upgrade.

Delos Cloud: Government Cloud with Microsoft at Its Core

Delos Cloud, an SAP subsidiary with Microsoft software and operation by Arvato, wants to launch for German public administration in early 2027. heise puts it bluntly: „Bei der Delos-Cloud handelt es sich um eine Cloud, die im Kern Microsoft-Technologie beinhaltet.“ (Delos Cloud is a cloud that at its core contains Microsoft technology.) A working group of the Gesellschaft für Informatik speaks of „Souveränitäts-Washing“ (sovereignty washing). Delos is certified to ISO 27001; C5 Type 1 and 2 are planned.[1]

Our view A German operator changes nothing about whose code runs and who writes the updates. What is sovereign is what you can keep running without the vendor. Delos still has to show that.

CRA: Reporting Duty Runs, the Rest Comes in 2027

Since 2026-09-11, the reporting duty of the Cyber Resilience Act (Art. 14) applies: manufacturers report attacked flaws through ENISA's central platform. Most other obligations apply from 2027-12-11. The EU Commission published guidance on this on 2026-07-27.[1] Cases like today's Barracuda or Xen Orchestra are therefore reportable for manufacturers who sell into the EU.

What Wednesday Is Worth

Patch the Outlook flaw and Chrome today; the rest of Patch Tuesday follows at the normal pace. Check Barracuda and Xen Orchestra first if they are exposed to the internet. PostgreSQL 14 gets its last release tomorrow, and then the upgrade is no longer a question.

It's Wednesday, November eleventh. Yesterday we were missing the Patch Tuesday numbers. Now they're in, and they're big.

According to BleepingComputer, Microsoft is fixing one thousand two hundred thirty-five flaws this November, a count that includes 142 Edge flaws. Microsoft itself counts one thousand ninety-three. Sixty-six of them are rated critical. One flaw in Outlook is already under attack. It lets attackers capture encrypted Windows passwords, known as NTLM hashes. It does take a click from the victim, though. CISA has added the flaw to its catalog, and the deadline for US agencies is November twenty-fourth.

Chrome also has another attacked zero-day, the eighth this year. The bug sits in the XML component. Updating to version one fifty-seven closes it.

At Barracuda, two flaws together give an attacker root access to the email gateway. According to BleepingComputer, so far only a few organizations are affected. CISA's deadline is already Friday. And in Xen Orchestra, the web console for Xen servers, a cookie with an empty value is enough to get admin rights. That one is being exploited too. Versions six point nine point one and six point ten fix it.

SAP is closing thirty-five flaws in November, four of them with the top score of ten. There's no word of attacks yet. At N-able, the hosted N-central platform stays offline, and there's still no hotfix.

According to Wiz, attackers took over two accounts of the data platform Qubole in October and used its trusted roles to get into the AWS accounts of dozens of companies. AWS says everything worked as designed. That's exactly the problem.

In npm, Sonatype found ten hijacked packages. Their malicious code reads its server's address from the Ethereum blockchain and runs as soon as the module loads.

On releases: Spring Boot four point two is at its second release candidate. PostgreSQL fourteen gets its final release tomorrow. And in Germany, the controversial Delos Cloud, built on Microsoft technology, is due to launch in early twenty twenty-seven.
