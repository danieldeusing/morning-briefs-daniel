Start of the week: the update is the attack surface — from a drone maker to an editor extension

The weekend was quieter than the week before, but not empty. Two cases from Friday and Saturday show the same pattern: attackers don't break into the product, they break into the route the product takes to the customer. At the German drone maker Quantum Systems it was the update server; at Cursor it was the cache of the extension marketplace. On top of that, two KEV deadlines end today, and heise reports ongoing attacks on UnrealIRCd. Our view: if you check only one thing this week, check where your updates come from and who signs them.

Due today: UnrealIRCd and Check Point

The two KEV entries from 11/13 have their deadline today, Monday 11/16: Check Point "Multiple Products" CVE-2026-123039 and UnrealIRCd CVE-2026-114713. According to the catalog, no new entries have been added since 11/13.[1]

Update since Sun heise reports on Sunday: "Attackers are currently targeting a vulnerability in UnrealIRCd and attacking IRC servers." The developer calls it the "most critical bug in UnrealIRCd in 15 years."[2] An ordinary user account is enough, but at least two linked IRC servers are needed. The attacker can then run commands with server or IRCOp rights, such as disconnecting users or banning them network-wide. "Code execution on the underlying system is not mentioned, however."[2]

  • Fix: 6.2.7.1. The hot patch bundle-20261107 applies without a restart, but did not work on FreeBSD. 6.2.8-rc1 helps there.[2]
  • Version 5: also affected, no updates. Disable the labeled-response module on all servers, with a full restart; a rehash is not enough.[2]
  • On the side: The developer fixed nine security issues in total, found in an AI-assisted internal audit.[2]
IRCOp: The operator of an IRC network, in other words the admin with house rules. They can kick out and ban users. The flaw hands an ordinary guest this key ring.
Our view We covered the Check Point case in detail on Saturday. For UnrealIRCd, the FreeBSD note is the important one: if you applied the hot patch and run FreeBSD, you may not be protected at all. Check the version number, not the good feeling.

Supply chain: two updates that were not updates

Quantum Systems: poisoned QBase update

The German drone maker Quantum Systems has confirmed an attack. According to Spiegel and the Financial Times, attackers took over the update server and distributed a tampered update: "the compromise occurred via a malicious QBase update file".[1] QBase is the software for ground control and mission planning. The attack was discovered at the end of October. According to the reports, the Bundeswehr kept its drones on the ground as a precaution and pulled them from an exercise. How many customers installed the update is unclear.[1] MAD, the domestic intelligence service and the BSI are involved, according to the report; the company does not name a perpetrator.[1]

Cursor: marketplace cache as an entry point

Cursor has confirmed a breach that began at a service provider. Attackers obtained an access key from Fireworks AI, copied data from a shared storage and found unredacted API credentials there.[2] With them, on 10/29 they placed fake versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 of the Claude Code extension in the cache of Cursor's VS Code marketplace. The extension "installed an attacker-controlled SSH key" and set itself up permanently.[2] The versions were online for "approximately 11 hours"; according to Cursor, "up to 5,000 users" are affected. Anthropic's genuine extension and Open VSX were not affected.[2]

Update-channel attack: Instead of breaking into a thousand houses, the attacker swaps the master key at the caretaker service. After all, every customer lets that service in voluntarily. That is exactly why the update server is so valuable.
Heads up If you installed or updated the Claude Code extension in Cursor during that window, follow Cursor's instructions and check ~/.ssh/authorized_keys for unknown keys.[2] The tooling side of the story is in the ai-dev brief.
Our view Both cases teach the same lesson. Signed updates alone are not enough if the attacker sits at the distribution point or holds a key from a poorly protected bucket. Credentials do not belong unencrypted in shared storage, not even in "read-only" buckets. And a cache in front of a marketplace is a product of its own, with its own attack surface.
[1] bleepingcomputer.com (11/13) · [2] bleepingcomputer.com (11/14)

N-central: Hotfix 2 is not enough

Update since Fri Attacks with crypto miners are hitting N-central, the remote-management software from N-able. Researchers at Huntback counted "21 compromised N-central servers, 19 of them already on HF2".[1] The attack path "4EverAble2Shell" keeps working while the Legacy API is active. The next hotfix, HF3, is not out: N-able "has postponed the release because of additional testing".[1] The two main flaws, CVE-2026-125883 and CVE-2026-125881, are rated critical.[1]

  • Disable the Legacy API, apply HF2, update agents and probes.
  • N-able has switched off the Intune integration as a precaution and published IoCs.
  • If there are traces of an attack, assume full compromise: turning off the API does not undo a breach.[1]
Our view Remote management is the master key to all of an IT provider's customers. A miner is the harmless variant here. If you have a provider that runs N-central, ask today about the state of the Legacy API.
[1] heise.de (11/15)

Microsoft cloud: first the outage, then the phishing

From Thursday evening, Microsoft cloud services were disrupted, including Exchange Online and Teams, some until Saturday. Microsoft names as the cause an error during a network capacity expansion that only took effect after recent network changes. Microsoft rules out an attack.[1]

Criminals are now exploiting the outage. Fake status emails cite the real incident number EX1550543. They claim that held-back messages will be deleted within 24 hours unless you click. The subject follows the pattern "Outage – [six-character code]", and the links are forwarded via SendGrid.[1] Microsoft has so far been unable to "confirm either credential harvesting or delivery of malware", but the pattern matches classic password phishing.[1]

Our view A real incident number makes a fake email believable. The rule is simple: check status only in the Microsoft 365 Admin Center, never through a link in an email. It is a good chance to tell the team exactly that once more.
[1] heise.de (11/15)

PostgreSQL 19 on Thursday

"PostgreSQL 19 should be made generally available on November 19, 2026." Before that comes the 11/12 update: 18.7, 17.12, 16.16, 15.20 and 14.25. "This is the final release of PostgreSQL 14."[1]

  • Rebuild damaged GIN indexes.
  • With pg_trgm: rebuild GiST indexes using gist_trgm_ops.
  • Rebuild B-tree indexes on bpchar columns.
  • After incremental backups, take a new full backup.[1]
REINDEX: An index is the keyword directory of a table. If the old version built it incorrectly, the directory stays wrong, even after the update. REINDEX rebuilds it.
Our view If you are still on 14, go to 18, not to 19 on day one. 19 earns our trust with its first minor release. REINDEX ... CONCURRENTLY spares you the lock on large tables.

What Monday is worth

Three short tasks: On FreeBSD, check the UnrealIRCd version, not just the hot patch. In Cursor, check the version of the Claude Code extension and the SSH keys. And plan the REINDEX for PostgreSQL before Thursday.

It's Monday, November sixteenth. The weekend was quieter than the week before, but two cases point to the same pattern: attackers aren't breaking into the product, they're breaking into the route it takes to the customer.

At the German drone maker Quantum Systems, attackers took over the update server and pushed out a tampered update for the ground-control software, according to Der Spiegel and the Financial Times. The German military kept its drones grounded as a precaution.

At Cursor, the breach began at a service provider. Using stolen credentials, attackers planted a fake Claude Code extension in the marketplace cache. It was online for about eleven hours and could install an attacker's SSH key. As many as five thousand users may have received it.

Today two deadlines from the U.S. agency CISA run out, for Check Point and for the chat server UnrealIRCd. According to heise, UnrealIRCd is under active attack. The key point: the hot patch doesn't work on FreeBSD, where only the release candidate six point two point eight helps.

On the remote-management software N-central, nineteen of twenty-one compromised servers already had the hotfix installed. The next hotfix has been postponed, so until then the only remedy is to switch off the legacy interface.

After the Microsoft cloud outage that began Thursday, fake status emails are circulating with the real incident number. Check the status only in the admin center, never through a link in an email.

PostgreSQL 19 is due on Thursday. Before that, the November twelfth update is worth applying, with several index rebuilds. And through November twenty-fourth, four more CISA deadlines are running, including Outlook and Chrome.
